## 2. Required substantive revision: distinguish functional normativity from morality

**Severity: HIGH — must be repaired.**

**Locations:** lines 23, 155–159, 225–229, 275–279, and corresponding conclusion at 301.

The paper correctly distinguishes causal implementation from a fitted reward and correctly denies that a growth score implies an intention. But the crucial paragraph at line 227 responds to the normative challenge by saying that sustaining a capacity need not be “categorically good.” That is primarily a moral or all-things-considered evaluative distinction. It does not answer the stronger biological-function objection: why does a causal contribution to maintaining an organization establish how the mechanism *ought to function*, including a ground for genuine malfunction rather than failure relative to an investigator's selected task?

Corti's argument is explicitly about this latter issue. His §3 distinguishes goodness from correctness and identifies the biological functional question as how a trait ought to work, including its possibility of malfunction. The counterfactual dependence of continued existence on an activity does not, he argues, by itself establish that functional norm. The paper must not make his objection look answered merely by separating biological maintenance from categorical moral goodness. Primary source checked: Luca Corti, “Organizational normativity and teleology: a critique,” *Synthese* 202 (2023), article 96, §3, [doi:10.1007/s11229-023-04255-7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-023-04255-7).

Line 229 compounds the problem. It places the restoration mechanism and the population-consequence explanation together, then says “Calling them purposive need not attribute consciousness or moral standing.” Absence of consciousness and moral standing is not the missing condition. A causal explanation can exclude alternatives and fail under perturbations without yet being a purpose explanation. Otherwise any counterfactually stable causal contribution could acquire purpose simply by describing its effect as a capacity.

### Required repair

Keep four levels explicit:

1. **Descriptive causal contribution:** the routine restores executable cells; a program changes demographic outcomes.
2. **Grounded functional attribution:** design, a suitable selection history, or a sufficiently defended regulatory/organizational account supports calling an outcome the function of the activity.
3. **Intrinsic biological norm:** success and malfunction are standards for the organization itself rather than solely for an imposed task. This is the contested organizational-naturalization claim.
4. **Moral or practical reason:** an agent or community has a justified reason to pursue or preserve that end.

The finite source securely supports the first level. Its designed architecture and stated task can support a specified function at the second level, provided that basis is acknowledged. Its population comparison is not automatically a function, goal, or intention of an individual. It does not establish the third or fourth level simply by coupling repair to growth.

The paper need not add a complete theory of intrinsic normativity. It should state its stopping point and make the conditional account precise. The strongest positive claim can remain that **realized correction is a distinct causal achievement, and it can support a function claim when the appropriate independent ground is supplied**. That preserves the article's substance while avoiding a smaller version of the original optimization-to-purpose leap.

The literature reviewer independently identified the same problem. That review should supply the finer comparison with Bich's organism-level regulatory requirements and Cuciniello's question about the provenance of goals. The manuscript should not imply that this register model already meets those stronger standards.

**Acceptance test:** after revision, a reader should be able to classify the restoration and growth explanations at the appropriate levels without inferring that the paper has naturalized intrinsic biological normativity. The conclusion should not reintroduce the broader claim by using unqualified “purposive” for both.

## 3. Required scope revision: the contracting flow is not yet a complete ontological counterexample

**Severity: HIGH for historical and inferential accuracy; locally repairable.**

**Locations:** line 43, repeated at line 119.

The contracting flow on a contractible state space establishes that boundedness and fixed-point convergence do not entail nontrivial first homology of that state space. This is a valid and useful mathematical separation. The stronger wording that it “defeats the universal topological necessity,” however, needs a bridge from the modeled flow to the original claim about an object's state-space **or interaction network**.

The original LaTeX, line 74, explicitly offers those two locations for its cycle. A physical realization of a contractible state-space flow can still have a cyclic interaction graph. Moreover, whether mere bounded flow satisfies the philosophically relevant persistence predicate cannot be settled just by describing the flow as contracting. The example therefore does not, by itself, rule out every causal or network-loop reading of the original ontology.

### Required repair

Narrow the conclusion to the implication actually refuted: **stable or invariant state dynamics alone do not entail nontrivial state-space homology**. Then say that transferring this result to objecthood or organizational closure requires an additional realization claim. The stronger original postulate remains unsupported as a universal criterion, but its alternatives should not be refuted by equivocation between state space and interaction graph.

The paper already has an adequate further argument: externally driven geometric cycling need not regenerate its driving conditions, while regulation can maintain a capacity without a nontrivial geometric orbit. Use that distinction to explain why trajectory topology, graph feedback, and regenerative dependencies answer different questions. No new universal “absence of all loops” counterexample is needed.

**Acceptance test:** lines 43 and 119 should state a mathematically exact separation and avoid pretending that all topological realizations of the original universal claim have been disproved by this one differential equation.

## 4. Recommended strengthening: make the common-witness contribution explicit

**Severity: MEDIUM — strengthen the argument rather than add further disclaimers.**

**Locations:** lines 25, 95–103, 235–241, 267–271.

The paper honestly admits that its fibre condition is elementary. Its strongest claim of added value lies elsewhere: independent demonstrations of predictive sufficiency, repair, or optimality do not establish that the same system can meet all three. The positive source supplies a common witness under shared resources, information, body state, and inherited execution rules.

This is present at lines 239 and 271, but is not sharply formulated as the positive explanatory demand. The reader may still conclude that the paper's entire result is “retain what an explanation needs.” The compatibility argument can be stated more directly: separately finding a repairing system and a growth-optimal system does not entail that one admissible system repairs and attains that optimum; the same-source construction addresses this conjunction while the representation comparisons identify which purported conjunctions silently change bearer or task.

This is a modest conceptual point, not a new logical theorem. It nevertheless gives the paper an organized reason to include its substantial model detail. Emphasizing it would connect the main normative discipline of explanation to the positive mechanism rather than letting the construction look like an extended illustration of a tautology.

**Suggested implementation:** one concise paragraph in the central synthesis, and a slightly sharper statement of the contribution in the introduction. Do not introduce another grand name or claim to have solved the general compatibility problem.

## 5. Recommended correction: do not manufacture a falsification test for an analytic condition

**Severity: MEDIUM.**

**Location:** line 297, with implications for the abstract's “defeasible discipline.”

The suggested failure condition says that explanatory entitlement could fail if a stronger claim were justified without preserving any distinction on which it depends. Under the paper's own definition of dependence, and allowing supplementation as at line 99, this looks contradictory rather than a substantive counterexample condition. It does not make an analytic fibre principle genuinely risky.

Separate three statuses instead: the factorization condition is analytic; identifying which distinctions an explanation actually needs is contestable; the realist and purposive interpretations of the model are defeasible and require evidence. The scope and value of the synthesis can be criticized even when the elementary implication is sound. This is more candid and sharper than proposing a merely verbal falsifier.

**Suggested implementation:** revise line 297 to identify failures of relevance attribution, claimed realization, or claimed superiority over an alternative explanation. Do not suggest that every philosophical contribution must be empirically falsifiable in the same way as a physical hypothesis.
