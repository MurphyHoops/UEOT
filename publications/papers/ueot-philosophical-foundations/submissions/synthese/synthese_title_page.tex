\noindent\textbf{Abstract (235 words):} When does predictive adequacy justify ontological claims about what persists as an individual or teleological claims about what its activity is for? Across computational mechanics, active inference, and pattern realism, predictive compression and Markov blankets are widely treated as guides to ontology and purpose. Within a self-contained stochastic model of damage, restoration, and reproduction, we establish two core separations. First, undamaged and self-repairing conservative founders induce the exact same probability law over the entire infinite sequence of future births while paying zero versus three resource units in initial repair. Across the four healthy and damaged conservative and proliferative founders executing their hereditary programs autonomously at fixed model parameters, adding a second birth census already exhausts the infinite-horizon birth law---separating latent programs while erasing repair expenditure---whereas adjoining initial cost measurement separates paid from unpaid histories while leaving damaged programs merged. Different evidence extensions thus induce incomparable refinements over protocol probability laws rather than single runs (where individual trajectories still overlap), showing that which evidence is added determines which explanatory question becomes identifiable. Second, benchmarked against the full public-history growth ceiling, autonomous proliferative execution can maximize expected lineage multiplication while lowering individual survival, whereas subcritical persistence preserves only frozen tokens with finite cumulative expected births. Governed by a typed principle of explanatory preservation, we calibrate three exclusionary checks for relational objecthood and stratify four bearer-indexed grounds of purpose separating conditional regulatory standards, prospective growth, and retrospective selection.
