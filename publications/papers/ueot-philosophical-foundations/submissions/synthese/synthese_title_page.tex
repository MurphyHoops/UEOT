\noindent\textbf{Abstract (196 words):} Across computational mechanics, predictive processing, active inference, and pattern-based metaphysics, predictive compression and statistical boundaries are widely treated as privileged guides to what persists as an individual and what its activity is for. This article argues that observational and predictive sufficiency systematically underdetermines both diachronic objecthood and purposive explanation. Underlying our formal results is a forward-marginalization asymmetry: predictive compression marginalizes over past causal pathways to retain forward output distributions, erasing the counterfactual invariance, restorative expenditure, and genealogical contrasts required for individuation and teleology. Governed by a typed principle of explanatory preservation, we defend a three-criterion relational realism and a four-rung bearer-indexed account of purpose within a self-contained stochastic regenerative model. First, minimal predictive quotients vary non-monotonically with protocol scope—yielding two, three, or four classes on the same four initial configurations—and couple infinite-horizon birth streams while erasing realized repair expenditure. Second, decoded symbolic instructions fail to determine executable agency without physical gate constraints, while partially observed viability separates finite-memory from reactive control and resists fixed-sample zero-risk certification. Third, proliferative investment can maximize expected lineage growth while reducing individual survival. Fourth, scalar and dual-criterion scores underdetermine functional norms, yet paid restoration and full-policy growth attainment are jointly realizable.
