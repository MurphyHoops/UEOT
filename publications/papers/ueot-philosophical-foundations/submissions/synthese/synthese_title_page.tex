\noindent\textbf{Abstract (194 words):} When does predictive adequacy justify ontological claims about what persists as an individual or teleological claims about what its activity is for? Across computational mechanics, active inference, and pattern realism, predictive compression and Markov blankets are widely treated as guides to ontology and purpose. Within a self-contained stochastic model of damage, restoration, and reproduction, we establish two core separations. First, undamaged and self-repairing conservative individuals induce the exact same probability law over the entire infinite sequence of future births while paying zero versus three resource units in initial repair. Extending birth observations separates latent hereditary programs while erasing repair expenditure; adjoining initial cost measurement separates paid from unpaid histories while leaving those programs merged. Different evidence extensions thus induce incomparable predictive refinements, showing that which kind of evidence is added determines which explanatory question becomes identifiable. Second, benchmarked against the full public-history growth ceiling, autonomous proliferative execution can maximize expected lineage multiplication while lowering individual survival. Governed by a typed principle of explanatory preservation, we calibrate three exclusionary criteria for relational objecthood---tested against symmetrical fission and reciprocal constraint closure---and stratify four bearer-indexed grounds of purpose separating conditional regulatory standards, prospective growth, and retrospective selection.
