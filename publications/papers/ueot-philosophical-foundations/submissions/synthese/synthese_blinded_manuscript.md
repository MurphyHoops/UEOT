# Introduction: The explanatory transfer problem

Consider an engineered or biological device that suffers internal damage to an active instruction register and restores that register by majority vote from surviving internal copies before generating its next external output. Compare it with an otherwise similar device whose active register was never damaged. Suppose that, once restoration is complete, the two devices induce exactly the same probability distribution over all subsequent observable outputs. For the task of predicting future output streams, recording whether initial damage occurred is strictly redundant: an optimal predictive compression merges the undamaged and restored histories into a single equivalence class. For reconstructing whether *this* individual bearer incurred a three-unit metabolic or energetic repair expenditure, however, that erased distinction is decisive. Now suppose further that switching from a conservative maintenance mode to a proliferative mode increases the expected number of descendants per cycle while lowering the probability that the original individual survives intact. Describing the proliferative mode as "promoting the system's persistence" or "serving the organism's adaptive optimum" is systematically ambiguous. Its truth value depends on whether the bearer of the explanation is the focal individual, an enduring organizational form re-instantiated across tokens, or an expanding lineage counted at a future census.

These cases exemplify a pervasive methodological hazard across philosophy of science, cognitive science, and theoretical biology, which we call the *explanatory transfer problem*. Over the past three decades, formidable formal frameworks have converged on predictive sufficiency, data compression, and long-run statistical optimization as foundational tools for individuating systems and explaining their directedness. In metaphysics and philosophy of science, pattern realism ties ontological commitment to non-redundant predictive compression [@dennett1987; @dennett1991; @ladymanRoss2007; @wimsatt2007]. In complex systems and reinforcement learning, computational mechanics and predictive state representations define minimal internal states as equivalence classes of histories sharing conditional future distributions [@crutchfieldYoung1989; @shaliziCrutchfield2001; @littmanSuttonSingh2001; @barnettCrutchfield2015]. In cognitive neuroscience and theoretical biology, predictive processing and the free-energy principle move even more ambitiously from statistical conditional independence—formalized via Markov blankets—to claims about autonomous individuality, self-organization, and active goal-seeking [@friston2010; @friston2013; @clark2013; @parrPezzuloFriston2022]. Meanwhile, inverse reinforcement learning and evolutionary optimization routinely characterize purposeful behavior by fitting scalar value or fitness functions under which observed trajectories emerge as optimal [@puterman1994; @ngRussell2000].

Each of these formalisms achieves genuine success within its home domain. Selective representation is indispensable to science: an output predictor is not defective because it omits internal energetic accounting, and a demographic branching model is not defective because it aggregates retained parents with newborn offspring. Trouble begins when success in a predictive or aggregative task is illicitly transferred to underwrite a conclusion about causal realization, diachronic persistence, executable agency, or functional purpose whose truth conditions depend on distinctions that the predictive representation has quotiented away. Recent critiques of Markov-blanket metaphysics [@raja2021; @bruineberg2022] and organizational normativity [@corti2023; @bich2024; @cuciniello2026] have exposed fragments of this gap from different angles, either questioning whether statistical boundaries demarcate physical agents or asking whether causal self-maintenance suffices for teleological norms. Yet what remains missing is a unified formal and philosophical diagnosis of *exact* information loss across predictive, interventional, genealogical, and evaluative descriptions—paired with constructive conditions showing when these descriptions genuinely cohere in one and the same process.

This article supplies that diagnosis and constructive synthesis. Underlying our formal results is a structural *forward-marginalization asymmetry*: predictive sufficiency marginalizes over past causal pathways and internal mechanisms to retain only equivalence classes of forward observable distributions, whereas diachronic objecthood and purposive explanation depend on counterfactual perturbation stability, paid restorative work, and bearer-specific selective or regulatory contrasts—precisely the modal, energetic, and genealogical distinctions that forward marginalization erases. Consequently, object individuation and purposive attribution must be governed by a typed *principle of explanatory preservation*: a representation adequate for predicting a declared family of observables licenses an inference about persistence, causal mechanism, or purpose only if—together with explicitly stated auxiliary premises—it preserves the invariants required by that target claim at its stated grain of precision. Neither predictive equivalence nor behavioral optimality delivers ontology or teleology for free. Instead:

1. **Constrained relational objecthood.** Against both substance essentialism and pure predictive instrumentalism, an organized process warrants defeasible realist commitment as an enduring individual to the extent that its internal relations jointly satisfy three exclusionary criteria: *(i) non-trivial predictive–control compression* across declared observation protocols, *(ii) counterfactual invariance under targeted structural and environmental interventions* [@hacking1983; @woodward2003; @craver2007], and *(iii) realization-cost and constraint accountability*, whereby at least one enabling boundary or operational constraint is endogenously maintained rather than passively inherited or externally imposed [@jonas1966; @rosen1991; @montevilMossio2015].
2. **Bearer-indexed grounds of purpose.** Against both scalar-optimization imperialism and eliminativist skepticism, purposive explanation is neither a relabeling of stationary distributions nor a monolithic biological primitive. Every well-formed functional or purposive claim is indexed to a quadruple—*diachronic bearer, contrast class, causal realization, and evaluative ground*—stratified across four non-interchangeable levels: *(Rung 1) systemic causal contribution* [@cummins1975], *(Rung 2) endogenous regulatory and organizational viability* [@rosenblueth1943; @mossio2009; @diPaolo2005; @bich2024], *(Rung 3) historical stabilization via differential retention or differential reproduction* [@wright1973; @millikan1984; @godfreySmith1994; @garson2019], and *(Rung 4) reflective practical and moral normativity* [@korsgaard1996; @scanlon1998; @street2006].
3. **Constructive joint realizability.** Warnings against conflation would be methodologically sterile if repair, resource accounting, internal execution, and long-run demographic performance could only ever be exhibited in disconnected toy models. By constructing a self-contained stochastic regenerative model $\mathcal{M}$ and proving all formal claims directly (with complete derivations in the Appendix), we show both *where* predictive and evaluative reductions provably fail and *exactly when* paid physical restoration, gate-mediated internal execution, genealogical continuity, and optimal expected lineage growth are jointly realized by a single damaged founder and its descendants.

The paper proceeds in six steps. Section 2 formalizes protocol-indexed predictive equivalence, establishes the typed preservation and belief-quotient conditions, and defends our three-criterion account of relational objecthood against pure compressionism and Markov-blanket metaphysics. Section 3 introduces the unified stochastic regenerative model $\mathcal{M}$, proving the two–three–four task-partition separation, repair-cost erasure, and the von Neumann separation between decoded symbolic information and physical gate execution. Section 4 distinguishes dynamical stability, cybernetic servocontrol, and organizational constraint closure, establishes finite-memory viability separation and fixed-sample risk-certification impossibility, and resolves diachronic bearer ambiguities across token retention, lineage branching, and fission. Section 5 proves the underdetermination of scalar and dual-criterion objectives, derives the exact full-policy expected growth ceiling $\rho_* = \max\{1,\lambda_0,\lambda_1\}$ alongside organism–lineage and deadline conflicts, and develops the four-rung stratification of purposive grounds in dialogue with contemporary organizational and etiological theories. Section 6 applies this discipline to diagnose cross-bearer and cross-grain fallacies in active inference, causal emergence, and evolutionary teleology, replies to four major philosophical objections, and outlines severe experimental discrimination protocols. Section 7 concludes.

# Predictive equivalence, sufficient quotients, and relational objecthood

## Protocol-indexed predictive minimality and its limits

To see precisely what successful prediction establishes—and what it leaves out—we begin with the strongest general case for predictive state construction. Let $(\mathcal{H}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}, \mu)$ be a probability space of realized system histories up to a present decision point, and let $\mathcal{I}$ be a countable index set of declared observation or open-loop test protocols. Each protocol $i \in \mathcal{I}$ specifies a measurable outcome space $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ and a Markov response kernel $K_i: \mathcal{H} \times \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i} \to [0,1]$ assigning to each history $h \in \mathcal{H}$ its conditional probability law $K_i(h, \cdot)$ over future outcomes under protocol $i$ [@blackwell1953; @kallenberg2021]. Define the *canonical predictive representation* relative to the protocol suite $\mathcal{P} = \{K_i\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}$ as the product map

$$
C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) := \bigl(K_i(h, \cdot)\bigr)_{i \in \mathcal{I}}.
$$

Two histories $h, h' \in \mathcal{H}$ are *predictively equivalent* under $\mathcal{P}$, written $h \sim_{\mathcal{P}} h'$, if and only if $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = C_{\mathcal{P}}(h')$, meaning that they induce identical outcome distributions across every declared test in $\mathcal{P}$.

**Proposition 1 (Countable-protocol predictive minimality).** *Suppose $\mathcal{I}$ is countable and each outcome space $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ is countably generated, equipping the space of probability measures $\Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ with its canonical evaluation $\sigma$-algebra. Let $q: (\mathcal{H}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}) \to (\mathcal{Z}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Z}})$ be any measurable statistic that is predictively sufficient for $\mathcal{P}$ on $(\mathcal{H}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}, \mu)$, in the sense that for every $i \in \mathcal{I}$ there exists a measurable kernel $\widetilde{K}_i: \mathcal{Z} \times \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i} \to [0,1]$ satisfying $K_i(h, \cdot) = \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), \cdot)$ for $\mu$-almost every $h \in \mathcal{H}$. Then there exists a measurable decoder $\psi: \mathcal{Z} \to \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ such that $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = \psi(q(h))$ for $\mu$-almost every $h \in \mathcal{H}$.*

Proposition 1 (proved in Appendix A.1) generalizes the classical minimal-sufficiency results of computational mechanics [@crutchfieldYoung1989; @shaliziCrutchfield2001, Theorems 1–3], predictive state representations [@littmanSuttonSingh2001], and input–output $\varepsilon$-transducers [@barnettCrutchfield2015] to arbitrary countable protocol families. Once an investigator fixes the reference process $\mu$ and the protocol suite $\mathcal{P}$, the canonical representation $C_{\mathcal{P}}$ is not an arbitrary convention: every sufficient compression must factor through $C_{\mathcal{P}}$ up to $\mu$-null sets, and any coarser map destroys predictive accuracy on at least one test in $\mathcal{P}$.

Yet three mathematical and philosophical boundaries are built directly into Proposition 1, and overlooking any of them invites ontological error:

First, *measure-theoretic regularity is non-trivial*. Countability of $\mathcal{I}$ is indispensable: on an uncountable protocol index, the union of protocol-specific $\mu$-null sets need not be $\mu$-null, and on an uncountable product space the cylindrical $\sigma$-algebra governs only countably many coordinates at a time [@kallenberg2021]. Moreover, while Proposition 1 requires only measurable response kernels once $\{K_i\}$ are supplied, deriving well-behaved recursive Bayesian updates from an underlying latent stochastic process requires standard Borel state and observation spaces so that regular conditional probabilities exist uniquely almost everywhere [@astrom1965; @kaelbling1998]. Nothing in predictive minimality assigns canonical values to counterfactual branches of $\mu$-measure zero that the system never visits under its observational regime.

Second, *conditioning on supplied inputs is not causal intervention*. Input–output transducers [@barnettCrutchfield2015] and partially observed Markov decision processes [@kaelbling1998] index future distributions by action sequences $a_{1:T}$. Mathematically, however, passive conditional probabilities $\mathbb{P}(Y_{1:T} \mid H = h, A_{1:T} = a_{1:T})$ coincide with interventional laws $\mathbb{P}(Y_{1:T} \mid H = h, \mathrm{do}(A_{1:T} = a_{1:T}))$ only under non-confounding and non-anticipatory causal assumptions connecting the action-selection channel to latent disturbances [@pearl2009; @woodward2003]. If an external controller peeks at contemporaneous environmental noise before selecting an action, or if surgical replacement resets a hidden physical state rather than evolving from the terminal state of $h$, observational transducer equivalence breaks apart from interventional equivalence.

Third, and most fundamentally, *predictive classes are response types, not diachronic individuals*. Two spatially separate machines built in different centuries can occupy the exact same canonical state $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h)$. One organism can migrate across distinct predictive classes as it ages, suffers injury, and recovers. Enlarging the protocol suite from $\mathcal{P}$ to $\mathcal{P}' \supset \mathcal{P}$ strictly refines $\sim_{\mathcal{P}'}$ without splitting one physical organism into two. Confusing a predictive equivalence class with an enduring entity mistakes an invariance of conditional expectation for numerical or historical identity [@wiggins2001].

## Typed explanatory preservation and quotient descent

When, then, may an investigator legitimately use a compressed representation $q: \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{Z}$—obtained via predictive sufficiency, coarse-graining, or state aggregation—to support a further scientific or philosophical explanation? Let $F: \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{V}$ be the semantic invariant or target quantity required by the intended conclusion. At the level of exact set-theoretic attribution from $q(s)$ alone, a map $\bar{F}: \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{V}$ satisfies $F = \bar{F} \circ q$ if and only if $F$ is constant on every fiber $q^{-1}(z)$ for $z \in q(\mathcal{S})$. Taken in isolation, this elementary factorization lemma is analytic. Its philosophical bite emerges only when we recognize that scientific explanations target *different modal and probabilistic types* of invariants, and that representations routinely succeed at one type while silently violating preservation for another.

To turn factorization into a substantive methodological discipline, we formulate the *Typed Principle of Explanatory Preservation*. Before transferring warrant from a representation $q$ to a target explanation $\mathcal{E}$, one must explicitly specify the target tuple $\langle \mathcal{B},\, F,\, \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{prec}},\, \mathcal{I}\rangle$—identifying the diachronic bearer $\mathcal{B}$, target invariant $F$, precision mode $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{prec}}$, and admissible intervention class $\mathcal{I}$—and verify preservation at the corresponding tier:

1. **Exact token-level attribution.** If $\mathcal{E}$ asserts a determinate realized property of a specific token history $h$—such as whether *this* device paid a repair cost at $t=1$, or whether *this* token survived to $t=2$—then the joint evidence $(q(h), E_{\mathrm{aux}}(h))$ must be fiber-constant with respect to that realized value on the support of admissible histories.
2. **Distributional and aggregate attribution.** Scientific explanations frequently target an ensemble expectation or probability law rather than a realized token value. Suppose $q$ merges two micro-histories $h_0, h_1$ whose repair expenditures are $0$ and $3$, respectively. From $q(h)$ alone, exact token-level expenditure is unidentifiable. Yet if justified auxiliary evidence supplies a prior assigning probability $1/2$ to each branch within that fiber, the combined representation $(q, \mu_{\text{fiber}})$ *does* preserve the population fee distribution $\frac{1}{2}\delta_0 + \frac{1}{2}\delta_3$ and expected expenditure $3/2$. Preservation forbids promoting that aggregate expectation into an individual historical reconstruction; it does not outlaw statistical macro-explanation.
3. **Closed-loop belief and control descent.** When a coarse observation map $\pi_O: \mathcal{O} \to \widetilde{\mathcal{O}}$ compresses raw observations in a partially observed control process, preserving one-step expectations is insufficient for multi-step agency. As formalized in Proposition 2, a predictive or control quotient descends coherently across horizons only under dynamic operator closure [@burkeRosenblatt1958; @kemenySnell1960; @givanDeanGreig2003].
4. **Counterfactual and interventional transport.** If $\mathcal{E}$ invokes what would happen under structural ablations or action changes $\mathrm{do}(\iota) \in \mathcal{I}$, the quotient $q$ must commute with every interventional transition kernel $P^{\mathrm{do}(\iota)}$, not merely with the passive observational kernel $P^{\mathrm{obs}}$ [@pearl2009; @woodward2000].

**Proposition 2 (Exact belief-quotient descent via closed test spaces).** *Let $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{O}, T, O)$ be a finite partially observed Markov control model with belief simplex $\Delta(\mathcal{X})$, and let $\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$ be a linear subspace of state-test functions inducing the belief equivalence $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} b'$ iff $\langle b, f\rangle = \langle b', f\rangle$ for all $f \in \mathcal{F}$. For each action $a \in \mathcal{A}$ and observation $o \in \mathcal{O}$, define the unnormalized one-step Bayes–Bellman update operator $(U_{a,o} f)(x) := \sum_{x' \in \mathcal{X}} T(x' \mid x, a)\, O(o \mid a, x')\, f(x')$. Suppose $\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$. Then the quotient $\Delta(\mathcal{X})/{\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}}$ jointly preserves (i) one-step observation probabilities $p(o \mid b, a)$ and (ii) normalized Bayesian posterior equivalence $\mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o) \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \mathrm{Bayes}(b',a,o)$ across all positive-probability branches $(a,o)$ if and only if $\mathcal{F}$ is invariant under $U_{a,o}$ for all $a \in \mathcal{A}$ and $o \in \mathcal{O}$.*

Proposition 2 (proved in Appendix A.1) pinpoints why static data compression so often fails dynamic agency: two belief states can agree on every immediate reward or one-step output expectation ($f \in \mathcal{F}_0$) while diverging after one observation update because $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F}_0 \not\subseteq \mathcal{F}_0$. Preservation across time requires stability under the composition of causal transition and observation.

## Against pure compressionism and blanket metaphysics: three criteria for relational objecthood

With Proposition 1 and Proposition 2 in hand, we can situate our ontological proposal within contemporary debates over realism and individuation. Constructive empiricists hold that predictive adequacy over observables exhausts science's epistemic warrant [@vanFraassen1980], whereas epistemic and ontic structural realists argue that mathematical relations preserved across theory change capture genuine worldly structure [@worrall1989; @ladyman1998]. Seeking a naturalized middle ground for special-science entities, Dennett's [-@dennett1991] *Real Patterns* and Ladyman and Ross's [-@ladymanRoss2007] *Rainforest Realism* tie real objecthood directly to projectibility and non-redundant information compression: a pattern is real if tracking it yields better-than-bit-map predictive leverage. In a parallel biological register, proponents of the free-energy principle propose that any random dynamical system possessing a stationary distribution with a *Markov blanket*—a statistical partition into internal, external, sensory, and active variables rendering internal and external states conditionally independent given sensory and active blanket states (typically paired with sparse coupling excluding direct internal-to-external and external-to-internal links)—thereby individuates an autonomous, self-evidencing object [@friston2013; @parrPezzuloFriston2022].

Both compressionism and blanket metaphysics capture an important negative truth: enduring natural objects need not be microphysical Lockean substances with unchanging intrinsic essences [@whitehead1929; @dupre1993; @dupreNicholson2018]. Yet both suffer from an underdetermination problem that predictive equivalence alone cannot cure. As @bruineberg2022 and @raja2021 forcefully demonstrate, *Pearl blankets* (conditional independence relations in an investigator's graphical model) do not automatically delineate *metaphysical or physical boundaries*: a purely passive linear diffusion process, two uncoupled pendulums observed through a shared linear combination, or an arbitrary slice of a stationary Gaussian field can exhibit exact conditional-independence blankets without possessing any physical membrane, causal cohesion, or self-maintaining agency [@biehl2021]. Likewise, pure Dennettian compression admits too much in one direction and too little in another: a clever macro-variable can compress observations of an epiphenomenal shadow, while—as we prove in Section 3—an exact infinite-horizon output compression can erase the very internal restoration work that keeps an object intact.

We therefore defend *Constrained Relational Objecthood*: an organized process in a domain warrants defeasible realist recognition as a distinct diachronic object—rather than an arbitrary mereological fusion or observer-projected artifact—when three jointly constraining criteria are met:

1. **Non-trivial predictive–control compression.** Relative to independently specified observation and interaction protocols, tracking the candidate organization yields genuine sufficiency and dimensionality reduction over raw micro-histories (Proposition 1), surviving triangulation across multiple measurement channels [@wimsatt2007; @ladymanRoss2007].
2. **Counterfactual and interventional invariance.** The organization's internal dependencies remain invariant across a non-degenerate range of physical interventions $\mathrm{do}(\iota)$ [@hacking1983; @woodward2003], supporting mutual manipulability between whole-system capacities and constituent operations [@craver2007] rather than mere observational correlation.
3. **Realization-cost and constraint accountability.** Where diachronic persistence through material or informational turnover is claimed, the process physically accounts for the energetic, material, or informational expenditure required to maintain or restore its enabling constraints [@jonas1966; @rosen1991; @montevilMossio2015].

Crucially, these three criteria support *pluralism under worldly constraint* [@cartwright1999; @dupre1993]. Choosing which phenomenon to measure is pragmatic; whether a candidate organization satisfies predictive sufficiency, survives physical perturbation, and balances its restorative budget is an objective, empirically defeasible fact about the world.

# A unified stochastic regenerative model of repair, execution, and reproduction

In much of the philosophical literature, predictive compression, self-repair, genealogical continuity, and evolutionary optimality are analyzed using separate, stylized illustrations—an $\varepsilon$-machine here, a chemical autocatalyst there, a branching process somewhere else. That fragmentation hides the sharpest question: *how do predictive quotients, physical implementation constraints, and demographic benchmarks interact within one and the same causal mechanism?* To answer this with complete formal transparency, we construct a self-contained finite stochastic process $\mathcal{M}$ (fully specified with proofs in Appendix A.4–A.5) in which every state variable, physical gate, resource unit, genealogy tag, and transition probability is explicit.

## Physical architecture, internal programs, and resource accounting

At any discrete census $t \in \mathbb{N}$, a living individual (or *token*) in $\mathcal{M}$ is a physical register assembly equipped with five structural components:

- **Two redundant instruction rows ($r_H, r_D$).** Row $r_H$ encodes the action to execute when diagnosed healthy ($H$), and row $r_D$ encodes the action to execute when diagnosed damaged ($D$), where each binary action bit designates either conservative maintenance (`care`, bit $0$) or proliferative investment (`bloom`, bit $1$). Crucially, each row consists of *three physical cells*: one *active cell* read directly by the execution hardware, and two *backup cells*.
- **Physical execution gate ($g \in \{\mathrm{open}, \mathrm{closed}\}$).** Raw action execution physically requires $g = \mathrm{open}$; if $g = \mathrm{closed}$, active-cell bits cannot actuate proliferative hardware even if their symbolic values happen to look intact.
- **Bootstrap restoration unit ($b_{\mathrm{boot}} \in \{0,1\}$).** When $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$, an internal majority-vote circuit can read the three cells of each row, overwrite damaged active cells with the majority bit, and reopen the gate $g$.
- **Resource wallet and six-unit transaction budget.** Every surviving or newborn token enters a census carrying $1$ baseline resource unit and receives a prescribed environmental inflow of $5$ units, yielding a strict per-step budget of $6$ units. Internal majority-vote repair costs $3$ units; `care` execution costs $1$ unit; `bloom` execution costs $2$ units; and $2$ units must be held in escrow to provision up to two potential surviving/offspring tokens ($1$ unit each) for the next census. Unspent escrow is cleared at census closure.
- **Genealogical identity and silent physical tag ($\tau$).** Every token carries a historical lineage marker distinguishing *parental retention* (the same token surviving from $t$ to $t+1$) from *strict birth* (a newly formed offspring token first executing at $t+1$), plus a causally inert physical label $\tau \in \mathcal{T}$ (analogous to isotopic or spatial tagging) that enters no transition or cost rule.

Two canonical *hereditary internal programs* will serve as our primary comparison class: $\theta_0 = (\mathrm{care}, \mathrm{care})$, whose intact active and backup rows all store $0$, and $\theta_1 = (\mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{care})$, whose healthy row stores $1$ and damaged row stores $0$. Standard environmental damage flips both active bits while leaving backup bits and $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$ intact, and closes the execution gate ($g = \mathrm{closed}$); the token's internal diagnostic state $d \in \{H, D\}$ is read directly from its own gate-and-parity hardware rather than supplied by an external oracle. Upon any executed step, surviving and newborn tokens first replicate the intact or newly restored three-cell rows with $g = \mathrm{open}$ before encountering independent post-step active-cell lesion with probability $q$ (Figure 1a). Consequently, when a damaged token ($D$) carrying $\theta_0$ or $\theta_1$ undergoes paid restoration, it spends $3$ of its $6$ budget units to recover its true active rows via majority vote and reopen $g = \mathrm{open}$. Because only $3$ units remain and $2$ must be escrowed for potential outputs, *only `care` ($1$ unit) is physically affordable after repair*: $3 + 1 + 2 = 6$. By contrast, if a damaged body requests `bloom` ($2$ units), total required prepayment ($3 + 2 + 2 = 7 > 6$) exceeds the six-unit budget; the transaction hardware *rejects* the unaffordable request, refunds the $5$-unit inflow, preserves the damaged token unchanged with $1$ unit, and does not expose it to that step's actuation noise. Finally, an explicit `stop` action terminates the token with zero outputs.

## Task-relative predictive partitions and the erasure of restorative cost

Consider now the four canonical single-founder initial configurations $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 H, \theta_1 D\}$ operating autonomously under their stored internal programs in the proliferative regime $\gamma > \delta$ and $s + b > 1$ (where `bloom` yields a net expected output gain $\gamma - \delta > 0$ and `care` is supercritical). Let $B_t \in \mathbb{N}$ denote the population-wide count of *strict births* at census $t$ (excluding retained parents), and let $R_1 \in \{0, 3\}$ denote the founder's realized repair expenditure at $t=1$. How many minimal predictive states (Proposition 1) does $\mathcal{M}$ possess? As Proposition 3 demonstrates, the question has no protocol-free answer.

**Proposition 3 (Two–three–four task-dependent predictive separation).** *Fix any parameter vector $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q)$ in the strict regime with $\gamma > \delta$ and $s + b > 1$, and restrict $\mathcal{M}$ to autonomous internal execution from the four founder configurations $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 H, \theta_1 D\}$. Then:*

1. *For predicting the first-step strict-birth count $B_1$, the canonical predictive partition has **two** minimal classes: $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$ (each having $B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(b)$) and $\{\theta_1 H\}$ (having $B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(b+\gamma)$).*
2. *For predicting the complete joint birth record $(B_1, \dots, B_T)$ across all finite horizons $T \ge 1$ (and hence the infinite birth process $(B_t)_{t \ge 1}$), the canonical predictive partition has **three** minimal classes: $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 D\}$, and $\{\theta_1 H\}$. Specifically, $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ induce identical joint laws on $(B_t)_{t \ge 1}$, whereas $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ diverge at $t=2$ with $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)(1-q)\gamma > 0$. At $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$, $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = 27/64$ whereas $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] = 39/64$.*
3. *For predicting the joint pair $(B_1, R_1)$ at $t=1$ alone, the canonical partition again has **three** classes—$\{\theta_0 H\}$, $\{\theta_1 H\}$, and $\{\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$—incomparable with the three-class partition in (2). Only when $R_1$ is adjoined to multi-step birth records $(B_1, B_2, \dots)$ does the canonical partition refine to **four** singleton classes.*

Proposition 3 (proved in Appendix A.4) makes the explanatory transfer problem unmistakable. Look first at the collapse of $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ in Row 2 of Table 2. Because the damaged $\theta_0 D$ founder pays $3$ units at $t=1$ to majority-restore its registers and then executes the exact same `care` transition as $\theta_0 H$, their post-repair output configurations can be coupled path-by-path for all future time $t \ge 1$. Even an omniscient observer recording the *entire infinite-horizon sequence of reproductive outputs* $(B_1, B_2, \dots)$ assigns $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ to the exact same causal state. Yet $\theta_0 H$ spent $R_1 = 0$ on self-repair while $\theta_0 D$ spent $R_1 = 3$. ## Lawful tag quotients versus von Neumann's syntactic–constructor gap

Our preservation framework is not an indiscriminate plea for microphysical maximalism. Within $\mathcal{M}$, erasing the causally silent physical tag $\tau$ defines a non-injective projection $\pi_\tau$ that merges physically distinct raw tokens while *commuting exactly* with step transitions, six-unit budget accounting, repair expenditures, genealogical parent/offspring labels, and expected outcomes under every permitted public-history policy. Eliminating $\tau$ is a paradigm of *lawful explanatory compression*.

**Proposition 4 (Lawful silent-tag lumpability versus decoded-sequence gate separation).** *(a) Projecting raw assemblies in $\mathcal{M}$ by erasing the silent tag $\tau$ defines a strong Markov lumpability commuting with step transitions, six-unit budget feasibility, repair expenditures, genealogical labels, and expected outcomes across all policies in $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$. (b) Projecting assemblies onto their majority-decoded symbolic program $\theta \in \{\theta_0, \theta_1\}$ (even together with row-parity agreement) while erasing the physical execution gate $g \in \{\mathrm{open}, \mathrm{closed}\}$ fails to determine raw executable transitions: two assemblies with identical intact three-cell rows encoding $\theta_1 = (\mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{care})$ diverge under raw actuation without gate-opening repair—executing `bloom` when $g = \mathrm{open}$ (strict-birth probability $b+\gamma$) while blocking actuation when $g = \mathrm{closed}$.*

Proposition 4 (proved in Appendix A.4) exposes why two superficially plausible informational compressions fail catastrophically. First, projecting a token onto its stored program $\theta$ while erasing its diagnostic state ($H$ vs. $D$) destroys action affordability: `bloom` is feasible on $H$ ($4 \le 6$) but rejected on $D$ ($7 > 6$). Second, as Proposition 4(b) establishes, projecting a token onto its *decoded symbolic instruction* while erasing the physical gate variable $g \in \{\mathrm{open}, \mathrm{closed}\}$ destroys raw executable capacity even when every row cell is bit-for-bit intact.

This provides a crisp stochastic realization of John von Neumann's [-@vonNeumann1966] foundational insight into self-reproducing automata. Von Neumann showed that biological and computational self-reproduction requires separating the *passive syntactic description* (tape $I$, copied uninterpreted during replication) from the *active physical constructor* (mechanism $A+B+C$, gated and energized to translate instructions into causal work) [@rosen1991; @deacon2011]. Purely informational definitions of agency or genetics that equate an organism's state with its decoded sequence erase the physical enabling constraints—gates, polymerase machinery, metabolic ATP budgets—that turn syntax into causal execution. Microbial physiology illustrates both separations concretely: in bacterial methyl-directed DNA mismatch repair, hemimethylated template strands preserve backup sequence information while ATP-consuming MutS–MutL–MutH complexes restore damaged active strands and reopen stalled replisome progression; two daughter cells with identical post-repair division distributions nevertheless differ in depleted intracellular ATP reserves ($R_1$), and unmethylated sequence identity without replisome gate clearance fails to support replication.

# Persistence, organizational closure, and diachronic bearers

## Separating dynamical stability, cybernetic servocontrol, and constraint closure

A recurring temptation in systems theory and natural philosophy is to search for a single topological or dynamical signature—such as a closed phase-space orbit, non-trivial first homology $H_1 \neq 0$, or existence of an ergodic stationary density—that characterizes all persistent objects [@friston2013]. Careful separation of dynamical, cybernetic, and biological mechanisms shows why any such universal signature conflates four fundamentally distinct modes of persistence (Table 3).

First, *passive dynamical invariance* requires neither topological loops nor restorative work. Let $(\mathcal{X}, d)$ be a metric space carrying a continuous semiflow $(\varphi_t)_{t \ge 0}$. Whenever a forward orbit $\{\varphi_t(x_0) : t \ge 0\}$ is precompact and eventually resides inside a closed domain $D \subseteq \mathcal{X}$, its dynamical $\omega$-limit set $\omega(x_0) = \bigcap_{s \ge 0} \overline{\{\varphi_t(x_0) : t \ge s\}}$ is non-empty, compact, and strictly $\varphi$-invariant. Yet on the contractible Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^n$ (where $H_1(\mathbb{R}^n) = 0$), the simple dissipative flow $\dot{x} = -x$ stays inside every closed ball around the origin and converges monotonically to $\omega(x_0) = \{0\}$ without any geometric cycle, feedback sensor, or component replacement. Conversely, a frictionless harmonic oscillator traces closed orbits with non-trivial loop topology on its energy shell while performing zero self-repair if perturbed onto a neighboring shell. Phase-space homology and attractor stability simply do not track organizational self-maintenance.

| 1. Passive dynamical invariance | Conservative law or dissipative potential ($\dot{x}=-x$; crystal lattice) | Perturbation shifts orbit or equilibrium; zero endogenous restoration of constraints | Topological dynamics; classical mechanics |
| 2. Externally scaffolded servocontrol | Fixed sensor–actuator negative feedback regulating target variable | Regulates state variable, but cutting sensor/heater triggers zero self-rebuild of hardware | @rosenblueth1943; @conantAshby1970 |

| 4. Adaptive / finite-memory viability | History-sensitive policy shifts modes to remain inside viability kernel $K^\star$ | Modulates behavioral trajectory before lethal boundary contact; separates $m \ge 1$ from $m=0$ | @aubinCellina1984; @diPaolo2005; @bich2024 |

Second, *externally scaffolded cybernetic servocontrol* [@rosenblueth1943; @conantAshby1970] introduces genuine error-correcting feedback: a thermostat or Watt governor senses deviations of room temperature or shaft speed from a reference range and actuates a compensating response. By Conant and Ashby's [-@conantAshby1970] Good Regulator principle, effective disturbance rejection constrains the controller's mapping from disturbances to actions. Yet in an ordinary servomechanism, every physical constraint of the controller itself—its bimetallic strip, wiring, and casing—is externally manufactured and passively endures on a slower timescale. The thermostat regulates *room temperature*; it does nothing to regenerate *itself*.

Third, *organizational constraint closure*—rooted in Kant's [-@kant1790, §§64–65] analysis of *Naturzwecke* as beings in which parts reciprocally produce and maintain one another, and developed through autopoiesis [@varela1974; @maturanaVarela1980; @weberVarela2002], $(M,R)$-systems [@rosen1991], autocatalytic work–constraint cycles [@kauffman1993; @deacon2011], and closure of constraints [@montevilMossio2015]—requires a stricter constitutive loop. A collection of constraints $\mathcal{C} = \{c_1, \dots, c_k\}$ exhibits constraint closure when the thermodynamic or informational processes channeled by $c_i$ actively regenerate or repair at least one other enabling constraint $c_j$ that would otherwise degrade on the timescale of inquiry [@jonas1966; @thompson2007]. Our regenerative model $\mathcal{M}$ captures a minimal, fully transparent instance of this third regime without pretending to total autopoietic self-creation: its active instruction cells and execution gate $g$ are enabling constraints on future metabolic action that suffer stochastic degradation ($q > 0$) and are physically reconstructed from internal backups using budgeted energetic inflow, while still relying on trusted primitives ($b_{\mathrm{boot}}$, environmental supply).

Fourth, as @diPaolo2005 demonstrated in critiquing binary autopoiesis (where an organization either persists or dies without gradation), genuine agency requires *adaptivity*: the capacity to monitor graded proximity to viability boundaries [@aubinCellina1984] and modulate internal or interactive modes accordingly [@barandiaran2009; @bich2024].

## Interventional vulnerability, finite-memory viability, and fixed-sample limits

How should organizational dependence be tested without sneaking universal topological assumptions through the back door? Fix a target capability certificate $\mathcal{C}$ over a horizon $T$ (for example, positive probability of restored execution) and a declared downward-closed repertoire $\mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathcal{E})$ of structural component deletions $D \subseteq \mathcal{E}$ [@woodward2003; @craver2007]. Let $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I}) \subseteq \mathcal{I}$ denote the family of finite deletion sets in $\mathcal{I}$ under which $\mathcal{C}$ fails.

**Proposition 5 (Minimal interventional cut-set reconstruction).** *Suppose $\mathcal{I}$ is closed under subsets and failure of $\mathcal{C}$ is monotone with respect to deletions in $\mathcal{I}$ (if $D \in \mathcal{F}$ and $D \subseteq D' \in \mathcal{I}$, then $D' \in \mathcal{F}$). Then $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$ is uniquely determined by its antichain of inclusion-minimal destructive cut-sets $\mathcal{V}_{\min}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$: a deletion $D \in \mathcal{I}$ destroys $\mathcal{C}$ if and only if $M \subseteq D$ for some $M \in \mathcal{V}_{\min}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$.*

Proposition 5 (proved in Appendix A.2) turns organizational integrity into a crisp interventional hypergraph while exposing three crucial caveats. First, monotonicity is a substantive causal hypothesis, not a logical truism: in biological networks with inhibitory pathways or latent backups, ablating an inhibitor can *restore* a blocked function, violating monotonicity. Second, $\mathcal{V}_{\min}$ is indexed to $\mathcal{I}$: if allowed interventions perturb only a causally decoupled variable $u$ while capability $q(x,u) = x$ depends solely on $x$, then $\mathcal{V}_{\min} = \emptyset$—not because the system is indestructible, but because the test repertoire missed its causal support. Third, one must distinguish *erasing observational readout access* from *destroying the underlying mechanism*: cutting a telemetry wire blinds the observer's certificate while leaving endogenous repair intact.

In $\mathcal{M}$, interventional ablations isolate three distinct enabling conditions for self-repair: *(i) cutting the bootstrap unit* ($b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 0$) leaves backups and funds intact but blocks internal restoration; *(ii) clamping the repair-stage balance to $2 < 3$ units* leaves hardware intact but starves paid restoration; and *(iii) erasing all three cells of both rows* destroys the conserved mutual information [@coverThomas2006] so that no deterministic decoder can recover both $\theta_0$ and $\theta_1$ from the post-damage state. None of these failures can be diagnosed from passive output histories prior to perturbation.

Two further mathematical results—formalized in Proposition 6 (proved in Appendix A.2)—reveal why maintaining viability in partially observed environments outstrips memoryless reactivity and finite black-box sampling:

**Proposition 6 (Finite-memory viability separation and fixed-sample risk impossibility).**
*(a) Let $K^\star = \operatorname{gfp}(F_S) \subseteq \mathcal{X}$ be the maximal viable state kernel of a finite controlled transition system inside a safe set $S \subseteq \mathcal{X}$, where $F_S(U) = \{x \in S : \exists a \in \mathcal{A},\ \mathrm{supp}\,T(\cdot \mid x,a) \subseteq U\}$. Even though $K^\star$ is a subset of states, under partial observation $h: \mathcal{X} \to \mathcal{O}$ there exist five-state systems in which **every** (possibly randomized) memoryless observation policy $\pi_0: \mathcal{O} \to \Delta(\mathcal{A})$ exits $S$ within two steps with probability $1/2$, whereas a deterministic **one-step finite-memory controller** $\pi_1: \mathcal{O} \times \mathcal{O} \to \mathcal{A}$ maintains perpetual viability $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_1}(X_t \in K^\star\ \forall t \ge 0) = 1$.*
*(b) Fix any finite sample size $n \ge 1$ and significance level $\alpha \in [0, 1)$. Let $\psi: \{0,1\}^n \to [0,1]$ be any (possibly randomized) statistical certification rule mapping $n$ independent indicator draws of out-of-support violation events $Z_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(r)$ to a certification probability $\psi(Z_{1:n}) \in [0,1]$. If $\psi$ respects the zero-violation soundness bound $\mathbb{E}_r[\psi(Z_{1:n})] \le \alpha$ for all positive violation rates $r \in (0,1]$, then by continuity $\mathbb{E}_0[\psi(Z_{1:n})] \le \alpha$; conversely, no fixed-$n$ black-box test can certify $r = 0$ with probability $1-\beta > \alpha$ while uniformly bounding false certification across arbitrarily small positive hazard rates $r = \varepsilon > 0$ [@leCam1973; @mayo2018].*

Philosophically, Proposition 6 closes two major loopholes. Part (a), illustrated in Figure 2a, shows why internal memory registers—extending an agent's effective horizon beyond instantaneous sensory surfaces [@sethTsakiris2018; @kaelbling1998; @papadimitriouTsitsiklis1987]—can be *constitutively necessary* for viable persistence even when the underlying safe region $K^\star$ is state-defined. Part (b) proves why purely inductive, model-free prediction from a finite training sample cannot by itself certify strict structural safety or organizational closure: without mechanistic prior bounds on transition support, arbitrarily small unvisited failure modes remain statistically indistinguishable from zero risk over $n$ trials.

## Token persistence, genealogical lineages, and symmetrical fission

Even when physical restoration and viability are established, *whose* persistence has been explained? Processual and immunological philosophers of biology rightly emphasize that organisms persist through continuous metabolic and cellular turnover rather than static material identity [@whitehead1929; @pradeu2012; @dupreNicholson2018], while evolutionary theorists show that biological individuality splinters across physiological, immunological, and Darwinian selection criteria [@clarke2010; @godfreySmith2009; @maynardSmithSzathmary1995].

Our regenerative process $\mathcal{M}$ makes this split formally explicit by recording *parental token retention* and *strict offspring birth* as distinct causal events. When a healthy $\theta_0 H$ founder survives with probability $s$ and produces a healthy newborn with probability $b(1-q)$, the retained parent and the newborn subsequently possess identical physical registers, identical predictive classes, and identical forward capabilities. Yet they are numerically distinct tokens with distinct causal histories: one continued an existing metabolic trajectory; the other originated in a reproductive branching event [@wiggins2001].

Symmetrical fission sharpens the boundary of purely causal criteria [@parfit1984]. Suppose an amoeba or duplicator divides cleanly into two daughters $y_1, y_2$, each equally causally and informationally continuous with the predecessor $x$. Because $y_1 \neq y_2$, numerical identity—which is symmetric and transitive—cannot hold between $x$ and both daughters. Any theory must choose among three conventions: selecting a privileged continuation (asymmetrical parent/bud convention, as in $\mathcal{M}$), holding that $x$ ceases to exist at division while initiating two new tokens (clonal binary fission), or shifting the explanatory bearer from an individual token to a *genealogical lineage*. Predictive equivalence across future outputs is completely silent among these three options.

Furthermore, conditioning a branching or absorbing Markov process on long-run non-extinction alters its probability law in a mathematically subtle way that teleological theories often misread. For an absorbing chain on a transient viable set, the *quasi-stationary distribution* (QSD) $\nu_{\mathrm{QSD}}$—the limiting state distribution $\lim_{t\to\infty} \mathbb{P}(X_t \in \cdot \mid \tau_{\mathrm{ext}} > t)$ among trajectories still alive at $t$—differs strictly in general from the stationary law $\varpi(x) \propto \nu_{\mathrm{QSD}}(x) h(x)$ of the *Doob $h$-transformed survivor process* conditioned on surviving forever, weighted by reproductive or survival value $h(x)$ [@doob1957; @darrochSeneta1965; @collet2013]. In $\mathcal{M}$'s marked genealogy, weighting lineages by reproductive value yields a valid statistical many-to-one identity across ancestral paths, yet that reweighted kernel neither represents uniform sampling of living individuals nor defines a causally executable policy available to a single body. As @cuciniello2026 observes, organizational teleology cannot slide from maintaining a present token to preserving a cross-generational form without accounting for this shift of diachronic bearer.

# Optimization, bearer divergence, and the grounds of purpose

## Underdetermination of evaluation: reward shaping, dual-criterion gauge, and rank obstructions

Turn now from objecthood to our second overarching question: when does a successful representation of a system's dynamics justify claiming that an activity occurs *for* an outcome? A pervasive strategy in decision theory, behavioral ecology, and cognitive science is *optimality attribution*: exhibit a scalar reward, utility, or free-energy functional under which the system's observed behavior is optimal [@puterman1994; @friston2010]. Four formal obstructions show why behavioral optimization, taken alone, never identifies an endogenous purpose.

First, *inverse optimality is massively underdetermined*. As @ngRussell2000 [§3.1] established for Markov decision processes, any observed policy is trivially optimal under the constant zero reward, and in discounted dynamic programming [@blackwell1965] adding any potential-based shaping term $r'(x,a,x') = r(x,a,x') + \beta \psi(x') - \psi(x)$ leaves the set of optimal policies completely invariant while altering state valuations arbitrarily. Even more starkly, fixing a set of physically feasible transitions and bare observational evidence leaves opposite evaluative rankings over initial actions logically compatible unless an independent functional or historical premise constrains the objective.

Second, *preferences and viability constraints need not admit any scalar representation*. Classic representational measurement theory proves that numerical utility exists only under completeness, transitivity, and Archimedean continuity axioms [@krantz1971]. When an organism or institution faces genuinely incomparable multidimensional survival and epistemic constraints—a non-total preorder over admissible trajectories—no real-valued scalar objective preserves both strict improvements and genuine incomparabilities. Even where a scalar criterion exists, optimal control defines a *set-valued correspondence* $\mathcal{A}^\star(z) \subseteq \mathcal{A}$ rather than a unique direction: at exact price-and-discount thresholds, active repair and passive idling tie strictly.

Third, *combined resource–information functionals suffer affine gauge non-identifiability and local rank obstructions*. Many unified theories of agency—including active inference's expected free energy, which combines pragmatic/extrinsic value with epistemic/intrinsic information gain [@parrPezzuloFriston2022], and thermodynamic–informational trade-offs—postulate a composite objective $J = R - \lambda I$ balancing a physical or metabolic return $R$ against an informational functional $I$ at trade-off rate $\lambda > 0$. (Note that $I$ here denotes task-specific uncertainty or information cost, not Tononi's [-@tononi2004] causal-partition integrated information $\Phi^{\mathrm{IIT}}$, which measures irreducible whole-over-parts cause–effect repertoire structure.) Whenever downstream behavior or model selection depends only on the composite score $J$, the affine gauge transformation

$$
R' = R + \lambda \chi, \qquad I' = I + \chi
$$

leaves $J' = R' - \lambda I' = J$ identically unchanged for any state function $\chi$: observing composite optimality cannot disentangle metabolic drive from epistemic drive without independent physical and informational anchors. Worse still, one cannot assume that multidimensional behavioral responses always factor smoothly through a universal two-axis $(R, I)$ bottleneck:

**Proposition 7 (Local rank-three obstruction to universal smooth two-axis factorization).** *In the regenerative process $\mathcal{M}$ under autonomous $\theta_0 = (\mathrm{care},\mathrm{care})$ execution from a healthy state with fixed $(\delta, \gamma)$, consider the observable one-step response map $\Psi(s, b, q) := (s,\, b,\, bq)$ recording parental retention probability, strict-birth probability, and joint probability of birth with damaged offspring over the open parameter domain $U = \{(s,b,q) : 0 < \delta < s < 1,\ 0 < b < 1-\gamma,\ 0 < q < 1\}$. The Jacobian matrix $D\Psi(s,b,q)$ has determinant $\det D\Psi(s,b,q) = b > 0$ everywhere on $U$. By the chain rule, $\Psi$ cannot factor on any non-empty open subset of $U$ as $g \circ f$ through $C^1$ maps $f: U \to \mathbb{R}^2$ and $g: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{R}^3$.*

Proposition 7 (proved in Appendix A.3) blocks any attempt to treat two-dimensional resource–information coordinates as an a priori universal bottleneck for organized processes.

## Exact growth ceiling, joint realization, and organism–lineage conflict

What happens, instead, when we derive demographic performance *directly from the physical transactions, six-unit budget, and hereditary code* of $\mathcal{M}$ rather than fitting an ex post reward? Recall from Table 1 that one-step expected total output (retained parent plus strict newborn) is $k_c := s + b$ under `care` and $k_b := s - \delta + b + \gamma$ under `bloom`. Because each surviving or newborn token enters the next census damaged with probability $q$ (where repair forces `care`) and healthy with probability $1-q$, the two hereditary programs $\theta_0 = (\mathrm{care},\mathrm{care})$ and $\theta_1 = (\mathrm{bloom},\mathrm{care})$ carry intrinsic per-capita multiplication rates

$$
\lambda_0 := k_c = s + b, \qquad \lambda_1 := (1 - q) k_b + q k_c.
$$

Let $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ denote the full class of *admissible public-history policies*: at each census $t$, the controller may use the entire observable history of diagnoses, public transaction receipts, and population counts—together with arbitrary joint randomization across living tokens—to issue action requests (`care`, `bloom`, `stop`), but may *not* peek at contemporaneous actuation/damage noise before acting or read causally silent tags. Each living token at census $t$ occupies an independent local six-unit resource site ($1$ carried escrow unit plus $5$ local inflow units), contrasting open per-capita resource regimes with globally capped finite habitats where exponential branching $\rho_* > 1$ is bounded by carrying capacity. Let $N_t$ denote total living population size at census $t$.

**Theorem 1 (Full-policy expected growth ceiling and same-process joint realization).** *Fix any parameter vector $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q)$ in the strict probabilistic domain of Table 1 and any finite non-empty canonical initial population $P_0$. Define*

$$
\rho_* := \max\{1,\, \lambda_0,\, \lambda_1\}.
$$

1. **Uniform upper ceiling and optimality.** *There exists a finite constant $C > 0$ (depending only on the parameters and $P_0$, independent of $\pi$ and $t$) such that $\mathbb{E}_\pi[N_t] \le C \rho_*^t$ for every admissible policy $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ and every finite census $t \ge 0$. Moreover, an explicit stationary policy achieves two-sided bounds $c_1 \rho_*^t \le \mathbb{E}[N_t] \le c_2 \rho_*^t$ ($c_1 > 0$), so $\rho_*$ is the least positive uniform exponential upper base across $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$.*
2. **Exact same-process joint realization from a damaged founder.** *Starting from a single damaged founder carrying stored program $\theta_i$ ($i \in \{0,1\}$), paid majority-vote physical restoration ($R_1 = 3$), gate-mediated internal execution of the restored code, faithful hereditary transmission, and two-sided attainment of the full-policy growth ceiling $\rho_*$ hold **jointly in $\mathcal{M}$** if and only if:*

$$
\begin{aligned}
\theta_0\text{ repairs and attains }\rho_* &\iff \gamma \le \delta \ \text{and}\ k_c \ge 1,\\
\theta_1\text{ repairs and attains }\rho_* &\iff \gamma \ge \delta \ \text{and}\ \lambda_1 \ge 1.
\end{aligned}
$$

Theorem 1 (proved in Appendix A.5) delivers both our constructive compatibility benchmark and three sharp philosophical separations concerning purpose and persistence:

First, *implementation semantics shape what counts as optimal* (Figure 2b). Why does the constant $1$ appear inside $\rho_* = \max\{1, \lambda_0, \lambda_1\}$? Because in subcritical environments where both $\lambda_0 < 1$ and $\lambda_1 < 1$ (Regime I of Figure 2b), repeatedly submitting the unaffordable `bloom` request on a damaged token triggers transaction rejection, preserving that single token without actuation noise ($N_t \equiv 1 = 1^t$)—analogous to metabolically dormant bacterial persister cells surviving antibiotic or starvation regimes that drive actively dividing phenotypes ($\lambda_0, \lambda_1 < 1$) toward extinction, whereas iteroparous maintenance (`care`, Regime II) and semelparous or high-fecundity proliferative investment (`bloom`, Regime III) dominate distinct ecological regions. Far from asserting a universal law that inactivity prevents decay, Regime I exposes how optimal-control conclusions depend on the physical semantics of arrested actuation: if rejected requests suffered background attrition, the unit floor would vanish.

Second, *proliferative optimization splits the organism from the lineage*. Whenever $\gamma > \delta > 0$ and $\lambda_1 \ge 1$, Theorem 1 certifies that $\theta_1$ uniquely attains $\rho_* = \lambda_1 > \lambda_0$. Yet on every healthy token, executing `bloom` *strictly reduces* that individual's one-step survival probability from $s$ to $s - \delta$ in exchange for raising strict births from $b$ to $b + \gamma$. What maximizes expected lineage proliferation actively endangers the individual organism performing it. This furnishes an exact mechanistic counterpart to multilevel selection theory [@okasha2006; @maynardSmithSzathmary1995] and to Garson's [-@garson2019] crucial distinction between *differential retention* (persisting longer as the same token) and *differential reproduction* (multiplying copies). Calling `bloom` "adaptive for persistence" without indexing the bearer conflates two opposing causal tendencies.

Third, *asymptotic growth rates diverge from both finite-deadline expectations and single-population stochastic survival*. Evaluate $\mathcal{M}$ at the critical rational point $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (2/3, 1/8, 1/4, 1/4, 1/3)$. Here $k_c = 11/12$, $k_b = 25/24$, and $\lambda_1 = \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{25}{24} + \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{11}{12} = 1 = \rho_*$. Starting from a damaged $\theta_1 D$ founder (which must repair and run `care` at $t=1$), expected population size is $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1}[N_t] = 11/12 < 1$ at *every* positive census $t \ge 1$, whereas repeated transaction refusal maintains $N_t = 1$ on every path—even though both share the exact asymptotic exponential base $\rho_* = 1$. Moreover, even in the strictly supercritical regime $(3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$ where $\lambda_1 = 29/24 > \lambda_0 = 9/8 > 1$, starting two independent healthy founders ($\theta_1 H$ and $\theta_0 H$) yields an exact first-census event of probability $(1 - (s-\delta))(1 - (b+\gamma))(1 - (1-s)(1-b)) = \frac{3}{8}\cdot\frac{3}{8}\cdot\frac{27}{32} = 243/2048 \approx 0.1187$ in which *the higher-growth $\theta_1$ lineage goes completely extinct at $t=1$ while the slower $\theta_0$ lineage survives*. At that same first census, the unconditional expected $\theta_1$-share (setting $0/0 := 0$) is $2095/4096 \approx 0.5115$, the share conditioned on non-extinction is $2095/4006 \approx 0.5230$, and the ratio of expected counts $\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(1)}]/\mathbb{E}[N_1]$ is $10/19 \approx 0.5263$ [@price1970; @athreyaNey1972]. High expected growth guarantees neither pathwise victory in one finite population nor interchangeability of expectations and ratios; probabilistic selection requires explicit quantification over a finite horizon $T$ and $n$ independent cohorts.

## Four stratified grounds of purpose and the normativity challenge

How, then, are functional and purposive attributions legitimately grounded? In classical philosophy of biology, Cummins's [-@cummins1975] *causal-role analysis* explains how a part contributes to a system-level capacity, whereas *selected-effects theories* [@wright1973; @millikan1984; @neander1991; @godfreySmith1994] explain *why* the part exists via ancestral selection—grounding malfunction norms at the price of classifying newly arisen homeostatic traits as non-functional despite full causal efficacy ("Swampman" cases). Seeking an ahistorical yet normative third way, *organizational accounts* [@mossio2009; @montevilMossio2015] ground a trait's function in its contribution to maintaining a closed regime of constraints on which the trait's own existence depends, recently enriched by Bich's [-@bich2024] regulatory-control criterion and scrutinized by @corti2023 and @cuciniello2026.

Corti's [-@corti2023, §3] critique cuts to the heart of organizational teleology—echoing Bedau's [-@bedau1992] challenge regarding telic value and Davies's [-@davies2001] causal-role deflationism: *why does the purely causal fact that a closed system will cease to exist unless component $C$ performs activity $A$ generate an objective biological norm that $C$ **ought** to perform $A$ (so that failure counts as genuine malfunction rather than mere state transition)?* Dismissing Corti's objection by saying "biological functions do not entail moral obligations" misses his target: Corti challenges *intrinsic biological functional correctness* itself, not just ethics.

Our preservation and bearer-indexing framework resolves this impasse not by postulating a magical derivation of categorical oughts from bare causal loops, but by *stratifying four distinct evidential grounds* that teleological language conflates (Table 4):

Look at what Table 4 clarifies about both Corti's challenge and our regenerative model $\mathcal{M}$:

1. **Meeting Corti and Bedau honestly at Rung 2.** @corti2023 is entirely right that *mere existential dependence* does not yield *unconditional or categorical* normativity: a self-sustaining hurricane or autocatalytic toxin has no unconditional claim to persist, and if a perturbation shifts a closed network into a *different* stable regime, static closure alone cannot say whether the shift is "malfunction" or "innovation" [@cuciniello2026, §§4–6]. What Rung 2 *does* establish—when constraint closure is paired with dedicated state-sensitive regulatory architecture [@nagel1977; @diPaolo2005; @bich2024; @walsh2015]—is an *objective counterfactual asymmetry internal to the system's viability*: in $\mathcal{M}$, the diagnostic sensor, majority-vote restorer, and execution gate $g$ exist specifically in a causal configuration that detects departures from intact rows and expends three resource units to reinstate the pre-damage code. In an engineered realization of $\mathcal{M}$, that reference standard is fixed by explicit construction design; in a living cell's DNA mismatch-repair pathway, that reference standard is stabilized jointly by Rung 2 regulatory architecture and Rung 3 selective history. 2. **Why Rung 2 and Rung 3 cannot be collapsed—and why bearer indexing reconciles them.** Because healthy `bloom` degrades Rung 2 token viability ($s - \delta < s$) while advancing Rung 3 lineage selection ($\lambda_1 > \lambda_0$), organizational teleology (Rung 2) and evolutionary selected-effects teleology (Rung 3) are *not rival definitions of a single relation*. They answer purposive questions indexed to *different bearers* (the physiological organism vs. the Darwinian lineage; @godfreySmith2009) or *different retention processes* (synchronic persistence vs. neural/ecological differential retention; @garson2019). By contrast, $\mathcal{M}$'s growth rate $\rho_*$ in isolation—prior to cohort selection history (Rung 3) or internal representation of population size (which tokens in $\mathcal{M}$ wholly lack)—is a *demographic performance metric*, not an intrinsic intention of the individual body.
3. **Protecting Rung 4 autonomy from evolutionary and institutional survivalism.** Even when Rungs 1–3 all align—as in a self-reproducing social institution or evolved behavioral disposition—persisting or out-reproducing rivals never automatically generates a Rung 4 practical or moral reason to endorse that persistence [@korsgaard1996; @street2006; @scanlon1998]. Predatory institutions can exhibit formidable constraint closure; autonomous agents can reflectively sacrifice individual or lineage survival for justified commitments [@list2019] (Figure 3).

# Explanatory discipline, competitor diagnostics, and testable discriminations

## Diagnosing cross-bearer and cross-grain fallacies across contemporary programs

The value of a philosophical framework lies in the inferential errors it catches and the rival insights it integrates without distortion. Applying Typed Explanatory Preservation alongside Bearer-Indexed Purpose diagnoses three live patterns of overreach in contemporary science:

**Active inference and Markov-blanket pan-teleology.** In active inference [@friston2010; @friston2013; @parrPezzuloFriston2022], systems possessing a non-equilibrium steady-state density $p^*$ with a Markov blanket are frequently described as *acting to minimize surprisal* $-\ln p^*(o)$ *via its variational free-energy upper bound* and therefore *pursuing self-evidencing persistence*. Our separations isolate three distinct leaps inside that transition. First, a passive Pearl blanket in observational coordinates need not correspond to an interventional physical boundary or an open/closed actuation gate (Section 2.3; Proposition 4b; @bruineberg2022; @raja2021). Second, rewriting stationary occupancy $-\ln p^*$ as an objective function does not resolve reward/gauge underdetermination ($R' = R + \lambda\chi,\ I' = I + \chi$) or identify whether internal states pay restorative costs (Proposition 3; @biehl2021). Third, when biological reproduction enters, minimizing surprise for a physiological organism diverges sharply from maximizing expected lineage representation (Theorem 1). Active inference becomes explanatorily powerful precisely when supplemented—as in embodied interoceptive regulation [@sethTsakiris2018]—by independent evidence of Rung 2 regulatory hardware and budget-accountable control.

**Computational mechanics and macro-scale causal emergence.** Computational mechanics [@shaliziCrutchfield2001; @barnettCrutchfield2015] and information-theoretic causal emergence [@hoel2013] demonstrate rigorously that coarse-grained macro-states can achieve minimal predictive complexity or higher effective information under a chosen intervention distribution than noisy micro-states. Our Proposition 2 and Proposition 3 show why both achievements are strictly protocol-indexed: a macro-partition that maximizes predictive efficiency on birth streams $(B_t)_{t \ge 1}$ simultaneously erases paid recovery $R_1$, and changing the admissible intervention repertoire from external state resets to budget-constrained internal requests changes which macro-quotient commutes with causation [@woodward2003]. Multi-scale pluralism is vindicated, provided no single coarse-graining is crowned the unconditional causal level.

**Organizational versus evolutionary teleology.** Rather than forcing a choice between organizational regulatory accounts [@mossio2009; @bich2024] and generalized selected-effects accounts [@millikan1984; @garson2019], preservation exposes their exact interface. @bich2024 [§§4–5] requires regulatory subsystems to modulate constitutive activities dynamically in response to internal/environmental variations; our gate-and-diagnosis mechanism satisfies state-sensitive switching locally while showing why representations that retain only decoded code ($\theta$) erase the regulatory gate ($g$). @cuciniello2026 [§§4–6] asks how organizational teleology handles diachronic transformations where regulatory goals evolve; our separation of retained parent tokens, strict offspring, and finite-cohort selection bounds pinpoints exactly where intra-organismal Rung 2 explanations hand off the baton to inter-generational Rung 3 explanations—and what genealogical data must be preserved across that handoff.

## Constructive unification without reductionism: why joint realization matters

Philosophers of science since @friedman1974 and @kitcher1989 have debated whether explanatory unification consists in deriving diverse phenomena from a small stock of argument patterns or in tracing shared causal mechanisms [@cartwright1999; @craver2007]. A skeptic might ask whether warning against invalid transfers merely leaves science with a fragmented checklist of caveats.

Theorem 1 answers that skepticism with a concrete logical point. In modal and formal modeling, proving separate consistency claims—that *some* model $\mathcal{M}_1$ exhibits paid majority-vote self-repair ($\exists x\, R(x)$) and *some* model $\mathcal{M}_2$ attains an optimal branching rate ($\exists y\, O(y)$)—does *not* establish joint realizability $\exists z\, [R(z) \land O(z)]$ under shared physical and informational constraints. Indeed, in $\mathcal{M}$, those demands actively pull against one another: repair consumes half of the six-unit budget ($3/6$), starving damaged bodies of the two-unit `bloom` action; damage recurs stochastically on $q$ of all descendants; and public-history controllers cannot anticipate contemporaneous noise. Demonstrating that one and the same damaged founder repairs its own physical cells, executes its restored internal code through an actual hardware gate, propagates that code across generations, and attains the full-policy expected growth ceiling $\rho_*$—precisely on the closed-form regimes $\gamma \le \delta \land k_c \ge 1$ and $\gamma \ge \delta \land \lambda_1 \ge 1$—proves *constrained compatibility* rather than vacuous juxtaposition. Unification here is neither physical reduction of biology and society to one master equation nor loose metaphorical analogy; it is the rigorous testing of whether multiple explanatory obligations can be jointly satisfied by a common causal process.

## Objections, replies, and severe empirical discrimination protocols

We close our philosophical argument by addressing four pointed objections.

**Objection 1: Constructive empiricism suffices.** *An empiricist [@vanFraassen1980] can accept Propositions 1–7 and Theorem 1 as theorems about models while denying that predictive plus interventional success warrants realist commitment to relational organizations.*
**Reply.** We grant that no mathematical theorem deductively compels a philosopher who rejects all inference beyond observable regularities. Our realism is comparative and abductive [@hacking1983; @woodward2003]. When an organizational hypothesis positing internal backup rows, an execution gate $g$, and a three-unit repair budget jointly predicts *both* ordinary output coupling ($\theta_0 H \sim \theta_0 D$ on $(B_t)_{t \ge 1}$) *and* sharp counterfactual divergences under targeted ablations (cutting $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$, restricting supply to $2$ units, closing $g$), whereas any purely output-equivalent black-box representation fails on every perturbation, treating those organizational relations as real causal structure explains why the independent perturbation protocols cohere. That commitment remains fallible and proportionate to interventional severity [@mayo2018].

**Objection 2: The preservation principle is trivially analytic.** *Saying that an explanation must preserve the distinctions it needs is just elementary set theory ($F = \bar{F} \circ q$).*
**Reply.** The set-theoretic fiber condition is indeed analytic once $F$ and $q$ are fixed; the non-trivial philosophical and mathematical work lies in discovering *which* widely trusted compressions silently destroy *which* explanatory invariants. Prior to formal analysis, it is far from obvious that the *entire infinite-horizon reproductive record* erases initial repair expenditure (Proposition 3), that *identical decoded instructions* fail to determine raw execution (Proposition 4b), that *memoryless safety sets* strictly require *finite-memory controllers* under partial observation (Proposition 6a), or that *equal asymptotic growth exponents* ($\rho_* = 1$) conceal permanent finite-time deficits ($11/12 < 1$). Analyticity of the lens is a virtue when the lens exposes substantive hidden non-identifiabilities.

**Objection 3: A model with prescribed backups and inflows begs the question of natural purpose.** *Because $\mathcal{M}$ stipulates majority-vote hardware and five-unit inflows, its repair routine exhibits only artifact design, not the evolutionary origin of biological autonomy.*
**Reply.** Distinguishing *how an organization originates* from *whether an existing organization implements endogenous restoration* is essential to clarity, not a defect. Every living cell likewise depends on exogenous free-energy inflows and inherited biochemical scaffolding that it did not create ex nihilo [@jonas1966; @montevilMossio2015]. What $\mathcal{M}$ proves is a non-trivial *sufficiency and boundary thesis*: external energy supply does not render internal restoration fictitious (since cutting $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$ or backups halts recovery despite abundant inflow), while Rung 2 restorative closure does not explain de novo evolutionary origination (which requires Rung 3 historical variation and selection). Keeping those explanatory burdens separate prevents both vitalist mystification and reductive dismissal.

**Objection 4: Does the framework generate severe empirical discriminations?** *What concrete experimental protocol would test these distinctions in real physical or biological systems?*
**Reply.** In synthetic biology—such as engineered genetic toggle switches, CRISPR-based cellular memory arrays, or metabolic repair circuits—and in fault-tolerant autonomous robotics, our framework prescribes a four-arm *severe testing protocol* [@craver2007; @mayo2018]: *(Arm 1: Observational Equivalence Baseline)* identify induced damaged and undamaged preparations whose downstream division or output counts match statistically; *(Arm 2: Metabolic/ATP Calorimetry)* measure transient resource consumption during the recovery window to test hidden $R_1$ separation despite identical output laws; *(Arm 3: Orthogonal Gate vs. Sequence Ablation)* inhibit transcriptional actuation machinery ($g$) without mutating stored nucleotide sequences ($\theta$), testing von Neumann gate separation; and *(Arm 4: Sub-threshold Nutrient Titration & Cohort Extinction)* titrate per-cycle energy inflow across the repair-plus-division threshold while tracking marked mother-cell retention separately from daughter births in microfluidics traps. Any model inferring internal metabolic cost, gate integrity, or mother-cell longevity solely from unlabeled colony growth curves will be empirically refuted along Arms 2–4.

# Conclusion

Prediction is an indispensable scientific achievement, yet predictive equivalence is neither a criterion of diachronic identity nor a self-sufficient ground of purpose. By proving that minimal predictive quotients shift across protocols, erase realized restorative expenditure even over infinite output horizons, lose physical gate executability despite intact symbolic code, and fail to certify zero-violation viability from fixed black-box samples, we have shown precisely why ontology and teleology cannot be read directly off compressed observation models or fitted scalar scores.

What replaces reductive compressionism is neither metaphysical skepticism nor vitalist teleology, but a disciplined partnership between *constrained relational realism* and *bearer-indexed purposive explanation*. Organized processes earn realist standing when predictive sufficiency converges with counterfactual intervention invariance and physical cost accountability. Purposive attributions earn naturalistic standing when explicitly indexed to their bearer and stratified across systemic causal contribution, endogenous regulatory viability, selective retention or reproduction history, and reflective practical reason. As our stochastic regenerative process demonstrates, paid physical self-repair, gate-mediated internal execution, genealogical continuity, and optimal expected lineage proliferation can indeed inhabit one and the same causal world—not because nature marches toward a single universal optimum, but because distinct explanatory relations can be jointly satisfied when their preservation conditions and boundaries are respected.

## Predictive minimality and belief-quotient descent (Propositions 1 and 2)

*Proof of Proposition 1.* Because each $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ is countably generated, $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ is generated by a countable $\pi$-system $\mathcal{G}_i = \{G_{i,m}\}_{m \in \mathbb{N}}$ closed under finite intersections (with $\mathcal{Y}_i \in \mathcal{G}_i$). By Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem [@kallenberg2021], two probability measures on $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ coincide on all of $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ iff they agree on $\mathcal{G}_i$, and the evaluation $\sigma$-algebra on $\Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ is generated by $\{ \nu \mapsto \nu(G_{i,m}) : m \in \mathbb{N}\}$ (whence $h \mapsto K_i(h, \cdot)$ and the product map $C_{\mathcal{P}}$ are measurable). By sufficiency of $q$, for each $i \in \mathcal{I}$ there exists a measurable kernel $\widetilde{K}_i$ and a measurable null set $N_i \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ with $\mu(N_i) = 0$ such that $K_i(h, A) = \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), A)$ for all $h \notin N_i$ and $A \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ (checking equality on the countable generator $\mathcal{G}_i$ ensures measurability of the exceptional set $N_i = \bigcup_{m \in \mathbb{N}} \{h : K_i(h, G_{i,m}) \neq \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), G_{i,m})\}$). Define the measurable product decoder $\psi: \mathcal{Z} \to \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ by $\psi(z) := (\widetilde{K}_i(z, \cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}}$. Since $\mathcal{I}$ is countable, $N := \bigcup_{i \in \mathcal{I}} N_i \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ satisfies $\mu(N) \le \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \mu(N_i) = 0$, and for every $h \in \mathcal{H} \setminus N$, $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = (K_i(h,\cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}} = (\widetilde{K}_i(q(h),\cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}} = \psi(q(h))$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 2.* For any belief $b \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, action $a \in \mathcal{A}$, and observation $o \in \mathcal{O}$, the unnormalized updated measure $b'_{a,o}(x') = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} b(x) T(x' \mid x,a) O(o \mid a,x')$ satisfies $\langle b'_{a,o}, f\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o} f\rangle$ for every $f \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$, and in particular $p(o \mid b, a) = \langle b'_{a,o}, \mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$.

(*Sufficiency*) Suppose $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ for all $(a,o)$, and let $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$. Since $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$, $p(o \mid b, a) = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle \tilde{b}, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = p(o \mid \tilde{b}, a)$, establishing (i). Whenever $p(o \mid b,a) > 0$, every $f \in \mathcal{F}$ satisfies $U_{a,o}f \in \mathcal{F}$ and hence
$$
\langle \mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o), f\rangle = \frac{\langle b, U_{a,o}f\rangle}{p(o \mid b,a)} = \frac{\langle \tilde{b}, U_{a,o}f\rangle}{p(o \mid \tilde{b},a)} = \langle \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o), f\rangle,
$$
establishing $\mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o) \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o)$ and thus (ii).

(*Necessity*) Suppose (i) and (ii) hold for all $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$ and $(a,o)$. Fix any $(a,o)$ and any $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$; since $\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$, we have $\langle v, \mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Choose the strictly positive uniform belief $b^\circ(x) = 1/|\mathcal{X}|$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough that $\tilde{b} := b^\circ + \varepsilon v \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, so $b^\circ \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$. By (i), $\langle b^\circ, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle b^\circ + \varepsilon v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$, forcing $\langle v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Since $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$ was arbitrary, $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in (\mathcal{F}^\perp)^\perp = \mathcal{F}$. Next, fix any $f \in \mathcal{F}$. If $p_0 := p(o \mid b^\circ, a) = 0$, positivity of $b^\circ$ and non-negativity of $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}$ imply $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \equiv 0$, whence $|U_{a,o}f| \le \|f\|_\infty U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} = 0$ and $U_{a,o}f = 0 \in \mathcal{F}$. If instead $p_0 > 0$, then $p(o \mid \tilde{b}, a) = p_0 > 0$ by (i), and (ii) yields $\langle b^\circ, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p_0 = \langle b^\circ + \varepsilon v, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p_0$, so $\langle v, U_{a,o}f\rangle = 0$ for all $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$. Therefore $U_{a,o}f \in (\mathcal{F}^\perp)^\perp = \mathcal{F}$. $\square$

## Cut-set reconstruction, finite-memory separation, and fixed-sample impossibility (Propositions 5 and 6)

*Proof of Proposition 5.* Let $\mathcal{V}_{\min}$ be the set of $\subseteq$-minimal elements of $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$. If $M \subseteq D \in \mathcal{I}$ for some $M \in \mathcal{V}_{\min} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$, then $D \in \mathcal{F}$ by monotonicity. Conversely, if $D \in \mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{I}$, finiteness of $D$ implies $|D| \in \mathbb{N}$, and downward subset closure of $\mathcal{I}$ ensures that every subset of $D$ belongs to $\mathcal{I}$; choosing a subset $M \subseteq D$ with $M \in \mathcal{F}$ of minimum cardinality yields $M \in \mathcal{V}_{\min}$ with $M \subseteq D$. Uniqueness holds because no two distinct elements of an antichain are comparable: if $\mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{A}_2$ are two antichains generating $\mathcal{F}$ upward in $\mathcal{I}$, each $M \in \mathcal{A}_1$ contains some $M' \in \mathcal{A}_2$ which contains some $M'' \in \mathcal{A}_1$, forcing $M = M'' = M'$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 6.*
**(a)** *Finite-memory separation witness.* Consider the five-state system $\mathcal{X} = \{x_L, x_R, y_L, y_R, \bot\}$ with safe set $S = \{x_L, x_R, y_L, y_R\}$, actions $\mathcal{A} = \{a_L, a_R\}$, and observation space $\mathcal{O} = \{o_L, o_R, o_M, o_\bot\}$ given by $h(x_L) = o_L$, $h(x_R) = o_R$, $h(y_L) = h(y_R) = o_M$, and $h(\bot) = o_\bot$. Define deterministic transitions as follows: from $x_L$, both actions transition to $y_L$; from $x_R$, both actions transition to $y_R$; from $y_L$, action $a_L$ returns to $x_L$ whereas $a_R$ transitions to the absorbing failure state $\bot$; from $y_R$, action $a_R$ returns to $x_R$ whereas $a_L$ transitions to $\bot$; and $\bot$ absorbs under both actions. Since $y_L$ has safe action $a_L$ and $y_R$ has safe action $a_R$, $F_S(S) = S$ and hence $K^\star = \operatorname{gfp}(F_S) = S$. Under the initial law $\mu_0 = \frac{1}{2}\delta_{x_L} + \frac{1}{2}\delta_{x_R}$, at $t=0$ the controller observes $o_0 \in \{o_L, o_R\}$ noiselessly, and at $t=1$ the system occupies $\{y_L, y_R\}$ with probability $1/2$ each while emitting the pooled observation $o_1 = o_M$. Any memoryless observation policy $\pi_0: \mathcal{O} \to \Delta(\mathcal{A})$ must select a single action probability $p := \pi_0(a_L \mid o_M) \in [0,1]$ at $o_M$, incurring two-step failure probability $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_0}(X_2 = \bot) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - p) + \frac{1}{2} p = \frac{1}{2}$. By contrast, the one-step memory controller $\pi_1(o_{t-1}, o_t)$ that plays $a_L$ on $(o_L, o_M)$ and $a_R$ on $(o_R, o_M)$ (and $a_L$ otherwise) satisfies $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_1}(X_t \in K^\star\ \forall t \ge 0) = 1$.

**(b)** *Fixed-sample impossibility.* Under $Z_1, \dots, Z_n \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(r)$, represent any (possibly randomized) certification rule by $\psi: \{0,1\}^n \to [0,1]$, where $\psi(z)$ is the probability of certifying zero violation on sample $z$. The certification probability $g(r) := \mathbb{E}_r[\psi(Z_{1:n})] = \sum_{z \in \{0,1\}^n} \psi(z) r^{\sum z_i} (1-r)^{n - \sum z_i}$ satisfies $|g(0) - g(\varepsilon)| \le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0)^{\otimes n}, \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)^{\otimes n}) = 1 - (1-\varepsilon)^n \le n\varepsilon$. Consequently, if $g(r) \le \alpha$ for all $r \in (0,1]$, letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ yields $g(0) \le \alpha$; equivalently, if $g(0) \ge 1 - \beta > \alpha$, then $g(\varepsilon) \ge 1 - \beta - n\varepsilon > \alpha$ for every $\varepsilon \in (0, (1 - \beta - \alpha)/n)$. $\square$

## Smooth bottleneck obstruction (Proposition 7)

*Proof of Proposition 7.* On $U$, $\Psi(s,b,q) = (s, b, bq)$ is $C^\infty$ with Jacobian matrix
$$
D\Psi(s,b,q) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & q & b \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det D\Psi(s,b,q) = b > 0.
$$
Thus $\operatorname{rank} D\Psi(u) = 3$ for every $u \in U$. If $\Psi = g \circ f$ on any non-empty open set $V \subseteq U$ with $f \in C^1(V, \mathbb{R}^2)$ and $g \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^2, \mathbb{R}^3)$, the chain rule gives $D\Psi(u) = Dg(f(u))\, Df(u)$ at $u \in V$, forcing $3 = \operatorname{rank} D\Psi(u) \le \min\{\operatorname{rank} Dg(f(u)),\, \operatorname{rank} Df(u)\} \le 2$, a contradiction. $\square$

## Regenerative process $\mathcal{M}$, the 2–3–4 partition, and quotients (Propositions 3 and 4)

*Exact transition specification of $\mathcal{M}$.* Each step on a living token proceeds through diagnosis $d \in \{H, D\}$ (where $d = H$ iff row parity matches backups and $g = \mathrm{open}$), action request $a \in \{\mathrm{care}, \mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{stop}\}$, budget check against $6$ units, actuation, and post-step damage:

- `stop`: parent terminated, $0$ strict births, expenditure $0$.
- Healthy ($d = H$, $g = \mathrm{open}$, $R = 0$): `care` costs $1 + 2 = 3 \le 6$, retains parent w.p. $s$ and creates $1$ strict newborn w.p. $b$ independently; `bloom` costs $2 + 2 = 4 \le 6$, retains parent w.p. $s - \delta$ and creates $1$ strict newborn w.p. $b + \gamma$ independently.
- Damaged ($d = D$, active bits flipped or $g = \mathrm{closed}$, backups intact, $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$): `care` pays $R = 3$ to majority-restore active bits and set $g = \mathrm{open}$, then spends $1 + 2 = 3$ ($3+3=6 \le 6$) to execute `care` with retention $s$ and strict birth $b$; `bloom` requires $3 + 2 + 2 = 7 > 6$, so the transaction is *rejected* ($R = 0$, damaged parent retained w.p. $1$, $0$ strict births).
- Every retained parent or strict newborn from an executed (`care` or `bloom`) step carries the parent's intact restored backup/active rows and suffers independent post-step environmental damage w.p. $q$ (becoming $D$) and stays $H$ w.p. $1-q$.

*Proof of Proposition 3.* Under autonomous internal execution:
**(1)** *At $t=1$*: $\theta_0 H$ runs healthy `care` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b), R_1 = 0$); $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ both pay $R_1 = 3$, restore their codes, and run `care` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b)$); $\theta_1 H$ runs healthy `bloom` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b+\gamma), R_1 = 0$). Since $\gamma > 0$, $b + \gamma \neq b$, giving exactly two $B_1$-classes: $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$ and $\{\theta_1 H\}$.

**(2)** *All finite birth records $(B_1,\dots,B_T)$*: After actuation at $t=1$, both $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ yield $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_1) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(s) \otimes \mathrm{Bern}(b)$ of tokens all carrying $\theta_0$, independently damaged w.p. $q$. Hence their post-$t=1$ population states have identical distributions, and by Markov induction $(B_1, \dots, B_T)$ has the exact same joint law under $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ for every $T \ge 1$. Meanwhile, $\theta_1 H$ already differs at $B_1$. Finally, compare $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ at $t=2$: at the end of $t=1$, both have $N_1 = I_{\mathrm{ret}} + B_1$ living tokens with $\mathbb{E}[N_1] = s + b > 0$, each healthy at $t=2$ w.p. $1-q$ and damaged w.p. $q$. Under $\theta_0 D$, every token at $t=2$ executes `care` (expected strict births $b$), so $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)b$. Under $\theta_1 D$, each healthy token at $t=2$ executes `bloom` (expected strict births $b+\gamma$) while each damaged token repairs and executes `care` ($b$), so $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] = (s+b)\bigl[(1-q)(b+\gamma) + q b\bigr] = (s+b)\bigl[b + (1-q)\gamma\bigr]$. Because $s+b > 0$, $1-q > 0$, and $\gamma > 0$, $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)(1-q)\gamma > 0$, separating $\theta_1 D$ from $\theta_0 D$ (and since $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 H}[B_1] = b+\gamma > b = \mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_1]$, $\theta_1 D \not\sim \theta_1 H$). Thus there are exactly three classes: $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 H\}$. Substituting $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$ gives $s+b = 9/8$, so $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = \frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{3}{8} = \frac{27}{64}$ and $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] = \frac{9}{8}\bigl(\frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\bigr) = \frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{13}{24} = \frac{39}{64}$.

**(3)** *Adding $R_1$*: On $(B_1, R_1)$ alone, $(\theta_0 H) \mapsto (\mathrm{Bern}(b), 0)$, $(\theta_1 H) \mapsto (\mathrm{Bern}(b+\gamma), 0)$, and both $\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D \mapsto (\mathrm{Bern}(b), 3)$, yielding three classes $\{\theta_0 H\}, \{\theta_1 H\}, \{\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$. Adjoining $R_1$ to $(B_1, B_2, \dots)$ splits $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$ via $R_1 = 0 \neq 3$, yielding all four singletons. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 4 (Tag erasure vs. gate erasure).* **(a)** Because no transition probability, affordability check, or payment depends on $\tau$, erasing $\tau$ defines a strong Markov lumpability [@burkeRosenblatt1958] commuting with every public-history policy in $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$. **(b)** Consider two tokens with identical intact three-cell rows $(1,1,1)$ and $(0,0,0)$—both having majority-decoded program $\theta_1 = (\mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{care})$ and zero row-parity discrepancy—one with $g = \mathrm{open}$ and one with $g = \mathrm{closed}$. Projecting onto decoded sequence (and even row parity) maps both assemblies to the same informational state, yet raw actuation without three-unit gate restoration executes `bloom` on $g = \mathrm{open}$ (strict-birth probability $b+\gamma$) and is physically blocked by $g = \mathrm{closed}$. $\square$

## Full-policy growth ceiling, attainment, and extinction bounds (Theorem 1)

*Proof of Theorem 1.*
**(1)** *Reproductive-potential upper envelope and optimality of $\rho_*$.* Define the positive single-token potential $v: \{H, D\} \to (0, \infty)$ by
$$
v(H) := \max\{k_c, k_b\}, \qquad v(D) := k_c,
$$
so $0 < v_{\min} := k_c \le v(x) \le v_{\max} := \max\{k_c, k_b\}$ for $x \in \{H, D\}$. For any single-token diagnosis $x$ and action request $a \in \{\mathrm{care}, \mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{stop}\}$, let $(L_a v)(x)$ denote the expected post-damage sum of $v$ across the resulting one-step outputs. From any executed step with expected total output count $k \in \{k_c, k_b\}$, each output is independently $H$ w.p. $1-q$ and $D$ w.p. $q$, yielding $(L_a v)(x) = k\bigl[(1-q)v(H) + q v(D)\bigr]$. Consider two exhaustive parameter cases:

- *Case 1: $\gamma \le \delta$ ($k_b \le k_c$).* Here $\lambda_1 \le \lambda_0 = k_c$, $\rho_* = \max\{1, k_c\}$, and $v(H) = v(D) = k_c$. Executed `care` on $H$ or $D$ gives $(L_{\mathrm{care}} v)(x) = k_c \cdot k_c = \lambda_0 v(x) \le \rho_* v(x)$; executed `bloom` on $H$ gives $(L_{\mathrm{bloom}} v)(H) = k_b k_c \le k_c^2 = \lambda_0 v(H) \le \rho_* v(H)$; rejected $(D, \mathrm{bloom})$ preserves the $D$-token unchanged, giving $(L_{\mathrm{bloom}} v)(D) = v(D) \le \max\{1, \lambda_0\} v(D) = \rho_* v(D)$; and `stop` gives $0 \le \rho_* v(x)$.
- *Case 2: $\gamma > \delta$ ($k_b > k_c$).* Here $\lambda_1 = (1-q)k_b + q k_c > k_c = \lambda_0$, $\rho_* = \max\{1, \lambda_1\}$, and $v(H) = k_b > k_c = v(D)$. Checking each pair: $(L_{\mathrm{bloom}} v)(H) = k_b[(1-q)k_b + q k_c] = k_b \lambda_1 = \lambda_1 v(H) \le \rho_* v(H)$; $(L_{\mathrm{care}} v)(H) = k_c \lambda_1 < k_b \lambda_1 = \lambda_1 v(H) \le \rho_* v(H)$; $(L_{\mathrm{care}} v)(D) = k_c[(1-q)k_b + q k_c] = k_c \lambda_1 = \lambda_1 v(D) \le \rho_* v(D)$; rejected $(D, \mathrm{bloom})$ gives $v(D) \le \max\{1, \lambda_1\} v(D) = \rho_* v(D)$; and `stop` gives $0 \le \rho_* v(x)$.

In both cases, $(L_a v)(x) \le \rho_* v(x)$ holds uniformly for all $(x,a)$. For any policy $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$, step-$(t+1)$ actuation and damage noises are independent of the public history $\mathcal{F}_t$ and current randomized requests. Setting $V(P_t) := \sum_{u \in P_t} v(x_u)$ gives $\mathbb{E}_\pi[V(P_{t+1}) \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \le \rho_* V(P_t)$, whence by induction
$$
\mathbb{E}_\pi[N_t] \le \frac{1}{v_{\min}} \mathbb{E}_\pi[V(P_t)] \le \frac{v_{\max} |P_0|}{k_c} \rho_*^t =: C \rho_*^t \qquad (\forall \pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}},\ \forall t \ge 0).
$$
To establish tightness and minimality of $\rho_*$ from any non-empty $P_0$: if $\rho_* = 1$, define the stationary policy $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$ that requests `care` on $H$-tokens and `bloom` on $D$-tokens—either $P_0$ already contains a $D$-token (preserved w.p. $1$ for all $t \ge 0$) or one step of `care` on an $H$-token yields a surviving $D$-token w.p. at least $s q > 0$, maintaining $\min\{1, s q\}\cdot 1^t \le \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}}[N_t] \le C \cdot 1^t$; if $\rho_* = \lambda_0 \ge \max\{1, \lambda_1\}$, stationary `care` ($\theta_0$) yields $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0}[N_t] = |P_0| \lambda_0^t$; and if $\rho_* = \lambda_1 \ge \max\{1, \lambda_0\}$ ($\gamma \ge \delta$), both $(H, \mathrm{bloom})$ and $(D, \mathrm{care})$ satisfy exact eigen-equality $(L_{\theta_1(x)} v)(x) = \lambda_1 v(x)$, so $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1}[V(P_t)] = V(P_0) \lambda_1^t$ and hence $\frac{V(P_0)}{v_{\max}} \rho_*^t \le \mathbb{E}_{\theta_1}[N_t] \le \frac{V(P_0)}{v_{\min}} \rho_*^t$ for all $t \ge 0$.

**(2)** *Exact attainment conditions from a damaged founder.*

- From $\theta_0 D$: paid repair ($R_1 = 3$) and `care` at $t=1$ followed by `care` thereafter give $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[N_t] = k_c^t = \lambda_0^t$, which is $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$ iff $\lambda_0 = \rho_* \iff k_c \ge 1$ and $\lambda_0 \ge \lambda_1 \iff (1-q)(\delta - \gamma) \ge 0 \iff \gamma \le \delta$.
- From $\theta_1 D$: paid repair ($R_1 = 3$) and `care` at $t=1$ yield $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_1] = k_c$, with every surviving or newborn token entering step $t \ge 2$ independently $H$ w.p. $1-q$ (running `bloom`, mean $k_b$) and $D$ w.p. $q$ (repairing and running `care`, mean $k_c$). By induction, $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_t] = k_c \lambda_1^{t-1}$ for every $t \ge 1$, which is $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$ iff $\lambda_1 = \rho_* \iff \lambda_1 \ge 1$ and $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_0 \iff \gamma \ge \delta$.

*Critical $11/12$ identity and first-census extinction/share identities.* At the critical parameter vector $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (2/3, 1/8, 1/4, 1/4, 1/3)$, we have $k_c = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{11}{12}$, $k_b = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{25}{24}$, and $\lambda_1 = \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{25}{24} + \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{11}{12} = 1$, giving $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_t] = \frac{11}{12} \cdot 1^{t-1} = \frac{11}{12}$ for all $t \ge 1$.

At the supercritical parameter vector $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$ with independent healthy founders $\theta_1 H$ ($I_{\mathrm{ret}}^{(1)} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(5/8)$, $B_1^{(1)} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(5/8)$) and $\theta_0 H$ ($I_{\mathrm{ret}}^{(0)} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(3/4)$, $B_1^{(0)} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(3/8)$):

- $\mathbb{P}(N_1^{(1)} = 0,1,2) = \bigl(\frac{9}{64}, \frac{30}{64}, \frac{25}{64}\bigr)$ with $\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(1)}] = \frac{5}{4}$, and $\mathbb{P}(N_1^{(0)} = 0,1,2) = \bigl(\frac{5}{32}, \frac{18}{32}, \frac{9}{32}\bigr)$ with $\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(0)}] = \frac{9}{8}$.
- $\mathbb{P}(N_1^{(1)} = 0 \land N_1^{(0)} > 0) = \frac{9}{64} \cdot \frac{27}{32} = \frac{243}{2048}$; and $\frac{\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(1)}]}{\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(1)} + N_1^{(0)}]} = \frac{5/4}{19/8} = \frac{10}{19}$.
- Setting $S_1 := \frac{N_1^{(1)}}{N_1^{(1)} + N_1^{(0)}}$ ($0/0 := 0$), summing over $(n_1, n_0) \in \{1,2\} \times \{0,1,2\}$ yields
  $$
  \mathbb{E}[S_1] = \frac{30}{64}\Bigl(\frac{5}{32} + \frac{9}{32} + \frac{3}{32}\Bigr) + \frac{25}{64}\Bigl(\frac{5}{32} + \frac{12}{32} + \frac{9}{64}\Bigr) = \frac{30}{64}\cdot\frac{17}{32} + \frac{25}{64}\cdot\frac{43}{64} = \frac{2095}{4096}.
  $$
- Since $\mathbb{P}(N_1^{(1)} + N_1^{(0)} > 0) = 1 - \frac{9}{64}\cdot\frac{5}{32} = \frac{4006}{4096}$, $\mathbb{E}[S_1 \mid N_1 > 0] = \frac{2095}{4006}$. $\square$
