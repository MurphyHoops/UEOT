# Expanded formal definitions and proofs

To ensure that every formal claim in the article is self-contained and independently verifiable without external manuscripts, this supplement provides expanded derivations of Propositions 1–7, Theorem 1 (including the subcritical finite-cumulative-births corollary), the three-step parameter-variation boundary for Proposition 3, and the explicit competing-mechanism transition laws of Section 6.2.

## Predictive minimality and belief-quotient descent (Propositions 1 and 2)

*Proof of Proposition 1.* Because each $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ is countably generated, $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ is generated by a countable $\pi$-system $\mathcal{G}_i = \{G_{i,m}\}_{m \in \mathbb{N}}$ closed under finite intersections (with $\mathcal{Y}_i \in \mathcal{G}_i$). By Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem [@kallenberg2021], two probability measures on $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ coincide on all of $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ iff they agree on $\mathcal{G}_i$, and the evaluation $\sigma$-algebra on $\Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ is generated by $\{ \nu \mapsto \nu(G_{i,m}) : m \in \mathbb{N}\}$. By sufficiency of $q$, for each $i \in \mathcal{I}$ there exists a measurable kernel $\widetilde{K}_i$ and a measurable null set $N_i \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ with $\mu(N_i) = 0$ such that $K_i(h, A) = \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), A)$ for all $h \notin N_i$ and $A \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ (checking equality on the countable generator $\mathcal{G}_i$ ensures measurability of the exceptional set $N_i = \bigcup_{m \in \mathbb{N}} \{h : K_i(h, G_{i,m}) \neq \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), G_{i,m})\}$). Define the measurable product decoder $\psi: \mathcal{Z} \to \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ by $\psi(z) := (\widetilde{K}_i(z, \cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}}$. Since $\mathcal{I}$ is countable, $N := \bigcup_{i \in \mathcal{I}} N_i \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ satisfies $\mu(N) \le \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \mu(N_i) = 0$, and for every $h \in \mathcal{H} \setminus N$, $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = (K_i(h,\cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}} = (\widetilde{K}_i(q(h),\cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}} = \psi(q(h))$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 2.* For any belief $b \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, action $a \in \mathcal{A}$, and observation $o \in \mathcal{O}$, the unnormalized updated measure $b'_{a,o}(x') = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} b(x) T(x' \mid x,a) O(o \mid a,x')$ satisfies $\langle b'_{a,o}, f\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o} f\rangle$ for every $f \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$, and in particular $p(o \mid b, a) = \langle b'_{a,o}, \mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$.

(*Sufficiency*) Suppose $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ for all $(a,o)$, and let $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$. Since $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$, $p(o \mid b, a) = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle \tilde{b}, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = p(o \mid \tilde{b}, a)$, establishing (i). Whenever $p(o \mid b,a) > 0$, every $f \in \mathcal{F}$ satisfies $U_{a,o}f \in \mathcal{F}$ and hence
$$
\langle \mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o), f\rangle = \frac{\langle b, U_{a,o}f\rangle}{p(o \mid b,a)} = \frac{\langle \tilde{b}, U_{a,o}f\rangle}{p(o \mid \tilde{b},a)} = \langle \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o), f\rangle,
$$
establishing $\mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o) \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o)$ and thus (ii).

(*Necessity on $\Delta(\mathcal{X})$ and reachable-fiber boundary*) Suppose (i) and (ii) hold for all $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$ in $\Delta(\mathcal{X})$ and $(a,o)$. Fix any $(a,o)$ and any $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$; since $\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$, we have $\langle v, \mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Choose the strictly positive uniform belief $b^\circ(x) = 1/|\mathcal{X}|$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough that $\tilde{b} := b^\circ + \varepsilon v \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, so $b^\circ \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$. By (i), $\langle b^\circ, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle b^\circ + \varepsilon v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$, forcing $\langle v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Since $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$ was arbitrary, $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in (\mathcal{F}^\perp)^\perp = \mathcal{F}$ [@poupartBoutilier2003]. Next, fix any $f \in \mathcal{F}$. If $p_0 := p(o \mid b^\circ, a) = 0$, positivity of $b^\circ$ and non-negativity of $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}$ imply $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \equiv 0$, whence $|U_{a,o}f| \le \|f\|_\infty U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} = 0$ and $U_{a,o}f = 0 \in \mathcal{F}$. If instead $p_0 > 0$, then $p(o \mid \tilde{b}, a) = p_0 > 0$ by (i), and (ii) yields $\langle b^\circ, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p_0 = \langle b^\circ + \varepsilon v, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p_0$, so $\langle v, U_{a,o}f\rangle = 0$ for all $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$, whence $U_{a,o}f \in (\mathcal{F}^\perp)^\perp = \mathcal{F}$. On a forward-update-closed reachable subset $\mathcal{R} \subseteq \Delta(\mathcal{X})$ from a fixed prior, exact quotient recursion requires matching observation laws and posterior $\mathcal{F}$-expectations only across reachable pairs in the same $\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}$-fiber, not invariance on $\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{R})$ (under the 3-state cycle $0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 0$ with uninformative observations from $\delta_0$, $\mathcal{R}=\{\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2\}$ spans $\mathbb{R}^3$, yet $\mathcal{F}=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1},(0,1,2)\}$ separates $\mathcal{R}$ injectively with no merged fibers while $U(0,1,2)=(1,2,0)\notin\mathcal{F}$). $\square$

## Cut-set reconstruction, finite-memory separation, and fixed-sample impossibility (Propositions 5, 6, and 7)

*Proof of Proposition 5.* Let $\mathcal{V}_{\min}$ be the set of $\subseteq$-minimal elements of $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$. If $M \subseteq D \in \mathcal{I}$ for some $M \in \mathcal{V}_{\min} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$, then $D \in \mathcal{F}$ by monotonicity. Conversely, if $D \in \mathcal{F}$, finiteness of $D$ and downward closure of $\mathcal{I}$ imply $|D| \in \mathbb{N}$; choosing a subset $M \subseteq D$ with $M \in \mathcal{F}$ of minimum cardinality yields $M \in \mathcal{V}_{\min}$ with $M \subseteq D$. Uniqueness holds because no two distinct elements of an antichain are comparable. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 6.*
**(a)** Consider $\mathcal{X} = \{x_L, x_R, y_L, y_R, \bot\}$ with safe set $S = \{x_L, x_R, y_L, y_R\}$, actions $\mathcal{A} = \{a_L, a_R\}$, and observations $\mathcal{O} = \{o_L, o_R, o_M, o_\bot\}$ given by $h(x_L) = o_L$, $h(x_R) = o_R$, $h(y_L) = h(y_R) = o_M$, and $h(\bot) = o_\bot$. From $x_L$, both actions transition to $y_L$; from $x_R$, both transition to $y_R$; from $y_L$, $a_L \to x_L$ and $a_R \to \bot$; from $y_R$, $a_R \to x_R$ and $a_L \to \bot$; and $\bot$ absorbs ($K^\star = \operatorname{gfp}(F_S) = S$). Under $\mu_0 = \frac{1}{2}\delta_{x_L} + \frac{1}{2}\delta_{x_R}$, at $t=1$ the system occupies $\{y_L, y_R\}$ equally with pooled observation $o_M$: any memoryless $\pi_0(a_L \mid o_M) = p \in [0,1]$ incurs $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_0}(X_2 = \bot) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - p) + \frac{1}{2} p = \frac{1}{2}$, whereas $\pi_1(o_L, o_M) = a_L$, $\pi_1(o_R, o_M) = a_R$ maintains $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_1}(X_t \in K^\star\ \forall t \ge 0) = 1$.

**(b)** Under $Z_1, \dots, Z_n \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(r)$ and $\psi: \{0,1\}^n \to [0,1]$, $g(r) := \mathbb{E}_r[\psi(Z_{1:n})]$ satisfies $|g(0) - g(\varepsilon)| \le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0)^{\otimes n}, \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)^{\otimes n}) = 1 - (1-\varepsilon)^n \le n\varepsilon$ [@leCam1973; @mayo2018], so $\sup_{r \in (0,1]} g(r) \le \alpha \implies g(0) \le \alpha$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 7.* On $U$, $\Psi(s,b,q) = (s, b, bq)$ has $\det D\Psi(s,b,q) = b > 0$ ($\operatorname{rank} D\Psi = 3$), contradicting $\operatorname{rank}(Dg \cdot Df) \le 2$ for any $C^1$ factorization through $\mathbb{R}^2$. $\square$

## Regenerative process $\mathcal{M}$, Blackwell non-simulability, and 3-step parameter boundary (Propositions 3 and 4)

*Exact transition specification of $\mathcal{M}$.* Each step on a living token proceeds through internal/public diagnosis $d \in \{H, D\}$ (where $d = H$ iff row parity matches backups and $g = \mathrm{open}$), action request $a \in \{\mathrm{care}, \mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{stop}\}$, budget check against $6$ units, actuation, and post-step damage:

- `stop`: parent terminated, $0$ strict births, expenditure $0$.
- Healthy ($d = H$, $g = \mathrm{open}$, $R = 0$): `care` costs $1 + 2 = 3 \le 6$, retains parent w.p. $s$ and creates $1$ strict newborn w.p. $b$ independently; `bloom` costs $2 + 2 = 4 \le 6$, retains parent w.p. $s - \delta$ and creates $1$ strict newborn w.p. $b + \gamma$ independently.
- Damaged ($d = D$, active bits flipped or $g = \mathrm{closed}$, backups intact, $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$): `care` pays $R = 3$ to majority-restore active bits and set $g = \mathrm{open}$, then spends $1 + 2 = 3$ ($3+3=6 \le 6$) to execute `care` with retention $s$ and strict birth $b$; `bloom` requires $3 + 2 + 2 = 7 > 6$, so the transaction is *rejected* ($R = 0$, damaged parent retained w.p. $1$, $0$ strict births).
- Every retained parent or strict newborn from an executed step copies intact restored rows and suffers independent post-step environmental damage w.p. $q$ (becoming $D$) and stays $H$ w.p. $1-q$.

*Proof of Proposition 3.* Fix any strict Table 1 parameter vector $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q)$ ($0 < \delta < s < 1$, $b > 0$, $\gamma > 0$, $b+\gamma < 1$, $0 < q < 1$, without assuming $\gamma > \delta$ or $s+b > 1$) and restrict $\mathcal{M}$ to autonomous execution of the stored hereditary program from the four single-founder configurations $\Theta_{\mathrm{init}} := \{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 H, \theta_1 D\}$:

**(1)** *At $t=1$*: $\theta_0 H$ runs healthy `care` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b), R_1 = 0$); $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ both pay $R_1 = 3$, restore their codes, and run `care` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b)$); $\theta_1 H$ runs healthy `bloom` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b+\gamma), R_1 = 0$). Since $\gamma > 0$, $b + \gamma \neq b$, giving two minimal $B_1$ law classes: $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$ and $\{\theta_1 H\}$.

**(2)** *Two-step saturation across all finite birth records $(B_1,\dots,B_T)$*: After $t=1$, both $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ leave $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_1) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(s) \otimes \mathrm{Bern}(b)$ of $\theta_0$-tokens independently damaged w.p. $q$ (with unspent escrow cleared), inducing identical joint laws on $(B_1, \dots, B_T)$ for all $T \ge 1$ and hence on $(B_t)_{t\ge 1}$. Comparing $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ at $t=2$: both have $\mathbb{E}[N_1] = s + b > 0$, each token healthy at $t=2$ w.p. $1-q$ and damaged w.p. $q$, giving $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)b$ versus $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] = (s+b)\bigl[(1-q)(b+\gamma) + q b\bigr]$; since $(s+b)(1-q)\gamma > 0$, $\theta_1 D \not\sim \theta_0 D$, yielding three law classes $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 H\}$ ($27/64$ vs. $39/64$ at $(3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$).

**(3)** *Adding $R_1$ and single-run overlap*: On $(B_1, R_1)$, $(\theta_0 H) \mapsto \mathrm{Bern}(b)\otimes\delta_0$, $(\theta_1 H) \mapsto \mathrm{Bern}(b+\gamma)\otimes\delta_0$, and $\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D \mapsto \mathrm{Bern}(b)\otimes\delta_3$, yielding three classes $\{\theta_0 H\}, \{\theta_1 H\}, \{\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$ incomparable with (2). Adjoining $R_1$ to $(B_1, B_2)$ splits $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$ into four distinct probability laws, whereas individual sample trajectories still overlap (for example, $(B_1, B_2, R_1) = (0, 0, 0)$ occurs with positive probability under both $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_1 H$). $\square$

*Blackwell post-processing non-simulability.* In Blackwell's [-@blackwell1953] comparison of statistical experiments on parameter space $\Theta_{\mathrm{init}}$ [@barnettCrutchfield2015], protocol $\mathcal{P}_A = (B_1, B_2)$ Blackwell-dominates $\mathcal{P}_B = (B_1, R_1)$ iff there exists a founder-independent Markov kernel $K$ mapping outcomes of $(B_1, B_2)$ to distributions on $\{0,1\}\times\{0,3\}$ such that $K \circ \mathcal{L}_x(B_1, B_2) = \mathcal{L}_x(B_1, R_1)$ for all $x \in \Theta_{\mathrm{init}}$. Because $\mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 H}(B_1, B_2) = \mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 D}(B_1, B_2)$, any such $K$ forces $K \circ \mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 H}(B_1, B_2) = K \circ \mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 D}(B_1, B_2)$, contradicting $\mathbb{P}_{\theta_0 H}(R_1 = 3) = 0 \neq 1 = \mathbb{P}_{\theta_0 D}(R_1 = 3)$. Conversely, because $\mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 D}(B_1, R_1) = \mathcal{L}_{\theta_1 D}(B_1, R_1) = \mathrm{Bern}(b)\otimes\delta_3$, any founder-independent kernel $K'$ applied to $(B_1, R_1)$ assigns identical output laws to $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$, contradicting $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] \neq \mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2]$. Hence $(B_1, B_2)$ and $(B_1, R_1)$ are mutually non-simulable under randomized post-processing.

*Three-step parameter-variation boundary witness ($\delta = 1/8$ vs. $\delta' = 1/4$).* Two-step birth saturation in Proposition 3(2) holds on $\Theta_{\mathrm{init}}$ *at fixed parameters*. Once unknown mechanism parameters enter the inference target, $t = 3$ carries strictly new birth information. Initialize a single damaged proliferative founder $\theta_1 D$ at fixed $(s, b, \gamma, q) = (3/4, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$ while varying the maternal `bloom` survival penalty $\delta \in (0, 3/4)$. At $t=1$, $\theta_1 D$ repairs and executes `care`, yielding $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_1) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(3/4)\otimes\mathrm{Bern}(3/8)$ independent of $\delta$, with $(N_1, B_1)$ probabilities $\mathbb{P}(N_1=0, B_1=0) = 5/32$, $\mathbb{P}(N_1=1, B_1=0) = 15/32$, $\mathbb{P}(N_1=1, B_1=1) = 3/32$, and $\mathbb{P}(N_1=2, B_1=1) = 9/32$. Each token in $P_1$ is independently $H$ at $t=2$ w.p. $1-q = 2/3$ (executing `bloom` with strict-birth probability $b+\gamma = 5/8$) or $D$ w.p. $q = 1/3$ (executing repaired `care` with strict-birth probability $b = 3/8$), giving marginal step-2 strict-birth probability $u := \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{5}{8} + \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{8} = \frac{13}{24}$ per $P_1$-token—completely independent of step-2 maternal survival $s - \delta$. Consequently, for *every* $\delta \in (0, 3/4)$ (in particular $\delta = 1/8$ and $\delta' = 1/4$), the exact joint distribution of $(B_1, B_2)$ on $\{0,1\}\times\{0,1,2\}$ is identical:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{P}(B_1=0, B_2=0) &= \frac{5}{32} + \frac{15}{32}\cdot\frac{11}{24} = \frac{95}{256},\\
\mathbb{P}(B_1=0, B_2=1) &= \frac{15}{32}\cdot\frac{13}{24} = \frac{65}{256},\\
\mathbb{P}(B_1=0, B_2=2) &= 0,\\
\mathbb{P}(B_1=1, B_2=0) &= \frac{3}{32}\cdot\frac{11}{24} + \frac{9}{32}\left(\frac{11}{24}\right)^2 = \frac{209}{2048},\\
\mathbb{P}(B_1=1, B_2=1) &= \frac{3}{32}\cdot\frac{13}{24} + \frac{9}{32}\cdot 2\cdot\frac{13}{24}\cdot\frac{11}{24} = \frac{195}{1024},\\
\mathbb{P}(B_1=1, B_2=2) &= \frac{9}{32}\left(\frac{13}{24}\right)^2 = \frac{169}{2048}.
\end{aligned}
$$
At $t=2$, however, maternal `bloom` survival $s - \delta$ governs expected surviving/offspring population size $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D,\delta}[N_2] = k_c\,\lambda_1(\delta)$, where $k_c = s+b = 9/8$ and $\lambda_1(\delta) = (1-q)(s - \delta + b + \gamma) + q k_c$. Because each token in $P_2$ generates $u = 13/24$ expected strict births at $t=3$, third-step expected births split strictly between $\delta = 1/8$ ($k_b = 5/4$, $\lambda_1 = 29/24$) and $\delta' = 1/4$ ($k_b' = 9/8$, $\lambda_1' = 9/8$):
$$
\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D,\delta=1/8}[B_3] = \frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{29}{24}\cdot\frac{13}{24} = \frac{377}{512},
\qquad
\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D,\delta'=1/4}[B_3] = \frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{13}{24} = \frac{351}{512}.
$$

*Proof of Proposition 4.* **(a)** Because no transition probability, affordability check, or payment depends on $\tau$, erasing $\tau$ defines a strong Markov lumpability [@burkeRosenblatt1958] commuting with every $\tau$-blind policy lifted from the quotient (in particular, across $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$). **(b)** Two intact $\theta_1$-decoded assemblies $(1,1,1), (0,0,0)$ with $g = \mathrm{open}$ versus $g = \mathrm{closed}$ diverge under raw actuation without gate-opening repair. $\square$

## Full-policy growth ceiling, subcritical finite-births bound, and switching witness (Theorem 1)

*Proof of Theorem 1.*
**(1)** *Upper envelope and tightness across $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$.* Define $v(H) := \max\{k_c, k_b\}$ and $v(D) := k_c$ ($0 < k_c \le v(x) \le v_{\max}$), and set $\bar{\lambda} := \max\{\lambda_0, \lambda_1\}$ so $\rho_* = \max\{1, \bar{\lambda}\}$. Any actively executed step (`care` on $H$ or $D$, or `bloom` on $H$) with mean output $k \in \{k_c, k_b\}$ satisfies $(L_a v)(x) = k\bigl[(1-q)v(H) + q v(D)\bigr]$. In *Case 1* ($\gamma \le \delta$, $k_b \le k_c$), $\bar{\lambda} = \lambda_0 = k_c$, $v \equiv k_c$, and $(L_a v)(x) \le k_c v(x) = \bar{\lambda} v(x)$ for every actively executed action. In *Case 2* ($\gamma > \delta$, $k_b > k_c$), $\bar{\lambda} = \lambda_1 = (1-q)k_b + q k_c > \lambda_0$, $v(H) = k_b > k_c = v(D)$, and $(L_{\mathrm{bloom}}v)(H) = k_b\lambda_1 = \bar{\lambda} v(H)$, $(L_{\mathrm{care}}v)(H) = k_c\lambda_1 < \bar{\lambda} v(H)$, and $(L_{\mathrm{care}}v)(D) = k_c\lambda_1 = \bar{\lambda} v(D)$. Meanwhile, rejected $(D,\mathrm{bloom})$ preserves the $D$-token unchanged ($(L_{\mathrm{bloom}}v)(D) = v(D) \le \rho_* v(D)$) with $0$ births, and `stop` yields $(L_{\mathrm{stop}}v)(x) = 0 \le \rho_* v(x)$ with $0$ births. Summing $V(P_t) := \sum_{u\in P_t} v(x_u)$ under any $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ gives $\mathbb{E}_\pi[V(P_{t+1}) \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \le \rho_* V(P_t)$ and $\mathbb{E}_\pi[N_t] \le \frac{v_{\max}|P_0|}{k_c}\rho_*^t$. Tightness holds via $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}} \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ (`care` on $H$, `bloom` on $D$) when $\rho_* = 1$ ($\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}}[N_t] \ge \min\{1, sq\}$), stationary $\theta_0$ when $\rho_* = \lambda_0$, and stationary $\theta_1$ when $\rho_* = \lambda_1$ ($\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1}[V(P_t)] = V(P_0)\lambda_1^t$).

*Subcritical finite-cumulative-births corollary ($\bar{\lambda} < 1$).* Suppose $\bar{\lambda} := \max\{\lambda_0, \lambda_1\} < 1$ (so $\rho_* = 1$). At step $t \ge 1$ (transitioning $P_{t-1} \to P_t$ and producing strict births $B_t$), let $A_t$ denote the number of tokens in $P_{t-1}$ that actively execute `care` or healthy `bloom`. On each of those $A_t$ active tokens, $(L_a v)(x) - v(x) \le -(1 - \bar{\lambda})v(x) \le -(1 - \bar{\lambda})k_c < 0$, whereas rejected $(D,\mathrm{bloom})$ changes $v(D)$ by $0$ and `stop` changes $v(x)$ by $-v(x) \le 0$. Conditioning on $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$ and taking expectations gives
$$
\mathbb{E}_\pi\bigl[V(P_{t-1}) - V(P_t)\bigm|\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\bigr] \ge (1 - \bar{\lambda}) k_c\,\mathbb{E}_\pi[A_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}].
$$
Telescoping from $t=1$ to $T$ and using $V(P_T) \ge 0$ yields $(1 - \bar{\lambda})k_c \sum_{t=1}^T \mathbb{E}_\pi[A_t] \le V(P_0) - \mathbb{E}_\pi[V(P_T)] \le V(P_0)$. Because rejected $(D,\mathrm{bloom})$ and `stop` generate zero births and each active token generates at most $b + \gamma$ expected strict births ($\mathbb{E}_\pi[B_t] \le (b+\gamma)\mathbb{E}_\pi[A_t]$), monotone convergence as $T \to \infty$ yields
$$
\mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\sum_{t=1}^\infty B_t\right] \le \frac{(b + \gamma) V(P_0)}{(1 - \bar{\lambda}) k_c} < \infty
\qquad (\forall \pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}),
$$
whence $\sum_{t=1}^\infty B_t < \infty$ $\mathbb{P}_\pi$-almost surely. For instance, from a single founder at $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (1/2, 1/4, 1/4, 1/8, 1/3)$ ($k_c = 3/4$, $k_b = 5/8$, $\bar{\lambda} = 3/4$, $V(P_0) = 3/4$), the universal upper bound is $\frac{(3/8)(3/4)}{(1/4)(3/4)} = \frac{3}{2}$, bounding both $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$ on $D$ ($0$ births) and autonomous $\theta_0 D$ ($\sum_{t=1}^\infty (3/4)^{t-1}(1/4) = 1$ expected total birth). Thus subcritical attainment of $\rho_* = 1$ preserves only arrested tokens rather than ongoing operational work or continuing lineage reproduction.

**(2)** *Autonomous hereditary attainment versus switching policies.* Under autonomous multi-generational execution of $\theta_0$ from $\theta_0 D$, $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[N_t] = k_c^t = \Theta(\rho_*^t) \iff \gamma \le \delta \land k_c \ge 1$. Under autonomous execution of $\theta_1$ from $\theta_1 D$, $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_t] = k_c \lambda_1^{t-1} = \Theta(\rho_*^t) \iff \gamma \ge \delta \land \lambda_1 \ge 1$. By contrast, at $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (1/2, 1/4, 1/4, 1/8, 1/3)$ ($\rho_* = 1$), a switching controller $\pi_{\mathrm{sw}} \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ running `care` at $t=1$ ($R_1 = 3$, reopening $g$) and $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$ for $t \ge 2$ retains a damaged parent at $t=2$ w.p. $sq = 1/6$ (and leaves at least one $D$-token w.p. $1 - \frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{11}{12} = \frac{17}{72}$), achieving $\frac{1}{6} \le \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{sw}}}[N_t] \le C$. The critical $(2/3,1/8,1/4,1/4,1/3)$ and supercritical $(3/4,1/8,3/8,1/4,1/3)$ rational identities follow identically as in Appendix A of the main manuscript. $\square$

## Explicit competing-mechanism transition laws: $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ versus $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ (Section 6.2)

To formalize the causal-abstraction and interventional comparison in Section 6.2 [@rubenstein2017], initialize a single damaged conservative founder $(\theta_0, D)$ (active bits of $(r_H, r_D)$ flipped so parity disagrees with intact zero backups, $g = \mathrm{closed}$, per-step six-unit budget $1 + 5 = 6$) under autonomous conservative execution (`care` requested whenever living):

1. **Bootstrap majority-vote mechanism ($\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$).** On $(D, \mathrm{care})$ with six-unit budget:
   - If $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$, majority voting reads the three cells of $(r_H, r_D)$, pays $R_t = 3$ plus $1$ actuation and $2$ escrow ($3+1+2=6$), restores intact $(r_H, r_D)$ with $g \leftarrow \mathrm{open}$, and executes `care`, producing $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_t) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(s)\otimes\mathrm{Bern}(b)$ of $\theta_0$-tokens that each suffer post-step damage ($d = D, g = \mathrm{closed}$) independently w.p. $q$.
   - Under surgical ablation $\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 0)$, majority voting cannot execute on $D$: $g$ remains $\mathrm{closed}$, no restoration or actuation fee is spent ($R_t = 0$), zero births occur ($B_t = 0$), and the unrecovered $(\theta_0, D)$ token remains absorbed in $(D, g=\mathrm{closed})$ for all $t \ge 1$.
2. **Bootstrap-independent auxiliary-latch mechanism ($\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$).** Carries a hardwired auxiliary `care`-latch that does not read $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$. On $(D, \mathrm{care})$ with six-unit budget, for *both* $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$ and $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 0$, the auxiliary latch pays $R_t = 3$ plus $1$ actuation and $2$ escrow ($3+1+2=6$), restores intact $(r_H, r_D)$ and sets $g \leftarrow \mathrm{open}$ by step end, and enters the *exact same* post-step law $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_t) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(s)\otimes\mathrm{Bern}(b)$ of $\theta_0$-tokens independently damaged w.p. $q$ (re-engaging identically whenever $D$ recurs at later steps).

*Observational-plus-calorimetric tie versus interventional separation.* Under unperturbed $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$, $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ and $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ execute identical step kernels on both $(\theta_0, H)$ and $(\theta_0, D)$, inducing the **exact same infinite-horizon joint law** on $((B_t, R_t))_{t\ge 1}$ (at $(3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$, $R_1 = 3$, $\mathbb{E}[B_1] = 3/8$, $\mathbb{E}[R_2] = 3q(s+b) = 9/8$, and $\mathbb{E}[B_2] = (s+b)b = 27/64$ under both mechanisms). Under $\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 0)$ from $(\theta_0, D)$, $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ yields recovery probability $0$ with $(R_1, B_1, B_2) = (0, 0, 0)$ w.p. $1$ ($\mathbb{E}[B_1 \mid \mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)] = 0$), whereas $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ recovers w.p. $1$ with $R_1 = 3$, $\mathbb{E}[B_1 \mid \mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)] = b = 3/8 > 0$, and $\mathbb{E}[B_2 \mid \mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)] = 27/64$.
