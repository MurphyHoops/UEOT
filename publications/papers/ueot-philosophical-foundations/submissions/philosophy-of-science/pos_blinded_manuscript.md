# Introduction: The explanatory transfer problem

Consider an engineered or biological device that suffers internal damage to an active instruction register and restores that register by majority vote from surviving internal copies before generating its next external output. Compare it with an otherwise similar device whose active register was never damaged. Once restoration completes, both devices induce the *exact same probability law* over the entire infinite sequence of subsequent reproductive outputs: neither larger samples nor longer observation windows can distinguish undamaged operation from paid restoration. Compare further two ways of enriching a one-step birth observation across damaged conservative and proliferative founders: extending future birth censuses separates their latent hereditary programs while still erasing the conservative founder's three-unit repair expenditure, whereas adjoining a first-step metabolic cost measurement separates paid from unpaid histories while leaving the two damaged programs merged. Different protocol expansions thus produce *mutually incomparable refinements*, establishing a sharper thesis than the truism that prediction differs from explanation: *which class of evidence is added determines which class of explanatory question becomes identifiable*. Suppose finally that switching from conservative maintenance to proliferation raises expected descendants per cycle while lowering the parent individual's survival probability. Calling proliferation "adaptive for persistence" is systematically ambiguous until one specifies whether the bearer is the parent token, a re-instantiated organization, or a lineage counted at a future census.

These cases exemplify the *explanatory transfer problem*. Across philosophy of science, cognitive science, and theoretical biology, predictive compression and statistical optimization are widely treated as privileged guides to ontology and teleology. Pattern realism ties ontological commitment to non-redundant predictive compression [@dennett1987; @dennett1991; @ladymanRoss2007; @wimsatt2007]. Computational mechanics and predictive state representations identify minimal states relative to conditional future laws [@crutchfieldYoung1989; @shaliziCrutchfield2001; @littmanSuttonSingh2001; @barnettCrutchfield2015]. Active inference moves from stationary Markov blankets to claims about autonomous individuality and goal-seeking [@friston2010; @friston2013; @clark2013; @parrPezzuloFriston2022], while inverse reinforcement learning fits scalar rewards to observed behavior [@puterman1994; @ngRussell2000].

Selective representation is indispensable to science; trouble begins when predictive adequacy on one channel is illicitly transferred to underwrite claims about causal mechanism, persistence, or purpose whose truth conditions depend on distinctions quotiented away. Recent critiques of Markov-blanket metaphysics [@raja2021; @bruineberg2022] and organizational normativity [@corti2023; @bich2024; @cuciniello2026] expose fragments of this gap, yet lack a unified formal diagnosis of exact cross-protocol non-identifiability paired with constructive compatibility conditions.

Underlying our results is a *forward-marginalization asymmetry*: predictive sufficiency marginalizes over past causal pathways to retain equivalence classes of forward output laws, erasing counterfactual invariance, paid restorative work, and genealogical contrasts. Governed by a typed *principle of explanatory preservation*, this article defends three calibrated theses (all formal proofs appear in Appendix A):

1. **Constrained relational objecthood.** Three exclusionary criteria—*(i) predictive–control compression*, *(ii) counterfactual intervention invariance* [@hacking1983; @woodward2003; @craver2007], and *(iii) realization-cost accountability* [@jonas1966; @rosen1991; @montevilMossio2015]—eliminate passive blankets and cost-free shadows while leaving residual bearer conventions under symmetrical fission explicit.
2. **Bearer-indexed grounds of purpose.** Purposive claims are indexed to a bearer, contrast class, mechanism, and ground stratified across four levels: *(Rung 1) systemic role* [@cummins1975], *(Rung 2) conditional regulatory and repair standards* [@rosenblueth1943; @mossio2009; @diPaolo2005; @bich2024], *(Rung 3) retrospective selective retention or reproduction* [@wright1973; @millikan1984; @godfreySmith1994; @garson2019]—sharply separated from prospective growth—and *(Rung 4) practical reason* [@korsgaard1996; @scanlon1998; @street2006].
3. **Benchmarking autonomous programs against full-policy ceilings.** Within a unified regenerative model $\mathcal{M}$, we benchmark *autonomous hereditary execution* against the *full public-history growth ceiling* $\rho_* = \max\{1, \lambda_0, \lambda_1\}$, separating hereditary lineage persistence from plastic dormancy switching.

Section 2 establishes predictive minimality, belief-quotient descent, and relational objecthood. Section 3 constructs $\mathcal{M}$ and proves incomparable predictive refinements and the syntactic–constructor gap. Section 4 separates paid repair from full reciprocal constraint closure, proves finite-memory viability, and tests objecthood criteria against symmetrical fission. Section 5 proves evaluative underdetermination, the full-policy versus hereditary growth theorem, and the four-rung purposive stratification. Section 6 diagnoses contemporary overreaches, replies to four objections, and outlines experimental tests; Section 7 concludes.

# Predictive equivalence, sufficient quotients, and relational objecthood

## Protocol-indexed predictive minimality and its limits

Let $(\mathcal{H}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}, \mu)$ be a probability space of realized histories up to a decision point, and let $\mathcal{I}$ be a countable index set of observation or open-loop test protocols, each assigning to $h \in \mathcal{H}$ a Markov response kernel $K_i(h, \cdot)$ on a countably generated space $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ [@blackwell1953; @kallenberg2021]. The *canonical predictive representation* for $\mathcal{P} = \{K_i\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}$ is $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) := (K_i(h, \cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}}$, inducing $h \sim_{\mathcal{P}} h' \iff C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = C_{\mathcal{P}}(h')$.

**Proposition 1 (Countable-protocol predictive minimality).** *Suppose $\mathcal{I}$ is countable and each $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ is countably generated. If $q: (\mathcal{H}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}) \to (\mathcal{Z}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Z}})$ is predictively sufficient for $\mathcal{P}$ on $(\mathcal{H}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}, \mu)$—so for every $i \in \mathcal{I}$ there exists a measurable kernel $\widetilde{K}_i$ with $K_i(h, \cdot) = \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), \cdot)$ $\mu$-almost everywhere—then there exists a measurable decoder $\psi: \mathcal{Z} \to \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ such that $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = \psi(q(h))$ for $\mu$-almost every $h \in \mathcal{H}$.*

Proposition 1 generalizes causal-state minimality [@crutchfieldYoung1989; @shaliziCrutchfield2001], predictive state representations [@littmanSuttonSingh2001], and $\varepsilon$-transducers [@barnettCrutchfield2015]. Computational mechanics thus already indexes minimality to a chosen input–output process. Three boundaries govern its explanatory transfer: countability of $\mathcal{I}$ and standard Borel spaces are needed for almost-everywhere factorization and recursive filtering [@astrom1965; @kaelbling1998]; passive input conditioning matches $\mathrm{do}(A=a)$ only under non-confounding, non-anticipatory action semantics [@pearl2009; @woodward2003]; and *predictive classes are response types, not enduring tokens* [@wiggins2001].

## Typed explanatory preservation and quotient descent

Exact attribution of $F: \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{V}$ from $q: \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{Z}$ requires $F = \bar{F} \circ q$ (fiber-constancy). Our *Typed Principle of Explanatory Preservation* requires specifying $\langle \mathcal{B},\, F,\, \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{prec}},\, \mathcal{I}\rangle$—bearer, invariant, precision mode, and interventions—across four tiers: *(1) token-level attribution*, requiring fiber-constancy on $(q(h), E_{\mathrm{aux}}(h))$; *(2) distributional attribution*, where a justified fiber prior $(1/2, 1/2)$ over costs $\{0,3\}$ preserves mean cost $3/2$ without identifying individual histories; *(3) closed-loop belief descent* [@burkeRosenblatt1958; @kemenySnell1960; @poupartBoutilier2003; @givanDeanGreig2003]; and *(4) counterfactual transport* across every interventional kernel $P^{\mathrm{do}(\iota)}$ ($\iota \in \mathcal{I}$) [@woodward2000; @rubenstein2017].

**Proposition 2 (Exact belief-quotient descent via closed test spaces).** *Let $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{O}, T, O)$ be a finite partially observed Markov control model, and let $\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$ be a linear subspace with $\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$ inducing $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} b' \iff \forall f \in \mathcal{F},\ \langle b, f\rangle = \langle b', f\rangle$. Define $(U_{a,o} f)(x) := \sum_{x'} T(x' \mid x, a) O(o \mid a, x') f(x')$. Then $\Delta(\mathcal{X})/{\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}}$ jointly preserves (i) one-step observation probabilities $p(o \mid b, a)$ and (ii) normalized posterior equivalence $\mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o) \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \mathrm{Bayes}(b',a,o)$ across all positive-probability branches $(a,o)$ if and only if $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ for all $(a,o)$.*

Sufficiency in Proposition 2 holds on any $\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}$-saturated belief set, whereas necessity uses interior perturbations across $\Delta(\mathcal{X})$ (on a forward-update-closed reachable set $\mathcal{R}$ from a fixed prior, matching observation laws and posterior $\mathcal{F}$-expectations is required only across reachable pairs in the same $\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}$-fiber, not linear invariance on $\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{R})$). Crucially, observational closure $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ does *not* preserve sunk repair costs or surgical interventions without expenditure tracking (Tier 1) and $\mathrm{do}(\iota)$-consistency (Tier 4).

## Against pure compressionism and blanket metaphysics

Constructive empiricists restrict epistemic warrant to observable adequacy [@vanFraassen1980], whereas structural realists defend relations preserved across theory change [@worrall1989; @ladyman1998]. Seeking naturalized special-science ontology, Dennett's [-@dennett1991] *Real Patterns* and Ladyman and Ross's [-@ladymanRoss2007] *Rainforest Realism* ground objecthood in projectible compression, while free-energy theorists treat stationary *Markov blankets* as individuating self-evidencing agents [@friston2013; @parrPezzuloFriston2022].

While rightly rejecting Lockean essences [@whitehead1929; @dupre1993; @dupreNicholson2018], passive output compression alone underdetermines causal persistence. Epistemic *Pearl blankets* appear in passive linear diffusions and Gaussian field slices lacking physical cohesion [@raja2021; @bruineberg2022; @biehl2021], while minimal output compression erases paid restorative work (Proposition 3). A pattern realist following @dennett1991 or @ladymanRoss2007 can accept this counterexample by incorporating interventional and calorimetric channels into a richer pattern; our advance specifies *which added channel licenses which explanatory claim*. Under *Constrained Relational Objecthood*, three diagnostic checks—*(1) multi-channel predictive–control compression* [@wimsatt2007], *(2) counterfactual intervention invariance* [@hacking1983; @woodward2003; @craver2007], and *(3) realization-cost accountability* [@jonas1966; @rosen1991; @montevilMossio2015]—exclude under-evidenced individuation claims from passive output blankets [@cartwright1999], while symmetrical fission (Section 4.3) shows where index continuation remains underdetermined.

# A unified stochastic regenerative model of repair, execution, and reproduction

## Physical architecture, internal programs, and resource accounting

We construct a finite stochastic regenerative process $\mathcal{M}$ (Table 1; Figure 1a; Appendix A.3). At census $t \in \mathbb{N}$, each living token possesses: *(i) two three-bit instruction rows* $(r_H, r_D)$—one *active cell* and two *backup cells* per row—encoding conservative `care` ($0$) or proliferative `bloom` ($1$); *(ii) a physical execution gate* $g \in \{\mathrm{open}, \mathrm{closed}\}$ required for raw actuation; *(iii) a bootstrap majority-vote unit* $b_{\mathrm{boot}} \in \{0,1\}$; *(iv) a six-unit per-step budget* ($1$ carried escrow unit plus $5$ local inflow units: repair costs $3$, `care` costs $1$, `bloom` costs $2$, and $2$ units escrow up to two outputs); and *(v) genealogical identity* (parental retention vs. strict birth) plus a causally silent physical tag $\tau$.

Compare hereditary programs $\theta_0 = (\mathrm{care}, \mathrm{care})$ and $\theta_1 = (\mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{care})$. Standard damage flips both active bits while preserving backups and $b_{\mathrm{boot}}=1$, closing $g = \mathrm{closed}$; each token's internal gate/parity diagnostic $d \in \{H, D\}$ is read without external oracles and included in the public diagnostic/receipt interface readable by policies in $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$. Damaged `care` spends $3$ units on majority restoration, reopens $g$, and spends $1+2=3$ units on `care` ($3+3=6$). Damaged `bloom` requires $3+2+2=7 > 6$ units: hardware *rejects* the request, refunds inflow, and retains the damaged token w.p. $1$ without actuation noise. Executed steps copy restored three-cell rows before independent post-step active-cell damage strikes each output w.p. $q$; `stop` terminates with zero outputs.

| Healthy `bloom` ($H, \mathrm{bloom}$) | Retains parent w.p. $s - \delta$; 1 strict newborn w.p. $b + \gamma$ | Repair $0 +$ action $2 +$ escrow $2 \le 6$ |

## Task-relative predictive partitions and incomparable refinements

On the four single-founder configurations $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 H, \theta_1 D\}$ across *any* strict parameter vector in Table 1 (independent of demographic growth regimes), let $B_t$ count strict births at census $t$ and $R_1 \in \{0,3\}$ denote initial repair expenditure.

**Proposition 3 (Two–three–four predictive separation and incomparable refinements).** *Under autonomous internal execution in $\mathcal{M}$ across the entire strict domain of Table 1: (1) predicting $B_1$ yields **two** minimal classes, $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$ and $\{\theta_1 H\}$; (2) predicting all finite birth records $(B_1,\dots,B_T)$ ($\forall T \ge 1$, hence $(B_t)_{t\ge 1}$) refines $(B_1)$ to **three** classes, $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 D\}$, and $\{\theta_1 H\}$, coupling $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ identically for all $t \ge 1$ while separating $\theta_0 D$ from $\theta_1 D$ at $t=2$ ($\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)(1-q)\gamma > 0$; $27/64$ vs. $39/64$ at $(3/4,1/8,3/8,1/4,1/3)$); (3) predicting $(B_1, R_1)$ refines $(B_1)$ to a mutually incomparable **three**-class partition $\{\theta_0 H\}, \{\theta_1 H\}, \{\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$, while adjoining $R_1$ to $(B_1, B_2, \dots)$ yields their common refinement into **four** singletons.*

## Lawful tag quotients versus von Neumann's syntactic–constructor gap

**Proposition 4 (Silent-tag lumpability versus decoded-sequence gate separation).** *(a) Erasing silent tag $\tau$ defines a strong Markov lumpability [@burkeRosenblatt1958] commuting with transitions, six-unit budgets, repair costs, genealogy, and expectations across all $\tau$-blind policies lifted from the quotient (in particular, across $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$). (b) Projecting onto majority-decoded code $\theta \in \{\theta_0, \theta_1\}$ (even with intact row parity) while erasing physical gate $g \in \{\mathrm{open}, \mathrm{closed}\}$ fails to determine raw actuation: two intact $\theta_1$ assemblies execute `bloom` when $g=\mathrm{open}$ ($b+\gamma$) but block raw actuation when $g=\mathrm{closed}$.*

Proposition 4 realizes von Neumann's [-@vonNeumann1966] separation between passive syntactic tape and active gated constructor [@rosen1991; @deacon2011]. Biologically, this model is inspired by *Escherichia coli* methyl-directed DNA mismatch repair: biochemical experiments establish that hemimethylated `GATC` strands guide ATP-dependent `MutS–MutL–MutH` initiation coupled to active replication forks [@auWelshModrich1992; @hasanLeach2015], motivating the testable prediction (Section 6.2) that repaired and undamaged sister lineages can share post-recovery division distributions while differing in transient ATP depletion ($R_1$).

# Persistence, paid repair, and diachronic bearers

## Dynamical stability, servocontrol, paid repair, and reciprocal closure

Universal loop or density signatures [@friston2013] conflate four distinct regimes (Table 3). First, *passive dynamical invariance* ($\dot{x} = -x$ on contractible $\mathbb{R}^n$, $H_1(\mathbb{R}^n) = 0$) yields compact $\omega$-limit sets $\omega(x_0) = \{0\}$ without cycles or repair. Second, *externally scaffolded servocontrol* [@rosenblueth1943; @conantAshby1970] regulates target variables via fixed hardware it never regenerates. Third, *paid endogenous repair and constraint closure* [@kant1790, §§64–65; @varela1974; @maturanaVarela1980; @weberVarela2002; @rosen1991; @kauffman1993; @deacon2011; @mossio2009; @montevilMossio2015] use metabolic work to regenerate degrading enabling constraints [@jonas1966; @thompson2007]. Keeping our accounting exact, $\mathcal{M}$ is a *minimal model of paid endogenous repair and state-sensitive regulation*: on fast census steps $\tau_{\mathrm{step}}=1$, active rows and gate $g$ degrade at rate $q$ and are rebuilt via $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$ and backups, whereas $b_{\mathrm{boot}}=1$ is held fixed on a slower timescale $\tau_{\mathrm{boot}} \gg 1$. Full reciprocal Montévil–Mossio closure requires an additional slow-timescale loop maintaining $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$ and backup templates themselves. Fourth, *adaptive viability* [@aubinCellina1984; @diPaolo2005; @barandiaran2009; @bich2024] modulates modes relative to viability boundaries.

## Minimal cut-sets, finite-memory viability, and fixed-sample limits

Under monotone deletion failure on a downward-closed finite repertoire $\mathcal{I}$, destructive perturbations are uniquely represented by the antichain of minimal cut-sets $\mathcal{V}_{\min}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$ (**Proposition 5**, Appendix A.2)—isolating in $\mathcal{M}$ bootstrap severance ($b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0$), underfunding ($2 < 3$), and full three-cell erasure [@coverThomas2006], while failing under disinhibition. Moreover (**Proposition 6**, Appendix A.2): *(a)* on a five-state system with maximal viable kernel $K^\star = \operatorname{gfp}(F_S) = S$ under pooled observation $h$, *every* memoryless policy $\pi_0: \mathcal{O} \to \Delta(\mathcal{A})$ exits $S$ in two steps w.p. $1/2$ whereas one-step memory $\pi_1: \mathcal{O}^2 \to \mathcal{A}$ maintains $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_1}(X_t \in K^\star\ \forall t \ge 0) = 1$ (Figure 2a) [@kaelbling1998; @papadimitriouTsitsiklis1987; @sethTsakiris2018]; and *(b)* for $Z_i \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(r)$, bounding $\mathbb{E}_r[\psi(Z_{1:n})] \le \alpha$ across all $r \in (0,1]$ forces $\mathbb{E}_0[\psi] \le \alpha$ [@leCam1973; @mayo2018].

## Symmetrical fission as a stress test of relational objecthood

Organisms persist through turnover while individuality splinters across physiological, immunological, and Darwinian criteria [@whitehead1929; @clarke2010; @godfreySmith2009; @pradeu2012; @maynardSmithSzathmary1995; @dupreNicholson2018]. Process $\mathcal{M}$ separates parental retention from strict birth despite identical post-step predictive classes [@wiggins2001]. Symmetrical fission into two equally continuous, intervention-stable, cost-accountable daughters $y_1 \neq y_2$ [@parfit1984] stress-tests Criteria (1)–(3) of Section 2.3: *both* branches satisfy all three criteria equally, yet transitivity blocks numerical identity with both. Criteria (1)–(3) thus narrow candidates to cohesive processes while leaving the residual choice among asymmetrical parent indexing (as in $\mathcal{M}$), clonal termination into two new tokens, and lineage tracking to explicit bearer indexing. Likewise, an absorbing chain's *quasi-stationary distribution* $\nu_{\mathrm{QSD}}$ differs from the *Doob $h$-transformed* survivor law $\varpi(x) \propto \nu_{\mathrm{QSD}}(x)h(x)$ [@doob1957; @darrochSeneta1965; @collet2013; @cuciniello2026].

# Optimization, bearer divergence, and the grounds of purpose

## Evaluative underdetermination: reward shaping, gauge, and bottleneck rank

Behavioral optimality never suffices for purpose: in inverse reinforcement learning, observed policies fit an infinite cone of rewards [@ngRussell2000, §3.1]; in discounted dynamic programming ($\beta \in [0,1)$, or episodic settings with zero-potential proper absorbing termination; @blackwell1965; @puterman1994), potential-based reward shaping $r'(x,a,x') = r(x,a,x') + \beta\psi(x') - \psi(x)$ preserves optimal policies [@ngHaradaRussell1999]; non-total multidimensional preorders lack scalar utilities [@krantz1971]; and composite resource–information scores $J = R - \lambda I$—comparable to expected free energy [@parrPezzuloFriston2022] and distinct from Tononi's [-@tononi2004] $\Phi^{\mathrm{IIT}}$—admit exact affine gauge freedom $R' = R + \lambda\chi$, $I' = I + \chi$. Furthermore (**Proposition 7**, Appendix A.2), under healthy $\theta_0$ execution, the demographic-damage response map $\Psi(s,b,q) := (s, b, bq)$ has $\det D\Psi = b > 0$ on the open Table 1 domain: while this does not by itself refute scalar policy objectives, it rules out *losslessly reconstructing $(s,b,bq)$ through any smooth two-variable $(R,I)$ bottleneck*.

## Full-policy growth ceiling versus autonomous hereditary attainment

With per-token local six-unit sites, one-step output means are $k_c = s+b$ and $k_b = s-\delta+b+\gamma$, giving hereditary rates $\lambda_0 = k_c$ and $\lambda_1 = (1-q)k_b + q k_c$. Define the admissible policy class $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ explicitly: at each census $t$, $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ reads the public history $\mathcal{F}_t$ of living counts, per-token internal/public diagnoses $d_u \in \{H, D\}$, genealogical marks, and past expenditure receipts (including $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$, which plays `care` on $H$ and `bloom` on $D$), using arbitrary joint randomization across living tokens while remaining blind to silent tags $\tau$ and contemporaneous/future actuation or damage noise.

**Theorem 1 (Full-policy growth ceiling versus autonomous hereditary attainment).** *For any strict parameters in Table 1 and finite non-empty initial population $P_0$, let $\rho_* := \max\{1, \lambda_0, \lambda_1\}$. (1) Across **all** policies $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$, there exists $C > 0$ such that $\mathbb{E}_\pi[N_t] \le C \rho_*^t$ for all $t \ge 0$, and a stationary policy in $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ attains $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$. (2) Under **autonomous multi-generational execution of the fixed hereditary program $\theta_i$** ($i \in \{0,1\}$) from a single damaged founder $\theta_i D$, paid first-step repair ($R_1=3$), gate-mediated execution, and $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$ attainment hold jointly iff $(\gamma \le \delta \land k_c \ge 1)$ for $\theta_0$, and iff $(\gamma \ge \delta \land \lambda_1 \ge 1)$ for $\theta_1$.*

Separating the two quantifiers in Theorem 1 is essential: if a controller in $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ may *abandon* hereditary execution after $t=1$, paid repair and $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$ attainment hold even in the subcritical regime. At $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (1/2, 1/4, 1/4, 1/8, 1/3)$, $k_c = 3/4$, $k_b = 5/8$, $\lambda_1 = 2/3$, and $\rho_* = 1$: neither autonomous $\theta_0$ nor autonomous $\theta_1$ attains $\rho_*=1$, yet running `care` at $t=1$ ($R_1=3$, reopening $g$) and switching to $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$ for $t \ge 2$ retains a damaged parent at $t=2$ w.p. $sq = 1/6$ (and leaves at least one $D$-token w.p. $17/72$), yielding $\Theta(1)$ via arrested dormancy rather than active lineage reproduction.

Theorem 1 (Figure 2b) yields three further separations. First, the subcritical floor $\rho_* = 1$ (Regime I) arises from rejected damaged `bloom`—analogous to dormant bacterial persisters surviving harsh regimes where dividing lineages ($\lambda_0,\lambda_1 < 1$) decline. Second, in Regime III ($\gamma > \delta, \lambda_1 \ge 1$), autonomous `bloom` maximizes expected lineage growth while *reducing* parent survival ($s-\delta < s$), splitting organismal persistence from lineage multiplication [@okasha2006; @godfreySmith2009] and *differential retention* from *differential reproduction* [@garson2019]. Third, at critical $(2/3, 1/8, 1/4, 1/4, 1/3)$, $\lambda_1 = \rho_* = 1$ yet $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_t] = 11/12 < 1$ for all $t \ge 1$ ($k_c=11/12, k_b=25/24$). At supercritical $(3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$, independent healthy founders $\theta_1 H$ and $\theta_0 H$ satisfy $\mathbb{P}(N_1^{(1)} = 0 \land N_1^{(0)} > 0) = 243/2048$, so the higher-growth $\theta_1$ lineage goes extinct at $t=1$ while $\theta_0$ survives. Separately, defining the $\theta_1$ population share $S_1 := N_1^{(1)}/(N_1^{(1)} + N_1^{(0)})$ (with $S_1 := 0$ when $N_1^{(1)} = N_1^{(0)} = 0$), the unconditional expectation is $\mathbb{E}[S_1] = 2095/4096$, the non-extinction-conditioned expectation is $\mathbb{E}[S_1 \mid N_1^{(1)} + N_1^{(0)} > 0] = 2095/4006$, and the ratio of expectations is $\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(1)}]/\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(1)}+N_1^{(0)}] = 10/19$ [@price1970; @athreyaNey1972].

## Four stratified grounds of purpose and the normativity challenge

Causal-role analysis [@cummins1975] lacks malfunction norms [@davies2001], selected-effects etiology [@wright1973; @millikan1984; @neander1991; @godfreySmith1994] misses de novo regulation, and organizational accounts [@mossio2009; @bich2024] face Corti's [-@corti2023, §3] and Bedau's [-@bedau1992] challenge: why does existential dependence support a functional *ought*? # Competitor diagnostics, objections, and empirical tests

## Diagnosing cross-protocol leaps in contemporary programs

*(1) Active inference:* beyond Pearl-versus-Friston blankets [@bruineberg2022; @raja2021], internal rows $(r_H, r_D)$ act on births only along $(r_H, r_D) \to (d_t, g_t, a_t) \to B_t$ through diagnostic $d_t$, gate $g_t$, *and* executed action $a_t$ [@friston2013; @parrPezzuloFriston2022]—yet boundary birth laws $(B_t)_{t\ge 1}$ erase paid repair $R_1$, composite scores retain affine gauge freedom [@biehl2021], and parent survival diverges from lineage growth, requiring Rung 2 hardware anchors [@sethTsakiris2018]. *(2) Computational mechanics:* minimal $\varepsilon$-transducers [@shaliziCrutchfield2001; @barnettCrutchfield2015] and macro-emergence [@hoel2013] are protocol-relative; Proposition 3 maps their cross-protocol transfer boundary. *(3) Organizational vs. evolutionary teleology:* gate $g$ captures state-sensitive modulation [@bich2024] while genealogical records govern the handoff from Rung 2 tokens to Rung 3 cohorts [@cuciniello2026].

## Constructive unification and replies to four objections

Separate models $\exists x\,R(x)$ and $\exists y\,O(y)$ do not entail $\exists z\,[R(z)\land O(z)]$ when repair consumes half the six-unit budget ($3/6$) and blocks damaged `bloom` [@friedman1974; @kitcher1989]; Theorem 1 establishes exact constrained compatibility.

1. *Constructive empiricism suffices [@vanFraassen1980].* Formal theorems never compel realism; our case is comparative [@hacking1983; @woodward2003]. Under *identical initial damage* ($d_0 = D$), compare $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ (restoring rows via $b_{\mathrm{boot}}=1$, $R_1=3$) with $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ (an auxiliary bypass independent of $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$, matched on $(B_t)_{t\ge 1}$ and $R_1=3$): calorimetry rules out unpaid histories, while $\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)$ yields divergent predictions [@rubenstein2017] differentially supporting internal structure [@mayo2018].
2. *Preservation is trivially analytic.* Fiber factorization ($F = \bar{F}\circ q$) is analytic once $F$ and $q$ are fixed; the substantive work is proving *which* compressions destroy *which* invariants—including two-step birth saturation alongside infinite-horizon cost erasure (Proposition 3), sequence-versus-gate separation (Proposition 4b), finite-memory viability (Proposition 6a), and critical deficits ($11/12 < 1$).
3. *Stipulated scaffolding falls short of full closure.* Exogenous energy inflow is shared by all living cells and does not render repair fictitious [@jonas1966; @montevilMossio2015]. However, because $b_{\mathrm{boot}}=1$ is fixed on the modeled horizon, $\mathcal{M}$ establishes paid endogenous repair on the fast timescale (Table 3, 3a), leaving slow-timescale regeneration of $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$ (3b) and Rung 3 selection as explicit additional conditions.
4. *Empirical discrimination between rival mechanisms.* Mapping model variables onto interventions [@craver2007; @rubenstein2017] in microbial repair circuits [@auWelshModrich1992; @hasanLeach2015] under controlled template lesions ($d_0 = D$) tests cost-free *(H0)*, `MutS–MutL–MutH` repair *($\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$)*, and template-independent bypass *($\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$)* across: *(Arm 1)* matched post-recovery division laws; *(Arm 2)* single-cell ATP calorimetry ($R_1$); *(Arm 3)* selective `MutH` inhibition ($\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)$) versus replisome gate inhibition ($g$); and *(Arm 4)* microfluidic nutrient titration tracking mother-cell retention versus daughter births [@mayo2018].

# Conclusion

When two rival explanations share the exact same predictive law on an output channel, what additional evidence warrants accepting one over the other? Our results show that extending observations along different coordinates produces mutually incomparable refinements, that autonomous hereditary attainment of full-policy growth ceilings separates sharply from plastic dormancy switching, and that parent survival can oppose lineage multiplication. Calibrating relational objecthood and four-rung bearer-indexed purpose strictly to these evidential boundaries preserves explanatory unity without overclaiming beyond what mechanism, repair budgets, and selection history warrant.

# Self-contained proofs of Propositions 1–7 and Theorem 1

*Proof of Proposition 1.* Each countably generated $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ is generated by a countable $\pi$-system $\mathcal{G}_i = \{G_{i,m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}$ with $\mathcal{Y}_i \in \mathcal{G}_i$ [@kallenberg2021]. By sufficiency of $q$, for each $i \in \mathcal{I}$ there exists $\widetilde{K}_i$ such that $N_i := \bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}} \{h \in \mathcal{H} : K_i(h, G_{i,m}) \neq \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), G_{i,m})\} \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ satisfies $\mu(N_i) = 0$; by Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem, $K_i(h, \cdot) = \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), \cdot)$ for all $h \notin N_i$. Defining $\psi(z) := (\widetilde{K}_i(z, \cdot))_{i\in\mathcal{I}}$ and $N := \bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}} N_i$, countability of $\mathcal{I}$ gives $\mu(N) \le \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}} \mu(N_i) = 0$, with $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = \psi(q(h))$ on $\mathcal{H}\setminus N$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 2.* Let $b'_{a,o}(x') = \sum_{x} b(x) T(x'\mid x,a) O(o\mid a,x')$; then $\langle b'_{a,o}, f\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o}f\rangle$ and $p(o\mid b,a) = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$. (*Sufficiency*) If $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ and $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$, then $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$ gives $p(o\mid b,a) = p(o\mid \tilde{b},a)$, and for $p(o\mid b,a) > 0$, $\langle \mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o), f\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p(o\mid b,a) = \langle \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o), f\rangle$ ($\forall f \in \mathcal{F}$). (*Necessity*) Assume (i)–(ii) on $\Delta(\mathcal{X})$. For $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$, $\langle v, \mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Perturbing $b^\circ(x) = 1/|\mathcal{X}|$ to $\tilde{b} = b^\circ + \varepsilon v \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, (i) forces $\langle v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$ ($\forall v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$), so $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$. For $f \in \mathcal{F}$, if $p_0 := p(o\mid b^\circ, a) = 0$, positivity of $b^\circ$ gives $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \equiv 0$ and $U_{a,o}f = 0 \in \mathcal{F}$; if $p_0 > 0$, (ii) gives $\langle v, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p_0 = 0$ ($\forall v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$), so $U_{a,o}f \in \mathcal{F}$. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 1.* **(1)** Set $v(H) := \max\{k_c, k_b\}$ and $v(D) := k_c$ ($0 < k_c \le v(x) \le v_{\max}$).
