# Introduction: The explanatory transfer problem

Consider an engineered or biological device whose damaged active instruction register is restored by majority vote from surviving internal copies before generating its next output, alongside an undamaged twin. Once restoration completes, both induce the *exact same probability law* over the entire infinite sequence of subsequent births: neither larger samples nor longer horizons distinguish undamaged operation from paid restoration. Across four founders executing conservative or proliferative hereditary programs autonomously at fixed parameters, adding a second birth census already saturates infinite-horizon birth laws—separating latent programs while erasing three-unit repair expenditure—whereas adjoining first-step metabolic cost separates paid from unpaid histories while leaving damaged programs merged. Different protocol expansions thus produce *mutually incomparable refinements* over probability laws, establishing a sharper thesis than the truism that prediction differs from explanation: *which class of evidence is added determines which explanatory question becomes identifiable*. Finally, switching from conservative maintenance to proliferation can raise expected descendants per cycle while lowering parent survival, leaving "adaptive for persistence" ambiguous until its bearer—parent token, re-instantiated organization, or lineage—is specified.

These cases exemplify the *explanatory transfer problem*. Across science and philosophy, predictive compression and optimization are widely treated as guides to ontology and teleology: pattern realism ties objecthood to non-redundant compression [@dennett1987; @dennett1991; @ladymanRoss2007; @wimsatt2007]; computational mechanics and predictive state representations define minimal states from future laws [@crutchfieldYoung1989; @shaliziCrutchfield2001; @littmanSuttonSingh2001; @barnettCrutchfield2015]; active inference moves from Markov blankets to autonomous agency [@friston2010; @friston2013; @clark2013; @parrPezzuloFriston2022]; and inverse reinforcement learning fits scalar rewards to behavior [@puterman1994; @ngRussell2000].

Selective representation is indispensable; trouble begins when predictive adequacy on one channel is transferred to claims about mechanism, persistence, or purpose whose truth conditions depend on distinctions quotiented away. Recent critiques of blanket metaphysics [@raja2021; @bruineberg2022] and organizational normativity [@corti2023; @bich2024; @cuciniello2026] expose parts of this gap, yet lack a unified formal diagnosis of cross-protocol non-identifiability paired with constructive compatibility conditions.

Underlying our results is a *forward-marginalization asymmetry*: predictive sufficiency marginalizes over past pathways to retain equivalence classes of forward output laws, erasing counterfactual invariance, paid repair, and genealogy. Governed by a typed *principle of explanatory preservation*, we defend three theses (proofs in Appendix A):

1. **Constrained relational objecthood.** Three exclusionary criteria—*(i) predictive–control compression*, *(ii) counterfactual intervention invariance* [@hacking1983; @woodward2003; @craver2007], and *(iii) realization-cost accountability* [@jonas1966; @rosen1991; @montevilMossio2015]—eliminate passive blankets and cost-free shadows while leaving symmetrical fission conventions explicit.
2. **Bearer-indexed grounds of purpose.** Purposive claims are indexed to bearer, contrast class, mechanism, and ground across four levels: *(Rung 1) systemic role* [@cummins1975], *(Rung 2) conditional regulatory and repair standards* [@rosenblueth1943; @mossio2009; @diPaolo2005; @bich2024], *(Rung 3) retrospective selective retention or reproduction* [@wright1973; @millikan1984; @godfreySmith1994; @garson2019]—separated from prospective growth—and *(Rung 4) practical reason* [@korsgaard1996; @scanlon1998; @street2006].
3. **Benchmarking autonomous programs against full-policy ceilings.** Within a unified regenerative model $\mathcal{M}$, we benchmark *autonomous hereditary execution* against the *full public-history growth ceiling* $\rho_* = \max\{1, \lambda_0, \lambda_1\}$, separating hereditary persistence from dormancy switching.

Section 2 establishes predictive minimality, belief-quotient descent, and relational objecthood; Section 3 constructs $\mathcal{M}$ and proves incomparable refinements and gate separation; Section 4 analyzes paid repair, finite-memory viability, and fission; Section 5 proves evaluative underdetermination, Theorem 1, and four-rung purpose; Section 6 addresses rival programs, objections, and tests; Section 7 concludes.

# Predictive equivalence, sufficient quotients, and relational objecthood

## Protocol-indexed predictive minimality and its limits

Let $(\mathcal{H}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}, \mu)$ be a probability space of realized histories up to a decision point, and let $\mathcal{I}$ be a countable index set of observation or open-loop test protocols, each assigning to $h \in \mathcal{H}$ a Markov response kernel $K_i(h, \cdot)$ on a countably generated space $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ [@blackwell1953; @kallenberg2021]. The *canonical predictive representation* for $\mathcal{P} = \{K_i\}_{i \in \mathcal{I}}$ is $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) := (K_i(h, \cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}}$, inducing $h \sim_{\mathcal{P}} h' \iff C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = C_{\mathcal{P}}(h')$.

**Proposition 1 (Countable-protocol predictive minimality).** *Suppose $\mathcal{I}$ is countable and each $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ is countably generated. If $q: (\mathcal{H}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}) \to (\mathcal{Z}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Z}})$ is predictively sufficient for $\mathcal{P}$ on $(\mathcal{H}, \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}, \mu)$—so for every $i \in \mathcal{I}$ there exists a measurable kernel $\widetilde{K}_i$ with $K_i(h, \cdot) = \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), \cdot)$ $\mu$-almost everywhere—then there exists a measurable decoder $\psi: \mathcal{Z} \to \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ such that $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = \psi(q(h))$ for $\mu$-almost every $h \in \mathcal{H}$.*

Proposition 1 generalizes causal-state minimality [@crutchfieldYoung1989; @shaliziCrutchfield2001], predictive state representations [@littmanSuttonSingh2001], and $\varepsilon$-transducers [@barnettCrutchfield2015]. Computational mechanics thus indexes minimality to a chosen process, subject to three transfer boundaries: countability of $\mathcal{I}$ and standard Borel spaces underwrite almost-everywhere factorization and recursive filtering [@astrom1965; @kaelbling1998]; passive input conditioning matches $\mathrm{do}(A=a)$ only under non-confounding, non-anticipatory action semantics [@pearl2009; @woodward2003]; and *predictive classes are response types, not enduring tokens* [@wiggins2001].

## Typed explanatory preservation and quotient descent

Exact attribution of $F: \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{V}$ from $q: \mathcal{S} \to \mathcal{Z}$ requires $F = \bar{F} \circ q$ (fiber-constancy). Our *Typed Principle of Explanatory Preservation* requires specifying $\langle \mathcal{B},\, F,\, \mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{prec}},\, \mathcal{I}\rangle$—bearer, invariant, precision mode, and interventions—across four tiers: *(1) token-level attribution*, requiring fiber-constancy on $(q(h), E_{\mathrm{aux}}(h))$; *(2) distributional attribution*, where a justified fiber prior $(1/2, 1/2)$ over costs $\{0,3\}$ preserves mean cost $3/2$ without identifying individual histories; *(3) closed-loop belief descent* [@burkeRosenblatt1958; @kemenySnell1960; @poupartBoutilier2003; @givanDeanGreig2003]; and *(4) counterfactual transport* across every interventional kernel $P^{\mathrm{do}(\iota)}$ ($\iota \in \mathcal{I}$) [@woodward2000; @rubenstein2017].

**Proposition 2 (Exact belief-quotient descent via closed test spaces).** *Let $(\mathcal{X}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{O}, T, O)$ be a finite partially observed Markov control model, and let $\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$ be a linear subspace with $\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$ inducing $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} b' \iff \forall f \in \mathcal{F},\ \langle b, f\rangle = \langle b', f\rangle$. Define $(U_{a,o} f)(x) := \sum_{x'} T(x' \mid x, a) O(o \mid a, x') f(x')$. Then $\Delta(\mathcal{X})/{\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}}$ jointly preserves (i) one-step observation probabilities $p(o \mid b, a)$ and (ii) normalized posterior equivalence $\mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o) \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \mathrm{Bayes}(b',a,o)$ across all positive-probability branches $(a,o)$ if and only if $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ for all $(a,o)$.*

Sufficiency in Proposition 2 holds on any $\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}$-saturated belief set, whereas necessity uses interior perturbations on $\Delta(\mathcal{X})$ (on a forward-closed reachable set $\mathcal{R}$, exact recursion requires matching observation laws and posterior $\mathcal{F}$-expectations only on reachable pairs in the same $\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}$-fiber, not invariance on $\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{R})$). Crucially, $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ preserves neither sunk repair costs nor surgical interventions without expenditure tracking (Tier 1) and $\mathrm{do}(\iota)$-consistency (Tier 4).

## Against pure compressionism and blanket metaphysics

Constructive empiricists restrict warrant to observable adequacy [@vanFraassen1980], whereas structural realists defend relations preserved across theory change [@worrall1989; @ladyman1998]. Dennett's [-@dennett1991] *Real Patterns* and Ladyman and Ross's [-@ladymanRoss2007] *Rainforest Realism* ground objecthood in projectible compression, while free-energy theorists treat stationary *Markov blankets* as individuating self-evidencing agents [@friston2013; @parrPezzuloFriston2022].

While rightly rejecting Lockean essences [@whitehead1929; @dupre1993; @dupreNicholson2018], passive output compression underdetermines causal persistence: epistemic *Pearl blankets* arise in passive diffusions and Gaussian slices lacking cohesion [@raja2021; @bruineberg2022; @biehl2021], and minimal output compression erases paid repair (Proposition 3). Liberalized pattern realism can accommodate this by adding interventional and calorimetric channels; our advance specifies *which added channel licenses which claim*. *Constrained Relational Objecthood* requires *(1) multi-channel predictive–control compression* [@wimsatt2007], *(2) counterfactual intervention invariance* [@hacking1983; @woodward2003; @craver2007], and *(3) realization-cost accountability* [@jonas1966; @rosen1991; @montevilMossio2015], excluding passive output shadows [@cartwright1999] while leaving symmetrical fission (Section 4.3) explicit.

# A unified stochastic regenerative model of repair, execution, and reproduction

## Physical architecture, internal programs, and resource accounting

We construct a finite stochastic regenerative process $\mathcal{M}$ (Table 1; Figure 1a; Appendix A.3). At census $t \in \mathbb{N}$, each living token possesses: *(i) two three-bit instruction rows* $(r_H, r_D)$—one *active cell* and two *backup cells* per row—encoding conservative `care` ($0$) or proliferative `bloom` ($1$); *(ii) a physical execution gate* $g \in \{\mathrm{open}, \mathrm{closed}\}$ required for raw actuation; *(iii) a bootstrap majority-vote unit* $b_{\mathrm{boot}} \in \{0,1\}$; *(iv) a six-unit per-step budget* ($1$ carried escrow unit plus $5$ local inflow units: repair costs $3$, `care` costs $1$, `bloom` costs $2$, and $2$ units escrow up to two outputs); and *(v) genealogical identity* (parental retention vs. strict birth) plus a causally silent physical tag $\tau$.

Compare hereditary programs $\theta_0 = (\mathrm{care}, \mathrm{care})$ and $\theta_1 = (\mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{care})$. Standard damage flips both active bits while preserving backups and $b_{\mathrm{boot}}=1$, closing $g = \mathrm{closed}$; internal gate/parity diagnosis $d \in \{H, D\}$ is exposed on the public interface read by $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$. Damaged `care` spends $3$ units on majority restoration, reopens $g$, and spends $1+2=3$ on `care` ($6$ total). Damaged `bloom` requires $3+2+2=7 > 6$: hardware *rejects* it, refunds inflow, and retains the damaged token w.p. $1$. Executed steps copy restored rows before post-step damage hits each output independently w.p. $q$; `stop` terminates with zero outputs.

|:---|:---|:---|
| Healthy `care` ($H, \mathrm{care}$) | Retains parent w.p. $s$; 1 strict newborn w.p. $b$ | Repair $0 +$ action $1 +$ escrow $2 \le 6$ |
| Healthy `bloom` ($H, \mathrm{bloom}$) | Retains parent w.p. $s - \delta$; 1 strict newborn w.p. $b + \gamma$ | Repair $0 +$ action $2 +$ escrow $2 \le 6$ |
| Damaged repaired `care` ($D, \mathrm{care}$) | Majority-restores active bits, opens $g$, runs `care` ($s, b$) | Repair $3 +$ action $1 +$ escrow $2 = 6$ |
| Damaged `bloom` ($D, \mathrm{bloom}$) | Prepayment $7 > 6$: rejected; damaged token kept w.p. $1$ | No repair, inflow refunded, $0$ births |
| Output inheritance & damage | Outputs copy restored rows; active bits damaged w.p. $q$ | $H$ at $t+1$ w.p. $1-q$; $D$ w.p. $q$ |

## Task-relative predictive partitions and incomparable refinements

On the four single-founder configurations $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 H, \theta_1 D\}$ executing hereditary programs autonomously at any fixed strict parameter vector in Table 1 (independent of demographic growth regimes), let $B_t$ count strict births at census $t$ and $R_1 \in \{0,3\}$ denote initial repair expenditure.

**Proposition 3 (Two–three–four predictive separation, two-step saturation, and incomparable refinements).** *Under autonomous hereditary execution in $\mathcal{M}$ at any fixed strict parameter vector in Table 1: (1) predicting $B_1$ yields **two** minimal law classes, $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$ and $\{\theta_1 H\}$; (2) predicting $(B_1, B_2)$ already saturates all finite birth records $(B_1,\dots,B_T)$ ($\forall T \ge 1$, hence $(B_t)_{t\ge 1}$), refining $(B_1)$ to **three** classes, $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 D\}$, and $\{\theta_1 H\}$, coupling $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ identically for all $t \ge 1$ while separating $\theta_0 D$ from $\theta_1 D$ at $t=2$ ($\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)(1-q)\gamma > 0$; $27/64$ vs. $39/64$ at $(3/4,1/8,3/8,1/4,1/3)$); (3) predicting $(B_1, R_1)$ refines $(B_1)$ to an incomparable **three**-class partition $\{\theta_0 H\}, \{\theta_1 H\}, \{\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$, while $(B_1, B_2, R_1)$ yields **four** singleton law classes.*

## Lawful tag quotients versus von Neumann's syntactic–constructor gap

**Proposition 4 (Silent-tag lumpability versus decoded-sequence gate separation).** *(a) Erasing silent tag $\tau$ defines a strong Markov lumpability [@burkeRosenblatt1958] commuting with transitions, six-unit budgets, repair costs, genealogy, and expectations across all $\tau$-blind policies lifted from the quotient (in particular, across $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$). (b) Projecting onto majority-decoded code $\theta \in \{\theta_0, \theta_1\}$ (even with intact row parity) while erasing physical gate $g \in \{\mathrm{open}, \mathrm{closed}\}$ fails to determine raw actuation: two intact $\theta_1$ assemblies execute `bloom` when $g=\mathrm{open}$ ($b+\gamma$) but block raw actuation when $g=\mathrm{closed}$.*

Proposition 4 realizes von Neumann's [-@vonNeumann1966] separation between passive syntactic tape and active gated constructor [@rosen1991; @deacon2011], qualitatively motivated in biology by *Escherichia coli* methyl-directed mismatch repair, where hemimethylated `GATC` strands guide ATP-dependent `MutS–MutL–MutH` initiation at replication forks [@auWelshModrich1992; @hasanLeach2015] (Section 6.2).

# Persistence, paid repair, and diachronic bearers

## Dynamical stability, servocontrol, paid repair, and reciprocal closure

Universal loop or density signatures [@friston2013] conflate four regimes (Table 3). First, *passive invariance* ($\dot{x} = -x$ on contractible $\mathbb{R}^n$, $H_1(\mathbb{R}^n) = 0$) yields compact $\omega(x_0) = \{0\}$ without cycles or repair. Second, *external servocontrol* [@rosenblueth1943; @conantAshby1970] regulates targets via fixed hardware it never rebuilds. Third, *paid repair and constraint closure* [@kant1790, §§64–65; @varela1974; @maturanaVarela1980; @weberVarela2002; @rosen1991; @kauffman1993; @deacon2011; @mossio2009; @montevilMossio2015] expend work to regenerate degrading constraints [@jonas1966; @thompson2007]: in $\mathcal{M}$, active rows and $g$ degrade at rate $q$ on $\tau_{\mathrm{step}}=1$ and are rebuilt via backups and $b_{\mathrm{boot}}=1$ fixed on $\tau_{\mathrm{boot}} \gg 1$, whereas full Montévil–Mossio closure adds a slow loop regenerating $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$ and backups. Fourth, *adaptive viability* [@aubinCellina1984; @diPaolo2005; @barandiaran2009; @bich2024] modulates modes near viability boundaries.

## Minimal cut-sets, finite-memory viability, and fixed-sample limits

Under monotone deletion failure on a downward-closed finite repertoire $\mathcal{I}$, destructive perturbations are uniquely represented by the antichain of minimal cut-sets $\mathcal{V}_{\min}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$ (**Proposition 5**, Appendix A.2)—isolating in $\mathcal{M}$ bootstrap severance ($b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0$), underfunding ($2 < 3$), and full three-cell erasure [@coverThomas2006], while failing under disinhibition. Moreover (**Proposition 6**, Appendix A.2): *(a)* on a five-state system with maximal viable kernel $K^\star = \operatorname{gfp}(F_S) = S$ under pooled observation $h$, *every* memoryless policy $\pi_0: \mathcal{O} \to \Delta(\mathcal{A})$ exits $S$ in two steps w.p. $1/2$ whereas one-step memory $\pi_1: \mathcal{O}^2 \to \mathcal{A}$ maintains $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_1}(X_t \in K^\star\ \forall t \ge 0) = 1$ (Figure 2a) [@kaelbling1998; @papadimitriouTsitsiklis1987; @sethTsakiris2018]; and *(b)* for $Z_i \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(r)$, bounding $\mathbb{E}_r[\psi(Z_{1:n})] \le \alpha$ across all $r \in (0,1]$ forces $\mathbb{E}_0[\psi] \le \alpha$ [@leCam1973; @mayo2018].

## Symmetrical fission as a stress test of relational objecthood

Organisms persist through turnover while individuality splinters across physiological, immunological, and Darwinian criteria [@whitehead1929; @clarke2010; @godfreySmith2009; @pradeu2012; @maynardSmithSzathmary1995; @dupreNicholson2018]. Process $\mathcal{M}$ separates parental retention from strict birth despite identical post-step predictive classes [@wiggins2001]. Symmetrical fission into equally continuous, intervention-stable, cost-accountable daughters $y_1 \neq y_2$ [@parfit1984] stress-tests Criteria (1)–(3): *both* satisfy all three equally, yet transitivity blocks numerical identity with both, leaving parent indexing (as in $\mathcal{M}$), clonal termination, versus lineage tracking to explicit bearer conventions. Likewise, an absorbing chain's *quasi-stationary distribution* $\nu_{\mathrm{QSD}}$ differs from its *Doob $h$-transformed* survivor law $\varpi(x) \propto \nu_{\mathrm{QSD}}(x)h(x)$ [@doob1957; @darrochSeneta1965; @collet2013; @cuciniello2026].

# Optimization, bearer divergence, and the grounds of purpose

## Evaluative underdetermination: reward shaping, gauge, and bottleneck rank

Behavioral optimality never suffices for purpose: in inverse reinforcement learning, policies fit an infinite reward cone [@ngRussell2000, §3.1]; in discounted or proper-absorbing episodic dynamic programming [@blackwell1965; @puterman1994], potential shaping $r'(x,a,x') = r(x,a,x') + \beta\psi(x') - \psi(x)$ preserves optimal policies [@ngHaradaRussell1999]; non-total preorders lack scalar utilities [@krantz1971]; and resource–information scores $J = R - \lambda I$—comparable to expected free energy [@parrPezzuloFriston2022] and distinct from Tononi's [-@tononi2004] $\Phi^{\mathrm{IIT}}$—admit affine gauge freedom $R' = R + \lambda\chi$, $I' = I + \chi$. Moreover (**Proposition 7**, Appendix A.2), under healthy $\theta_0$, $\Psi(s,b,q) := (s, b, bq)$ has $\det D\Psi = b > 0$, ruling out *losslessly reconstructing $(s,b,bq)$ through any smooth two-variable $(R,I)$ bottleneck*.

## Full-policy growth ceiling versus autonomous hereditary attainment

With per-token six-unit sites, one-step output means are $k_c = s+b$ and $k_b = s-\delta+b+\gamma$, giving $\lambda_0 = k_c$ and $\lambda_1 = (1-q)k_b + q k_c$. Admissible policies $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ read public history $\mathcal{F}_t$ (living counts, diagnoses $d_u \in \{H, D\}$, genealogy, and past receipts—including $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$, playing `care` on $H$ and `bloom` on $D$) with arbitrary joint randomization across living tokens, blind to $\tau$ and contemporaneous/future noise.

**Theorem 1 (Full-policy growth ceiling versus autonomous hereditary attainment).** *For any strict parameters in Table 1 and finite non-empty initial population $P_0$, let $\rho_* := \max\{1, \lambda_0, \lambda_1\}$. (1) Across **all** policies $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$, there exists $C > 0$ such that $\mathbb{E}_\pi[N_t] \le C \rho_*^t$ for all $t \ge 0$, and a stationary policy in $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ attains $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$. (2) Under **autonomous multi-generational execution of the fixed hereditary program $\theta_i$** ($i \in \{0,1\}$) from a single damaged founder $\theta_i D$, paid first-step repair ($R_1=3$), gate-mediated execution, and $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$ attainment hold jointly iff $(\gamma \le \delta \land k_c \ge 1)$ for $\theta_0$, and iff $(\gamma \ge \delta \land \lambda_1 \ge 1)$ for $\theta_1$.*

Separating the two quantifiers in Theorem 1 is essential: if a controller in $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ may *abandon* hereditary execution after $t=1$, paid repair and $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$ attainment hold even in the subcritical regime. At $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (1/2, 1/4, 1/4, 1/8, 1/3)$, $k_c = 3/4$, $k_b = 5/8$, $\lambda_1 = 2/3$, and $\rho_* = 1$: neither autonomous $\theta_0$ nor autonomous $\theta_1$ attains $\rho_*=1$, yet running `care` at $t=1$ ($R_1=3$, reopening $g$) and switching to $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$ for $t \ge 2$ retains a damaged parent at $t=2$ w.p. $sq = 1/6$ (and leaves at least one $D$-token w.p. $17/72$), yielding $\Theta(1)$ via arrested dormancy rather than active lineage reproduction.

Theorem 1 (Figure 2b) yields three further separations. First, in subcritical Regime I ($\bar{\lambda} := \max\{\lambda_0,\lambda_1\} < 1$), $\rho_* = 1$ arises from rejected damaged `bloom`—analogous to dormant persisters—yet expected potential drops by at least $(1-\bar{\lambda})k_c > 0$ per actively executed step, forcing finite cumulative expected births $\mathbb{E}_\pi[\sum_{t\ge 1} B_t] \le (b+\gamma)V(P_0)/[(1-\bar{\lambda})k_c] < \infty$ for *every* $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$: attaining $\rho_*=1$ here preserves only frozen tokens rather than active organization or ongoing reproduction, so any claim of "good maintenance" must specify its bearer and criterion first. Second, in Regime III ($\gamma > \delta, \lambda_1 \ge 1$), autonomous `bloom` maximizes lineage growth while *reducing* parent survival ($s-\delta < s$), splitting organismal persistence from lineage multiplication [@okasha2006; @godfreySmith2009] and *differential retention* from *differential reproduction* [@garson2019]. Third, at critical $(2/3, 1/8, 1/4, 1/4, 1/3)$, $\lambda_1 = \rho_* = 1$ yet $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_t] = 11/12 < 1$ for all $t \ge 1$ ($k_c=11/12, k_b=25/24$). ## Four stratified grounds of purpose and the normativity challenge

Causal-role analysis [@cummins1975] lacks malfunction norms [@davies2001], selected-effects etiology [@wright1973; @millikan1984; @neander1991; @godfreySmith1994] misses de novo regulation, and organizational accounts [@mossio2009; @bich2024] face Corti's [-@corti2023, §3] and Bedau's [-@bedau1992] challenge: why does existential dependence support a functional *ought*? # Competitor diagnostics, objections, and empirical tests

## Diagnosing cross-protocol leaps in contemporary programs

*(1) Active inference:* inside each token, $(r_H, r_D)$ influences births only along $(r_H, r_D) \to (d_t, g_t, a_t) \to B_t$ through diagnostic $d_t$, gate $g_t$, *and* executed action $a_t$ (omitting $a_t$ fails local mediation since $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_1 H$ share $d_1=H, g_1=\mathrm{open}$ yet induce birth rates $b$ vs. $b+\gamma$). Such local mediation does not establish Friston's stationary nonequilibrium conditional-independence blanket assumptions [@friston2013; @parrPezzuloFriston2022; @bruineberg2022; @raja2021]; our distinct cross-protocol result is that *even an output interface sufficient to predict the entire birth stream $(B_t)_{t\ge 1}$ can erase paid repair $R_1$ ($3$ vs. $0$)*, while composite scores retain affine gauge freedom [@biehl2021] and parent survival diverges from lineage growth, requiring Rung 2 hardware anchors [@sethTsakiris2018]. *(2) Computational mechanics:* minimal $\varepsilon$-transducers [@shaliziCrutchfield2001; @barnettCrutchfield2015] and macro-emergence [@hoel2013] are protocol-relative; Proposition 3 maps their transfer boundary. *(3) Organizational vs. evolutionary teleology:* gate $g$ captures state-sensitive modulation [@bich2024] while genealogical records govern the handoff from Rung 2 tokens to Rung 3 cohorts [@cuciniello2026].

## Constructive unification and replies to four objections

Separate models $\exists x\,R(x)$ and $\exists y\,O(y)$ do not entail $\exists z\,[R(z)\land O(z)]$ when repair consumes half the six-unit budget ($3/6$) and blocks damaged `bloom` [@friedman1974; @kitcher1989]; Theorem 1 establishes exact constrained compatibility.

1. *Constructive empiricism suffices [@vanFraassen1980].* Formal theorems never compel realism; our case is comparative [@hacking1983; @woodward2003]. From damaged conservative founder $(\theta_0, D)$ ($r_H, r_D$ active bits flipped, $g=\mathrm{closed}$), specify two competing mechanisms under the six-unit budget ($3+1+2=6$): $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ majority-votes via $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$, whereas $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ uses a hardwired auxiliary `care`-latch not reading $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$. Under $b_{\mathrm{boot}}=1$, on $(D,\mathrm{care})$ both pay $R_t=3$, restore $(r_H,r_D)$ and $g \leftarrow \mathrm{open}$ at step end, and transition into the exact same post-step law $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_t) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(s)\otimes\mathrm{Bern}(b)$ of $\theta_0$-tokens (damaged w.p. $q$, re-engaging whenever $D$ recurs), inducing *identical* joint laws on $((B_t,R_t))_{t\ge 1}$. Under $\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)$, $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ fails voting ($g=\mathrm{closed}, R_t=0$, recovery probability $0$, $\mathbb{E}[B_1\mid \mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)]=0$, $B_t=0\ \forall t\ge 1$), whereas $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ recovers w.p. $1$ ($R_1=3$, $\mathbb{E}[B_1\mid \mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)]=b>0$; @rubenstein2017; Online Supplement), yielding differential severe-testing support [@mayo2018].
2. *Preservation is trivially analytic.* Fiber factorization ($F = \bar{F}\circ q$) is analytic once $F$ and $q$ are fixed; the substantive work is proving *which* compressions destroy *which* invariants—including fixed-parameter two-step birth saturation alongside cost erasure (Proposition 3), sequence-versus-gate separation (Proposition 4b), finite-memory viability (Proposition 6a), and critical deficits ($11/12 < 1$).
3. *Stipulated scaffolding falls short of full closure.* Exogenous energy inflow is shared by living cells and does not render repair fictitious [@jonas1966; @montevilMossio2015]. Because $b_{\mathrm{boot}}=1$ is fixed on the modeled horizon, $\mathcal{M}$ establishes fast-timescale paid repair (Table 3, 3a), leaving slow regeneration of $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$ (3b) and Rung 3 selection explicit.
4. *Empirical discrimination between rival mechanisms.* Mapping variables onto interventions [@craver2007; @rubenstein2017] on an *engineered fault-tolerant register* initialized at $(\theta_0, D)$ tests cost-free pass-through *(H0)* ($R_1=0$), majority-vote repair *($\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$)*, and auxiliary-latch bypass *($\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$)* across: *(Arm 1)* matched $(B_t)_{t\ge 1}$ laws under $b_{\mathrm{boot}}=1$; *(Arm 2)* per-cycle repair-energy metering ($R_1=0$ vs. $3$); *(Arm 3)* $\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)$ versus gate clamp $\mathrm{do}(g=\mathrm{closed})$ at intact $\theta_0$, separating $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ ($B_1=0$) from $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ ($\mathbb{E}[B_1]=b>0$); and *(Arm 4)* six-unit inflow titration tracking parent retention versus child spawning [@mayo2018]. In biology, *E. coli* `MutS–MutL–MutH` repair versus translesion bypass [@auWelshModrich1992; @hasanLeach2015] provides qualitative motivation, though bulk cellular ATP pools confound multiple pathways and cannot alone isolate $R_1$.

# Conclusion

When two rival explanations share the exact same predictive law on an output channel, what additional evidence warrants accepting one over the other? Our results show that extending observations along different coordinates produces mutually incomparable refinements, that autonomous hereditary attainment of full-policy growth ceilings separates sharply from plastic dormancy switching, and that parent survival can oppose lineage multiplication. Calibrating relational objecthood and four-rung bearer-indexed purpose strictly to these evidential boundaries preserves explanatory unity without overclaiming beyond what mechanism, repair budgets, and selection history warrant.

# Self-contained proofs of Propositions 1–7 and Theorem 1

*Proof of Proposition 1.* Each countably generated $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ is generated by a countable $\pi$-system $\mathcal{G}_i = \{G_{i,m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}}$ with $\mathcal{Y}_i \in \mathcal{G}_i$ [@kallenberg2021]. By sufficiency of $q$, for each $i \in \mathcal{I}$ there exists $\widetilde{K}_i$ such that $N_i := \bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}} \{h \in \mathcal{H} : K_i(h, G_{i,m}) \neq \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), G_{i,m})\} \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ satisfies $\mu(N_i) = 0$; by Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem, $K_i(h, \cdot) = \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), \cdot)$ for all $h \notin N_i$. Defining $\psi(z) := (\widetilde{K}_i(z, \cdot))_{i\in\mathcal{I}}$ and $N := \bigcup_{i\in\mathcal{I}} N_i$, countability of $\mathcal{I}$ gives $\mu(N) \le \sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}} \mu(N_i) = 0$, with $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = \psi(q(h))$ on $\mathcal{H}\setminus N$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 2.* Let $b'_{a,o}(x') = \sum_{x} b(x) T(x'\mid x,a) O(o\mid a,x')$; then $\langle b'_{a,o}, f\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o}f\rangle$ and $p(o\mid b,a) = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$. (*Sufficiency*) If $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ and $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$, then $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$ gives $p(o\mid b,a) = p(o\mid \tilde{b},a)$, and for $p(o\mid b,a) > 0$, $\langle \mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o), f\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p(o\mid b,a) = \langle \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o), f\rangle$ ($\forall f \in \mathcal{F}$). (*Necessity*) Assume (i)–(ii) on $\Delta(\mathcal{X})$. For $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$, $\langle v, \mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Perturbing $b^\circ(x) = 1/|\mathcal{X}|$ to $\tilde{b} = b^\circ + \varepsilon v \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, (i) forces $\langle v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$ ($\forall v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$), so $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$.
