# Cover Letter — Submission to *The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science*

Dear Editors of *The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science*,

I am pleased to submit **"What Prediction Leaves Out: Relational Objecthood and the Grounds of Purpose"** for consideration for publication in the *BJPS*.

The manuscript addresses a central question across philosophy of science, cognitive science, and philosophy of biology: when two rival explanations share the exact same predictive law on an output channel, what additional evidence warrants accepting one over the other? Within a self-contained stochastic model of damage, restoration, and reproduction, we establish that extending observations along different coordinates (future births versus initial repair expenditure) yields mutually incomparable predictive refinements, that autonomous hereditary attainment of full-policy growth ceilings separates sharply from plastic dormancy switching, and that proliferative investment can maximise expected lineage growth while lowering parent survival—calibrating three exclusionary criteria for relational objecthood and four bearer-indexed grounds of purpose.
