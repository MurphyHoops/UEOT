I am pleased to submit **"What Prediction Leaves Out: Relational Objecthood and the Grounds of Purpose"** for consideration for publication in the *BJPS*.

The manuscript addresses a central question across philosophy of science, cognitive science, and philosophy of biology: when two rival explanations share the exact same predictive law on an output channel, what additional evidence warrants accepting one over the other? Within a self-contained stochastic model of damage, restoration, and reproduction, we establish that two birth censuses already saturate the infinite birth law while erasing initial repair expenditure, that extending birth censuses versus adjoining first-step calorimetry yields mutually incomparable refinements over protocol probability laws, that selective structural interventions ($\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}}=0)$) separate competing recovery mechanisms under identical initial damage where both output laws and calorimetry tie, and that proliferative investment can maximise expected lineage multiplication while lowering parent survival—calibrating three diagnostic checks for relational objecthood and four bearer-indexed grounds of purpose.
