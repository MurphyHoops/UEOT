# Formal definitions and proofs

To ensure that every formal claim in the article is self-contained and independently verifiable without external manuscripts, this Appendix provides concise proofs of Propositions 1–7 and Theorem 1.

## Predictive minimality and belief-quotient descent (Propositions 1 and 2)

*Proof of Proposition 1.* Because each $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ is countably generated, $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ is generated by a countable $\pi$-system $\mathcal{G}_i = \{G_{i,m}\}_{m \in \mathbb{N}}$ closed under finite intersections (with $\mathcal{Y}_i \in \mathcal{G}_i$). By Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem [@kallenberg2021], two probability measures on $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ coincide on all of $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ iff they agree on $\mathcal{G}_i$, and the evaluation $\sigma$-algebra on $\Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ is generated by $\{ \nu \mapsto \nu(G_{i,m}) : m \in \mathbb{N}\}$. By sufficiency of $q$, for each $i \in \mathcal{I}$ there exists a measurable kernel $\widetilde{K}_i$ and a measurable null set $N_i \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ with $\mu(N_i) = 0$ such that $K_i(h, A) = \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), A)$ for all $h \notin N_i$ and $A \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ (indeed, checking equality on the countable generator $\mathcal{G}_i$ ensures measurability of the exceptional set $N_i = \bigcup_{m \in \mathbb{N}} \{h : K_i(h, G_{i,m}) \neq \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), G_{i,m})\}$). Define the measurable product decoder $\psi: \mathcal{Z} \to \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ by $\psi(z) := (\widetilde{K}_i(z, \cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}}$. Since $\mathcal{I}$ is countable, $N := \bigcup_{i \in \mathcal{I}} N_i \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ satisfies $\mu(N) \le \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \mu(N_i) = 0$, and for every $h \in \mathcal{H} \setminus N$, $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = (K_i(h,\cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}} = (\widetilde{K}_i(q(h),\cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}} = \psi(q(h))$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 2.* For any belief $b \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, action $a \in \mathcal{A}$, and observation $o \in \mathcal{O}$, the unnormalised updated measure $b'_{a,o}(x') = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} b(x) T(x' \mid x,a) O(o \mid a,x')$ satisfies $\langle b'_{a,o}, f\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o} f\rangle$ for every $f \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$, and in particular $p(o \mid b, a) = \langle b'_{a,o}, \mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$.

(*Sufficiency*) Suppose $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ for all $(a,o)$, and let $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$. Since $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$, $p(o \mid b, a) = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle \tilde{b}, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = p(o \mid \tilde{b}, a)$, establishing (i). Whenever $p(o \mid b,a) > 0$, every $f \in \mathcal{F}$ satisfies $U_{a,o}f \in \mathcal{F}$ and hence
$$
\langle \mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o), f\rangle = \frac{\langle b, U_{a,o}f\rangle}{p(o \mid b,a)} = \frac{\langle \tilde{b}, U_{a,o}f\rangle}{p(o \mid \tilde{b},a)} = \langle \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o), f\rangle,
$$
establishing $\mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o) \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o)$ and thus (ii).

(*Necessity*) Suppose (i) and (ii) hold for all $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$ and $(a,o)$. Fix any $(a,o)$ and any $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$; since $\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$, we have $\langle v, \mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Choose the strictly positive uniform belief $b^\circ(x) = 1/|\mathcal{X}|$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough that $\tilde{b} := b^\circ + \varepsilon v \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, so $b^\circ \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$. By (i), $\langle b^\circ, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle b^\circ + \varepsilon v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$, forcing $\langle v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Since $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$ was arbitrary, $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in (\mathcal{F}^\perp)^\perp = \mathcal{F}$. Next, fix any $f \in \mathcal{F}$. If $p_0 := p(o \mid b^\circ, a) = 0$, positivity of $b^\circ$ and non-negativity of $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}$ imply $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \equiv 0$, whence $|U_{a,o}f| \le \|f\|_\infty U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} = 0$ and $U_{a,o}f = 0 \in \mathcal{F}$. If instead $p_0 > 0$, then $p(o \mid \tilde{b}, a) = p_0 > 0$ by (i), and (ii) yields $\langle b^\circ, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p_0 = \langle b^\circ + \varepsilon v, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p_0$, so $\langle v, U_{a,o}f\rangle = 0$ for all $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$. Therefore $U_{a,o}f \in (\mathcal{F}^\perp)^\perp = \mathcal{F}$. $\square$

## Cut-set reconstruction, finite-memory separation, and fixed-sample impossibility (Propositions 5 and 6)

*Proof of Proposition 5.* Let $\mathcal{V}_{\min}$ be the set of $\subseteq$-minimal elements of $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$. If $M \subseteq D \in \mathcal{I}$ for some $M \in \mathcal{V}_{\min} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$, then $D \in \mathcal{F}$ by monotonicity. Conversely, if $D \in \mathcal{F}$, finiteness of $D$ and downward closure of $\mathcal{I}$ imply $|D| \in \mathbb{N}$; choosing a subset $M \subseteq D$ with $M \in \mathcal{F}$ of minimum cardinality yields $M \in \mathcal{V}_{\min}$ with $M \subseteq D$. Uniqueness holds because no two distinct elements of an antichain are comparable: if $\mathcal{A}_1, \mathcal{A}_2$ are two antichains generating $\mathcal{F}$ upward, each $M \in \mathcal{A}_1$ contains some $M' \in \mathcal{A}_2$ which contains some $M'' \in \mathcal{A}_1$, forcing $M = M'' = M'$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 6.*
**(a)** *Finite-memory separation witness.* Consider the five-state system $\mathcal{X} = \{x_L, x_R, y_L, y_R, \bot\}$ with safe set $S = \{x_L, x_R, y_L, y_R\}$, actions $\mathcal{A} = \{a_L, a_R\}$, and observation space $\mathcal{O} = \{o_L, o_R, o_M, o_\bot\}$ given by $h(x_L) = o_L$, $h(x_R) = o_R$, $h(y_L) = h(y_R) = o_M$, and $h(\bot) = o_\bot$. Define deterministic transitions as follows: from $x_L$, both actions transition to $y_L$; from $x_R$, both actions transition to $y_R$; from $y_L$, action $a_L$ returns to $x_L$ whereas $a_R$ transitions to the absorbing failure state $\bot$; from $y_R$, action $a_R$ returns to $x_R$ whereas $a_L$ transitions to $\bot$; and $\bot$ absorbs under both actions. Since $y_L$ has safe action $a_L$ and $y_R$ has safe action $a_R$, $F_S(S) = S$ and hence $K^\star = \operatorname{gfp}(F_S) = S$. Under the initial law $\mu_0 = \frac{1}{2}\delta_{x_L} + \frac{1}{2}\delta_{x_R}$, at $t=0$ the controller observes $o_0 \in \{o_L, o_R\}$ noiselessly, and at $t=1$ the system occupies $\{y_L, y_R\}$ with probability $1/2$ each while emitting the pooled observation $o_1 = o_M$. Any memoryless observation policy $\pi_0: \mathcal{O} \to \Delta(\mathcal{A})$ must select a single action probability $p := \pi_0(a_L \mid o_M) \in [0,1]$ at $o_M$, incurring two-step failure probability $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_0}(X_2 = \bot) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - p) + \frac{1}{2} p = \frac{1}{2}$. By contrast, the one-step memory controller $\pi_1(o_{t-1}, o_t)$ that plays $a_L$ on $(o_L, o_M)$ and $a_R$ on $(o_R, o_M)$ (and $a_L$ otherwise) satisfies $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_1}(X_t \in K^\star\ \forall t \ge 0) = 1$.

**(b)** *Fixed-sample impossibility.* Under $Z_1, \dots, Z_n \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(r)$, represent any (possibly randomised) certification rule by $\psi: \{0,1\}^n \to [0,1]$, where $\psi(z)$ is the probability of certifying zero violation on sample $z$. The certification probability $g(r) := \mathbb{E}_r[\psi(Z_{1:n})] = \sum_{z \in \{0,1\}^n} \psi(z) r^{\sum z_i} (1-r)^{n - \sum z_i}$ satisfies $|g(0) - g(\varepsilon)| \le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0)^{\otimes n}, \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)^{\otimes n}) = 1 - (1-\varepsilon)^n \le n\varepsilon$. Consequently, if $g(r) \le \alpha$ for all $r \in (0,1]$, letting $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ yields $g(0) \le \alpha$; equivalently, if $g(0) \ge 1 - \beta > \alpha$, then $g(\varepsilon) \ge 1 - \beta - n\varepsilon > \alpha$ for every $\varepsilon \in (0, (1 - \beta - \alpha)/n)$. $\square$

## Smooth bottleneck obstruction (Proposition 7)

*Proof of Proposition 7.* On $U$, $\Psi(s,b,q) = (s, b, bq)$ is $C^\infty$ with Jacobian matrix
$$
D\Psi(s,b,q) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & q & b \end{pmatrix}, \qquad \det D\Psi(s,b,q) = b > 0.
$$
Thus $\operatorname{rank} D\Psi(u) = 3$ for every $u \in U$. If $\Psi = g \circ f$ on any non-empty open set $V \subseteq U$ with $f \in C^1(V, \mathbb{R}^2)$ and $g \in C^1(\mathbb{R}^2, \mathbb{R}^3)$, the chain rule gives $D\Psi(u) = Dg(f(u))\, Df(u)$ at $u \in V$, forcing $3 = \operatorname{rank} D\Psi(u) \le \min\{\operatorname{rank} Dg(f(u)),\, \operatorname{rank} Df(u)\} \le 2$, a contradiction. $\square$

## Regenerative process $\mathcal{M}$, the 2–3–4 partition, and quotients (Propositions 3 and 4)

*Exact transition specification of $\mathcal{M}$.* Each step on a living token proceeds through diagnosis $d \in \{H, D\}$ (where $d = H$ iff row parity matches backups and $g = \mathrm{open}$), action request $a \in \{\mathrm{care}, \mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{stop}\}$, budget check against $6$ units, actuation, and post-step damage:

- `stop`: parent terminated, $0$ strict births, expenditure $0$.
- Healthy ($d = H$, $g = \mathrm{open}$, $R = 0$): `care` costs $1 + 2 = 3 \le 6$, retains parent w.p. $s$ and creates $1$ strict newborn w.p. $b$ independently; `bloom` costs $2 + 2 = 4 \le 6$, retains parent w.p. $s - \delta$ and creates $1$ strict newborn w.p. $b + \gamma$ independently.
- Damaged ($d = D$, active bits flipped or $g = \mathrm{closed}$, backups intact, $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$): `care` pays $R = 3$ to majority-restore active bits and set $g = \mathrm{open}$, then spends $1 + 2 = 3$ ($3+3=6 \le 6$) to execute `care` with retention $s$ and strict birth $b$; `bloom` requires $3 + 2 + 2 = 7 > 6$, so the transaction is *rejected* ($R = 0$, damaged parent retained w.p. $1$, $0$ strict births).
- Every retained parent or strict newborn from an executed (`care` or `bloom`) step carries the parent's intact restored backup/active rows and suffers independent post-step environmental damage w.p. $q$ (becoming $D$) and stays $H$ w.p. $1-q$.

*Proof of Proposition 3.* Under autonomous internal execution:
**(1)** *At $t=1$*: $\theta_0 H$ runs healthy `care` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b), R_1 = 0$); $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ both pay $R_1 = 3$, restore their codes, and run `care` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b)$); $\theta_1 H$ runs healthy `bloom` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b+\gamma), R_1 = 0$). Since $\gamma > 0$, $b + \gamma \neq b$, giving exactly two $B_1$-classes: $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$ and $\{\theta_1 H\}$.

**(2)** *All finite birth records $(B_1,\dots,B_T)$*: After actuation at $t=1$, both $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ yield $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_1) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(s) \otimes \mathrm{Bern}(b)$ of tokens all carrying $\theta_0$, independently damaged w.p. $q$. Hence their post-$t=1$ population states have identical distributions, and by Markov induction $(B_1, \dots, B_T)$ has the exact same joint law under $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ for every $T \ge 1$. Meanwhile, $\theta_1 H$ already differs at $B_1$. Finally, compare $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ at $t=2$: at the end of $t=1$, both have $N_1 = I_{\mathrm{ret}} + B_1$ living tokens with $\mathbb{E}[N_1] = s + b > 0$, each healthy at $t=2$ w.p. $1-q$ and damaged w.p. $q$. Under $\theta_0 D$, every token at $t=2$ executes `care` (expected strict births $b$), so $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)b$. Under $\theta_1 D$, each healthy token at $t=2$ executes `bloom` (expected strict births $b+\gamma$) while each damaged token repairs and executes `care` ($b$), so $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] = (s+b)\bigl[(1-q)(b+\gamma) + q b\bigr] = (s+b)\bigl[b + (1-q)\gamma\bigr]$. Because $s+b > 0$, $1-q > 0$, and $\gamma > 0$, $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)(1-q)\gamma > 0$, separating $\theta_1 D$ from $\theta_0 D$ (and since $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 H}[B_1] = b+\gamma > b = \mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_1]$, $\theta_1 D \not\sim \theta_1 H$). Thus there are exactly three classes: $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 H\}$. Substituting $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$ gives $s+b = 9/8$, so $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = \frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{3}{8} = \frac{27}{64}$ and $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] = \frac{9}{8}\bigl(\frac{3}{8} + \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\bigr) = \frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{13}{24} = \frac{39}{64}$.

**(3)** *Adding $R_1$*: On $(B_1, R_1)$ alone, $(\theta_0 H) \mapsto (\mathrm{Bern}(b), 0)$, $(\theta_1 H) \mapsto (\mathrm{Bern}(b+\gamma), 0)$, and both $\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D \mapsto (\mathrm{Bern}(b), 3)$, yielding three classes $\{\theta_0 H\}, \{\theta_1 H\}, \{\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$. Adjoining $R_1$ to $(B_1, B_2, \dots)$ splits $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$ via $R_1 = 0 \neq 3$, yielding all four singletons. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 4 (Tag erasure vs. gate erasure).* **(a)** Because no transition probability, affordability check, or payment depends on $\tau$, erasing $\tau$ defines a strong Markov lumpability [@burkeRosenblatt1958] commuting with every public-history policy. **(b)** Consider two tokens with intact three-cell rows $(1,1,1)$ and $(0,0,0)$ (majority-decoded programme $\theta_1 = (\mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{care})$), one with $g = \mathrm{open}$ and one with $g = \mathrm{closed}$. Majority decoding maps both to $\theta_1$, yet raw actuation without gate-opening repair executes `bloom` on $g = \mathrm{open}$ (birth rate $b+\gamma$) and is physically blocked by $g = \mathrm{closed}$. $\square$

## Full-policy growth ceiling, attainment, and extinction bounds (Theorem 1)

*Proof of Theorem 1.*
**(1)** *Reproductive-potential upper envelope and optimality of $\rho_*$.* Define the positive single-token potential $v: \{H, D\} \to (0, \infty)$ by
$$
v(H) := \max\{k_c, k_b\}, \qquad v(D) := k_c,
$$
so $0 < v_{\min} := k_c \le v(x) \le v_{\max} := \max\{k_c, k_b\}$ for $x \in \{H, D\}$. For any single-token diagnosis $x$ and action request $a \in \{\mathrm{care}, \mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{stop}\}$, let $(L_a v)(x)$ denote the expected post-damage sum of $v$ across the resulting one-step outputs. From any executed step with expected total output count $k \in \{k_c, k_b\}$, each output is independently $H$ w.p. $1-q$ and $D$ w.p. $q$, yielding $(L_a v)(x) = k\bigl[(1-q)v(H) + q v(D)\bigr]$. Consider two exhaustive parameter cases:

- *Case 1: $\gamma \le \delta$ ($k_b \le k_c$).* Here $\lambda_1 \le \lambda_0 = k_c$, $\rho_* = \max\{1, k_c\}$, and $v(H) = v(D) = k_c$. Executed `care` on $H$ or $D$ gives $(L_{\mathrm{care}} v)(x) = k_c \cdot k_c = \lambda_0 v(x) \le \rho_* v(x)$; executed `bloom` on $H$ gives $(L_{\mathrm{bloom}} v)(H) = k_b k_c \le k_c^2 = \lambda_0 v(H) \le \rho_* v(H)$; rejected $(D, \mathrm{bloom})$ preserves the $D$-token unchanged, giving $(L_{\mathrm{bloom}} v)(D) = v(D) \le \max\{1, \lambda_0\} v(D) = \rho_* v(D)$; and `stop` gives $0 \le \rho_* v(x)$.
- *Case 2: $\gamma > \delta$ ($k_b > k_c$).* Here $\lambda_1 = (1-q)k_b + q k_c > k_c = \lambda_0$, $\rho_* = \max\{1, \lambda_1\}$, and $v(H) = k_b > k_c = v(D)$. Checking each pair: $(L_{\mathrm{bloom}} v)(H) = k_b[(1-q)k_b + q k_c] = k_b \lambda_1 = \lambda_1 v(H) \le \rho_* v(H)$; $(L_{\mathrm{care}} v)(H) = k_c \lambda_1 < k_b \lambda_1 = \lambda_1 v(H) \le \rho_* v(H)$; $(L_{\mathrm{care}} v)(D) = k_c[(1-q)k_b + q k_c] = k_c \lambda_1 = \lambda_1 v(D) \le \rho_* v(D)$; rejected $(D, \mathrm{bloom})$ gives $v(D) \le \max\{1, \lambda_1\} v(D) = \rho_* v(D)$; and `stop` gives $0 \le \rho_* v(x)$.

In both cases, $(L_a v)(x) \le \rho_* v(x)$ holds uniformly for all $(x,a)$. For any policy $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$, step-$(t+1)$ actuation and damage noises are independent of the public history $\mathcal{F}_t$ and current randomised requests. Setting $V(P_t) := \sum_{u \in P_t} v(x_u)$ gives $\mathbb{E}_\pi[V(P_{t+1}) \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \le \rho_* V(P_t)$, whence by induction
$$
\mathbb{E}_\pi[N_t] \le \frac{1}{v_{\min}} \mathbb{E}_\pi[V(P_t)] \le \frac{v_{\max} |P_0|}{k_c} \rho_*^t =: C \rho_*^t \qquad (\forall \pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}},\ \forall t \ge 0).
$$
To establish tightness and minimality of $\rho_*$ from any non-empty $P_0$: if $\rho_* = 1$, define $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$ requesting `care` on $H$ and `bloom` on $D$—either $P_0$ already contains a $D$-token (preserved w.p. $1$) or executing `care` at $t=1$ on an $H$-token yields a surviving $D$-token w.p. at least $s q > 0$, maintaining $\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}}[N_t] \ge \min\{1, s q\} \cdot 1^t$; if $\rho_* = \lambda_0 \ge \max\{1, \lambda_1\}$, stationary `care` ($\theta_0$) yields $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0}[N_t] = |P_0| \lambda_0^t$; and if $\rho_* = \lambda_1 \ge \max\{1, \lambda_0\}$ ($\gamma \ge \delta$), both $(H, \mathrm{bloom})$ and $(D, \mathrm{care})$ satisfy exact eigen-equality $(L_{\theta_1(x)} v)(x) = \lambda_1 v(x)$, so $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1}[V(P_t)] = V(P_0) \lambda_1^t$ and hence $\frac{V(P_0)}{v_{\max}} \rho_*^t \le \mathbb{E}_{\theta_1}[N_t] \le \frac{V(P_0)}{v_{\min}} \rho_*^t$ for all $t \ge 0$.

**(2)** *Exact attainment conditions from a damaged founder.*

- From $\theta_0 D$: paid repair ($R_1 = 3$) and `care` at $t=1$ followed by `care` thereafter give $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[N_t] = k_c^t = \lambda_0^t$, which is $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$ iff $\lambda_0 = \rho_* \iff k_c \ge 1$ and $\lambda_0 \ge \lambda_1 \iff (1-q)(\delta - \gamma) \ge 0 \iff \gamma \le \delta$.
- From $\theta_1 D$: paid repair ($R_1 = 3$) and `care` at $t=1$ yield $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_1] = k_c$, with every surviving or newborn token entering step $t \ge 2$ independently $H$ w.p. $1-q$ (running `bloom`, mean $k_b$) and $D$ w.p. $q$ (repairing and running `care`, mean $k_c$). By induction, $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_t] = k_c \lambda_1^{t-1}$ for every $t \ge 1$, which is $\Theta(\rho_*^t)$ iff $\lambda_1 = \rho_* \iff \lambda_1 \ge 1$ and $\lambda_1 \ge \lambda_0 \iff \gamma \ge \delta$.

*Critical $11/12$ identity and first-census extinction/share identities.* At the critical parameter vector $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (2/3, 1/8, 1/4, 1/4, 1/3)$, we have $k_c = \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{11}{12}$, $k_b = \frac{2}{3} - \frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{4} = \frac{25}{24}$, and $\lambda_1 = \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{25}{24} + \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{11}{12} = 1$, giving $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_t] = \frac{11}{12} \cdot 1^{t-1} = \frac{11}{12}$ for all $t \ge 1$.

At the supercritical parameter vector $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$ with independent healthy founders $\theta_1 H$ ($I_{\mathrm{ret}}^{(1)} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(5/8)$, $B_1^{(1)} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(5/8)$) and $\theta_0 H$ ($I_{\mathrm{ret}}^{(0)} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(3/4)$, $B_1^{(0)} \sim \mathrm{Bern}(3/8)$):

- $\mathbb{P}(N_1^{(1)} = 0,1,2) = \bigl(\frac{9}{64}, \frac{30}{64}, \frac{25}{64}\bigr)$ with $\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(1)}] = \frac{5}{4}$, and $\mathbb{P}(N_1^{(0)} = 0,1,2) = \bigl(\frac{5}{32}, \frac{18}{32}, \frac{9}{32}\bigr)$ with $\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(0)}] = \frac{9}{8}$.
- $\mathbb{P}(N_1^{(1)} = 0 \land N_1^{(0)} > 0) = \frac{9}{64} \cdot \frac{27}{32} = \frac{243}{2048}$; and $\frac{\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(1)}]}{\mathbb{E}[N_1^{(1)} + N_1^{(0)}]} = \frac{5/4}{19/8} = \frac{10}{19}$.
- Setting $S_1 := \frac{N_1^{(1)}}{N_1^{(1)} + N_1^{(0)}}$ ($0/0 := 0$), summing over $(n_1, n_0) \in \{1,2\} \times \{0,1,2\}$ yields
  $$
  \mathbb{E}[S_1] = \frac{30}{64}\Bigl(\frac{5}{32} + \frac{9}{32} + \frac{3}{32}\Bigr) + \frac{25}{64}\Bigl(\frac{5}{32} + \frac{12}{32} + \frac{9}{64}\Bigr) = \frac{30}{64}\cdot\frac{17}{32} + \frac{25}{64}\cdot\frac{43}{64} = \frac{2095}{4096}.
  $$
- Since $\mathbb{P}(N_1^{(1)} + N_1^{(0)} > 0) = 1 - \frac{9}{64}\cdot\frac{5}{32} = \frac{4006}{4096}$, $\mathbb{E}[S_1 \mid N_1 > 0] = \frac{2095}{4006}$. $\square$
