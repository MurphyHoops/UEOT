## Predictive minimality and belief-quotient descent (Propositions 1 and 2)

*Proof of Proposition 1.* Because each $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ is countably generated, $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ is generated by a countable $\pi$-system $\mathcal{G}_i = \{G_{i,m}\}_{m \in \mathbb{N}}$ closed under finite intersections (with $\mathcal{Y}_i \in \mathcal{G}_i$). By Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem [@kallenberg2021], two probability measures on $(\mathcal{Y}_i, \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i})$ coincide on all of $\Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ iff they agree on $\mathcal{G}_i$, and the evaluation $\sigma$-algebra on $\Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ is generated by $\{ \nu \mapsto \nu(G_{i,m}) : m \in \mathbb{N}\}$. By sufficiency of $q$, for each $i \in \mathcal{I}$ there exists a measurable kernel $\widetilde{K}_i$ and a measurable null set $N_i \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ with $\mu(N_i) = 0$ such that $K_i(h, A) = \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), A)$ for all $h \notin N_i$ and $A \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{Y}_i}$ (checking equality on the countable generator $\mathcal{G}_i$ ensures measurability of the exceptional set $N_i = \bigcup_{m \in \mathbb{N}} \{h : K_i(h, G_{i,m}) \neq \widetilde{K}_i(q(h), G_{i,m})\}$). Define the measurable product decoder $\psi: \mathcal{Z} \to \prod_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \Delta(\mathcal{Y}_i)$ by $\psi(z) := (\widetilde{K}_i(z, \cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}}$. Since $\mathcal{I}$ is countable, $N := \bigcup_{i \in \mathcal{I}} N_i \in \Sigma_{\mathcal{H}}$ satisfies $\mu(N) \le \sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}} \mu(N_i) = 0$, and for every $h \in \mathcal{H} \setminus N$, $C_{\mathcal{P}}(h) = (K_i(h,\cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}} = (\widetilde{K}_i(q(h),\cdot))_{i \in \mathcal{I}} = \psi(q(h))$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 2.* For any belief $b \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, action $a \in \mathcal{A}$, and observation $o \in \mathcal{O}$, the unnormalised updated measure $b'_{a,o}(x') = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} b(x) T(x' \mid x,a) O(o \mid a,x')$ satisfies $\langle b'_{a,o}, f\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o} f\rangle$ for every $f \in \mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{X}}$, and in particular $p(o \mid b, a) = \langle b'_{a,o}, \mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$.

(*Sufficiency*) Suppose $U_{a,o}\mathcal{F} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$ for all $(a,o)$, and let $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$. Since $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$, $p(o \mid b, a) = \langle b, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle \tilde{b}, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = p(o \mid \tilde{b}, a)$, establishing (i). Whenever $p(o \mid b,a) > 0$, every $f \in \mathcal{F}$ satisfies $U_{a,o}f \in \mathcal{F}$ and hence
$$
\langle \mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o), f\rangle = \frac{\langle b, U_{a,o}f\rangle}{p(o \mid b,a)} = \frac{\langle \tilde{b}, U_{a,o}f\rangle}{p(o \mid \tilde{b},a)} = \langle \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o), f\rangle,
$$
establishing $\mathrm{Bayes}(b,a,o) \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \mathrm{Bayes}(\tilde{b},a,o)$ and thus (ii).

(*Necessity on $\Delta(\mathcal{X})$ and reachable-fibre boundary*) Suppose (i) and (ii) hold for all $b \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$ in $\Delta(\mathcal{X})$ and $(a,o)$. Fix any $(a,o)$ and any $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$; since $\mathbf{1} \in \mathcal{F}$, we have $\langle v, \mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Choose the strictly positive uniform belief $b^\circ(x) = 1/|\mathcal{X}|$ and $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough that $\tilde{b} := b^\circ + \varepsilon v \in \Delta(\mathcal{X})$, so $b^\circ \equiv_{\mathcal{F}} \tilde{b}$. By (i), $\langle b^\circ, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = \langle b^\circ + \varepsilon v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle$, forcing $\langle v, U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}\rangle = 0$. Since $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$ was arbitrary, $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \in (\mathcal{F}^\perp)^\perp = \mathcal{F}$ [@poupartBoutilier2003]. Next, fix any $f \in \mathcal{F}$. If $p_0 := p(o \mid b^\circ, a) = 0$, positivity of $b^\circ$ and non-negativity of $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1}$ imply $U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} \equiv 0$, whence $|U_{a,o}f| \le \|f\|_\infty U_{a,o}\mathbf{1} = 0$ and $U_{a,o}f = 0 \in \mathcal{F}$. If instead $p_0 > 0$, then $p(o \mid \tilde{b}, a) = p_0 > 0$ by (i), and (ii) yields $\langle b^\circ, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p_0 = \langle b^\circ + \varepsilon v, U_{a,o}f\rangle / p_0$, so $\langle v, U_{a,o}f\rangle = 0$ for all $v \in \mathcal{F}^\perp$, whence $U_{a,o}f \in (\mathcal{F}^\perp)^\perp = \mathcal{F}$. When restricting to a forward-update-closed reachable subset $\mathcal{R} \subseteq \Delta(\mathcal{X})$ from a fixed prior, exact recursion on $\mathcal{R}/{\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}}$ requires matching observation probabilities and posterior $\mathcal{F}$-expectations only across reachable pairs $(b, b') \in \mathcal{R}^2$ merged in the same $\equiv_{\mathcal{F}}$-fibre, rather than subspace invariance on $\operatorname{span}(\mathcal{R})$: on $\mathcal{X}=\{0,1,2\}$ under the deterministic cycle $0 \to 1 \to 2 \to 0$ with trivial observations from $\delta_0$, $\mathcal{R}=\{\delta_0,\delta_1,\delta_2\}$ spans $\mathbb{R}^3$, yet $\mathcal{F}=\operatorname{span}\{\mathbf{1},(0,1,2)\}$ takes distinct values $0,1,2$ on $\mathcal{R}$ (zero false merges, exact quotient recursion) while $U(0,1,2) = (1,2,0) \notin \mathcal{F}$. $\square$

## Cut-set reconstruction, finite-memory separation, and fixed-sample impossibility (Propositions 5, 6, and 7)

*Proof of Proposition 5.* Let $\mathcal{V}_{\min}$ be the set of $\subseteq$-minimal elements of $\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{C}, \mathcal{I})$. If $M \subseteq D \in \mathcal{I}$ for some $M \in \mathcal{V}_{\min} \subseteq \mathcal{F}$, then $D \in \mathcal{F}$ by monotonicity. Conversely, if $D \in \mathcal{F}$, finiteness of $D$ and downward closure of $\mathcal{I}$ imply $|D| \in \mathbb{N}$; choosing a subset $M \subseteq D$ with $M \in \mathcal{F}$ of minimum cardinality yields $M \in \mathcal{V}_{\min}$ with $M \subseteq D$. Uniqueness holds because no two distinct elements of an antichain are comparable. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 6.*
**(a)** Consider $\mathcal{X} = \{x_L, x_R, y_L, y_R, \bot\}$ with safe set $S = \{x_L, x_R, y_L, y_R\}$, actions $\mathcal{A} = \{a_L, a_R\}$, and observations $\mathcal{O} = \{o_L, o_R, o_M, o_\bot\}$ given by $h(x_L) = o_L$, $h(x_R) = o_R$, $h(y_L) = h(y_R) = o_M$, and $h(\bot) = o_\bot$. From $x_L$, both actions transition to $y_L$; from $x_R$, both transition to $y_R$; from $y_L$, $a_L \to x_L$ and $a_R \to \bot$; from $y_R$, $a_R \to x_R$ and $a_L \to \bot$; and $\bot$ absorbs ($K^\star = \operatorname{gfp}(F_S) = S$). Under $\mu_0 = \frac{1}{2}\delta_{x_L} + \frac{1}{2}\delta_{x_R}$, at $t=1$ the system occupies $\{y_L, y_R\}$ equally with pooled observation $o_M$: any memoryless $\pi_0(a_L \mid o_M) = p \in [0,1]$ incurs $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_0}(X_2 = \bot) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - p) + \frac{1}{2} p = \frac{1}{2}$, whereas $\pi_1(o_L, o_M) = a_L$, $\pi_1(o_R, o_M) = a_R$ maintains $\mathbb{P}_{\pi_1}(X_t \in K^\star\ \forall t \ge 0) = 1$.

**(b)** Under $Z_1, \dots, Z_n \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(r)$ and $\psi: \{0,1\}^n \to [0,1]$, $g(r) := \mathbb{E}_r[\psi(Z_{1:n})]$ satisfies $|g(0) - g(\varepsilon)| \le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mathrm{Bernoulli}(0)^{\otimes n}, \mathrm{Bernoulli}(\varepsilon)^{\otimes n}) = 1 - (1-\varepsilon)^n \le n\varepsilon$ [@leCam1973; @mayo2018], so $\sup_{r \in (0,1]} g(r) \le \alpha \implies g(0) \le \alpha$. $\square$

*Proof of Proposition 7.* On $U$, $\Psi(s,b,q) = (s, b, bq)$ has $\det D\Psi(s,b,q) = b > 0$ ($\operatorname{rank} D\Psi = 3$), contradicting $\operatorname{rank}(Dg \cdot Df) \le 2$ for any $C^1$ factorisation through $\mathbb{R}^2$. $\square$

## Regenerative process $\mathcal{M}$, Blackwell non-simulability, and the three-step parameter boundary (Propositions 3 and 4)

*Exact transition specification of $\mathcal{M}$.* Each step on a living token proceeds through internal/public diagnosis $d \in \{H, D\}$ (where $d = H$ iff row parity matches backups and $g = \mathrm{open}$), action request $a \in \{\mathrm{care}, \mathrm{bloom}, \mathrm{stop}\}$, budget check against $6$ units, actuation, and post-step damage:

- `stop`: parent terminated, $0$ strict births, expenditure $0$.
- Healthy ($d = H$, $g = \mathrm{open}$, $R = 0$): `care` costs $1 + 2 = 3 \le 6$, retains parent w.p. $s$ and creates $1$ strict newborn w.p. $b$ independently; `bloom` costs $2 + 2 = 4 \le 6$, retains parent w.p. $s - \delta$ and creates $1$ strict newborn w.p. $b + \gamma$ independently.
- Damaged ($d = D$, active bits flipped or $g = \mathrm{closed}$, backups intact, $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$): `care` pays $R = 3$ to majority-restore active bits and set $g = \mathrm{open}$, then spends $1 + 2 = 3$ ($3+3=6 \le 6$) to execute `care` with retention $s$ and strict birth $b$; `bloom` requires $3 + 2 + 2 = 7 > 6$, so the transaction is *rejected* ($R = 0$, damaged parent retained w.p. $1$, $0$ strict births).
- Every retained parent or strict newborn from an executed step copies intact restored rows and suffers independent post-step environmental damage w.p. $q$ (becoming $D$) and stays $H$ w.p. $1-q$.

*Proof of Proposition 3.* Fix *any* strict Table 1 parameter vector ($0 < \delta < s < 1$, $b > 0$, $\gamma > 0$, $b+\gamma < 1$, $0 < q < 1$, without assuming $\gamma > \delta$ or $s+b > 1$) and consider autonomous hereditary execution from $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 H, \theta_1 D\}$:
**(1)** *At $t=1$*: $\theta_0 H$ runs healthy `care` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b), R_1 = 0$); $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ both pay $R_1 = 3$, restore their codes, and run `care` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b)$); $\theta_1 H$ runs healthy `bloom` ($B_1 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b+\gamma), R_1 = 0$). Since $\gamma > 0$, $b + \gamma \neq b$, giving two $B_1$-classes: $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$ and $\{\theta_1 H\}$.

**(2)** *All finite birth records $(B_1,\dots,B_T)$ at fixed parameters*: After $t=1$, both $\theta_0 H$ and $\theta_0 D$ leave $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_1) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(s) \otimes \mathrm{Bern}(b)$ of $\theta_0$-tokens independently damaged w.p. $q$, inducing identical joint laws on $(B_1, \dots, B_T)$ for all $T \ge 1$. Comparing $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ at $t=2$: both have $\mathbb{E}[N_1] = s + b > 0$, each healthy at $t=2$ w.p. $1-q$ and damaged w.p. $q$, giving $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)b$ versus $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] = (s+b)\bigl[(1-q)(b+\gamma) + q b\bigr]$; since $(s+b)(1-q)\gamma > 0$, $\theta_1 D \not\sim \theta_0 D$, yielding three classes $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 D\}$, $\{\theta_1 H\}$ ($27/64$ vs. $39/64$ at $(3/4, 1/8, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$).

**(3)** *Adding $R_1$*: On $(B_1, R_1)$, $(\theta_0 H) \mapsto (\mathrm{Bern}(b), 0)$, $(\theta_1 H) \mapsto (\mathrm{Bern}(b+\gamma), 0)$, and $\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D \mapsto (\mathrm{Bern}(b), 3)$, yielding three classes $\{\theta_0 H\}, \{\theta_1 H\}, \{\theta_0 D, \theta_1 D\}$ incomparable with (2). Adjoining $R_1$ to $(B_1, B_2, \dots)$ splits $\{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D\}$ into four singletons. $\square$

*Blackwell experiment non-simulability and three-step parameter-variation boundary.* Across the four fixed-parameter founders $F \in \{\theta_0 H, \theta_0 D, \theta_1 H, \theta_1 D\}$, $(B_1, B_2)$ and $(B_1, R_1)$ are mutually incomparable as statistical experiments [@blackwell1953; @barnettCrutchfield2015]: for any uniform randomised post-processing kernel $M_1$ applied to $(B_1, B_2)$, equality of $\mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 H}(B_1, B_2) = \mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 D}(B_1, B_2)$ forces $M_1 \circ \mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 H}(B_1, B_2) = M_1 \circ \mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 D}(B_1, B_2)$, which cannot match $\mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 H}(B_1, R_1) \neq \mathcal{L}_{\theta_0 D}(B_1, R_1)$ ($R_1 = 0$ vs. $3$); symmetrically, any kernel $M_2$ applied to $(B_1, R_1)$ assigns identical post-processed laws to $\theta_0 D$ and $\theta_1 D$ (since both have $(B_1, R_1) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(b) \otimes \delta_3$), failing to reproduce $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[B_2] - \mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[B_2] = (s+b)(1-q)\gamma > 0$.

Furthermore, two-step saturation $(B_1, B_2) \equiv (B_t)_{t\ge 1}$ requires *fixed* model parameters. Once unknown parameters enter the inference target, $t=3$ carries strictly new information. Initialise $\theta_1 D$ at fixed $(s, b, \gamma, q) = (3/4, 3/8, 1/4, 1/3)$ and compare maternal `bloom` survival penalty $\delta = 1/8$ against $\delta' = 1/4$:

1. *Identical $(B_1, B_2)$ joint law:* At $t=1$, $\theta_1 D$ repairs ($R_1=3$) and executes `care`, whose retention $s=3/4$, birth $b=3/8$ ($\mathbb{E}[N_1] = 9/8$), and post-step damage $q=1/3$ do not involve $\delta$. At $t=2$, conditional on $(B_1, N_1)$ and step-$2$ diagnoses, each $H$-token generates a strict birth w.p. $b+\gamma = 5/8$ and each $D$-token repairs and generates a strict birth w.p. $b = 3/8$; because maternal `bloom` survival $s-\delta$ acts on $N_2$ only *after* $B_2$ is generated, the entire joint distribution on $(B_1, B_2) \in \{0,1\} \times \{0,1,2\}$—specifically $(0,0): \frac{95}{256}$, $(0,1): \frac{65}{256}$, $(0,2): 0$, $(1,0): \frac{209}{2048}$, $(1,1): \frac{195}{1024}$, $(1,2): \frac{169}{2048}$—is identical under $\delta = 1/8$ and $\delta' = 1/4$.
2. *Strict separation at $t=3$:* At the end of $t=2$, an $H$-token leaves mean outputs $k_b(\delta) = s - \delta + b + \gamma$ ($5/4$ for $\delta=1/8$ vs. $9/8$ for $\delta'=1/4$) while a $D$-token leaves $k_c = 9/8$, giving $\lambda_1(1/8) = \frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{5}{4} + \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{9}{8} = \frac{29}{24}$ and $\lambda_1(1/4) = \frac{9}{8}$, so $\mathbb{E}_\delta[N_2] = \frac{9}{8}\lambda_1(\delta)$. Every token in $N_2$ enters $t=3$ independently as $H$ w.p. $2/3$ and $D$ w.p. $1/3$, producing $\frac{2}{3}\cdot\frac{5}{8} + \frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{3}{8} = \frac{13}{24}$ expected step-$3$ births. Hence
$$
\mathbb{E}_{\delta=1/8}[B_3] = \frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{29}{24}\cdot\frac{13}{24} = \frac{377}{512} \neq \frac{351}{512} = \frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{9}{8}\cdot\frac{13}{24} = \mathbb{E}_{\delta'=1/4}[B_3].
$$

*Proof of Proposition 4.* **(a)** Because no transition probability, affordability check, or payment depends on $\tau$, erasing $\tau$ defines a strong Markov lumpability [@burkeRosenblatt1958] commuting with every $\tau$-blind policy lifted from the quotient (in particular, across $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$). **(b)** Two intact $\theta_1$-decoded assemblies $(1,1,1), (0,0,0)$ with $g = \mathrm{open}$ versus $g = \mathrm{closed}$ diverge under raw actuation without gate-opening repair. $\square$

## Full-policy growth ceiling, subcritical finite births, and rival mechanisms (Theorem 1 and Section 6.2)

*Proof of Theorem 1.*
**(1)** *Upper envelope and tightness across $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$.* Define $v(H) := \max\{k_c, k_b\}$ and $v(D) := k_c$ ($0 < k_c \le v(x) \le v_{\max}$). Any executed step with mean output $k \in \{k_c, k_b\}$ satisfies $(L_a v)(x) = k\bigl[(1-q)v(H) + q v(D)\bigr]$. In *Case 1* ($\gamma \le \delta$, $k_b \le k_c$), $\rho_* = \max\{1, k_c\}$, $v \equiv k_c$, and $(L_a v)(x) \le \rho_* v(x)$ across all $(x,a)$. In *Case 2* ($\gamma > \delta$, $k_b > k_c$), $\lambda_1 = (1-q)k_b + q k_c > \lambda_0$, $\rho_* = \max\{1, \lambda_1\}$, $v(H) = k_b > k_c = v(D)$, and again $(L_{\mathrm{bloom}}v)(H) = k_b\lambda_1 = \lambda_1 v(H) \le \rho_* v(H)$, $(L_{\mathrm{care}}v)(H) = k_c\lambda_1 < \lambda_1 v(H)$, $(L_{\mathrm{care}}v)(D) = k_c\lambda_1 = \lambda_1 v(D)$, and rejected $(D,\mathrm{bloom})$ yields $v(D) \le \rho_* v(D)$. Summing $V(P_t) := \sum_{u\in P_t} v(x_u)$ under any $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ yields $\mathbb{E}_\pi[V(P_{t+1}) \mid \mathcal{F}_t] \le \rho_* V(P_t)$ and $\mathbb{E}_\pi[N_t] \le \frac{v_{\max}|P_0|}{k_c}\rho_*^t$. Tightness holds via $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}} \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ (`care` on $H$, `bloom` on $D$) when $\rho_* = 1$ ($\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}}[N_t] \ge \min\{1, sq\}$), stationary $\theta_0$ when $\rho_* = \lambda_0$, and stationary $\theta_1$ when $\rho_* = \lambda_1$ ($\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1}[V(P_t)] = V(P_0)\lambda_1^t$).

**(2)** *Autonomous hereditary attainment versus switching policies.* Under autonomous multi-generational execution of $\theta_0$ from $\theta_0 D$, $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_0 D}[N_t] = k_c^t = \Theta(\rho_*^t) \iff \gamma \le \delta \land k_c \ge 1$. Under autonomous execution of $\theta_1$ from $\theta_1 D$, $\mathbb{E}_{\theta_1 D}[N_t] = k_c \lambda_1^{t-1} = \Theta(\rho_*^t) \iff \gamma \ge \delta \land \lambda_1 \ge 1$. By contrast, at $(s,\delta,b,\gamma,q) = (1/2, 1/4, 1/4, 1/8, 1/3)$ ($k_c = 3/4 < 1$, $k_b = 5/8 < 1$, $\lambda_1 = 2/3 < 1$, $\rho_* = 1$), a switching controller $\pi_{\mathrm{sw}} \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ that runs `care` at $t=1$ ($R_1 = 3$, reopening $g$) and $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$ for $t \ge 2$ retains a damaged parent at $t=2$ w.p. $sq = 1/6$ (and leaves at least one $D$-token w.p. $1 - \frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{11}{12} = \frac{17}{72}$), achieving $\frac{1}{6} \le \mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\mathrm{sw}}}[N_t] \le C$. The critical $(2/3,1/8,1/4,1/4,1/3)$ and supercritical $(3/4,1/8,3/8,1/4,1/3)$ rational identities follow identically as in the main text. $\square$

*Corollary (Subcritical finite cumulative expected births across $\Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$).* Suppose $\bar{\lambda} := \max\{\lambda_0, \lambda_1\} < 1$. In both Cases 1 and 2 above, every actively executed `care` or healthy `bloom` step on token $u \in A_t \subseteq P_{t-1}$ satisfies $(L_{a_u} v)(x_u) \le \bar{\lambda} v(x_u)$, dropping conditional expected potential by at least $(1 - \bar{\lambda})v(x_u) \ge (1 - \bar{\lambda})k_c > 0$, whereas frozen $(D, \mathrm{bloom})$ refusal yields $0$ potential drop and $0$ births (and `stop` yields $0 \le v(x_u)$ and $0$ births). Writing $M_t := |A_t|$, we have $\mathbb{E}_\pi[V(P_t) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \le V(P_{t-1}) - (1 - \bar{\lambda})k_c M_t$ and $\mathbb{E}_\pi[B_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}] \le (b + \gamma)M_t$. Telescoping over $t = 1, \dots, T$ and applying monotone convergence gives $\mathbb{E}_\pi[\sum_{t=1}^\infty B_t] \le \frac{(b+\gamma)V(P_0)}{(1-\bar{\lambda})k_c} < \infty$ for every $\pi \in \Pi_{\mathrm{pub}}$ (e.g., at $(1/2, 1/4, 1/4, 1/8, 1/3)$ from $\theta_0 D$, $\pi_{\mathrm{dorm}}$ yields $0$ births while autonomous $\theta_0 D$ yields $\sum_{t\ge 1} (3/4)^{t-1}(1/4) = 1 < \infty$). $\square$

*Explicit interventional transition rules for $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ versus $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ (Section 6.2).* Initialise a single damaged conservative founder $(\theta_0, D)$ ($g = \mathrm{closed}$, six-unit per-step budget):

- Under $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ (bootstrap majority-vote): on $(D, \mathrm{care})$, if $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$, voting restores intact $(r_H, r_D)$ and $g \leftarrow \mathrm{open}$ at repair cost $R_t = 3$, spends $1 + 2 = 3$ on `care`, and transitions into $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_t) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(s) \otimes \mathrm{Bern}(b)$ of $\theta_0$-tokens independently damaged w.p. $q$; under $\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 0)$, voting fails, $g$ remains $\mathrm{closed}$, $R_t = 0$, $B_t = 0$, and the token remains absorbed in unrecovered $D$ ($B_t = 0$ for all $t \ge 1$).
- Under $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$ (bootstrap-independent auxiliary latch): an independent hardwired auxiliary `care`-latch that does not read $b_{\mathrm{boot}}$ handles $(D, \mathrm{care})$ (re-engaging whenever $D$ recurs), paying $R_t = 3$ ($+1$ actuation, $+2$ escrow), restoring $(r_H, r_D)$ and $g \leftarrow \mathrm{open}$ at step end, and entering $(I_{\mathrm{ret}}, B_t) \sim \mathrm{Bern}(s) \otimes \mathrm{Bern}(b)$ of $\theta_0$-tokens damaged w.p. $q$ identically under both $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$ and $\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 0)$ [@rubenstein2017]. Thus $b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 1$ couples $((B_t, R_t))_{t\ge 1}$ identically across $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ and $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$, whereas $\mathrm{do}(b_{\mathrm{boot}} = 0)$ yields $(R_1 = 0, \mathbb{E}[B_1] = 0)$ under $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{vote}}$ versus $(R_1 = 3, \mathbb{E}[B_1] = b > 0)$ under $\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{bypass}}$.
