## 1. Research judgment

The proposed thesis — relationally individuated organizations persist through causal and historical continuity, while purposiveness requires independently grounded evaluative mechanisms — is defensible as a position, but **its general formulation is not a strong novelty claim**. Its principal ingredients and several of their connections are already developed in computational mechanics, process philosophy, organizational accounts of function, enactive agency, and interventionist explanation. There is no basis for claiming that UEOT first made objects relational, connected persistence to organized activity, naturalized purpose, or distinguished existence from normative obligation.

The manuscript has a plausible, narrower research contribution: demonstrate, through exact countermodels and carefully delimited positive cases, which inferences between predictive identity, physical organization, historical persistence, feasible control, and purposive explanation require additional premises. A useful philosophical result would explain **why one representation can be adequate for a predictive question and inadequate for a question about the persistence or purpose of that very system**. That result should constrain actual explanations, not merely sort terminology.

The formal developments may give this argument an unusually explicit and auditable implementation. Machine checking is evidence for the stated formal distinctions, not evidence of historical priority or a proof of the philosophical interpretation. The paper must therefore offer a substantive worked case in which the distinctions change the verdict.

## 3. Novelty claims to remove, retain, or condition

**Remove:** first relational ontology; first process account of persistence; first theory to combine self-maintenance and purpose; first formal theory of predictive identity; proof that all systems optimize the same objective; proof that mathematical unity is ontological unity; proof that ordinary objects are literally equivalence classes; a claim that existing organizational theories have ignored regulation or historical identity.

**Retain as documented internal development:** the revision of UEOT's own stronger original claims; the exact formal counterexamples and positive models that actually exist in the frozen repository; the use of those results to discipline a philosophical account. None of this alone proves external originality.

**Conditionally retain as the paper's proposed contribution:** a unified criterion for when evidence can be transferred between claims about prediction, realization, persistence, and purpose. Its value depends on showing that separate acceptable models can fail to refer to the same organization, the same admissible actions, or the same temporal lineage. The paper should give both a failed transfer and a successful one, with the additional premise plainly visible.

**Do not claim priority without further evidence:** an exhaustive novelty determination across all philosophy of science and philosophy of biology. This audit is targeted, not a comprehensive literature census.

## 4. Recommended argumentative concentration

The opening should use one organized system that undergoes replacement and damage. Two candidate descriptions predict its ordinary observable behavior equally well. Ask whether that licenses the same verdict about which organization survived and what its repair activity is for. Then exhibit the missing information rather than merely saying it is missing.

The first contribution should be epistemic and ontological in distinct steps: predictive adequacy gives a justified representation for a task; causal and historical evidence gives reasons to interpret some of its structure as an enduring worldly organization. This is a defeasible realist argument, not a deductive consequence of prediction.

The second should distinguish a causal account of an evaluative mechanism from an observer's arbitrary score. The mechanism's identification must have evidence other than the success of the fitted behavior. A regulatory set point, maintained constraint, selection history, or independently measured failure region can provide such evidence, but each supports a different kind of purposive claim. A scalar objective is optional, and uniqueness is not required.

The integration should then show why the two contributions constrain one another. A utility optimization over unavailable actions does not explain the system. A self-maintenance explanation that silently switches to a descendant lineage does not explain persistence of the original individual. A predicted output class that loses the destroyed repair mechanism cannot carry an unchanged claim about its autonomous recovery. These are specific explanatory errors the proposed framework can expose.

## 5. What the paper can and cannot conclude

A credible conclusion is that the revised UEOT supplies a disciplined form of relational and organizational realism, coupled to a conditional naturalization of purposive explanation. It can support a research article about explanatory entitlement and representational adequacy. It has not established a universally best ontology, one natural purpose for every object, or a complete theory of biological normativity.

The remaining intellectual risk is substantive: readers may judge the architecture to be a useful synthesis rather than a novel philosophical position. The response should be an illuminating countermodel and argument, not more terminology. Formal proofs sharpen the constraints; they do not settle that publication judgment.
