# Introduction: bridging structural safety, statistical calibration, and kernel trust

Designing and certifying autonomous controllers for partially observed stochastic systems requires reconciling three traditions whose foundational assumptions rarely align. In formal methods, supervisory control [@ramadgeWonham1987; @tabuada2009; @cassandrasLafortune2021], probabilistic model checking [@baierKatoen2008; @kwiatkowska2011; @dehnert2017], and shield synthesis for reinforcement learning [@chatterjee2016; @alshiekh2018; @junges2021] construct finite-memory strategies that enforce safety or almost-sure reachability invariants across unbounded horizons. Crucially, qualitative support-based synthesis assumes an *exact* relational specification of which state transitions carry strictly positive probability. In statistical machine learning and robust control, by contrast, transition kernels are unknown and must be estimated from finite sample trajectories [@kearnsSingh2002; @strehl2009; @nilimElGhaoui2005; @wiesemann2013], while practitioners routinely use the same training dataset both to select a coarse observation abstraction [@ravindranBarto2004; @ferns2004] and to bound downstream deployment failure—creating a classic post-selection inference hazard [@berk2013; @mayo2018]. Meanwhile, in interactive theorem proving [@nipkowPaulsonWenzel2002; @bertotCasteran2004; @mouraUllrich2021], recent formalizations have mechanized foundational measure-theoretic Markov processes and tabular dynamic programming [@hoelzl2017; @vajjha2021; @schaeffelerAbdulaziz2023], yet concrete rational horizon evaluations for partially observed belief trees often suffer severe kernel reduction blowup from repeated greatest-common-divisor normalization, tempting developments to trust external compilers via native evaluation axioms [@boespflug2011].

When these three layers are left disconnected, subtle soundness bugs slip between their interfaces. Consider four concrete failure modes that arise in naive combinations of synthesis, abstraction, and statistical testing:

1. *Reference-posterior circularity and dead-end prefixes.* In partially observed control, the posterior distribution over hidden states at a coarse action–observation history depends on the prior actions chosen along that history [@astrom1965; @smallwoodSondik1973; @kaelbling1998]. Proving that a synthesized policy visits only certified state–action pairs relative to a *pre-fixed* reference posterior fails if deploying the new controller shifts positive probability onto previously unvisited observation branches. Conversely, verifying that an action is locally certified at the present step does not imply that the resulting support admits any safe continuation at subsequent steps.
2. *The illusion of black-box zero-risk certification.* Because support-based shields require exact transition supports $R(x,a,z) \iff P(x,a)(z) > 0$, it is tempting to certify $P(x,a)(x^*) = 0$ empirically after observing zero visits to a hazardous state $x^*$ across $N$ independent trials. Yet without an independently justified minimum positive transition margin, an adversarial or unmodeled contamination rate $\varepsilon \in (0, 1/N)$ produces an all-safe training record with high probability $(1-\varepsilon)^N$ while breaking infinite-horizon almost-sure safety.
3. *Post-selection optimism and candidate-count inflation.* When an algorithm scans a family of candidate coarse observation maps $q_1, \dots, q_m$ on empirical transition data and deploys the first abstraction passing a defect threshold, conditioning naively on acceptance invalidates finite-sample concentration, whereas taking a union bound over all $m$ candidate abstractions inflates sample complexity even though all abstractions project the *same* underlying state–action transition rows. Moreover, even on standard Borel spaces where exact predictive compression succeeds [@crutchfieldYoung1989; @shaliziCrutchfield2001; @barnettCrutchfield2015], two latent beliefs that agree on every future observation test can assign opposite values to hidden-state safety predicates.
4. *Resource-gated fault ambiguity and compiler-trusted reflection.* In cyber-physical and fault-tolerant register architectures, probing whether faults act locally on a single dispatched unit or globally across backup units consumes prepaid operational budget. Under strict per-step prepayment, insufficient budget renders distinct fault mechanisms observationally indistinguishable; at intermediate horizons, deterministic controllers hit discrete payoff-lattice barriers that pre-committed randomized controllers strictly beat [@vonNeumann1928; @wald1950; @savage1951]. Certifying exact rational minimax regret across full observation trees via computational reflection [@boutin1997; @gregoireMahboubi2005] forfeits pure-kernel assurance if rational arithmetic triggers compiler-trusted evaluation.

This article presents a unified mathematical theory and complete pure-kernel Lean 4 formalization [@mouraUllrich2021; @mathlib2020] that closes each of these gaps with necessary-and-sufficient characterizations, executable algorithms, sharp impossibility theorems, and machine-checked counterexamples. Every theorem, witness, synthesizer, statistical risk oracle, and minimax regret bound reported in this paper has been formally verified in Lean 4 (version 4.33.1) over Mathlib, and audited across the entire compiled environment to depend *exclusively* on Lean's three standard foundational axioms—propositional extensionality (`propext`), classical choice (`Classical.choice`), and quotient soundness (`Quot.sound`) [@carneiro2019]—with zero incomplete proofs (`sorryAx`) and zero compiler-trust evaluation axioms (`Lean.ofReduceBool` / `native_decide`). At the same time, to ensure that the article can be read and refereed independently by control theorists, statisticians, and formal-methods researchers without inspecting Lean source files, all mathematical definitions, theorem statements, and complete proofs are presented self-containedly in Sections 2–6 and the Mathematical Appendix.

Our main contributions form a tightly integrated chain across four levels:

- **Exact trace-support viability, König quantifier exchange, and executable synthesis (Sections 2–3).** For finite state spaces $X$, finite action sets $A$, and *arbitrary* (possibly uncountable) observation spaces $Y$, Theorem 1 proves that on every coarse history of positive probability under an arbitrary causal policy $\pi$, the exact posterior support coincides with a policy-independent combinatorial trace-support recursion $S_n(g)$, yielding a non-circular equivalence between backward support viability $V(0, T, S_0(y))$ and existence of a causal policy achieving zero uncertified state–action occupancy $\nu_n^\pi(C_n^c) = 0$ across $[0,T)$. Theorem 2 establishes a König-type compactness equivalence [@konig1927] for time-inhomogeneous systems: arbitrarily long finite-horizon zero-uncovered policies exist if and only if a *single* causal policy $\pi_\infty$ certifies all times $n \in \mathbb{N}$ simultaneously, paired with an explicit infinite-action counterexample. Theorem 3 proves stabilization of the greatest winning-support fixed point $\mathcal{W}_*$ [@tarski1955; @aubinCellina1984; @aubin1991] in at most $2^{|X|} - 1$ iterations, connects closed-loop substochastic killed matrix powers $\mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{K}}^\top Q_K^T \widetilde{\mu}_0$ [@darrochSeneta1965; @seneta1981; @collet2013] to the Kolmogorov infinite-trajectory safety probability $\mathbb{P}(\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\, X_n \in K)$ [@kallenberg2021], and establishes soundness and completeness of a purely computable synthesizer `synthesize` returning deterministic finite-memory programs `Option (Program X Y A)`.
- **Fixed-sample impossibility and candidate-free calibrated post-selection risk (Section 4).** Theorem 4 proves that for any fixed sample size $N \ge 1$, significance level $\alpha \ge 0$, and *randomized* certification kernel $\psi : \{0,1\}^N \to \Delta(\{\mathrm{true}, \mathrm{false}\})$, uniform $\alpha$-validity over positive hazard rates $p \in (0,1)$ forces $\mathbb{P}_0(\psi = \mathrm{true}) \le \alpha$ at $p = 0$ [@leCam1973; @tsybakov2009]. Separating structural support priors from quantitative transition uncertainty, Theorem 5 constructs the exact joint training–selected-deployment law $J(\omega, h) = \mu(\omega) K_{s(\omega)}(h)$ and verifies a purely rational computable `NoisyFiniteRiskOracle`. Because a single simultaneous row-calibration event $G = \{\forall (x,a),\, d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{x,a}, \widehat{P}_{x,a}) \le u\}$ [@hoeffding1963; @mcdiarmid1989; @pinsker1964] controls empirical coarse-graining defects uniformly across *all* candidate abstractions $q_1, \dots, q_m$ via Markov pushforward contractivity [@dobrushin1956; @csiszar1975], selecting the first certified candidate incurs *zero candidate-count union-bound penalty* and satisfies the sharp unconditional joint bound $\mathbb{P}_J(\mathrm{accept} \land E) \le \min\{1, \alpha_N + (1-\alpha_N)\beta\}$.
- **Continuous observable belief quotients vs. prepaid fault-mechanism minimax regret (Sections 5–6).** On standard Borel state spaces $X$ [@giry1982; @bertsekasShreve1978; @hernandezLerma1996], Theorem 6 proves that the regular Bayesian posterior-belief transition kernel descends through an observable test coordinate $c(b) = b(q^{-1}\{1\}) \in [0,1]$ to a unique measurable Markov quotient kernel $\bar{K}$ [@burkeRosenblatt1958; @kemenySnell1960; @larsenSkou1991; @givanDeanGreig2003] if and only if the underlying kernel satisfies one-step source-fiber test closure (`ClosedTests`), while proving non-descent of latent rewards and safety sets [@jaeger2000; @littmanSuttonSingh2001]. When structural uncertainty instead concerns *how faults propagate* across redundant slots under a strict non-replenished prepayment wallet $B$ [@altman1999; @conantAshby1970; @pearl2009]—comparing local serial faults ($S$) against global correlated faults ($G$)—Theorem 7 establishes the exact three-step total-variation distinguishability $\Delta_{3,B} = r^2(1-r)\mathbf{1}_{B \ge c}$ [@blackwell1953; @coverThomas2006], derives a Bayes–minimax regret certificate [@bellman1957; @blackwell1965; @savage1951], and proves that at $(r, H, B, c, \lambda, d) = (1/2, 3, 2, 1, 1/4, 1)$ pre-run private randomization strictly lowers worst-case regret from the deterministic lattice minimum $1/32$ to the exact full-history minimax optimum $R^* = 3/128$.
- **Pure-kernel formalization architecture and GCD-free integer dynamic programming (Section 7).** We detail how executable synthesis and rational risk certification are cleanly decoupled from noncomputable measure-theoretic semantics, how unnormalized integer-weighted belief lists (`UBelief`) and dyadic scaling (`csolveInt`, `csourceInt`) eliminate rational fraction reduction so that exact multi-step POMDP oracle and Bayes values compile via pure-kernel `decide`, and how our environment-wide axiom audit compares against existing developments in Isabelle/HOL, Coq/Rocq, and Lean 4 [@affeldt2014; @eberl2015; @hoelzlNipkow2012; @hoelzl2017; @vajjha2021; @schaeffelerAbdulaziz2023].

# Trace-support viability and compact quantifier exchange under partial observation

## Microscopic and coarse path laws without reference-policy circularity

Let $X$ be a finite state space, $A$ be a finite nonempty action set, and $Y$ be an arbitrary observation space equipped with a deterministic observation map $q: X \to Y$. Crucially, we do *not* assume at the mathematical specification layer that $Y$ is finite or even countable: because $X$ is finite, at any step all observations realized with positive probability lie inside the finite image $q(X) \subseteq Y$. Time-inhomogeneous stochastic dynamics are specified by an initial state probability mass function (PMF) $p_0 \in \Delta(X)$ and a sequence of transition kernels $P_n: X \times A \to \Delta(X)$ for $n \in \mathbb{N}$ [@paz1971; @puterman1994]. At each step $n$, a task-specific safety or operational specification is represented by a binary state–action certification relation $C_n \subseteq X \times A$; a pair $(x,a) \in C_n$ is *certified* at step $n$, and $(x,a) \in C_n^c := (X \times A) \setminus C_n$ is *uncertified* (or *uncovered*).

A *coarse causal history* of length $n$ records the initial observation together with the sequence of applied actions and received observations:
$$
\mathcal{H}_0 := Y, \qquad \mathcal{H}_{n+1} := \mathcal{H}_n \times (A \times Y).
$$
Correspondingly, a *microscopic history* $h_n \in \mathcal{M}_n$ records the full state–action trajectory $\mathcal{M}_0 := X$ and $\mathcal{M}_{n+1} := \mathcal{M}_n \times (A \times X)$, projecting onto coarse histories via $\Pi_0(x_0) := q(x_0)$ and $\Pi_{n+1}(h_n, (a_n, x_{n+1})) := (\Pi_n(h_n), (a_n, q(x_{n+1})))$, with terminal state $\mathrm{last}_0(x_0) := x_0$ and $\mathrm{last}_{n+1}(h_n, (a_n, x_{n+1})) := x_{n+1}$. A *causal policy* is a sequence of history-dependent action kernels $\pi = (\pi_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ where $\pi_n: \mathcal{H}_n \to \Delta(A)$ may employ arbitrary real-valued randomization weights. Together with $(p_0, q, P)$, any causal policy $\pi$ induces a sequence of microscopic path PMFs $\mu_n^\pi \in \Delta(\mathcal{M}_n)$, coarse path PMFs $\bar{p}_n^\pi := (\Pi_n)_\# \mu_n^\pi \in \Delta(\mathcal{H}_n)$, and step-$n$ *deployment state–action occupancy laws* $\nu_n^\pi \in \Delta(X \times A)$ defined by
$$
\nu_n^\pi(x, a) := \sum_{\substack{h \in \mathcal{M}_n \\ \mathrm{last}_n(h) = x}} \mu_n^\pi(h)\, \pi_n(\Pi_n(h))(a).
$$
On any coarse history $g \in \mathcal{H}_n$ with $\bar{p}_n^\pi(g) > 0$, conditioning $\mu_n^\pi$ on the fiber $\Pi_n^{-1}(\{g\})$ and projecting onto $\mathrm{last}_n$ yields the exact terminal-state posterior $\rho_n^{\pi, g} \in \Delta(X)$.

While the numerical posterior $\rho_n^{\pi, g}$ depends in general on the quantitative transition probabilities $P_k$ and policy weights $\pi_k$, its *support* $\operatorname{supp}\rho_n^{\pi, g} := \{x \in X : \rho_n^{\pi, g}(x) > 0\}$ is governed by a purely combinatorial *trace-support recursion* that makes no reference to $\pi$. For any subset $S \subseteq X$, action $a \in A$, and observation $y \in Y$, define
$$
S_0(y) := \{x \in X : p_0(x) > 0 \land q(x) = y\},
$$
$$
\operatorname{Succ}_n(S, a, y) := \{x' \in X : q(x') = y \land \exists x \in S,\ P_n(x,a)(x') > 0\},
$$
and along any coarse history $g_{n+1} = (g_n, (a, y)) \in \mathcal{H}_{n+1}$ set $S_0(y_0)$ at $n=0$ and $S_{n+1}(g_n, (a, y)) := \operatorname{Succ}_n(S_n(g_n), a, y)$.

To characterize when a causal controller can avoid uncertified state–action pairs over $k$ remaining steps starting from step $n$ and possible state set $S \subseteq X$, define the *backward support-viability predicate* $V(n, k, S)$ recursively on $k \in \mathbb{N}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
V(n, 0, S) &:\iff \mathrm{True}, \\
V(n, k+1, S) &:\iff \exists a \in A,\ \bigl(\forall x \in S,\ C_n(x,a)\bigr) \\
&\qquad\quad \land \bigl(\forall y \in Y,\ \operatorname{Succ}_n(S,a,y) \neq \varnothing \implies V(n+1, k, \operatorname{Succ}_n(S,a,y))\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
Notice the strict quantifier discipline in $V(n, k+1, S)$: **one and the same action** $a \in A$ must simultaneously certify *every* state $x \in S$ indistinguishable under the current history, and **every** observation branch $y$ carrying non-empty successor support must remain viable for the remaining $k$ steps. Weakening $\exists a \forall x$ to $\forall x \exists a$ would illegally peek at the unobserved hidden state; weakening $\forall y$ to $\exists y$ would gamble on lucky observations [@madani2003; @chatterjee2016].

**Theorem 1 (Actual posterior-support bridge and finite-horizon viability equivalence).** *Let $X$ and $A$ be finite with $A$ nonempty, $Y$ arbitrary, $(p_0, q, P, C)$ fixed, $T \in \mathbb{N}$ a finite horizon, and $\pi_{\mathrm{base}}$ an arbitrary complete base policy supplying well-typed actions outside viable branches.*

1. **Exact posterior-support bridge.** *Under any causal policy $\pi$, an initial observation $y \in Y$ satisfies $\bar{p}_0^\pi(y) > 0$ iff $S_0(y) \neq \varnothing$; for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ and every $g \in \mathcal{H}_n$ with $\bar{p}_n^\pi(g) > 0$, $\operatorname{supp}\rho_n^{\pi, g} = S_n(g)$; and $\bar{p}_{n+1}^\pi(g, (a, y)) > 0$ iff $\bar{p}_n^\pi(g) > 0$, $\pi_n(g)(a) > 0$, and $\operatorname{Succ}_n(S_n(g), a, y) \neq \varnothing$.*
2. **Necessary and sufficient finite-horizon synthesis.** *The following two statements are equivalent:*
   $$
   \bigl(\forall y \in Y,\ S_0(y) \neq \varnothing \implies V(0, T, S_0(y))\bigr)
   \iff
   \bigl(\exists \pi,\ \forall n < T,\ \nu_n^\pi(C_n^c) = 0\bigr),
   $$
   *where $\nu_n^\pi$ is the actual deployment occupancy generated by $(p_0, q, P)$ and $\pi$ itself. In particular, whenever a randomized history-dependent policy achieves $\nu_n^\pi(C_n^c) = 0$ for all $n < T$, a deterministic history-dependent policy $\pi^V$ achieves the same guarantee.*

Theorem 1 (proved in Appendix A.1) eliminates two persistent pitfalls in partially observed safety analysis. First, because $S_n(g)$ depends only on the *recorded* actions inside $g$ and not on counterfactual randomization weights of $\pi$, replacing one microscopic prefix in $\Pi_n^{-1}(\{g\})$ by another preserves $\pi_n(g)(a) > 0$; this symmetry allows both sufficiency and necessity to be proved directly on the policy's own induced path measure without circularly fixing a reference posterior. Second, two minimal counterexamples—both formally verified alongside Theorem 1—demonstrate why neither naive fiber-based shielding nor myopic step certification can substitute for $V(n, k, S)$:

- **Trace support vs. current observation fiber ($4$-state witness).** Let $X = \{0,1\}^2$ write states as $(\text{phase } b, \text{side } s)$, with uniform initial support on $\{(0,0), (0,1)\}$, observations $q(0,s) = \mathrm{some}(s)$ and $q(1,0) = q(1,1) = \mathrm{none}$, deterministic transitions $(0,s) \xrightarrow{a} (1,s)$, and step-1 certification $C_1((1,s), a) \iff a = s$. At $n=1$, the pooled observation fiber $q^{-1}(\{\mathrm{none}\}) = \{(1,0), (1,1)\}$ admits *no* common certified action, so any memoryless shield operating on $q(X_1)$ fails. Yet the two positive-mass coarse histories $g_L = (\mathrm{some}(0), (0, \mathrm{none}))$ and $g_R = (\mathrm{some}(1), (0, \mathrm{none}))$ have singleton trace supports $S_1(g_L) = \{(1,0)\}$ and $S_1(g_R) = \{(1,1)\}$, achieving $\nu_1^\pi(C_1^c) = 0$.
- **Certified prefixes entering unavoidable dead ends ($T=2$ witness).** Take $X = A = \{0,1\}$, constant observation $q(x) \equiv \star$, uniform $p_0$, transition $X_1 = A_0$ followed by $X_2 = X_1$, and certification $C_n(x,a) \iff (n = 0 \lor x = 0)$. At $n = 0$, *both* actions $a = 0$ and $a = 1$ satisfy $C_0(x,a)$ across $S_0(\star) = \{0,1\}$. Yet choosing $a = 1$ transitions to $S_1(\star, (1, \star)) = \{1\}$, where $V(1, 1, \{1\})$ is false and $\nu_1(C_1^c) = 1$, whereas $a = 0$ reaches $\{0\}$ and maintains $\nu_n(C_n^c) = 0$ for $n \in \{0,1\}$. Checking one-step action admissibility without recursive lookahead is unsound even on viable plants.

## Compact quantifier exchange from arbitrary finite horizons to all-time policies

Suppose a time-inhomogeneous partially observed plant $(p_0, q, (P_n)_{n\ge 0}, (C_n)_{n\ge 0})$ admits, for *each* finite horizon $T \in \mathbb{N}$ separately, a policy $\pi^{(T)}$ satisfying $\nu_n^{\pi^{(T)}}(C_n^c) = 0$ for all $n < T$. Because $\pi^{(T)}$ may vary incompatibly with $T$, does there necessarily exist a *single* causal policy $\pi_\infty$ that certifies all steps $n \in \mathbb{N}$ simultaneously?

When the action set $A$ is allowed to be countably infinite (for which Theorem 1's finite-horizon trace-support identity still holds over finitely supported policies), the answer is *no*: consider $X = \{0, 1\}$ ($1$ unsafe), $A = \mathbb{N}$, trivial observation, and time-dependent dynamics where choosing action $a \in \mathbb{N}$ at $n = 0$ keeps the system in state $0$ up to step $n = a$ and transitions to the uncertified state $1$ at step $n = a + 1$. For every finite horizon $T$, choosing $a = T$ yields a policy certified on $[0, T)$, yet every single choice of initial action $a_0 \in \mathbb{N}$ fails at step $a_0 + 1$. When $A$ is *finite*, however, discrete König-type compactness [@konig1927] closes the quantifier gap completely—even when $(P_n, C_n)$ are non-stationary and $Y$ is infinite:

**Theorem 2 (All-time support viability and finite-action quantifier exchange).** *Let $X$ and $A$ be finite, with $A$ nonempty, $Y$ arbitrary, and $(p_0, q, (P_n)_{n\ge 0}, (C_n)_{n\ge 0})$ fixed with fallback policy $\pi_{\mathrm{base}}$. Define all-time support viability by $V_\infty(n, S) :\iff \forall k \in \mathbb{N},\ V(n, k, S)$. The following three conditions are equivalent:*

1. *Every nonempty initial observation support satisfies $V_\infty(0, S_0(y))$.*
2. *For every finite horizon $T \in \mathbb{N}$, there exists a causal policy $\pi_T$ such that $\forall n < T,\ \nu_n^{\pi_T}(C_n^c) = 0$.*
3. *There exists a single deterministic causal policy $\pi_\infty$ such that $\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\ \nu_n^{\pi_\infty}(C_n^c) = 0$.*

The key step in proving $(1) \implies (3)$ (detailed in Appendix A.1) observes that for any $(n, S)$ satisfying $V_\infty(n, S)$, the sets $A_k := \{a \in A : (\forall x \in S,\, C_n(x,a)) \land (\forall y,\, \operatorname{Succ}_n(S,a,y) \neq \varnothing \implies V(n+1, k, \operatorname{Succ}_n(S,a,y)))\}$ are nonempty by $V(n, k+1, S)$ and nested downward ($A_{k+1} \subseteq A_k$) because $V(n+1, k+1, S') \implies V(n+1, k, S')$. If $\bigcap_{k \in \mathbb{N}} A_k$ were empty, each $a \in A$ would fail at some horizon $k(a)$, and since $A$ is finite and nonempty, $K := \max_{a \in A} k(a)$ would satisfy $A_K = \varnothing$, contradicting $A_K \neq \varnothing$; hence $\bigcap_{k \in \mathbb{N}} A_k \neq \varnothing$. Selecting one action in this intersection at each $V_\infty$-viable history preserves $V_\infty$ along every positive-mass trajectory of $\pi_\infty$ itself.

# Greatest support fixed points, infinite-trajectory survival, and executable synthesis

## Greatest fixed points on the observation-fiber arena and Kolmogorov trajectory safety

While Theorem 2 constructs an all-time causal policy $\pi_\infty$ for general time-inhomogeneous systems, $\pi_\infty$ indexes actions by growing histories $g_n \in \mathcal{H}_n$ and invokes classical choice over infinite horizon intersections. For *time-homogeneous* support structures—where a decidable ternary relation $R \subseteq X \times A \times X$ represents the transition support $R(x,a,z) \iff P_n(x,a)(z) > 0$ and a decidable binary relation $C \subseteq X \times A$ represents step certification—both unbounded history growth and nonconstructive choice can be eliminated simultaneously [@papadimitriouTsitsiklis1987; @chatterjee2016; @junges2021].

Define the *observation-fiber support arena* $\mathcal{A}_q \subseteq 2^X \setminus \{\varnothing\}$ as the family of nonempty subsets $S \subseteq X$ lying inside a single observation fiber ($\exists y \in Y,\ S \subseteq q^{-1}(\{y\})$). Because the fibers $\{q^{-1}(\{y\})\}_{y \in q(X)}$ partition $X$ into disjoint finite sets of sizes $m_1 + \dots + m_r = |X|$, the cardinality of $\mathcal{A}_q$ satisfies the sharp bound
$$
|\mathcal{A}_q| = \sum_{i=1}^r (2^{m_i} - 1) \le 2^{|X|} - 1,
$$
with equality only when $q$ is constant on $X$. On subsets $\mathcal{W} \subseteq \mathcal{A}_q$, define the monotone one-step winning-support preimage operator $\Phi: 2^{\mathcal{A}_q} \to 2^{\mathcal{A}_q}$ by
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(\mathcal{W}) := \Bigl\{S \in \mathcal{A}_q : \exists a \in A,\ &\bigl(\forall x \in S,\ C(x,a)\bigr) \\
&\land \bigl(\forall y \in Y,\ \operatorname{Succ}_R(S,a,y) \neq \varnothing \implies \operatorname{Succ}_R(S,a,y) \in \mathcal{W}\bigr)\Bigr\},
\end{aligned}
$$
where $\operatorname{Succ}_R(S,a,y) := \{z \in X : q(z) = y \land \exists x \in S,\ R(x,a,z)\}$. By Tarski's lattice fixed-point theorem on the finite powerset $2^{\mathcal{A}_q}$ [@tarski1955; @baierKatoen2008], top-down Kleene iteration $\mathcal{W}_0 := \mathcal{A}_q$, $\mathcal{W}_{k+1} := \Phi(\mathcal{W}_k)$ shrinks strictly at most $|\mathcal{A}_q|$ times and stabilizes by step $B_X := 2^{|X|} - 1 \ge |\mathcal{A}_q|$ at the greatest fixed point $\mathcal{W}_* := \operatorname{gfp}(\Phi) = \mathcal{W}_{B_X}$, the discrete partially observed counterpart of Aubin's viability kernel [@aubinCellina1984; @aubin1991].

To connect step-by-step zero uncertified occupancy to *physical state-space safety* and *quasi-stationary survival operators* [@doob1957; @darrochSeneta1965; @collet2013], let $K \subseteq X$ be a designated safe state region. When a time-homogeneous source $P: X \times A \to \Delta(X)$ is placed in closed loop with a deterministic finite-memory controller $\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mem}} = (M, m_0, \alpha, \delta)$ having finite memory set $M \subseteq 2^X$, initial memory map $m_0: Y \to M$, action map $\alpha: M \to A$, and deterministic memory update $\delta: M \times A \times Y \to M$, the augmented pair $(X_n, M_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ forms a time-homogeneous Markov chain on $X \times M$. By the Ionescu–Tulcea extension theorem [@kallenberg2021], $(X_n, M_n)$ induces a unique Kolmogorov probability measure $\mathbb{P}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{mem}}}$ on the infinite trajectory space $(X \times M)^{\mathbb{N}}$. Restricting the augmented transition matrix (written in column-stochastic operator form acting on state–memory distributions) to the safe cylinder $\widetilde{K} := K \times M$ defines the substochastic *killed operator* $Q_K$ [@seneta1981; @hoelzl2017]. Notice also that when conditioning finite-window path laws on positive survival mass $\mathbb{P}(\forall n \le T,\, X_n \in K) > 0$, supplying an explicit total fallback kernel on zero-survival branches guarantees that all positive-mass conditioned prefix laws are completely independent of the fallback convention.

## Computable controller synthesis and end-to-end semantic soundness

In Lean 4, probability mass functions (`PMF`) over real/extended-nonnegative-real weights (`ENNReal`, i.e., $[0,\infty]$) live in the `noncomputable` mathematical specification fragment because real arithmetic is classical. A genuine verified controller synthesizer must therefore construct an *executable data structure* whose runtime initialization, action selection, and memory update execute purely over decidable finite types without invoking probability oracles or `Classical.choice` [@mouraUllrich2021; @schaeffelerAbdulaziz2023].

Fix finite types $X, A$ with decidable equality on $X, Y, A$, decidable relations $R(x,a,z)$ and $C(x,a)$, an explicit initial support table $I_0 \subseteq X$ (a `Finset X`), an ordered action enumeration $L_A = [a_1, \dots, a_k]$ covering $A$, and an explicit fallback action $a_{\mathrm{fb}} \in A$. Define an executable finite-state controller record `Program X Y A` consisting of three computable functions:

1. `initialMemory(y)` $:= \{x \in I_0 : q(x) = y\}$;
2. `update(S, a, y)` (`nextMemory q R`) $:= \{z \in X : q(z) = y \land \exists x \in S,\ R(x,a,z)\}$;
3. `action(S)`: scanning $a \in L_A$ in list order, return the first action passing `stepCheck(S, a)`—which verifies $S \neq \varnothing$, $\forall x \in S,\, C(x,a)$, and that for every $z \in X$ with $S' := \mathrm{update}(S, a, q(z)) \neq \varnothing$, the bounded recursive predicate `finiteRelationViabilityCheck` holds on $(B_X, S')$ at depth $B_X = 2^{|X|} - 1$; if $S = \varnothing$ or no action passes, return $a_{\mathrm{fb}}$.

The top-level computable procedure `synthesize`$(q, R, C, I_0, L_A, a_{\mathrm{fb}}) : \mathrm{Option}(\mathrm{Program}\ X\ Y\ A)$ checks three decidable guards—(i) $I_0 \neq \varnothing$, (ii) every $a \in A$ appears in $L_A$, and (iii) the initial check `finiteMemoryCheck` holds, verifying `finiteRelationViabilityCheck` on $(B_X, \mathrm{initialMemory}(q(x)))$ for every $x \in I_0$—returning `some prog` if all three hold and `none` otherwise.

**Theorem 3 (Greatest support fixed point, infinite-trajectory survival, and sound-and-complete executable synthesis).** *Let $X$ and $A$ be finite with decidable equality on $X, Y, A$, let $R$ and $C$ be decidable relations, let $L_A$ cover $A$ with fallback $a_{\mathrm{fb}} \in A$, and let $(p_0, (P_n)_{n\ge 0})$ be any stochastic plant satisfying exact support agreement $I_0 = \operatorname{supp} p_0$ and $\operatorname{supp} P_n(x,a) = \{z \in X : R(x,a,z)\}$ together with $C_n(x,a) \iff C(x,a)$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$ (note that the numerical transition probabilities $P_n(x,a)(z) > 0$ may vary arbitrarily with $n$ so long as their support equals $R$).*

1. **Greatest fixed point and memory separation.** *$\mathcal{W}_* = \mathcal{W}_{2^{|X|}-1}$ is the greatest post-fixed point of $\Phi$ on $\mathcal{A}_q$, and $S \in \mathcal{W}_*$ iff $V_\infty(0, S)$ holds. Moreover, there exist three-state partially observed plants in which every stationary memoryless observation-feedback policy $\pi_0: Y \to \Delta(A)$ incurs positive uncertified mass $\nu_0^{\pi_0}(C^c) + \nu_1^{\pi_0}(C^c) \ge 1 > 0$ (and hits an absorbing failure state within two steps with probability at least $3/4$), whereas a two-state finite-memory controller achieves $\nu_n(C^c) = 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.*
2. **Soundness and completeness of `synthesize`.** *`synthesize`$(q, R, C, I_0, L_A, a_{\mathrm{fb}})$ returns `some prog` if and only if there exists a causal policy $\pi$ satisfying $\nu_n^\pi(C_n^c) = 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. Whenever `synthesize` returns `some prog`, the deterministic policy induced by `prog` satisfies $\mathrm{memory}_n(\mathrm{prog}, g) = S_n(g)$ for every history $g \in \mathcal{H}_n$ (including zero-mass histories) and achieves $\nu_n^{\mathrm{prog}}(C_n^c) = 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.*
3. **Kolmogorov infinite-trajectory safety and killed-operator identity.** *Suppose in addition that $I_0 \subseteq K$ for a safe set $K \subseteq X$ and certified transitions preserve $K$ ($\forall x \in K,\, \forall a,\, C(x,a) \implies \forall z,\, R(x,a,z) \implies z \in K$). For any time-homogeneous Markov kernel $P$ with support $R$, the closed-loop augmented chain $(X_n, M_n)$ satisfies $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{prog}}(\forall n \le T,\, X_n \in K) = \mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{K}}^\top Q_K^T \widetilde{\mu}_0 = 1$ for every $T \in \mathbb{N}$, and in the monotone limit $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{prog}}(\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\, X_n \in K) = \lim_{T\to\infty} \mathbf{1}_{\widetilde{K}}^\top Q_K^T \widetilde{\mu}_0 = 1$.*

Theorem 3 (proved in Appendix A.2) cleanly separates *algorithmic execution* from *probabilistic verification*. Consider the concrete three-state hidden-phase cemetery plant $X = \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}, \bot\}$ (`Option Bool`, writing `false` and `true` for the safe states `some false`, `some true` in $K = \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$ and $\bot = \mathrm{none}$ for the absorbing cemetery), binary actions $A = \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$, completely uninformative constant observation $q(x) \equiv ()$, transition $f(\bot, a) = \bot$ and $f(b, a) = \neg b$ if $a = b$ else $\bot$ for $b \in \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$, and certification $C(x,a) \iff x = \mathrm{some}(a)$ (so $C(\bot, a)$ is false for all $a$). Executed inside the Lean kernel via `decide`, `synthesize` on $I_0 = \{\mathrm{false}\}$ with $L_A = [\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}]$ returns `some prog` whose internal memory alternates $\{\mathrm{false}\} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{false}} \{\mathrm{true}\} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{true}} \{\mathrm{false}\}$, guaranteeing $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{prog}}(\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\, X_n \in K) = 1$ and unit killed mass at every horizon. Conversely, on the mixed initial support $I_{\mathrm{mix}} = \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$, `synthesize` returns `none`—and by completeness (Theorem 3.2), this single finite computation *constructively refutes* the existence of *any* randomized full-history policy achieving all-time certification from the uniform initial law $\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\mathrm{false}} + \frac{1}{2}\delta_{\mathrm{true}}$.

# Fixed-sample impossibility and calibrated post-selection risk

## Impossibility of margin-free fixed-sample zero-support certification

Theorem 3 requires exact agreement $R(x,a,z) \iff P(x,a)(z) > 0$ between the Boolean support table supplied to `synthesize` and the true plant. Suppose an engineer does not possess a structural physics or hardware proof that an uncertified transition $z^*$ has exact probability zero, and attempts instead to *certify* $p := P(x,a)(z^*) = 0$ statistically from a fixed number $N \ge 1$ of independent transitions drawn from $(x,a)$ [@leCam1973; @tsybakov2009; @mayo2018]. Let $W = (W_1, \dots, W_N) \in \{0,1\}^N$ denote the binary indicator record of visits to $z^*$ under $W_k \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$. To encompass all possible randomized and deterministic statistical procedures, represent a certification rule by a Markov kernel $\psi: \{0,1\}^N \to \Delta(\{\mathrm{true}, \mathrm{false}\})$, where $\psi(w)(\mathrm{true}) \in [0,1]$ is the probability of issuing an exact-zero support certificate upon observing record $w$. Write $\Gamma_\psi(p) := \mathbb{E}_p[\psi(W)(\mathrm{true})]$ for the probability of certification under true hazard rate $p \in [0,1]$.

**Theorem 4 (Impossibility of uniform fixed-sample zero-support certification).** *Fix any finite sample count $N \ge 1$, any randomized certification rule $\psi: \{0,1\}^N \to \Delta(\{\mathrm{true}, \mathrm{false}\})$, and any false-certification tolerance $\alpha \ge 0$. Let $\rho := \psi(\mathbf{0}_N)(\mathrm{true})$ denote the rule's acceptance probability on the all-zero record $\mathbf{0}_N = (0, \dots, 0)$.*

1. *At $p = 0$, $\Gamma_\psi(0) = \rho$, and for every $p \in [0,1]$, $\Gamma_\psi(p) \ge \rho (1 - p)^N$.*
2. *Whenever $\Gamma_\psi(0) > \alpha$, the explicit strictly positive hazard rate $p_* := \frac{\rho - \alpha}{2 \rho N} \in (0, 1)$ satisfies $\Gamma_\psi(p_*) > \alpha$.*
3. *Consequently, if $\psi$ is uniformly $\alpha$-valid across all positive hazard rates—meaning $\Gamma_\psi(p) \le \alpha$ for all $p \in (0, 1)$—then $\Gamma_\psi(0) \le \alpha$: no fixed-sample rule has non-trivial uniform power above its size $\alpha$ to certify exact zero support.*

Theorem 4 (proved in Appendix A.3 via $(1-p)^N \ge 1 - Np$) establishes an irreducible epistemic boundary between *structural support guarantees* and *finite-sample statistical guarantees*. Observing zero catastrophic transitions in $N = 10^6$ black-box rollouts cannot by itself discharge the exact support premise $R$ of `synthesize` without an external lower margin $p \in \{0\} \cup [p_{\min}, 1]$ derived from domain physics or hardware isolation. When transition probabilities are learned from finite data without a zero-vs-margin gap, verification must shift from claiming $\mathbb{P}(\text{violation}) = 0$ to bounding *calibrated finite-horizon deployment risk*—including the statistical cost of data-dependent model and abstraction selection.

## Good-event post-selection risk and the computable noisy finite-risk oracle

Suppose we draw a training record $\omega \in \Omega$ from a registered sampling experiment $\mu \in \Delta(\Omega)$, apply a data-dependent selector $s: \Omega \to \mathcal{C}$ that inspects the entire training record $\omega$ to choose a candidate abstraction and controller $c = s(\omega)$ [@berk2013; @kearnsSingh2002; @strehl2009], and then deploy that selected controller on the *same* underlying unknown plant $P$, generating a finite-horizon microscopic trajectory $h \in \mathcal{M}_T$ drawn from the conditional deployment law $K_{s(\omega)} \in \Delta(\mathcal{M}_T)$. Together, training and subsequent deployment define the exact *joint training–selected-deployment measure*
$$
J(\omega, h) := \mu(\omega)\, K_{s(\omega)}(h), \qquad (\omega, h) \in \Omega \times \mathcal{M}_T.
$$
Fix a common microscopic failure event $E \subseteq \mathcal{M}_T$ (importantly, $E$ is anchored in the true microscopic trajectory space so that changing the macro-abstraction $q_j$ cannot redefine what counts as physical failure). How can we bound the unconditional joint failure probability $\mathbb{P}_J(E) := J(\Omega \times E)$ when both the chosen abstraction $q_{s(\omega)}$ and the empirical reference transition model depend on $\omega$?

Two structural insights yield a clean, candidate-count-free solution:

First, *risk budgets need only hold on a simultaneous row-calibration Good event $G \subseteq \Omega$*. Suppose $\mu(G^c) \le \alpha \in [0,1]$, and for every $\omega \in G$ the conditional deployment risk $r(\omega) := K_{s(\omega)}(E)$ satisfies $r(\omega) \le \beta \in [0,1]$. Splitting $\sum_{\omega \in \Omega} \mu(\omega) r(\omega)$ across $G$ and $G^c$ and bounding $r(\omega) \le 1$ on $G^c$ yields the exact mixture inequality
$$
\mathbb{P}_J(E) \le \mu(G^c) + (1 - \mu(G^c))\beta \le \min\{1,\, \alpha + (1 - \alpha)\beta\},
$$
requiring *zero* regularity or small-budget properties on out-of-calibration records $\omega \in G^c$. Indeed, in our formalized two-action product-sampling witness ($X = A = \{0,1\}$, $N = 256$ independent draws per action row with true failure rates $p_0(1) = 1/8$ and $p_1(1) = 1/4$, where the adaptive selector $A(\omega)$ deploys action $0$ w.p. $7/8$ and action $1$ w.p. $1/8$), the exact joint failure probability is $\mathbb{P}_J(E) = \frac{7}{8}\cdot\frac{1}{8} + \frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{1}{4} = \frac{9}{64}$; setting calibration radius $u = 1/8$ gives Hoeffding failure bound $\alpha = 16 e^{-8} \le 1/16$ and good-event budget $\beta = 1/2$, certifying $\mathbb{P}_J(E) \le \frac{1}{2} + 8 e^{-8} \le \frac{17}{32} < 1$. Yet the pathological all-ones record $\omega_{\mathrm{all}} = (1, \dots, 1) \in \{0,1\}^{512}$ occurs with strictly positive probability $(1/8)^{256}(1/4)^{256} > 0$ and yields clipped empirical budget $1 > \beta = 1/2$, proving that any theorem demanding a small empirical budget on *every* positive-mass training record is vacuously false under genuine i.i.d. sampling. Moreover, a two-branch selector with $\mu(G^c) = 1/4$, $r|_G = 1/2$, and $r|_{G^c} = 1$ attains $\mathbb{P}_J(E) = \frac{1}{4} + \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2} = \frac{5}{8} = \alpha + (1-\alpha)\beta$ with equality, establishing sharpness.

Second, *simultaneous microscopic row calibration eliminates candidate-count union bounds and double-charging*. Suppose we register a family of $m$ candidate observation abstractions $q_0, \dots, q_{m-1}: X \to Y$ (for finite $X, Y, A$) and collect $N \ge 1$ i.i.d. next-state samples $\omega_{x,a,1}, \dots, \omega_{x,a,N} \sim P(x,a)$ for each microscopic pair $(x,a) \in X \times A$ under the product experiment $\mu_N = \bigotimes_{(x,a)} P(x,a)^{\otimes N}$ (generalizing immediately to unequal row allocations $N_{x,a} \ge 1$). Let $\widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega)(z) := \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^N \mathbf{1}\{\omega_{x,a,k} = z\} \in \mathbb{Q}$ be the rational empirical distribution, and define the *single canonical calibration event*
$$
G_u := \Bigl\{\omega \in \Omega_N : \forall (x,a) \in X \times A,\ d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(P(x,a),\, \widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega)\bigr) \le u\Bigr\},
$$
whose complement satisfies Hoeffding's finite-subset concentration bound [@hoeffding1963]
$$
\mu_N(G_u^c) \le \alpha_N(u) := \min\Bigl\{1,\, |X|\,|A|\, 2^{|X|+1} \exp(-2 N u^2)\Bigr\}.
$$
Notice that $G_u$ depends *only* on the microscopic transition rows $(x,a) \in X \times A$—not on $m$ or on the candidates $\{q_j\}_{j=0}^{m-1}$. For any candidate $q: X \to Y$, define its computable rational *empirical lumpability defect* [@burkeRosenblatt1958; @ferns2004; @ravindranBarto2004]
$$
\widehat{\delta}_q(\omega) := \max_{\substack{x, x' \in X,\, a \in A \\ q(x) = q(x')}} d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(\widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega),\, \widehat{P}_{x',a}(\omega)\bigr) \in \mathbb{Q},
\qquad
\delta_q(P) := \max_{\substack{x, x' \in X,\, a \in A \\ q(x) = q(x')}} d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(P(x,a),\, P(x',a)\bigr),
$$
where $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(p, p') = 1 - \sum_{z \in X} \min\{p(z), p'(z)\}$ and $\delta_q(P)$ denotes the true microscopic defect. On $G_u$, the triangle inequality gives $|\widehat{\delta}_q(\omega) - \delta_q(P)| \le 2u$ simultaneously for *every* function $q: X \to Y$. Furthermore, choosing the minimum element $\mathrm{rep}_q(y) \in q^{-1}(\{y\})$ under a linear order on $X$ when $q^{-1}(\{y\}) \neq \varnothing$ (and a fixed fallback state $x_{\mathrm{fb}} \in X$ on empty fibers $y \notin q(X)$, which never receive positive mass) defines a total computable rational macro-reference kernel:
$$
\widehat{K}_q(\omega)(y, a) := q_\# \widehat{P}_{\mathrm{rep}_q(y), a}(\omega).
$$
Because pushforward by $q$ is a Markov contraction in total variation [@dobrushin1956; @pinsker1964], for every $x \in X$ and $a \in A$ on $G_u$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(q_\# P(x,a),\, \widehat{K}_q(\omega)(q(x), a)\bigr)
&\le d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(q_\# P(x,a),\, q_\# \widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega)\bigr)
   + d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(q_\# \widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega),\, q_\# \widehat{P}_{\mathrm{rep}_q(q(x)), a}(\omega)\bigr) \\
&\le u + \widehat{\delta}_q(\omega),
\end{aligned}
$$
charging *only one* sampling radius $u$ rather than $2u$.

Combining `synthesize` from Section 3 with this rational calibration chain yields the fully computable `NoisyFiniteRiskOracle`:

1. **Three-valued interval defect scan.** Given rational defect threshold $\tau \ge 0$ and radius $u \ge 0$, `noisyDecision`$(q_j, \omega, u, \tau)$ returns `certified` if $\widehat{\delta}_{q_j}(\omega) + 2u \le \tau$, `rejected` if $\widehat{\delta}_{q_j}(\omega) - 2u > \tau$, and `ambiguous` otherwise. Scanning $j = 0, \dots, m-1$ in order, `selectCandidate` stops at the first non-rejected index: if that index is `certified`, it outputs `some j`; if `ambiguous` or all $m$ candidates are `rejected`, it outputs `none` (halting deployment rather than cherry-picking past an ambiguous test).
2. **Rational reference-history risk and acceptance gate.** For the synthesized controller $\mathrm{prog}_j$ (`synthesize`$(q_j, R, C, I_0, L_A, a_{\mathrm{fb}}) = \mathrm{some\ prog}_j$) and macro failure indicator $\mathrm{bad}_j: \mathcal{H}_T \to \mathrm{Bool}$ covering $E$ ($E \subseteq (\Pi_T^{(q_j)})^{-1}(\{\mathrm{bad}_j = \mathrm{true}\})$), compute exact rational reference failure mass $\widehat{R}_T(\mathrm{prog}_j, q_j, \omega)$ under $\widehat{K}_{q_j}(\omega)$ and rational path budget
   $$
   \widehat{B}_T(\mathrm{prog}_j, q_j, \omega, u) := \min\Bigl\{1,\, \widehat{R}_T(\mathrm{prog}_j, q_j, \omega) + T \min\bigl\{1,\, u + \widehat{\delta}_{q_j}(\omega)\bigr\}\Bigr\} \in \mathbb{Q}.
   $$
   The total Boolean gate `selectedAccepts` returns `true` iff `selectCandidate` $= \mathrm{some}\ j$ and the candidate gate `accepts` holds ($N \ge 1$, $u \ge 0$, $\tau \ge 0$, $0 \le \beta \le 1$, and $\widehat{B}_T(\mathrm{prog}_j, q_j, \omega, u) \le \beta$).

# Continuous observable belief quotients on standard Borel spaces

Sections 2–4 tracked hidden states either combinatorially via finite supports $S_n \in \mathcal{A}_q$ or approximately via empirical lumpability defects $\widehat{\delta}_q$. In general partially observed Markov control on Polish or standard Borel state spaces $(X, \Sigma_X)$ [@bertsekasShreve1978; @hernandezLerma1996; @kallenberg2021], optimal and predictive controllers instead maintain a continuous *belief state* $b \in \mathcal{P}(X)$ in the Giry space of probability measures on $X$, equipped with the Giry $\sigma$-algebra $\Sigma_{\mathcal{P}(X)}$ generated by the evaluation maps $b \mapsto b(E)$ for $E \in \Sigma_X$ [@astrom1965; @giry1982]. Given a Markov transition kernel $P: X \times A \to \mathcal{P}(X)$ (`ProbabilityTheory.Kernel` in Mathlib) and a measurable binary observation map $q: X \to \{0,1\}$ (with deterministic observation kernel $O((x,a), \cdot) = \delta_{q(x)}$), each belief–action pair $(b,a)$ induces a predicted state measure $\widehat{b}_a := \int_X P(x,a)\, db(x)$, a predicted observation measure $\bar{o}_{b,a} := q_\# \widehat{b}_a \in \mathcal{P}(\{0,1\})$, and—because $X$ is a standard Borel space—a measurable regular conditional probability kernel (`posteriorKernel`) yielding the Bayesian posterior belief $\mathrm{Post}(b,a,y) \in \mathcal{P}(X)$ uniquely for $\bar{o}_{b,a}$-almost every $y \in \{0,1\}$. Pushing $\bar{o}_{b,a}$ forward through $y \mapsto \mathrm{Post}(b,a,y)$ defines the measurable *posterior-belief transition kernel* $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}: \mathcal{P}(X) \times A \to \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(X))$.

When can the infinite-dimensional belief space $\mathcal{P}(X)$ be compressed without loss to the one-dimensional observable coordinate $c_q: \mathcal{P}(X) \to [0,1]$ defined by $c_q(b) := b(q^{-1}(\{1\}))$? Assume $q$ is surjective, witnessed by explicit representatives $x_0, x_1 \in X$ with $q(x_0) = 0$ and $q(x_1) = 1$. Then $c_q$ maps $\mathcal{P}(X)$ surjectively onto the compact convex interval $[0,1]$ with explicit measurable affine section $\sigma_{x_0, x_1}: [0,1] \to \mathcal{P}(X)$ given by $\sigma_{x_0, x_1}(u) := (1 - u)\delta_{x_0} + u \delta_{x_1}$, satisfying $c_q(\sigma_{x_0, x_1}(u)) = u$ for all $u \in [0,1]$. Define the *closed-tests condition* (`ClosedTests`) on $(P, q)$ by requiring one-step observable transition invariance across $q$-fibers [@burkeRosenblatt1958; @kemenySnell1960; @larsenSkou1991; @givanDeanGreig2003]:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{ClosedTests}(P, q) :\iff \forall x, x' \in X,\ q(x) = q(x') \implies \\
\forall a \in A,\ P(x,a)\bigl(q^{-1}(\{1\})\bigr) = P(x',a)\bigl(q^{-1}(\{1\})\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$
For any finite action–observation test word $w = [(a_1, y_1), \dots, (a_k, y_k)] \in (A \times \{0,1\})^*$, define the multi-step test response $F_w: X \to [0,1]$ recursively by $F_{[]}(x) := 1$ and $F_{(a,y)::w'}(x) := \int_{q^{-1}(\{y\})} F_{w'}(z)\, P(x,a)(dz)$, with belief test expectation $T_w(b) := \int_X F_w(x)\, db(x)$ [@jaeger2000; @littmanSuttonSingh2001].

**Theorem 6 (Closed-test characterization of measurable Markov belief quotients).** *Let $(X, \Sigma_X)$ be a nonempty standard Borel space, $(A, \Sigma_A)$ a measurable action space, $P: X \times A \to \mathcal{P}(X)$ a Markov kernel, and $q: X \to \{0,1\}$ a measurable map with $q(x_0) = 0$ and $q(x_1) = 1$.*

1. **Necessary and sufficient belief-kernel descent.** *$\mathrm{ClosedTests}(P, q)$ holds if and only if there exists a unique Markov quotient kernel $\bar{K}: [0,1] \times A \to \mathcal{P}([0,1])$ intertwining $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}$ across every input belief $b \in \mathcal{P}(X)$ and action $a \in A$:*
   $$
   (c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(b, a) = \bar{K}\bigl(c_q(b), a\bigr).
   $$
   *Moreover, $\bar{K}$ is given explicitly by pulling $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}$ back along the affine section $\sigma_{x_0, x_1}$ and pushing forward by $c_q$; conversely, existence of **any** kernel $\bar{K}$ (even without assuming $\bar{K}$ is Markov) satisfying $(c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(b,a) = \bar{K}(c_q(b), a)$ for all point-mass beliefs $b = \delta_x$ already forces $\mathrm{ClosedTests}(P, q)$.*
2. **Multi-step predictive sufficiency vs. hidden-state non-descent.** *Whenever $\mathrm{ClosedTests}(P, q)$ holds, every multi-step test expectation $T_w(b)$ ($w \in (A \times \{0,1\})^*$), every observation-measurable reward $\int_X r(q(x))\, db(x) = (1 - c_q(b))r(0) + c_q(b)r(1)$, and observable-class probabilities factor solely through $c_q(b)$. Nevertheless, on the four-state parity-noise system $X = \{0,1\}^2$ with binary actions $A = \{0,1\}$, $q(x_1, x_2) = x_1$, Bernoulli$(1/4)$ noise $N$, and transition $(x_1, x_2) \xrightarrow{a, N} (x_1 \oplus (a \land N),\, x_2 \oplus N)$, $\mathrm{ClosedTests}(P, q)$ holds and $\delta_{(0,0)}, \delta_{(0,1)}$ induce identical expectations on **all** future action–observation words $w$, yet no function of $c_q(b)$ can represent the latent reward $\int \mathbf{1}\{x_2 = 1\}\, db$ or the latent safety probability $b(\{x_2 = 0\})$, and perturbing step transitions to $(\text{if } a = 1 \text{ then } x_2 \text{ else } x_1,\, x_2)$ destroys $\mathrm{ClosedTests}$ and eliminates all belief quotients.*

Theorem 6 (proved in Appendix A.5) highlights two delicate measure-theoretic points that informal POMDP abstractions gloss over. First, regular conditional probabilities on standard Borel spaces are determined only $\bar{o}_{b,a}$-almost everywhere; assigning arbitrary point values on null observation branches must never affect the pushforward coordinate law $(c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(b,a)$, which our proof establishes via almost-everywhere posterior projection ($q_\# \mathrm{Post}(b,a,y) = \delta_y$ for $\bar{o}_{b,a}$-a.e.\ $y$) yielding the exact scalar identity $(c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(b,a) = \{0 \mapsto \bar{o}_{b,a}(\{0\}),\, 1 \mapsto \bar{o}_{b,a}(\{1\})\}$. Second, Theorem 6.2 demonstrates formally why exact predictive equivalence [@shaliziCrutchfield2001; @littmanSuttonSingh2001] does *not* automatically preserve state-space safety: when hazardous failure resides in a dynamically decoupled or latent coordinate $x_2$, compressing beliefs to $c_q(b)$ preserves every observable prediction while erasing whether the system occupies $x_2 = 0$ or $x_2 = 1$.

# Prepaid resource constraints, fault-mechanism discrimination, and minimax regret

## Serial versus correlated fault propagation under strict prepayment

In Sections 2–5, state uncertainty arose from coarse sensors while the underlying transition mechanism $P$ was fixed. In fault-tolerant distributed stores, redundant avionics, and replicating register assemblies [@athreyaNey1972; @altman1999; @cassandrasLafortune2021], a second, deeper structural ambiguity arises: **how do faults propagate across redundant components when active repair requires prepaid operational budget?** If an action creates a healthy backup slot and a subsequent step suffers a fault, does that fault corrupt only the currently dispatched slot, or does a shared environmental shock simultaneously corrupt the undispatched backup?

To analyze this question with complete mathematical transparency, consider a dynamic register store initialized at step $t = 0$ with a single damaged root slot (`root0` holding bit $\neg g$, where $g \in \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\}$ is the public target bit) and an initially absent secondary backup slot (`child1` $= \mathrm{none}$). At each step $t \in \{0, \dots, H-1\}$, nature draws an independent tick mode $M_t \in \{\mathrm{normal}, \mathrm{fault}\}$ with $\mathbb{P}(M_t = \mathrm{normal}) = r \in [0,1] \cap \mathbb{Q}$ and $\mathbb{P}(M_t = \mathrm{fault}) = 1 - r$ (together with an independent fair birth label retained in world lineage records). Two candidate environmental fault mechanisms $m \in \{S, G\}$ govern how faults affect the store, with $m$ fixed before $t=0$ for the entire run:

- **Serial local fault ($S$).** A normal step executes the requested operation on the addressed slot: idling a healthy slot (`bit` $= g$) or writing $g$ to an existing slot produces one newborn (filling `child1` with a healthy bit $g$ if `child1` was `none`, and appending subsequent births to tail records), whereas writing $\neg g$ overwrites the addressed slot with $\neg g$ and produces zero births. A fault step on an existing addressed slot corrupts *only that addressed slot* to $\neg g$, leaves undispatched slots intact, charges zero write fee, and produces zero births.
- **Global correlated fault ($G$).** Normal-mode execution is identical to $S$. Whenever a tick enters fault mode ($M_t = \mathrm{fault}$), however—including during idle requests, requests to an absent `child1`, or post-`stop` ticks—*every currently living slot in the store* has its physical bit reset to $\neg g$.

At each step, the controller selects from a seven-action menu $\mathcal{A}_7$: permanent `stop` (which commits the current and all remaining steps to idling `root0` while world noise continues), `idle` at `root0` or `child1`, `write false` at `root0` or `child1`, or `write true` at `root0` or `child1`. Crucially, the controller operates under **two strict physical constraints**:

1. **Prepaid non-replenished wallet ($B$).** The controller begins with rational budget $B_0 = B \ge 0$ and write tariff $c > 0$. Every write request (`cap`$(a) = 1$; `stop` and `idle` have `cap`$(a) = 0$) is filtered *before* execution by the hard prepayment gate $c \cdot \mathrm{cap}(a_t) \le B_t$. One cannot submit an unaffordable write hoping that a fault or absent slot will waive the actual charge. Only a normal-mode write to an existing slot deducts $C_t = c$ ($B_{t+1} = B_t - c$); faulted writes, absent-slot writes, and idles deduct $C_t = 0$.
2. **Two-bit transaction receipts.** After each step, the controller observes *only* the two-bit receipt $o_t = (\mathrm{birth}_t, \mathrm{paid}_t) \in \{0,1\}^2$, recording whether a birth occurred and whether tariff $c$ was actually charged, without direct read access to internal slot bits, fault modes, or which mechanism $m \in \{S, G\}$ is active.

Notice immediately how $S$ and $G$ break naive state observers. Suppose $B \ge c$ and $0 < r < 1$. At $t=0$, the controller writes $g$ to `root0` and observes $o_0 = (1, 1)$ (occurring w.p. $r$ under both $S$ and $G$): `root0` is now repaired to $g$, `child1` is allocated with healthy bit $g$, and remaining wallet is $B - c$. At $t=1$, the controller idles `root0` and observes $o_1 = (0, 0)$ (occurring w.p. $1-r$ under both $S$ and $G$). After this identical positive-probability receipt prefix $[(1,1), (0,0)]$ (mass $r(1-r)$), the hidden store under $S$ has $(\mathrm{root0} = \neg g,\ \mathrm{child1} = g)$—so `child1` survives as an intact backup!—whereas under $G$ the fault wiped *both* slots to $(\mathrm{root0} = \neg g,\ \mathrm{child1} = \neg g)$. Under a prior $w = (w_S, w_G)$ over $\{S, G\}$, the marginal posterior on $m$ after $[(1,1), (0,0)]$ is still $(w_S, w_G)$, yet **mechanism identity and physical backup state have become strictly entangled**: multiplying the mechanism marginal by the state marginal would fabricate impossible worlds.

## Exact discrimination bounds, Bayes–minimax duality, and strict benefit of randomization

Fix horizon $H$, budget $B$, tariff $c > 0$, fee price $\lambda \ge 0$, and discount $d \in [0,1]$, evaluating any causal policy $\pi$ (permitted to use complete action–receipt histories plus arbitrary real-valued private randomness independent of $m$ and world noise) under mechanism $m \in \{S, G\}$ by net discounted payoff
$$
J_m(\pi) := \mathbb{E}_m^\pi\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{H-1} d^t \bigl(\mathrm{birth}_t - \lambda C_t\bigr)\right].
$$
Let $P_m^\pi$ denote the induced probability distribution over complete length-$H$ action–receipt histories (and private-seed decision tables), and let $\Delta_{H,B} := \sup_\pi d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_S^\pi, P_G^\pi)$ be the maximum total-variation distinguishability achievable by *any* legal adaptive experiment [@blackwell1953; @leCam1973; @coverThomas2006]. Simultaneously, let $V_m^* := \sup_\pi J_m(\pi)$ denote the oracle optimal value when $m$ is known (subject to the same receipt interface and wallet $B$), and define *worst-case minimax regret* [@wald1950; @savage1951] across unknown $m \in \{S, G\}$ by
$$
R_m(\pi) := V_m^* - J_m(\pi), \qquad R(\pi) := \max_{m \in \{S, G\}} R_m(\pi), \qquad R^* := \inf_\pi R(\pi).
$$
Notice that for any prior weights $w = (w_S, w_G) \in \Delta(\{S, G\})$, if $V_w^* := \sup_\pi (w_S J_S(\pi) + w_G J_G(\pi))$ is the Bayes-optimal value on the joint `(mechanism, store)` belief state [@bellman1957; @blackwell1965; @smallwoodSondik1973], then every legal policy $\pi$ satisfies $R(\pi) \ge w_S R_S(\pi) + w_G R_G(\pi) \ge w_S V_S^* + w_G V_G^* - V_w^*$. Thus, exhibiting a dual weight vector $w$ and a concrete legal policy $\rho$ satisfying the sandwich identity
$$
L(w) := w_S V_S^* + w_G V_G^* - V_w^* = \max_{m \in \{S,G\}} \bigl(V_m^* - J_m(\rho)\bigr) =: U(\rho)
$$
certifies *both* the exact minimax regret $R^* = L(w) = U(\rho)$ over all full-history real-randomized behavioral and private-seed policies (`RawCausalPolicy`) *and* optimality of $\rho$ [@vonNeumann1928].

| Policy / Quantity | Payoff $J_S(\cdot)$ under $S$ | Payoff $J_G(\cdot)$ under $G$ | Regret $R_S(\cdot)$ | Regret $R_G(\cdot)$ | Max Regret $R(\cdot)$ |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Oracle $S$-table $\pi_S$ | $41/32$ | $37/32$ | $0$ | $3/32$ | $3/32 = 12/128$ |
| Oracle $G$-table $\pi_G$ (best det.) | $40/32$ | $40/32$ | $1/32$ | $0$ | $1/32 = 4/128$ |
| Seeded mixture $\rho = \frac{1}{4}\pi_S + \frac{3}{4}\pi_G$ | $161/128$ | $157/128$ | $3/128$ | $3/128$ | **$3/128$** ($= R^*$) |

Dual weights $w = (3/4, 1/4)$ give $V_w^* = 5/4 = 40/32$ and $L(w) = \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{41}{32} + \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{40}{32} - \frac{5}{4} = \frac{3}{128}$.

**Theorem 7 (Exact fault-discrimination bound, minimax regret duality, and deterministic lattice gap).** *Consider the prepaid register fault model with mechanisms $\{S, G\}$ starting from a single damaged root.*

1. **Exact path-coupling distinguishability.** *Across all real-randomized adaptive history strategies (including shared private randomness between controller and terminal classifier), $\Delta_{H,B} = 0$ whenever $H \le 2$, whenever $B < c$, whenever $r \in \{0,1\}$, or whenever only `root0` is dispatched. At horizon $H = 3$,*
   $$
   \Delta_{3,B} = \begin{cases} r^2(1 - r), & B \ge c, \\ 0, & B < c, \end{cases}
   $$
   *attained when $B \ge c$ by the three-step probe `write g to root0` $\to$ `idle root0` $\to$ `idle child1`, whose receipt trace $[(1,1), (0,0), (1,0)]$ has probability $r^2(1-r)$ under $S$ and $0$ under $G$. Moreover, for every $H \ge 1$ and $r \in (0,1)$, shared all-fault mass $(1-r)^H$ forces every total binary classifier under equal priors to incur error at least $(1-r)^H / 2 > 0$ (and at $H=3, r=1/2$, minimum classification error is $\frac{1}{2}(1 - 1/8) = 7/16$). Nevertheless, observational indistinguishability need not force decision loss: at $(r, H, B, c, \lambda, d) = (1/2, 2, 2, 1, 1/4, 1)$, $\Delta_{2,2} = 0$ yet a single deterministic table attains $V_S^* = V_G^* = 13/16$ simultaneously.*
2. **Exact minimax regret and strict randomization advantage.** *Fix $(r, H, B, c, \lambda, d) = (1/2, 3, 2, 1, 1/4, 1)$ with $g = \mathrm{true}$. Then:*
   - *The full-menu oracle values are $V_S^* = 41/32$ and $V_G^* = 40/32 = 5/4$, with cross-evaluations $J_G(\pi_S) = 37/32$ and $J_S(\pi_G) = 40/32$;*
   - *Under dual weights $w = (3/4, 1/4)$, the joint-belief Bayes value is $V_w^* = 5/4$, yielding $L(w) = 3/128$;*
   - *Drawing an independent uniform four-point private seed $u \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ at initialization to execute $\pi_S$ if $u = 0$ and $\pi_G$ if $u \in \{1,2,3\}$ satisfies every prefix prepayment constraint $B_t = 2 - \sum_{j<t} C_j \ge 0$ pathwise and achieves $J_S(\rho) = 161/128$ and $J_G(\rho) = 157/128$, establishing $R^* = R(\rho) = 3/128$ over all real-randomized history strategies;*
   - *Every deterministic permitted-history strategy $\pi_{\mathrm{det}}$ satisfies $J_m(\pi_{\mathrm{det}}) \in \frac{1}{32}\mathbb{Z}$, forcing $R(\pi_{\mathrm{det}}) \ge 1/32 > 3/128$ (with $1/32$ attained by $\pi_G$).*

Theorem 7 (proved in Appendix A.6) illuminates the precise interplay between information, budget, and randomization (Figure 2). Why do $\pi_S$ and $\pi_G$ diverge at the third step? Only along the ambiguous prefix $[(1,1), (0,0)]$ (probability $1/4$, remaining wallet $1$, one step left): under $S$, idling `child1` is free and yields conditional expected payoff $1/2$, beating a paid re-write of `root0` ($1/2 - 1/4 \cdot 1/2 = 3/8$); under $G$, `child1` was wiped by the correlated fault, so idling `child1` yields $0$ while re-writing `root0` yields $3/8$. Note also two sharp conceptual distinctions: first, the dual weights $w = (3/4, 1/4)$ certifying the minimax lower bound are *opposite* to the deployment mixture weights $(1/4, 3/4)$ in $\rho$; second, although $H=3$ is the earliest horizon at which an experiment can distinguish $S$ from $G$ ($\Delta_{3,2} = 1/8$), the distinguishing receipt arrives at the *terminal* tick $t=2$, too late to inform earlier actions within that three-step task—which is precisely why hedging via pre-committed private randomness reduces worst-case regret from $4/128$ to $3/128$.

# Pure-kernel Lean 4 formalization architecture, GCD-free integer reduction, and related work

## Decoupling executable synthesis and rational oracles from classical measure semantics

A recurring architectural tension in formalizing stochastic control inside dependent type theory [@mouraUllrich2021; @mathlib2020; @carneiro2019] is that realistic theorems must speak about standard mathematical objects—Lebesgue integrals, regular conditional probabilities on standard Borel spaces, Kolmogorov infinite-trajectory extensions, and real exponential Hoeffding bounds—all of which in Mathlib are `noncomputable` and rely on `Classical.choice`, whereas synthesized controllers and statistical acceptance gates must be *genuinely executable programs* free of classical axioms at runtime.

Our Lean 4 architecture resolves this tension through a strict **two-layer semantic bridge**:

1. **Purely computable syntax and decision layer.** In `ExecutableSupportController`, the controller type `Program X Y A` stores `initialMemory : Y → Finset X`, `action : Finset X → A`, and `update : Finset X → A → Y → Finset X`. The synthesizer `synthesize` uses `Finset.filter`, `List.find?`, and bounded Boolean recursion up to $2^{|X|} - 1$. Likewise, in `NoisyFiniteRiskOracle`, empirical masses, $L_1$/TV overlaps $1 - \sum_z \min\{\widehat{p}(z), \widehat{p}'(z)\}$, fiber defects $\widehat{\delta}_q$, three-valued interval decisions (`certified`, `rejected`, `ambiguous`), representative selection (`Finset.min'` under an explicit `LinearOrder X`), reference history sums, and `selectedAccepts` are written entirely over computable Lean types (`Nat`, `Rat`, `Bool`, and `Option`). None of these definitions imports `PMF` or `Measure`.
2. **Noncomputable measure-theoretic interpretation layer.** Only in proof sections do we lift a `Program` via `PMF.pure` to a stochastic `FiniteMemoryController`, embed empirical count ratios into `PMF.toMeasure`, construct the Ionescu–Tulcea trajectory measure `stateTrajMeasure` on $(X \times \mathrm{Finset}\ X)^{\mathbb{N}}$, and apply Hoeffding concentration on the product row law (`productRowLaw` in `VariableRegisteredSamples`). Bridge lemmas (`empiricalTV_eq`, `referenceRisk_eq`, and `memory_eq_traceSupport`) prove exact equality between the rational/combinatorial runtime outputs and their measure-theoretic counterparts, transferring `accepts = true` directly into real probability bounds without ever requiring runtime code to compute with reals.

## Eliminating `native_decide` via GCD-free integer-scaled dynamic programming

The most instructive formal-verification obstacle in this work arose in certifying `exact_cached_data` and `exact_two_data` for Theorem 7 (`RegisterMechanismDecisionConsumer`). Evaluating the exact Bayes-optimal value $V_3^*(b_0, 2)$ over the joint `(FaultMechanism × Store)` belief space explores all $7$ actions and $4$ two-bit receipts across depth $3$ ($28$ action–observation branches per node). In the general rational POMDP solver (`FiniteJointDecisionSolver`), every positive-mass receipt $(a, o)$ updates the rational belief $b \in \Delta(\mathcal{W})$ via normalized Bayes division:
$$
b^{a,o}(w') = \frac{\sum_{w,z} b(w)\, P(z \mid w)\, \mathbf{1}\{O(w,a,z)=o,\ T(w,a,z)=w'\}}{m_b^a(o)} \in \mathbb{Q}.
$$
In Lean 4, each rational addition and division (`ℚ`) maintains coprime numerator/denominator normal form by invoking `Nat.gcd`. Inside compiled native code (`#eval` or `native_decide`), Euclidean GCD over small integers takes microseconds. Inside Lean's **logical kernel reducer** (`decide`), however, unfolding nested `Nat.gcd` recursion across hundreds of rational belief-normalization nodes triggers severe term blowup. A common shortcut in both Coq (`native_compute`; @boespflug2011) and Lean 4 (`native_decide`) delegates Boolean evaluation to the code generator/compiler, introducing the nonstandard compiler-trust axiom `Lean.ofReduceBool` (and `Lean.trustCompiler`), which expands the trusted computing base from the small logical kernel (~$10^4$ lines) to the entire compiler pipeline, runtime representation, and foreign primitives.

To achieve **100% pure-kernel verification** (`decide` with zero `Lean.ofReduceBool`), we designed an exact *unnormalized integer-scaled dynamic programming mirror* (`csolveInt` and `csourceInt`) and proved its bisimulation with the rational Bayes solver (`csolveInt_spec`):

1. **Unnormalized integer beliefs (`UBelief`).** Represent an unnormalized belief as an association list $U = [(w_1, u_1), \dots, (w_k, u_k)]$ with positive integer weights (`List (World × Nat)`), total weight $\mathrm{usum}(U) := \sum_i u_i \in \mathbb{N}$, and linear functional $\mathrm{uexpect}(U, f) := \sum_i u_i f(w_i)$. Define the representation invariant `Represents`$(U, b)$ by
   $$
   \mathrm{usum}(U) > 0 \quad \land \quad \forall f: \mathcal{W} \to \mathbb{Q},\ \mathrm{uexpect}(U, f) = \mathrm{usum}(U) \cdot \mathbb{E}_b[f].
   $$
   At $r = 1/2$, clearing the $1/2$ tick-mode denominator updates $U \mapsto U'_{a,o} := \mathrm{unext}(U, a, o)$ purely by integer list insertion (`uinsert`), satisfying the division-free identity $2\,\mathrm{usum}(U)\, m_b^a(o) = \mathrm{usum}(U'_{a,o})$ and preserving `Represents`$(U'_{a,o}, b^{a,o})$ whenever $\mathrm{usum}(U'_{a,o}) > 0$.
2. **Dyadic horizon-and-tariff scaling.** At price $\lambda = 1/4$, $4 \cdot \mathrm{gain}(a,o) = \mathrm{gain}_4(o) := 4\,\mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{birth}\} - \mathbf{1}\{\mathrm{paid}\} \in \mathbb{Z}$. Defining the integer dynamic program `csolveInt`$(n, (U, \mathrm{bal})) \in \mathbb{Z} \times \mathrm{CTree}_n$ recursively at remaining horizon $n+1$ by summing over $o \in \{0,1\}^2$ the branch score
   $$
   \mathrm{stepObs}(o) := \begin{cases}
   0, & \mathrm{usum}(U'_{a,o}) = 0, \\
   2^n \cdot \mathrm{usum}(U'_{a,o}) \cdot \mathrm{gain}_4(o) + \mathrm{csolveInt}_n(U'_{a,o}, \mathrm{bal}'_o)_1, & \mathrm{usum}(U'_{a,o}) > 0,
   \end{cases}
   $$
   and maximizing $\sum_{o \in \{0,1\}^2} \mathrm{stepObs}(o)$ across legal actions $a \in \mathrm{menuNat}(\mathrm{bal})$, satisfies—by induction on $n$ with *zero rational division*—the exact scaling invariant
   $$
   \bigl(\mathrm{csolveInt}_n(U, \mathrm{bal})_1 : \mathbb{Q}\bigr) = 4 \cdot 2^n \cdot \mathrm{usum}(U) \cdot V_n^*(b, \mathrm{bal}),
   $$
   together with exact equality of returned policy trees (`toTable = solve`) because positive scaling by $c_{n+1} := 4 \cdot 2^{n+1} \cdot \mathrm{usum}(U) > 0$ preserves strict action inequalities ($\mathrm{score}_{\mathbb{Z}}(a_1) < \mathrm{score}_{\mathbb{Z}}(a_2) \iff Q(a_1) < Q(a_2)$) and tie-breaking order (`cselectScored_map`).
3. **Instantaneous pure-kernel `decide` certification.** Initializing $U_{\mathrm{knownS}} = [(w_S, 1)]$ ($\mathrm{usum} = 1$, scale $4 \cdot 2^3 \cdot 1 = 32$), $U_{\mathrm{knownG}} = [(w_G, 1)]$ (scale $32$), and $U_{\mathrm{dual}} = [(w_S, 3), (w_G, 1)]$ ($\mathrm{usum} = 4$, scale $4 \cdot 2^3 \cdot 4 = 128$), Lean's pure kernel `decide` reduces the seven integer equalities
   $$
   \mathrm{csolveInt}_3(U_S, 2)_1 = 41,\quad \mathrm{csolveInt}_3(U_G, 2)_1 = 40,\quad \mathrm{csolveInt}_3(U_{\mathrm{dual}}, 2)_1 = 160,
   $$
   $$
   \begin{aligned}
   \mathrm{csourceInt}(\pi_S, w_S) &= 41, \qquad \mathrm{csourceInt}(\pi_S, w_G) = 37, \\
   \mathrm{csourceInt}(\pi_G, w_S) &= 40, \qquad \mathrm{csourceInt}(\pi_G, w_G) = 40
   \end{aligned}
   $$
   in pure ring arithmetic (`Nat`/`Int`), immediately yielding $41/32$, $40/32 = 5/4$, $160/128 = 5/4$, and $37/32$ (and similarly $13 / (4 \cdot 2^2) = 13/16$ at $H=2$ via `kernel_int_h2`).

# Conclusion and open formalization problems

Certifying autonomous decision-makers under partial observation requires respecting the exact epistemic contract of each mathematical layer. When exact structural transition supports are justified by domain architecture, trace-support recursion and greatest fixed points on the observation-fiber arena yield a sound and complete computable controller synthesizer (`synthesize`) guaranteeing unit killed survival mass and almost-sure perpetual safety without finite-observation or reference-posterior assumptions. When transitions are known only through finite black-box samples, uniform exact-zero support certification is provably impossible, but pairing structural safety synthesis with simultaneous row calibration (`NoisyFiniteRiskOracle`) delivers rigorous, candidate-count-free bounds on joint post-selection acceptance and deployment failure. When continuous beliefs are compressed to observable test coordinates, `ClosedTests` characterizes exact Markov descent while exposing hidden-safety non-descent; and when prepaid interventions confront ambiguous local versus global fault propagation, GCD-free integer dynamic programming certifies exact randomized minimax regret purely inside Lean's logical kernel.

Four concrete open problems invite future formalization work:

1. *Memoized winning-support synthesis and complexity bounds.* Our verified synthesizer reuses recursive bounded viability checking up to depth $2^{|X|}-1$ for proof simplicity; formalizing a memoized bottom-up DAG table over $\mathcal{A}_q$ with verified EXPTIME/polynomial-in-$|\mathcal{A}_q|$ step bounds would connect directly to large-scale shield benchmarks [@junges2021].
2. *Approximate $\varepsilon$-`ClosedTests` long-horizon belief filtering.* Extending Theorem 6 from exact `ClosedTests` ($\varepsilon = 0$) to $\varepsilon$-approximate test closure by coupling one-step belief-quotient defects with Dobrushin/Hilbert-metric filter contraction [@dobrushin1956] would yield infinite-horizon discounted and ergodic error bounds.
3. *Adaptive sequential stopping vs. fixed row allocations.* Extending Theorem 4 and Theorem 5 from fixed row allocations $N_{x,a}$ to sequentially stopped martingale confidence sequences [@wald1950; @abbasiYadkori2011] would characterize exact sample-complexity trade-offs when transition margins are unknown.
4. *Asymptotic minimax fault regret for $m \ge 3$ fault topologies.* Generalizing Theorem 7's exact $H=3$ $\{S, G\}$ duality ($R^* = 3/128$) to $m \ge 3$ hierarchical fault-propagation graphs and horizons $H \to \infty$ would establish sharp asymptotic minimax regret scaling under finite prepayment budgets.

## Posterior-support bridge, viability equivalence, and quantifier exchange (Theorems 1 and 2)

*Proof of Theorem 1.*
**(1)** *Support bridge.* For $n = 0$, $\bar{p}_0^\pi(y) = \sum_{x \in X : q(x)=y} p_0(x)$. Since $X$ is finite and $p_0(x) \ge 0$, this finite sum is strictly positive iff $\exists x \in X$ with $p_0(x) > 0$ and $q(x) = y$, i.e., $S_0(y) \neq \varnothing$; for such $y$, $\rho_0^{\pi, y}(x) = p_0(x)\mathbf{1}\{q(x)=y\}/\bar{p}_0^\pi(y) > 0 \iff x \in S_0(y)$. Now assume inductively at step $n$ that for every $g \in \mathcal{H}_n$, $\bar{p}_n^\pi(g) > 0 \iff \exists h \in \mathcal{M}_n$ with $\Pi_n(h) = g$ and $\mu_n^\pi(h) > 0$, and that on $\bar{p}_n^\pi(g) > 0$, $\{ \mathrm{last}_n(h) : \Pi_n(h)=g,\ \mu_n^\pi(h)>0 \} = S_n(g) = \operatorname{supp}\rho_n^{\pi,g}$. For $g' = (g, (a, y)) \in \mathcal{H}_{n+1}$, by definition of the causal transition kernel,
$$
\bar{p}_{n+1}^\pi(g, (a, y)) = \sum_{\substack{h \in \mathcal{M}_n \\ \Pi_n(h) = g}} \mu_n^\pi(h)\, \pi_n(g)(a) \sum_{\substack{x' \in X \\ q(x') = y}} P_n(\mathrm{last}_n(h), a)(x').
$$
Notice that $\pi_n(g)(a)$ depends on $h$ *only* through $g = \Pi_n(h)$. Because all factors are non-negative and sums are finite, $\bar{p}_{n+1}^\pi(g, (a, y)) > 0$ iff $\pi_n(g)(a) > 0$ and there exist $h \in \mathcal{M}_n$ with $\Pi_n(h) = g, \mu_n^\pi(h) > 0$ and $x' \in X$ with $q(x') = y$ and $P_n(\mathrm{last}_n(h), a)(x') > 0$. By the induction hypothesis, the existence of such $h$ is equivalent to $\bar{p}_n^\pi(g) > 0$ and $\mathrm{last}_n(h) \in S_n(g)$, which together with $q(x') = y$ and $P_n(\mathrm{last}_n(h),a)(x') > 0$ is equivalent to $x' \in \operatorname{Succ}_n(S_n(g), a, y) = S_{n+1}(g')$. Normalizing by $\bar{p}_{n+1}^\pi(g') > 0$ gives $\operatorname{supp}\rho_{n+1}^{\pi, g'} = S_{n+1}(g')$.

**(2)** *Viability equivalence.* First observe that by expanding
$$
\nu_n^\pi(x,a) = \sum_{g \in \mathcal{H}_n : \bar{p}_n^\pi(g)>0} \bar{p}_n^\pi(g)\, \rho_n^{\pi,g}(x)\, \pi_n(g)(a)
$$
(via pushforward of the finite support $\operatorname{supp}\mu_n^\pi$), Part (1) implies
$$
\nu_n^\pi(C_n^c) = 0 \iff \forall g \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_n^\pi,\ \forall a \in \operatorname{supp}\pi_n(g),\ \forall x \in S_n(g),\ C_n(x,a).
$$
(*Sufficiency*) Suppose $\forall y \in Y,\ S_0(y) \neq \varnothing \implies V(0, T, S_0(y))$. Define the deterministic policy $\pi^V$ at step $n$ and history $g \in \mathcal{H}_n$ as follows: if $n < T$ and $V(n, T-n, S_n(g))$ holds, select a witness action $a^* \in A$ from the existential definition of $V(n, T-n, S_n(g))$ and set $\pi_n^V(g) := \delta_{a^*}$; otherwise set $\pi_n^V(g) := \pi_{\mathrm{base}, n}(g)$. By induction on $n \in \{0, \dots, T\}$, every $g \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_n^{\pi^V}$ satisfies $V(n, T-n, S_n(g))$: at $n=0$, $\bar{p}_0^{\pi^V}(y) > 0 \implies S_0(y) \neq \varnothing \implies V(0, T, S_0(y))$; if true at $n < T$, then for $(g, (a, y)) \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_{n+1}^{\pi^V}$, Part (1) gives $g \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_n^{\pi^V}$, $a = a^*$ (the unique element of $\operatorname{supp}\pi_n^V(g)$), and $\operatorname{Succ}_n(S_n(g), a^*, y) \neq \varnothing$, whence the universal successor clause of $V(n, T-n, S_n(g))$ yields $V(n+1, T-n-1, S_{n+1}(g, (a^*, y)))$. Moreover, at every $n < T$ and $g \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_n^{\pi^V}$, $a^*$ satisfies $\forall x \in S_n(g),\, C_n(x, a^*)$, proving $\nu_n^{\pi^V}(C_n^c) = 0$.
(*Necessity*) Suppose $\pi$ satisfies $\nu_n^\pi(C_n^c) = 0$ for all $n < T$. We prove by backward induction on $k \in \{0, \dots, T\}$ that for $n = T - k$, every $g \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_n^\pi$ satisfies $V(n, k, S_n(g))$. For $k = 0$, $V(T, 0, S) = \mathrm{True}$. Assuming the claim for $k < T$ (so $n = T - (k+1) < T$) and fixing $g \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_n^\pi$, choose any $a \in \operatorname{supp}\pi_n(g)$ (nonempty as $\pi_n(g) \in \Delta(A)$). Since $\nu_n^\pi(C_n^c) = 0$, $\forall x \in S_n(g),\, C_n(x,a)$. For any $y \in Y$ with $\operatorname{Succ}_n(S_n(g), a, y) \neq \varnothing$, Part (1) implies $(g, (a, y)) \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_{n+1}^\pi$, so the induction hypothesis gives $V(n+1, k, \operatorname{Succ}_n(S_n(g), a, y))$. Hence $V(n, k+1, S_n(g))$ holds. Evaluating at $n = 0$ ($k = T$) on $S_0(y) \neq \varnothing$ ($\iff y \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_0^\pi$) completes the proof. $\square$

*Proof of Theorem 2.* $(3) \implies (2)$ is immediate by setting $\pi_T := \pi_\infty$, and $(2) \iff (1)$ applies Theorem 1.2 at every $T \in \mathbb{N}$. To prove $(1) \implies (3)$, first note by easy induction on $k$ that $V(n, k+1, S) \implies V(n, k, S)$ for all $n, S$. Fix any $(n, S)$ satisfying $V_\infty(n, S)$ ($\forall k,\, V(n, k, S)$), and for each $k \in \mathbb{N}$ define
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_k := \Bigl\{a \in A : \bigl(\forall x \in S,\ C_n(x,a)\bigr) \land \bigl(\forall y \in Y,\ &\operatorname{Succ}_n(S,a,y) \neq \varnothing \\
&\implies V(n+1, k, \operatorname{Succ}_n(S,a,y))\bigr)\Bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
$$
By $V(n, k+1, S)$, each $D_k$ is nonempty, and monotonicity of $V$ in $k$ gives $D_{k+1} \subseteq D_k$, hence $D_j \subseteq D_i$ whenever $i \le j$. Suppose toward a contradiction that $\bigcap_{k \in \mathbb{N}} D_k = \varnothing$. Then for every $a \in A$ there exists $k(a) \in \mathbb{N}$ with $a \notin D_{k(a)}$. Because $A$ is finite and nonempty, $K := \max_{a \in A} k(a) \in \mathbb{N}$ is well-defined, and since $D_K \neq \varnothing$ we can pick $a^\star \in D_K$. Since $k(a^\star) \le K$, downward nesting gives $a^\star \in D_{k(a^\star)}$, contradicting $a^\star \notin D_{k(a^\star)}$. Thus $\bigcap_{k \in \mathbb{N}} D_k \neq \varnothing$, yielding an action $a^\star \in A$ certifying $S$ at step $n$ and sending every nonempty $\operatorname{Succ}_n(S, a^\star, y)$ into $V_\infty(n+1, \cdot)$. Defining $\pi_{\infty, n}(g) := \delta_{a^\star(n, S_n(g))}$ whenever $V_\infty(n, S_n(g))$ holds (and $\pi_{\mathrm{base}, n}(g)$ otherwise), forward induction via Theorem 1.1 shows $V_\infty(n, S_n(g))$ holds on every $g \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_n^{\pi_\infty}$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$, whence $\nu_n^{\pi_\infty}(C_n^c) = 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$. $\square$

## Greatest fixed point, infinite-trajectory safety, and executable synthesis (Theorem 3)

Since $\mathcal{W}_0 = \mathcal{A}_q$ and $\Phi$ is $\subseteq$-monotone, $\mathcal{W}_{k+1} \subseteq \mathcal{W}_k$ for all $k$. For the three-state separation witness $X = \{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}, \bot\}$ with constant observation $q(x) \equiv ()$ and initial law $p_0 = \delta_{\mathrm{false}}$, step $n=0$ starts at $X_0 = \mathrm{false}$ and requires $a_0 = \mathrm{false}$ ($C(\mathrm{false}, a) \iff a = \mathrm{false}$), which transitions deterministically to $X_1 = \mathrm{true}$ and requires $a_1 = \mathrm{true}$ ($C(\mathrm{true}, a) \iff a = \mathrm{true}$), whereas $C(\bot, a)$ is false for all $a$. Any stationary memoryless policy $\pi_0: \{()\} \to \Delta(\{\mathrm{false}, \mathrm{true}\})$ assigns a fixed weight $\theta = \pi_0(())(\mathrm{true}) \in [0,1]$ at both $n=0$ and $n=1$: at $n=0$, $\nu_0^{\pi_0}(C^c) = \theta$, sending mass $\theta$ into $X_1 = \bot$ and mass $1-\theta$ into $X_1 = \mathrm{true}$; at $n=1$, the absorbing cemetery branch $X_1 = \bot$ incurs uncertified mass $\theta \cdot 1 = \theta$ while the $X_1 = \mathrm{true}$ branch incurs uncertified mass $(1-\theta)^2$, so $\nu_1^{\pi_0}(C^c) = \theta + (1-\theta)^2 \ge 3/4$. Meanwhile, the two-state support controller alternating $\{\mathrm{false}\} \leftrightarrow \{\mathrm{true}\}$ achieves $\nu_n(C^c) = 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

By exactness of $(I_0, R)$, `initialMemory`$(y) = S_0(y)$ and `nextMemory`$(S, a, y) = \operatorname{Succ}_n(S, a, y)$ for all $n$, so induction on $n$ gives $\mathrm{memory}_n(\mathrm{prog}, g) = S_n(g)$ for all $g \in \mathcal{H}_n$. Moreover, `finiteMemoryCheck` evaluates `finiteRelationViabilityCheck`$(B_X, S_0(q(x)))$ across $x \in I_0$, which by Part (1) holds iff $\forall y,\, S_0(y) \neq \varnothing \implies S_0(y) \in \mathcal{W}_* \iff V_\infty(0, S_0(y))$, which by Theorem 2 holds iff $\exists \pi,\, \forall n,\, \nu_n^\pi(C_n^c) = 0$. When `synthesize` $= \mathrm{some\ prog}$, for every $S \in \mathcal{W}_*$ there exists $a \in A \subseteq L_A$ passing `stepCheck`$(S, a)$; hence `List.find?` returns `some b` with `stepCheck`$(S, b) = \mathrm{true}$ (never falling back to $a_{\mathrm{fb}}$), certifying all $x \in S$ and keeping all nonempty successors in $\mathcal{W}_*$. Induction over $g \in \operatorname{supp}\bar{p}_n^{\mathrm{prog}}$ via Theorem 1.1 yields $S_n(g) \in \mathcal{W}_*$ and $\nu_n^{\mathrm{prog}}(C_n^c) = 0$ for all $n \in \mathbb{N}$.

## Fixed-sample zero-support impossibility (Theorem 4)

*Proof of Theorem 4.* Under $W = (W_1, \dots, W_N) \stackrel{\mathrm{i.i.d.}}{\sim} \mathrm{Bernoulli}(p)$, $\mathbb{P}_p(W = \mathbf{0}_N) = (1 - p)^N$; in particular, at $p = 0$, $\mathbb{P}_0(W = \mathbf{0}_N) = 1$ and $\mathbb{P}_0(W = w) = 0$ for $w \neq \mathbf{0}_N$. Expanding the bind expectation over $\{0,1\}^N$ gives
$$
\Gamma_\psi(p) = \sum_{w \in \{0,1\}^N} \mathbb{P}_p(W = w)\, \psi(w)(\mathrm{true}).
$$
Evaluating at $p = 0$ yields $\Gamma_\psi(0) = \psi(\mathbf{0}_N)(\mathrm{true}) = \rho$, and retaining only the non-negative summand $w = \mathbf{0}_N$ for general $p \in [0,1]$ yields $\Gamma_\psi(p) \ge \mathbb{P}_p(W = \mathbf{0}_N)\, \psi(\mathbf{0}_N)(\mathrm{true}) = \rho (1 - p)^N$, proving **(1)**.

For **(2)**, assume $\rho = \Gamma_\psi(0) > \alpha \ge 0$, so $\rho > 0$ and $\rho - \alpha > 0$. Since $N \ge 1$, the denominator $2\rho N > 0$, and $p_* := \frac{\rho - \alpha}{2\rho N}$ satisfies $p_* > 0$ and $p_* \le \frac{\rho}{2\rho \cdot 1} = \frac{1}{2} < 1$, so $p_* \in (0, 1)$. By Bernoulli's inequality on $-p_* \ge -1$, $(1 - p_*)^N \ge 1 - N p_*$. Multiplying by $\rho > 0$ and substituting $N \rho p_* = \frac{\rho - \alpha}{2}$ gives
$$
\Gamma_\psi(p_*) \ge \rho (1 - p_*)^N \ge \rho (1 - N p_*) = \rho - \frac{\rho - \alpha}{2} = \frac{\rho + \alpha}{2} > \alpha.
$$
Part **(3)** follows immediately by contraposition: if $\Gamma_\psi(p) \le \alpha$ for all $p \in (0,1)$, then $\Gamma_\psi(0) > \alpha$ is impossible, so $\Gamma_\psi(0) \le \alpha$. $\square$

## Calibrated post-selection risk and the noisy finite-risk oracle (Theorem 5)

*Proof of Theorem 5.*
**(1)** *Simultaneous defect calibration and first-PASS soundness.* Fix $\omega \in G_u$, so $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P(x,a), \widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega)) \le u$ for every $(x,a) \in X \times A$. For any candidate $q: X \to Y$ and any pair $x, x' \in X$ with $q(x) = q(x')$ and action $a \in A$, the triangle inequality gives
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigl|d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega), \widehat{P}_{x',a}(\omega)) - d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P(x,a), P(x',a))\bigr|
&\le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega), P(x,a)) \\
&\quad + d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P(x',a), \widehat{P}_{x',a}(\omega)) \le 2u.
\end{aligned}
$$
Taking maxima over $\{(x, x', a) : q(x) = q(x')\}$ yields $|\widehat{\delta}_q(\omega) - \delta_q(P)| \le 2u$. Consequently, if `noisyDecision`$(q, \omega, u, \tau) = \mathrm{certified}$ ($\widehat{\delta}_q(\omega) + 2u \le \tau$), then $\delta_q(P) \le \widehat{\delta}_q(\omega) + 2u \le \tau$; if `noisyDecision` $= \mathrm{rejected}$ ($\widehat{\delta}_q(\omega) - 2u > \tau$), then $\delta_q(P) \ge \widehat{\delta}_q(\omega) - 2u > \tau$. Because `selectCandidate` scans $i = 0, \dots, m-1$ sequentially, halts at the first index where `noisyDecision` $\neq \mathrm{rejected}$, and returns `some j` only when that halting index satisfies `noisyDecision` $= \mathrm{certified}$, every $i < j$ was `rejected` ($\delta_{q_i}(P) > \tau$) while $j$ is `certified` ($\delta_{q_j}(P) \le \tau$).

Next, for any $x \in X$ and $a \in A$, let $x_{\mathrm{rep}} := \mathrm{rep}_{q_j}(q_j(x))$, which satisfies $q_j(x_{\mathrm{rep}}) = q_j(x)$ since $x \in q_j^{-1}(\{q_j(x)\}) \neq \varnothing$. By data-processing contraction of total variation under $q_j$ ($d_{\mathrm{TV}}(q_\# \nu_1, q_\# \nu_2) \le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(\nu_1, \nu_2)$), on $\omega \in G_u$ we have
$$
\begin{aligned}
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl((q_j)_\# P(x,a),\, \widehat{K}_{q_j}(\omega)(q_j(x), a)\bigr)
&\le d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(P(x,a), \widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega)\bigr) + d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl(\widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega), \widehat{P}_{x_{\mathrm{rep}}, a}(\omega)\bigr) \\
&\le u + \widehat{\delta}_{q_j}(\omega),
\end{aligned}
$$
and trivially $\le 1$, hence $\le \varepsilon_j(\omega) := \min\{1, u + \widehat{\delta}_{q_j}(\omega)\}$ across all certified pairs. By standard step-by-step path coupling between the pushed-forward true coarse trajectory law $(\Pi_T^{(q_j)})_\# K_j$ and the macro-reference trajectory law $\widehat{\mathbb{P}}_T^{(j,\omega)}$ under the same causal policy $\mathrm{prog}_j$, one-step row discrepancies accumulate additively up to uncertified occupancy $\kappa_n = \nu_n^{\mathrm{prog}_j}(C^c)$:
$$
d_{\mathrm{TV}}\bigl((\Pi_T^{(q_j)})_\# K_j,\, \widehat{\mathbb{P}}_T^{(j,\omega)}\bigr) \le \sum_{n=0}^{T-1} \bigl[\varepsilon_j(\omega) + (1 - \varepsilon_j(\omega))\nu_n^{\mathrm{prog}_j}(C^c)\bigr].
$$
By Theorem 3.2, `synthesize`$(q_j, \dots) = \mathrm{some\ prog}_j$ guarantees $\nu_n^{\mathrm{prog}_j}(C^c) = 0$ for every $n$, eliminating the second term. Since $E \subseteq (\Pi_T^{(q_j)})^{-1}(\{\mathrm{bad}_j = \mathrm{true}\})$, we obtain $K_j(E) \le (\Pi_T^{(q_j)})_\# K_j(\{\mathrm{bad}_j = \mathrm{true}\}) \le \widehat{B}_T(\mathrm{prog}_j, q_j, \omega, u)$. Thus `accepts` $= \mathrm{true}$ ($\widehat{B}_T \le \beta$) implies $K_j(E) \le \beta$ on $G_u$, while Theorem 3.3 simultaneously yields $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{prog}_j}(\forall n,\, X_n \in K) = 1$.

**(2)** *Concentration of $G_u$ and joint post-selection bound.* For each pair $(x,a) \in X \times A$ and subset $B \subseteq X$, $\widehat{P}_{x,a}(\omega)(B) - P(x,a)(B)$ is the average of $N$ i.i.d. centered $[0,1]$-valued indicators $\mathbf{1}\{\omega_{x,a,k} \in B\} - P(x,a)(B)$, so Hoeffding's inequality [@hoeffding1963] gives $\mu_N(|\widehat{P}_{x,a}(B) - P(x,a)(B)| > u) \le 2 e^{-2Nu^2}$ for every $u \ge 0$. By Scheffé's variational identity $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(p, q) = \max_{B \subseteq X} |p(B) - q(B)|$, unioning over all $2^{|X|}$ subsets $B \subseteq X$ gives $\mu_N(d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P(x,a), \widehat{P}_{x,a}) > u) = \mu_N(\max_{B \subseteq X} |\widehat{P}_{x,a}(B) - P(x,a)(B)| > u) \le 2 \cdot 2^{|X|} e^{-2Nu^2}$. Summing over $|X|\,|A|$ rows and clipping at $1$ yields $\mu_N(G_u^c) \le \alpha_N(u) \in [0,1]$. Finally, let $F(\omega) := \{h \in \mathcal{M}_T : \mathrm{selectedAccepts}(\omega) = \mathrm{true} \land h \in E\}$. For $\omega \in G_u$, either `selectCandidate` $= \mathrm{none}$ (so `selectedAccepts` $= \mathrm{false}$ and $K_{s(\omega)}(F(\omega)) = 0 \le \beta$), or `selectCandidate` $= \mathrm{some}\ j$: if `accepts` $= \mathrm{false}$, again $F(\omega) = \varnothing$ ($0 \le \beta$); if `accepts` $= \mathrm{true}$, Part (1) gives $K_j(F(\omega)) = K_j(E) \le \beta$. Splitting $J(\{(\omega, h) : h \in F(\omega)\}) = \sum_{\omega \in G_u} \mu_N(\omega) K_{s(\omega)}(F(\omega)) + \sum_{\omega \in G_u^c} \mu_N(\omega) K_{s(\omega)}(F(\omega))$ gives $\le (1 - \mu_N(G_u^c))\beta + \mu_N(G_u^c) \cdot 1 = \beta + (1 - \beta)\mu_N(G_u^c) \le \min\{1, \alpha_N(u) + (1 - \alpha_N(u))\beta\}$ since $1 - \beta \ge 0$. $\square$

## Continuous observable belief quotients on standard Borel spaces (Theorem 6)

*Proof of Theorem 6.*
**(1)** Let $v: \{0,1\} \to [0,1]$ map $0 \mapsto 0, 1 \mapsto 1$. Under the deterministic observation kernel $O((x,a), \cdot) = \delta_{q(x)}$, for any $b \in \mathcal{P}(X)$ and $a \in A$, the joint state–observation prediction measure on $X \times \{0,1\}$ is $(\mathrm{id} \times q)_\# \widehat{b}_a$. By disintegration on the standard Borel space $X$, for $\bar{o}_{b,a}$-almost every $y \in \{0,1\}$, the regular posterior $\mathrm{Post}(b,a,y)$ concentrates on $q^{-1}(\{y\})$, so $q_\# \mathrm{Post}(b,a,y) = \delta_y$ and hence $c_q(\mathrm{Post}(b,a,y)) = v(y)$ for $\bar{o}_{b,a}$-a.e. $y$. Because $\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(b,a) = (\mathrm{Post}(b,a,\cdot))_\# \bar{o}_{b,a}$, pushing forward by $c_q$ yields the exact identity
$$
(c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(b, a) = v_\# \bar{o}_{b,a} \qquad (\forall b \in \mathcal{P}(X),\ \forall a \in A).
$$
(*Sufficiency & uniqueness*) Assume $\mathrm{ClosedTests}(P, q)$. Let $\mathrm{repr}(0) := x_0$ and $\mathrm{repr}(1) := x_1$, so $q(\mathrm{repr}(y)) = y$. For every $x \in X$, $q(x) = q(\mathrm{repr}(q(x)))$, so $\mathrm{ClosedTests}$ gives $P(x,a)(q^{-1}(\{1\})) = P(\mathrm{repr}(q(x)), a)(q^{-1}(\{1\}))$; since probabilities on $\{0,1\}$ sum to $1$, $q_\# P(x,a) = q_\# P(\mathrm{repr}(q(x)), a)$. Integrating over $x \sim b$ gives, for any measurable $S \subseteq \{0,1\}$,
$$
\begin{aligned}
\bar{o}_{b,a}(S) &= \int_X (q_\# P(x,a))(S)\, db(x)
= \int_X \bigl(q_\# P(\mathrm{repr}(q(x)), a)\bigr)(S)\, db(x) \\
&= \int_{\{0,1\}} \bigl(q_\# P(\mathrm{repr}(y), a)\bigr)(S)\, d(q_\# b)(y).
\end{aligned}
$$
Because two measures on $\{0,1\}$ coincide iff they assign the same mass to $\{1\}$, $c_q(b) = c_q(b') \implies q_\# b = q_\# b' \implies \bar{o}_{b,a} = \bar{o}_{b',a}$. Define the measurable Markov kernel $\bar{K}: [0,1] \times A \to \mathcal{P}([0,1])$ by $\bar{K}(u, a) := (c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(\sigma_{x_0,x_1}(u), a)$. Since $c_q(\sigma_{x_0,x_1}(c_q(b))) = c_q(b)$, we have $\bar{o}_{b,a} = \bar{o}_{\sigma_{x_0,x_1}(c_q(b)), a}$ and hence $(c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(b,a) = v_\# \bar{o}_{b,a} = \bar{K}(c_q(b), a)$. If any kernel $K'$ also satisfies $(c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(b,a) = K'(c_q(b), a)$ for all $(b,a)$, evaluating at $b = \sigma_{x_0,x_1}(u)$ gives $K'(u,a) = \bar{K}(u,a)$ via $c_q(\sigma_{x_0,x_1}(u)) = u$.
(*Necessity*) Conversely, suppose $(c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(b,a) = K'(c_q(b), a)$ holds for some kernel $K'$. Take any $x, x' \in X$ with $q(x) = q(x')$ and any $a \in A$. For point-mass beliefs, $c_q(\delta_x) = v(q(x)) = v(q(x')) = c_q(\delta_{x'})$, so $(c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(\delta_x, a) = (c_q)_\# \mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{bel}}(\delta_{x'}, a)$, i.e., $v_\# \bar{o}_{\delta_x, a} = v_\# \bar{o}_{\delta_{x'}, a}$. Evaluating both measures on the singleton $\{1\} \subseteq [0,1]$ (whose $v$-preimage is $\{1\} \subseteq \{0,1\}$) yields $P(x,a)(q^{-1}(\{1\})) = \bar{o}_{\delta_x, a}(\{1\}) = \bar{o}_{\delta_{x'}, a}(\{1\}) = P(x',a)(q^{-1}(\{1\}))$, establishing $\mathrm{ClosedTests}(P, q)$.

**(2)** Under $\mathrm{ClosedTests}(P, q)$, induction on word length $|w|$ shows $F_w(x) = g_w(q(x))$ for a function $g_w: \{0,1\} \to [0,1]$ (base $g_{[]} \equiv 1$; step $F_{(a,y)::w'}(x) = \int \mathbf{1}\{q(z)=y\} g_{w'}(q(z))\, P(x,a)(dz)$ depends only on $q_\# P(x,a)$, which depends only on $q(x)$), so $T_w(b) = \int g_w\, d(q_\# b)$ depends only on $c_q(b)$. On $X = \{0,1\}^2$ with $A = \{0,1\}$, $q(x_1,x_2) = x_1$, and transition $(x_1 \oplus (a \land N),\, x_2 \oplus N)$ ($N \sim \mathrm{Bern}(1/4)$), $q(x') = x_1 \oplus (a \land N)$ is independent of $x_2$, so $\mathrm{ClosedTests}$ holds; $\delta_{(0,0)}$ and $\delta_{(0,1)}$ both have $c_q = 0$ (hence identical $T_w$ for all $w$), yet $\int \mathbf{1}\{x_2=1\}\, d\delta_{(0,0)} = 0 \neq 1 = \int \mathbf{1}\{x_2=1\}\, d\delta_{(0,1)}$ and $\delta_{(0,0)}(\{x_2=0\}) = 1 \neq 0 = \delta_{(0,1)}(\{x_2=0\})$. Under the perturbed deterministic transition $(\text{if } a = 1 \text{ then } x_2 \text{ else } x_1,\, x_2)$ at $a = 1$, $(0,0) \mapsto (0,0)$ ($q = 0$) whereas $(0,1) \mapsto (1,1)$ ($q = 1$) despite $q(0,0) = 0 = q(0,1)$, violating $\mathrm{ClosedTests}$ and ruling out any quotient kernel by Part (1). $\square$

## Prepaid fault-mechanism discrimination and minimax regret (Theorem 7)

*Proof of Theorem 7.*
**(1)** *Coupling and distinguishability bounds.* Fix any real-randomized strategy $\pi$ and private random seed realization $\xi$ (which reduces $\pi(\xi)$ to a deterministic receipt-indexed action tree and terminal decision rule; integrating over $\xi$ preserves TV bounds by convexity). Couple $S$ and $G$ step-by-step on the exact same tick-mode sequence $(M_0, \dots, M_{H-1}) \in \{\mathrm{normal}, \mathrm{fault}\}^H$. Initially both stores equal $(\mathrm{root0} = \neg g,\ \mathrm{child1} = \mathrm{none})$. Observe:

- If $B < c$, no write request is ever affordable ($c \cdot 1 > B$), so only `stop` and `idle` are legal; from $(\neg g, \mathrm{none})$, no birth or charge ever occurs under either $S$ or $G$, giving identical receipt $(0,0)^H$ and $\Delta_{H,B} = 0$.
- If $r = 0$ (all ticks fault) or $r = 1$ (no tick faults, so $S$ and $G$ execute identical transitions), $P_S^\pi = P_G^\pi$ and $\Delta_{H,B} = 0$.
- If only `root0` is ever addressed, `child1`'s bit is never queried, and `root0` experiences the exact same bit transitions under $S$ and $G$ on every mode sequence (since a fault on a `root0` request sets `root0` to $\neg g$ under both $S$ and $G$, and a normal step updates `root0` identically), yielding $\Delta_{H,B} = 0$.
- For $H \le 2$ with arbitrary legal actions: at $t = 0$, `child1` is `none`, so both $S$ and $G$ emit identical $o_0$ and reach identical $t=1$ stores. At $t = 1$, since the two stores at the start of $t=1$ are identical, any legal action $a_1$ produces identical receipt $o_1$ under $S$ and $G$ (their post-$t=1$ hidden `child1` bits can diverge only if $t=0$ created a healthy `child1`, i.e. $M_0 = \mathrm{normal}$, and $t=1$ suffered $M_1 = \mathrm{fault}$ on a `root0` operation—which still emits $o_1 = (0,0)$ under both). Hence $P_S^\pi = P_G^\pi$ at $H \le 2$, proving $\Delta_{H,B} = 0$ for $H \le 2$.
- At $H = 3$: by the preceding bullet, $o_0$ and $o_1$ match on *every* mode word $(M_0, M_1, M_2)$, and the post-$t=1$ stores differ *only* if $M_0 = \mathrm{normal}$ and $M_1 = \mathrm{fault}$, while at $t=2$ a fault ($M_2 = \mathrm{fault}$) forces $o_2 = (0,0)$ regardless of store state. Thus $(o_0, o_1, o_2)$ can differ between $S$ and $G$ *only* on the unique mode word $(M_0, M_1, M_2) = (\mathrm{normal}, \mathrm{fault}, \mathrm{normal})$, which has probability $r(1-r)r = r^2(1-r)$. By the coupling inequality, $d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_S^\pi, P_G^\pi) \le r^2(1-r)$ for every $\pi$. Conversely, when $B \ge c$, the fixed legal sequence $a_0 = \text{write }g\text{ to root0}$, $a_1 = \text{idle root0}$, $a_2 = \text{idle child1}$ emits $[(1,1), (0,0), (1,0)]$ iff $(M_0, M_1, M_2) = (\mathrm{normal}, \mathrm{fault}, \mathrm{normal})$ under $S$ (probability $r^2(1-r)$) and *never* under $G$ (probability $0$), attaining $\Delta_{3,B} = r^2(1-r)$.

For binary classification under equal priors $\frac{1}{2}\delta_S + \frac{1}{2}\delta_G$, minimum Bayes error is $\frac{1}{2}(1 - d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_S^\pi, P_G^\pi))$ ($= \frac{1}{2}(1 - 1/8) = 7/16$ at $H=3, r=1/2$), and since the all-fault word $M_{0:H-1} = \mathrm{fault}^H$ has mass $(1-r)^H$ and produces identical trajectories under $S$ and $G$, $1 - d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_S^\pi, P_G^\pi) \ge (1-r)^H$, giving error $\ge (1-r)^H/2$. At $(r,H,B,c,\lambda,d) = (1/2, 2, 2, 1, 1/4, 1)$, writing $g$ to `root0` at $t=0$ (expected step-0 net payoff $\frac{1}{2}(1 - \frac{1}{4}) = \frac{3}{8}$; w.p. $1/2$ repairs `root0` with remaining wallet $1$, w.p. $1/2$ faults with remaining wallet $2$) followed at $t=1$ by idling `root0` after $(1,1)$ (expected payoff $\frac{1}{2}\cdot 1 = \frac{1}{2}$) and writing $g$ to `root0` after $(0,0)$ (expected payoff $\frac{3}{8}$) yields total payoff $\frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{8} = \frac{13}{16} = V_S^* = V_G^*$.

**(2)** *Exact three-step minimax regret and deterministic lattice gap.* Fix $(r, H, B, c, \lambda, d) = (1/2, 3, 2, 1, 1/4, 1)$. Exhaustive dynamic programming (verified inside the kernel via `csolveInt_spec` and `kernel_int_h3`) establishes the exact optimal values over all $7$-action tables:

- Under known $S$: $\pi_S$ writes $g$ to `root0` at $t=0$ (step-0 mean $3/8$):
  - Branch $o_0 = (1,1)$ (prob. $1/2$, wallet $1$, `root0` $=g$, `child1` $=g$): idles `root0` at $t=1$ (step-1 mean $1/2$); if $o_1 = (1,0)$ (cond. prob. $1/2$), idles `root0` at $t=2$ (mean $1/2$); if $o_1 = (0,0)$ (cond. prob. $1/2$, `root0` $=\neg g$ while `child1` $=g$ under $S$), idles `child1` at $t=2$ (mean $1/2$ under $S$, **but $0$ under $G$**). Thus the $o_0=(1,1)$ continuation yields $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 1$ under $S$, and $\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot 0 = \frac{3}{4}$ under $G$.
  - Branch $o_0 = (0,0)$ (prob. $1/2$, wallet $2$, `root0` $=\neg g$, `child1` $=\mathrm{none}$): reduces to the $H=2, B=2$ problem solved above, yielding continuation value $13/16$ under *both* $S$ and $G$.
  Hence $V_S^* = J_S(\pi_S) = \frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot 1 + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{13}{16} = \frac{12 + 16 + 13}{32} = \frac{41}{32}$, whereas $J_G(\pi_S) = \frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{13}{16} = \frac{37}{32}$.
- Under known $G$: $\pi_G$ differs from $\pi_S$ *only* after $[(1,1), (0,0)]$ (prob. $1/4$, wallet $1$), where $\pi_G$ writes $g$ to `root0` (mean $\frac{1}{2}(1 - \frac{1}{4}) = \frac{3}{8}$ under both $S$ and $G$). Hence $V_G^* = J_G(\pi_G) = J_S(\pi_G) = \frac{3}{8} + \frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{3}{8}\bigr) + \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{13}{16} = \frac{40}{32} = \frac{5}{4}$.
- Under dual prior $w = (3/4, 1/4)$: at the ambiguous prefix $[(1,1), (0,0)]$, the posterior over $\{S, G\}$ remains $(3/4, 1/4)$; idling `child1` yields Bayes continuation $\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4}\cdot 0 = \frac{3}{8}$, tying with writing $g$ to `root0` ($3/8$), while no earlier deviation can beat writing $g$ at $t=0$ and idling `root0` after $(1,1)$. Thus $V_w^* = \frac{5}{4}$, giving the universal minimax lower bound across *all* real-randomized strategies:
  $$
  R^* \ge L(w) = \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{41}{32} + \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{40}{32} - \frac{5}{4} = \frac{123 + 40 - 160}{128} = \frac{3}{128}.
  $$
- Simultaneously, the four-point seeded strategy $\rho = \frac{1}{4}\pi_S + \frac{3}{4}\pi_G$ satisfies $J_S(\rho) = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{41}{32} + \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{40}{32} = \frac{161}{128}$ ($R_S(\rho) = \frac{164 - 161}{128} = \frac{3}{128}$) and $J_G(\rho) = \frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{37}{32} + \frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{40}{32} = \frac{157}{128}$ ($R_G(\rho) = \frac{160 - 157}{128} = \frac{3}{128}$), proving $R^* = \frac{3}{128}$.
- Finally, for any deterministic strategy $\pi_{\mathrm{det}}$, each of the $2^3 = 8$ equiprobable mode sequences $(M_0, M_1, M_2) \in \{\mathrm{normal}, \mathrm{fault}\}^3$ (probability $1/8$ each) induces an integer birth count in $\mathbb{Z}$ and an integer fee count in $\mathbb{Z}$ scaled by $\lambda = 1/4$, so pathwise net payoff lies in $\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{Z}$ and expected payoff $J_m(\pi_{\mathrm{det}})$ lies in $\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{1}{4}\mathbb{Z} = \frac{1}{32}\mathbb{Z}$. Because $V_S^* = 41/32 \in \frac{1}{32}\mathbb{Z}$ and $V_G^* = 40/32 \in \frac{1}{32}\mathbb{Z}$, $R(\pi_{\mathrm{det}}) \in \frac{1}{32}\mathbb{Z}$. Since $R(\pi_{\mathrm{det}}) \ge R^* = 3/128 > 0$, integrality forces $R(\pi_{\mathrm{det}}) \ge 1/32 = 4/128$ (attained by $\pi_G$). $\square$
