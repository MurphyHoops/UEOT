# Cover Letter — Submission to *Journal of Automated Reasoning*

Dear Editors-in-Chief of the *Journal of Automated Reasoning*,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, **"Pure-Kernel Verified Controller Synthesis, Fixed-Sample Impossibility, and Calibrated Post-Selection Risk under Partial Observation,"** for publication as an original research article in the *Journal of Automated Reasoning*.

Interactive theorem provers have made landmark progress in formalizing measure-theoretic Markov processes and fully observed tabular Markov decision processes—notably in *Journal of Automated Reasoning* developments such as Hölzl (2017) in Isabelle/HOL, Affeldt, Hagiwara, and Sénizergues (2014) in Coq, and executable tabular MDP solvers such as Schäffeler and Abdulaziz (2023). Yet certifying autonomous controllers under *partial observation* when transition probabilities are estimated from finite data exposes three verification gaps:
1. Qualitative POMDP shield and winning-region methods (e.g., Junges et al. 2021; Carr et al. 2023) require exact transition supports outside a theorem prover, whereas data-driven pipelines estimate transitions from finite samples and select coarse state abstractions *after* inspecting the training data.
2. Continuous belief-state quotients on standard Borel spaces fail Markov closure unless observable test spaces are closed under transition pullbacks, and can erase hidden-state safety invariants even when predictive observation laws match identically.
3. Exact rational dynamic-programming proofs over partially observed belief trees suffer kernel reduction blowup from repeated `Nat.gcd` fraction normalization, tempting developments to introduce compiler-trust axioms (`native_decide` / `Lean.ofReduceBool`).

This article addresses these gaps through an end-to-end semantic chain and a Lean 4 (v4.33.1 / Mathlib) formalization across 1,051 compiled library modules (1,030 declaration-defining modules audited across 19,987 constants and 13,004 theorems):
- **Executable support synthesis and infinite-trajectory safety semantics (Theorems 1–3):** Without requiring finite enumeration of the observation type $Y$ at the Lean interface, we prove equality between positive-mass Bayesian posterior supports and combinatorial trace-support recursion, establish König-type finite-action compactness $(\forall T,\,\exists\pi_T) \iff (\exists\pi_\infty)$ (contrasting finite qualitative action signatures with a countable-state countdown compactness failure), and verify soundness and completeness of an executable finite-memory synthesizer `synthesize : Option (Program X Y A)` linked directly to killed substochastic matrix powers $Q_K^T$ and Kolmogorov infinite-trajectory safety $\mathbb{P}_{\mathrm{prog}}(\forall n \in \mathbb{N},\, X_n \in K) = 1$.
- **Fixed-sample zero-support impossibility and calibrated post-selection risk (Theorems 4–5):** Pairing prior structural supports with per-row empirical samples, we prove that no fixed-sample black-box test can uniformly certify exact zero transition leakage above level $\alpha$, and verify a computable rational `NoisyFiniteRiskOracle` controlling microscopic fiber row-discrepancies across arbitrary finite candidate families under a single simultaneous row-calibration event—bounding unconditional joint acceptance-and-failure probability $\mathbb{P}_J(\mathrm{accept} \land E) \le \min\{1, \alpha_N + (1-\alpha_N)\beta\}$ without candidate-count union bounds, while transparently reporting fiber-discrepancy conservatism, per-row sample scaling, and exact binomial acceptance/abstention rates ($99.988\%$ true acceptance vs. $5.97 \times 10^{-71}$ false acceptance at $N = 4096$).
- **Measurable belief quotients, prepaid fault minimax regret, and GCD-free kernel `decide` (Theorems 6–7 & Section 7):** We characterize binary-observation Giry belief quotients via `ClosedTests`, prove exact three-step local-versus-global fault distinguishability $\Delta_{3,B} = r^2(1-r)\mathbf{1}_{B \ge c}$ and full-history randomized minimax regret $R^* = 3/128 < 1/32$, and introduce unnormalized integer-weighted dynamic programming (`UBelief`, `csolveInt`) so that all seven-action belief-tree certificates check inside Lean's kernel via `decide` (`10.7 s` on Apple Silicon) with zero `sorryAx` and zero `Lean.ofReduceBool`, leaving only `{propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound}` under `scripts/ExhaustiveAxiomAudit.lean`.
