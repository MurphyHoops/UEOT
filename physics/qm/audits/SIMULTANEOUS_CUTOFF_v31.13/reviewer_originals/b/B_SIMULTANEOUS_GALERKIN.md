## 2. P-MICRO-06: self-adjoint source, energy budget, and boundary flow

### 2.1 Self-adjointness and its exact imported theorem

On finite Fock vectors,

\[
A^2=n+P_e=G,\qquad B^2=n+P_g=G+I-2P_e\le n+I.
\tag{B4}
\]

The red and blue operators have the self-adjoint block realizations established in Chapter 68. For `psi in D(n)`,

\[
\|(gA+hB)\psi\|\le (|g|+|h|)\|(n+I)^{1/2}\psi\|.
\]

For every `epsilon>0`, Cauchy--Schwarz followed by the scalar arithmetic-geometric-mean inequality gives

\[
\|(n+I)^{1/2}\psi\|
\le \sqrt{\|n\psi\|\,\|\psi\|}+\|\psi\|
\le \epsilon\|n\psi\|+(1+1/(4\epsilon))\|\psi\|.
\tag{B5}
\]

Therefore the symmetric linear coupling is infinitesimally n-bounded. The standard Kato--Rellich theorem is the explicit external mathematical dependency: a symmetric perturbation with relative operator bound less than one preserves self-adjointness on the domain of its self-adjoint unperturbed operator, and preserves its operator cores. Applying it to `omega n+Delta P_e` gives H and H0 self-adjoint on `D(n)` for every finite real g,h and every omega>0. No small-coupling assumption is needed. Finite Fock vectors are operator cores. H0 strongly commutes with G because its finite blocks have fixed G and its direct-sum realization is the same self-adjoint operator.

The imported theorem must not be relabelled as a new UEOT result or as machine-checked here. Its classical provenance includes T. Kato, *Fundamental properties of Hamiltonian operators of Schrödinger type*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 70 (1951), 195--211, DOI `10.1090/S0002-9947-1951-0041010-X`. The relative-bound verification (B5) is the application made here.

For (B2), let `X=a+a†` be the canonical self-adjoint oscillator quadrature, agreeing with the finite Fock expression. In the sigma_x eigenbasis choose `H_x=x X direct-sum (-x X)` on `D(X) direct-sum D(X)` (with the bounded zero operator when x=0). Its unitary is conditional displacement. This fixes the source domain even though omega=0. The finite Fock vectors and their red-only evolutions lie in the domains required for the finite Duhamel identities below.

### 2.2 Energy growth is derived from the blue source

Define the self-adjoint blue quadrature

\[
C=i(a^\dagger\sigma_+-a\sigma_-),\qquad
C^2=B^2\le G+I.
\]

It has the same two-dimensional block domains as B. The rigorous proof begins in the finite matrices. Put `G_K=J_K†GJ_K` and `C_K=J_K†CJ_K`. Since P_K commutes with G, the finite commutator is `i[H_K,G_K]=-2h C_K`, while

\[
C_K^2\le J_K^\dagger C^2J_K\le G_K+I.
\]

Thus ordinary finite-dimensional differentiation gives

\[
\left|\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{Tr}(G_K\rho_K(t))\right|
\le 2|h|\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}(G_K\rho_K(t))+1}.
\]

Integration proves, in every finite Galerkin model,

\[
\sqrt{E_K(t)+1}\le \sqrt{E_K(0)+1}+\int_0^t|h(s)|ds.
\tag{B6}
\]

To pass to the exact source, use the finite-support Galerkin convergence established by P-MICRO-07 below. Its proof uses only self-adjoint source domains, G preservation by H0, and the block bound B²<=G+I; it does **not** use this energy-growth result. For every fixed finite-support input it gives trace-norm convergence of the embedded finite output to the exact output as K tends to infinity. Lower semicontinuity of the positive G expectation then transfers (B6) to that exact output. Finally approximate any normal finite-energy input by its normalized low-G projection. These inputs converge in trace norm and have mean G no greater than the original mean. Exact unitary continuity and the same lower semicontinuity pass the bound to the original input. This proves

\[
\sqrt{E(t)+1}\le \sqrt{E(0)+1}+\int_0^t|h(s)|ds
\]

for the general source and the force subfamily without an unjustified differentiation of an arbitrary finite-energy state. The logical order is source self-adjointness, finite-support Duhamel bound, finite-matrix energy estimate, then lower-semicontinuous limits. No infinite-dimensional form-domain invariance is assumed to prove its own energy bound.

For an **unnormalized** positive prefix state sigma of mass m and excitation e, the homogeneous version is

\[
e_{\rm out}+m\le \bigl(\sqrt{e+m}+x\sqrt m\bigr)^2,
\qquad x=\int|h(s)|ds.
\tag{B7}
\]

Zero mass means a zero contribution and requires no normalization. If all outgoing branches are retained and their exposure is bounded, summing their budgets and applying Cauchy--Schwarz gives a complete-level energy budget. A bound on the aggregate mean is not a bound on every rare normalized branch. In the present source the flags have state-independent probabilities, but that special fact must be declared before using a shared pathwise normalized budget.

### 2.3 Actual boundary defect for a Galerkin time slot

The exact finite embedding defect is

\[
HJ_K-J_KH_K=(I-P_K)HJ_K=h(I-P_K)BJ_K.
\tag{B8}
\]

All H0 terms disappear because they preserve G. Direct calculation from the blue blocks gives

\[
J_K^\dagger B(I-P_K)BJ_K=W_K,
\]

\[
W_K=(K+1)|g,K\rangle\langle g,K|
   +K|g,K-1\rangle\langle g,K-1|.
\tag{B9}
\]

For K=0 the second term is omitted; no negative oscillator level is introduced. Formula (B9) measures the actual two outward blue boundary edges. It is not inferred from a global average-energy statement.

Let `sigma>=0` be a finite-carrier SR prefix state of mass m and let `sigma(s)=(U_K(s) tensor I_R) sigma (U_K(s)† tensor I_R)`. Duhamel with a true infinite suffix and a finite prefix gives

\[
U(t)J_K-J_KU_K(t)
=-i\int_0^t U(t-s)(HJ_K-J_KH_K)U_K(s)ds.
\tag{B10}
\]

Applying (B10) to a purification of sigma, retaining the reference, and converting vector difference to trace distance yields

\[
\frac12\|U(t)J_K\sigma J_K^\dagger U(t)^\dagger
       -J_KU_K(t)\sigma U_K(t)^\dagger J_K^\dagger\|_1
\le |h|\int_0^t\sqrt{m\,\operatorname{Tr}[(W_K\otimes I_R)\sigma(s)]}\,ds.
\tag{B11}
\]

The tensor identities and embeddings are suppressed only in the left-hand typography. The extra factor `sqrt(m)` is essential: a norm-`sqrt(m)` purification gives an unnormalized trace-distance bound `sqrt(m)` times its vector error. Treating sigma as normalized would erase the real prefix mass.

This is an a posteriori boundary-flow bound for the genuine compressed evolution. A pointwise floating evaluation of its integral is not yet a certified integral bound. Exact or interval quadrature, with a rigorously bounded variation/remainder, is needed if it is used as a certified numerical output. The uniform finite-remainder result next avoids that quadrature requirement.

## 3. P-MICRO-07: finite Duhamel remainder for the true compression

### 3.1 Precise statement

Choose integers `N,L>=0` and `K=N+2L`. Let the input be supported in P_N, and consider the exact H and the compressed H_K from (B1), or the force realization (B2). Set

\[
x=\int|h(s)|ds,\qquad
c_j(N,x)=\frac{x^j}{j!}\prod_{k=1}^j\sqrt{N+2k-1},\quad c_0=1.
\tag{B12}
\]

Then

\[
\|(U-J_KU_KJ_K^\dagger)P_N\|
\le 2c_{L+1}(N,x).
\tag{B13}
\]

Consequently, for every normal joint input supported in `P_N tensor I_R`,

\[
D\bigl((U\otimes I_R)\rho(U^\dagger\otimes I_R),
 (J_KU_KJ_K^\dagger\otimes I_R)\rho
 (J_KU_K^\dagger J_K^\dagger\otimes I_R)\bigr)
\le d_{N,L}(x):=\min\{1,2c_{L+1}(N,x)\}.
\tag{B14}
\]

For mass m inputs the right side is multiplied by m. The finite operator is only used on supported inputs; no full-space CPTP claim is made for the bare compression without its preparation/failure completion.

The statement also holds for finitely many piecewise constant source segments and G-preserving unitary gates, with x the sum of absolute blue exposures along the path and the **same** cutoff K throughout. It does not require a new growing carrier per slot.

### 3.2 Domain-safe finite expansion

The crucial orientation is

\[
U(t)=U_0(t)-ih\int_0^t U(t-s)B U_0(s)ds.
\tag{B15}
\]

The true U remains on the left as a suffix. Its right input `B U0(s) psi`, for a finite-support psi, again has finite support. The equality follows by differentiating `U(t-s)U0(s)psi`: `U0(s)psi` belongs to the common domains, so no unproved interchange involving an arbitrary unbounded prefix is used.

Iterate (B15) on its leftmost U exactly L+1 times. The first L orders consist only of G-preserving U0 propagators interspersed with j blue B operators. The remainder of order L+1 has a true unitary suffix on the left and only those controlled factors to the right. No infinite Dyson expansion or convergence theorem is needed for this finite identity.

After k-1 blue factors, support is contained in `P_(N+2k-2)`. From B²<=G+I, the next B has norm at most `sqrt(N+2k-1)` on that support. The ordered time-simplex integral of the absolute coupling product is `x^j/j!`. Thus the remainder is bounded by `c_(L+1)(N,x)`.

Perform the same finite expansion for the true matrix generator H_K. Its H0 part is the exact restriction, and every order j<=L agrees term by term with the infinite expansion because all intermediate supports fit in P_K. The finite remainder is also at most `c_(L+1)`: projections cannot enlarge norm or support. Subtracting the two expansions proves (B13). Purification, the pure-state trace-distance/vector-distance inequality, and partial trace prove (B14).

The initial suggestion of summing an infinite tail would have produced the valid but weaker factor `2 sum_(j>L)c_j`. The finite-remainder orientation, proposed by role A and independently checked by B, avoids that extra geometric-tail factor and its unnecessary convergence obligation. It must not be replaced by an expansion that leaves uncontrolled true evolution on the **right** of the blue factors.

For fixed finite N and x, the ratio `c_(j+1)/c_j=x sqrt(N+2j+1)/(j+1)` tends to zero. Hence choosing L large makes the bound arbitrarily small. This observation establishes convergence of the **error bounds**; it is not used to justify an infinite operator expansion.

## 5. Cross-check of the nominal-parameter bridge

The source parameter x is unknown. It is not supplied to the predictor. In the force subfamily the infinite comparison must proceed in this order:

`C_infinity(x) -> C_infinity(xhat) -> C_K(xhat)`.

Let `X=a+a†`. As a quadratic-form inequality,

\[
X^2\le 4n+2I.
\tag{B21}
\]

For example this follows from `||(a+a†)psi||²<=2||a psi||²+2||a†psi||²`. The actual infinite force unitary commutes strongly with X², and every spin-only phase does too. Low-spectral filtering gives a pass state with mean G at most one, so it initially has `Tr X²<=6`; every infinite nominal reference prefix retains this same bound. On each force occurrence,

\[
D(U_x\sigma U_x^\dagger,U_{\widehat x}\sigma U_{\widehat x}^\dagger)
\le |x-\widehat x|\sqrt{\operatorname{Tr}X^2\sigma}
\le \sqrt6\,|x-\widehat x|.
\tag{B22}
\]

Pathwise telescoping and `E_Q[#1]=1` give the same bound for the complete Q output. Thus an independently calibrated uniform displacement radius `Delta_x` contributes at most `sqrt(6) Delta_x`, in addition to the Galerkin bound at the **nominal** parameter. Finite H_K need not preserve the infinite X² expectation. The parameter proof therefore cannot be moved to finite reference prefixes without a new argument. ## 6. Required controls and numerical scope

1. At h=0 the blue boundary defect vanishes, H0 preserves every G cutoff, and the finite model is exact on supported input. This is the zero-defect positive control.
2. Simultaneous force from finite support still produces infinite tails for nonzero x, as C-MICRO-02 already proves. A hard-support statement is therefore still false.
3. The normalized projection of the exact evolved state is generally different from `exp(-it P_KHP_K)rho exp(it P_KHP_K)`. On initial `|g,1>`, K=1 and force area x, the finite model has amplitudes proportional to `(cos x,-i sin x)` on `(|g,1>,|e,0>)`, whereas the normalized projected infinite output has amplitude ratio `-ix/(1-x²)`. The different ratios give an explicit counterexample to equating projection-tail error with actual Galerkin dynamics.
4. A finite matrix exponential by itself supplies neither self-adjointness of the infinite generator nor a universal truncation theorem. The declared general-Rabi numerical stress case is `omega=1,Delta=3/5,g=2/5,h=1/10,t=1/5,N=1,L=3`, with a two-dimensional reference; a larger finite carrier comparison is only a finite diagnostic. 5. In the force subfamily, finite matrix elements of the exact displacement can be evaluated directly using associated Laguerre polynomials. An overlap with a normalized finite predictor determines the true infinite-versus-finite pure-state distance without pretending that a sufficiently large matrix is the infinite oracle.
