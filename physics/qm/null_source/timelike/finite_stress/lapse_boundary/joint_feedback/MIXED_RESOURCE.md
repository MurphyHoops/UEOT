# Sharp endpoint preparation resource for normal geometry targets

## M0. Statement and its physical contract

Use exactly the canonical endpoint map, incoming Fock embedding, fixed geometry/source partition, and actual high modes in [ENDPOINT_NORMALITY.md](ENDPOINT_NORMALITY.md), E1–E2. There are two real fields and \(2k^2\) real oscillators in shell \(k\), with \(\omega_k=\sqrt{k^2-1}\). The resource is the **incoming high-mode excitation**
\[
H_{{\rm exc},K}=\sum_{k=3}^K\sum_{r=1}^{2k^2}\omega_k a_{kr}^\dagger a_{kr}.
\]
For any normal input \(\rho_K\), let
\[
\gamma_K=\operatorname{Tr}_{S}(V_K\rho_K V_K^\dagger),
\qquad E_K=\operatorname{Tr}\rho_KH_{{\rm exc},K}.
\]
All source correlations and geometry/source entanglement are allowed. The inputs may depend on \(K\). Low modes and the allowed controlled low-mode unitary are retained, and do not enter this resource.

**The sharp asymptotic theorem.** If \(\gamma_K\to\gamma\) in trace norm, where \(\gamma\) is a normal geometry state and \(\operatorname{Tr}\gamma\kappa^2<\infty\), then
\[
\boxed{\liminf_{K\to\infty}\frac{E_K}{K^2}\ \ge\
 \frac{F_Q(\gamma,\kappa)}{16}.}                                      \tag{M1}
\]
No uniform second-moment bound is imposed on the approximating \(\gamma_K\). Conversely, a sequence of normal inputs with output marginal **exactly \(\gamma\) at every cutoff** attains this leading coefficient. Thus
\[
\boxed{\lim_{K\to\infty}\frac{\inf_{\rho_K:\,\gamma_K=\gamma}E_K}{K^2}
 =\frac{F_Q(\gamma,\kappa)}{16}.}                                     \tag{M2}
\]
The infimum in M2 is not asserted to have a finite-\(K\) mixed-state minimizer. Exact finite-\(K\) formulas for pure targets and disjoint-support mixtures remain those in ENDPOINT_NORMALITY. The coefficient depends on the stated two-field degeneracy and normalization.

\(F_Q\) is symmetric-logarithmic-derivative quantum Fisher information for the unitary parameter generated by \(\kappa\). Its variance/convex-roof identity is established literature; the new result on this page is the sharp resource bound and construction for this actual endpoint map, including arbitrary normal correlations and trace-convergent targets. No novelty claim is made for the Fisher-information identity itself.

This theorem is a resource statement in a specified representation. It is not a theorem about total conserved energy, renormalized apparatus work, an autonomous preparation, or a solution of the joint Einstein–CTP equations.

## M1. Actual high-mode normalization and sums

Set \(d=\kappa-\kappa_*\). Choose each oscillator's phase so that its physical coordinate is \(q=\sqrt{2X_k}\,Q\), where \([Q,P]=i\), \(a=(Q+iP)/\sqrt2\), and \(X_k=|v_k|^2\). The high endpoint factor is \(\exp(i d X_kQ^2)\). The actual incoming Jost estimate is
\[
X_k=\frac{W_k}{2\omega_k},\quad |W_k-1|\le 8\mu/\omega_k^2,\quad
0<\mu<.1,\quad \frac1{4\omega_k}\le X_k\le\frac2{\omega_k}.
\]
For a fixed lower shell \(J\), sum over every real oscillator in \(J\le k\le K\):
\[
S_K=\sum\omega X^2,\quad R_K=\sum\omega X^4,\quad
E_{0,K}=\tfrac12\sum\omega.
\]
Consequently
\[
S_K/K^2\longrightarrow\tfrac14,\qquad
R_K=O(\log K),\qquad E_{0,K}=O(K^4),\qquad \omega X\le2.              \tag{M3}
\]
All constants may depend on the fixed actual history and \(J\); no cutoff-dependent change of background is made.

## M2. Fisher information, full second moments, and an estimator bound

First restrict geometry to any fixed bounded \(\kappa\) interval. Write \(A=d\) on that Hilbert space, \(M=\max(1,\|A\|)\), and \(P_\gamma=\operatorname{supp}\gamma\). The bounded self-adjoint solution
\[
Y_\gamma=2\int_0^\infty e^{-t\gamma}\sqrt\gamma A\sqrt\gamma e^{-t\gamma}\,dt
\quad\hbox{on }P_\gamma,\qquad Y_\gamma=0\hbox{ on }\ker\gamma
\]
satisfies
\[
\gamma Y_\gamma+Y_\gamma\gamma=2\sqrt\gamma A\sqrt\gamma,
\qquad \|Y_\gamma\|\le\|A\|.
\]
For positive \(A\) the integral is an increasing strong integral bounded by
\(2\|A\|\int_0^\infty\gamma e^{-2t\gamma}dt=\|A\|P_\gamma\); positive and negative parts give the general statement. No inverse of \(\gamma\) is assumed bounded.

Define
\[
C(\gamma)=\operatorname{Tr}\gamma A^2-\operatorname{Tr}\gamma Y_\gamma^2
          =\tfrac14 F_Q(\gamma,A).                                  \tag{M4}
\]
The first term is the **full** \(A^2\) expectation. Replacing it by
\((P_\gamma A P_\gamma)^2\) would delete transitions out of the support and can give a false zero.

Here is the estimator inequality used below. If \(G(B)\) is a positive operator measure with \(G(\mathbb R)=\gamma\), then \(G(B)\le\gamma\) for every measurable set \(B\). The bounded forms on \(\operatorname{Ran}\sqrt\gamma\) give a POVM \(F(B)\) on \(P_\gamma\) such that
\(G(B)=\sqrt\gamma F(B)\sqrt\gamma\).
For every bounded real function \(b\), positivity of
\[
\int \operatorname{Tr}F(dz)(Y_\gamma-b(z))\gamma(Y_\gamma-b(z))
\]
and the Sylvester equation give
\[
\int \operatorname{Tr}G(dz)(A-b(z))^2\ \ge\ C(\gamma).               \tag{M5}
\]
One proves this first for simple functions and then by uniform approximation. This uses an operator-valued measure, not the generally false pointwise assertion \(G_z\le\gamma\). Conditional states need not be pure.

For finite second moments with unbounded \(\kappa\), write
\(\gamma=\sum_i\lambda_i|\phi_i\rangle\langle\phi_i|\), \(\lambda_i>0\). Then \(\phi_i\in D(\kappa)\), and
\[
C(\gamma)=\operatorname{Tr}\gamma\kappa^2
 -\sum_{i,j}\frac{2\lambda_i\lambda_j}{\lambda_i+\lambda_j}
       |\langle\phi_i,\kappa\phi_j\rangle|^2
 =\tfrac14F_Q(\gamma,\kappa).                                      \tag{M6}
\]
Both nonnegative sums are finite. The second is bounded by the first. Kernel contributions are retained in the full first term. Equivalently, with
\(\dot\gamma=-i[\kappa,\gamma]\in{\cal S}_1\),
\[
F_Q(\gamma,\kappa)=
 \sup_{L=L^\dagger\ {\rm bounded}}
 \{2\operatorname{Tr}(\dot\gamma L)-\operatorname{Tr}(\gamma L^2)\}.   \tag{M7}
\]
The usual SLD completion of squares proves this on finite matrix subspaces; increasing finite subspaces in the spectral decomposition, including projections of \(\kappa\phi_i\) into the kernel, gives M6–M7 by positive-series convergence. This formulation also proves lower semicontinuity when both \(\gamma\) and \(\dot\gamma\) converge in trace norm.

## M3. Energy and domains on a bounded geometry interval

Keep a band \(J\le k\le K\), with \(J\) fixed large enough that
\(4M^2X_{\max,J}^2<1\). Dropping lower-shell energy is legitimate because it is positive. On the endpoint output state \(\rho\), put
\[
H_0=\sum\omega a^\dagger a,\quad
D=\sum\omega X\{P,Q\},\quad Z=\sum\omega X^2Q^2.
\]
The exact incoming band energy is
\[
E_{\rm band}=\operatorname{Tr}\rho(H_0-AD+2A^2Z).                  \tag{M8}
\]
It equals \(\sum\omega\operatorname{Tr}\rho b^\dagger b\), where
\[
b=a-iXA(a+a^\dagger),\qquad
a=(1+iXA)b+iXA b^\dagger.
\]
The bounded controlled shear, or its direct integral over the spectrum of \(A\), identifies the oscillator form domains. Thus finite incoming band energy implies
\[
T:=\operatorname{Tr}\rho H_0
 \le (2+4\|A\|^2X_{\max,J}^2)E_{\rm band}+2\|A\|^2S_K.             \tag{M9}
\]
The estimate holds for normal mixed states by Hilbert–Schmidt/positive-form approximation, not only finite oscillator vectors.

At every finite cutoff \(D\) is the self-adjoint sum of oscillator dilation generators. On the oscillator Schwartz core
\(\|D\psi\|\le c_K\|(H_0+1)\psi\|\); closure and the square-root operator inequality give \(|D|\le c_K(H_0+1)\) as forms. Hence finite \(T\) gives an absolute first moment of \(D\). No finite \(D^2\) moment is required in what follows.

## M4. A Gaussian comparison channel, with its cost retained

This channel is a proof device, not a proposed physical measuring apparatus. Set
\[
Y=\frac{D}{2S_K},\qquad
M_z=(2\pi\delta^2)^{-1/4}
       \exp[-(Y-z)^2/(4\delta^2)],\quad \rho_z=M_z\rho M_z .
\]
It preserves the geometry marginal. Spectral calculus and Tonelli give
\[
\int\operatorname{Tr}\rho_z(Y-z)^2\,dz=\delta^2                 \tag{M10}
\]
even if \(\operatorname{Tr}\rho Y^2=\infty\). The nonselective channel is the Gaussian average of \(e^{itY}(\cdot)e^{-itY}\) with
\(\operatorname{Var}(t)=1/(4\delta^2)\).

Since \(e^{itY}Q_i e^{-itY}=e^{\omega_iX_i t/S_K}Q_i\), its dual multiplies \(Q_i^2\) and \(P_i^2\) by
\[
\lambda_i=\exp[(\omega_iX_i)^2/(2\delta^2S_K^2)].
\]
It leaves \(D\) unchanged. Put
\(\beta=\max_i(\lambda_i-1)\le\exp[2/(\delta^2S_K^2)]-1\).
For \(\bar\rho=\int\rho_z dz\),
\[
\bar T\le T+\beta(T+E_{0,K}),
\]
\[
0\le\bar E_{\rm band}-E_{\rm band}
 \le\beta(1+4M^2X_{\max,J}^2)(T+E_{0,K}).                         \tag{M11}
\]
These are finite quadratic-form integrals of controlled dilations. Gaussian exponential integrability makes the post-channel \(H_0\) energy finite and the conditional energies finite almost everywhere. In particular, M11 does not silently discard measurement disturbance.

## M5. Scalar stability, clipping, and the conditional Fisher bound

For any real \(|b|\le M\), the exact scalar oscillator frequency is
\(\sqrt{1-4b^2X^2}\). Its ground energy and the square-root remainder give
\[
H_0-bD+b^2S_K\ge-4M^4R_K.                                       \tag{M12}
\]
Define \(b(z)=\max(-M,\min(z,M))\). The identity
\[
H_0-AD+S_KA^2
 =(H_0-bD+b^2S_K)+S_K(A-b)^2+(b-A)(D-2S_Kb)
\]
has the correct sign of every cross term. In the last term use
\(D-2S_Kb=2S_K[(Y-z)+(z-b)]\). The clipped-tail contribution
\((b-A)(z-b)\) is a positive operator. M10 and Cauchy–Schwarz bound the integrated remaining contribution below by \(-4MS_K\delta\).

The conditional geometry measure \(G(dz)=\operatorname{Tr}_S\rho_z dz\) has total \(\gamma\). Inserting M5 therefore gives
\[
\operatorname{Tr}\bar\rho(H_0-AD+S_KA^2)
 \ge S_K C(\gamma)-4MS_K\delta-4M^4R_K.                            \tag{M13}
\]
This step covers arbitrary entangled and mixed conditional states.

The replacement of \(2A^2Z\) by \(S_KA^2\) also has a controlled cost. Let \(b_0=\operatorname{Tr}\gamma A^2\le M^2\),
\(t_i=\operatorname{Tr}\bar\rho A^2N_i\). Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz yields
\[
|\operatorname{Tr}\bar\rho A^2a_i^2|
 \le\sqrt{t_i(t_i+b_0)}
 \le t_i+\sqrt{b_0t_i}.
\]
Retain the positive \(t_i\) from \(Q_i^2=N_i+\tfrac12+
(a_i^2+a_i^{\dagger2})/2\). Then
\[
2\operatorname{Tr}\bar\rho A^2Z-S_Kb_0
 \ge-2\sqrt{b_0M^2\bar T R_K}.                                   \tag{M14}
\]
A bound which dropped that number term would introduce an avoidable order-\(K^2\) error.

Combining M11–M14 gives the concrete lower estimate
\[
\begin{split}
E_K\ge{}&S_K C(\gamma)-4MS_K\delta-4M^4R_K
 -2\sqrt{b_0M^2\bar T R_K}\\
&-\beta(1+4M^2X_{\max,J}^2)(T+E_{0,K}).                            \tag{M15}
\end{split}
\]
The same estimate applies to a different target at each \(K\), with all constants uniform on the fixed bounded geometry interval.

## M6. Uniform asymptotics and trace-convergent bounded targets

Work on any subsequence where \(E_K/K^2\) is bounded; an infinite lower limit needs no proof. M9 gives \(T=O(K^2)\). Choose
\(\delta=K^{-1/4}\). Then
\[
\beta=O(K^{-7/2}),\quad \bar T=O(K^2).
\]
The four errors in M15 are respectively
\(O(K^{7/4}),O(\log K),O(K\sqrt{\log K}),O(K^{1/2})\), all \(o(K^2)\).
Their constants depend on the interval, history and energy-ratio bound, but not target rank, smallest eigenvalue, or choice of source correlations.

Thus \(E_K\ge S_K C(\gamma_K)-o(K^2)\) uniformly for bounded-interval targets. If \(\gamma_K\to\gamma\) in trace norm there, bounded \(A\) implies
\(-i[A,\gamma_K]\to-i[A,\gamma]\) in trace norm. M7 gives lower semicontinuity of \(C\), proving M1 on the interval. This does not require continuity of the optimizer or a uniform inverse of \(\gamma_K\).

## M7. General normal targets and approximants without moment bounds

Let \(Q_R=1_{[1/R,R]}(\kappa)\), distinct from the support projection \(P_\gamma\). This spectral projection strongly commutes with the endpoint map and with \(H_{{\rm exc},K}\). If
\[
p_{K,R}=\operatorname{Tr}\gamma_KQ_R>0,\qquad
\gamma_{K,R}=Q_R\gamma_KQ_R/p_{K,R},
\]
the corresponding normal compressed input has energy \(E_{K,R}\), and positivity gives
\[
E_K\ge p_{K,R}E_{K,R}.                                           \tag{M16}
\]
For each fixed \(R\) with \(p_R=\operatorname{Tr}\gamma Q_R>0\), trace convergence gives \(p_{K,R}\to p_R\) and
\(\gamma_{K,R}\to\gamma_R=Q_R\gamma Q_R/p_R\). On an energy-bounded subsequence the compressed energy ratio is bounded. M6 implies
\[
\liminf_K E_K/K^2\ge p_R C(\gamma_R)/4 .
\]
Finite \(\operatorname{Tr}\gamma\kappa^2\) makes
\(\kappa\gamma=(\kappa\sqrt\gamma)\sqrt\gamma\) trace class. By Hilbert–Schmidt approximation and \(Q_R\kappa=\kappa Q_R\),
\[
\gamma_R\to\gamma,\qquad
-i[\kappa,\gamma_R]\to-i[\kappa,\gamma]
\quad\hbox{in }{\cal S}_1.
\]
M7 implies \(C(\gamma)\le\liminf_R C(\gamma_R)\).
Extending any interval SLD test by \(Q_RLQ_R\) also gives
\(p_RC(\gamma_R)\le C(\gamma)\); the same argument for nested intervals makes this quantity increasing. Since
\(C(\gamma_R)\le\operatorname{Tr}\gamma\kappa^2/p_R\) and \(p_R\to1\),
\[
p_R C(\gamma_R)\uparrow C(\gamma).
\]
Taking \(R\to\infty\) after the fixed-\(R\) lower bound proves M1. No exchange of the cutoff and interval limits, or moment assumption on the \(\gamma_K\), is used.

For the present \(\kappa\) multiplication operator on \(L^2(\mathbb R_+)\),
\(F_Q(\gamma,\kappa)>0\) for every such normal trace-one target. Indeed zero Fisher information would force \([\kappa,\gamma]=0\) by M7. Finite second moments then give strong commutation with \(\kappa\). A positive eigenvalue of the nonzero compact operator \(\gamma\) would supply a nonzero finite-dimensional reducing subspace of \(\kappa\), and hence a normal eigenvector of multiplication by \(\kappa\), which has no point spectrum. There is no target-independent positive constant.

## M8. Matching upper construction with the target held exactly fixed

For a finite-rank target whose support lies in \(D(\kappa)\), define the finite matrix
\[
(Y_\gamma)_{ij}=
 \frac{2\sqrt{\lambda_i\lambda_j}}{\lambda_i+\lambda_j}
 \langle\phi_i,(\kappa-\kappa_*)\phi_j\rangle
\]
on that support, and diagonalize it. This does not claim a bounded Sylvester operator on the full unbounded-\(\kappa\) space. If its eigenvectors are \(y_j\), define
\(p_j=\langle y_j,\gamma y_j\rangle\) and
\(\psi_j=\sqrt\gamma y_j/\sqrt{p_j}\) when \(p_j>0\).
The Sylvester identity gives
\[
\gamma=\sum_jp_j|\psi_j\rangle\langle\psi_j|,\qquad
\sum_jp_j\operatorname{Var}_{\psi_j}\kappa=C(\gamma).               \tag{M17}
\]
The full second-moment form is retained even when the finite-rank support is not invariant under \(\kappa\).

For an arbitrary normal \(\gamma\) with finite second moment, use a finite spectral prefix
\(s_n=\sum_{i\le n}\lambda_i\),
\(\tau_n=s_n^{-1}\sum_{i\le n}\lambda_i|\phi_i\rangle\langle\phi_i|\).
Take its ensemble M17 with total weight \(s_n\), followed by the original countable spectral tail. This is an exact ensemble of \(\gamma\), with average variance
\[
V_n=s_n C(\tau_n)+\sum_{i>n}\lambda_i\operatorname{Var}_{\phi_i}\kappa
 \longrightarrow C(\gamma).                                    \tag{M18}
\]
To see convergence, \(s_n C(\tau_n)\) is the difference of the two increasing finite positive sums in M6 restricted to \(i,j\le n\). Each sum converges, and the tail second moment tends to zero.

For each pure ensemble member of variance \(v\), use the finite-\(K\) optimal Gaussian source preparation already derived in ENDPOINT_NORMALITY:
\[
{\cal E}_K(v)=\sum_{k=3}^K k^2\omega_k
          \big(\sqrt{1+4vX_k^2}-1\big),\qquad
{\cal E}_K(v)/K^2\to v/4 .
\]
The exact inequality
\[
0\le{\cal E}_K(v)\le v S_{{\rm full},K}                           \tag{M19}
\]
permits dominated convergence for the countable ensemble using only its finite average variance, not a fourth moment or a bounded set of component variances. A normal density mixture of these pure optimizers keeps the geometry output exactly \(\gamma\). For fixed \(n\) its cost/K² tends to \(V_n/4\). Then \(n\to\infty\), or a diagonal choice \(n(K)\), yields the upper half of M2.

In fact it has no trace-norm convergent incoming subsequence in the common incoming Fock embedding: ENDPOINT_NORMALITY E3 proves that any such subsequence is uniformly vacuum-tail tight and its geometry output would escape every fixed normal target. Its full source output need not converge either. A different representation or co-transported algebra requires a separate construction; the resource-infimum argument does not supply full-field dynamics.

## M9. An actual coherence-dependent preparation cost

For the disjoint central/far packets of NEAR_RECOVERY, let \(m=(1-\delta)h\),
\(w=\delta h/(R-m)\), and normalized packets \(\psi_c,\psi_R\). When comparing the original initial local geometry readings, include its common phase \(e^{i b_{\rm geo}\kappa^2/2}\) in both packets; a constant relative phase alone does not replace that preparation. Hold the chosen finite-cutoff packet, root and phase fixed in the resource limit. This phase commutes with \(\kappa\), so all formulas below are unchanged. Their within-packet variances are
\[
v_c=\mu_2\delta(1-\delta)^2h^2,\qquad v_R=\mu_2R^2/100.
\]
Consider the actual normal geometry family, with a fixed phase \(\theta\),
\[
\gamma_\chi=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-w&\chi e^{i\theta}\sqrt{w(1-w)}\\
\chi e^{-i\theta}\sqrt{w(1-w)}&w
\end{pmatrix}_{\psi_c,\psi_R},
\qquad 0\le\chi\le1 .
\]
Every member has the same full \(\kappa\) probability law, mean and variance. Nevertheless, retaining leakage out of this two-packet span gives
\[
\frac14F_Q(\gamma_\chi,\kappa)
 =v_{\rm within}+\chi^2v_{\rm between},
\]
\[
v_{\rm within}=(1-w)v_c+w v_R,\qquad
v_{\rm between}=w(1-w)(R-m)^2=\delta h(R-h).                       \tag{M20}
\]
Hence the sharp coefficient in M1–M2 is
\[
\frac{v_{\rm within}+\chi^2v_{\rm between}}4 .
\]
The rank-two formula for \(\chi<1\) and pure-target variance at \(\chi=1\) agree. The actual near-recovery root also implies
\(v_{\rm within}>\mu_2/(500h)\); dephasing does not make the resource zero.

For this family
\[
D_{\rm tr}(\gamma_\chi,\gamma_\xi)
 =|\chi-\xi|\sqrt{w(1-w)}.
\]
Small trace distance can coexist with a large change in the coefficient when the packet separation is large; the sharp lower bound concerns a fixed limiting target, not a uniform continuity claim over unbounded geometry supports. Disjoint support also makes all legitimate finite initial local differential jets insensitive to this coherence, but does not identify their later joint histories. In particular, the inverse-dressed source preparation attaining the resource coefficient is not the old source preparation and need not preserve its momentum or constraint budget.

## M10. Closure and next physical gate

This closes the general mixed-target leading-order gap left open by the earlier leakage bound, and extends the result to arbitrary normal finite-second-moment targets and trace-convergent approximants. ENDPOINT_NORMALITY's nonregular fixed-observable limit and subquadratic-resource exclusion remain compatible with it.

The construction changes the old complete bare source, does not impose its previous mean/pressure/constraint budget, and does not prepare the actual local apparatus. Generic normal targets need not lie in the raw \(Q_3\) joint-dynamics domain. A controlled endpoint re-expression must also transport observables; this resource theorem for a fixed partition does not turn a passive representation change into physical decoherence.

The next physical acceptance must specify a common full-field state/algebra and dynamics, count the source, controller and interaction stress in one conserved budget, and realize the preparation by the original allowed dynamics. Fisher information quantifies one necessary endpoint resource; it does not supply those missing objects.
