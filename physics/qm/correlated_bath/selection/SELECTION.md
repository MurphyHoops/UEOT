# Which finite operational sectors survive continuous reversible control?

Research note A · 18 November 2026 · pinned UEOT candidate `44b6cd200cd01eccf15d4dc817311388d317a1af`

**Decision.** If every pair of pure states of every declared system can be joined by allowed continuously reversible controls, nontrivial classical simplexes and standard direct-sum superselection systems are excluded. Real and complex matrix systems both survive. Even adding local tomography on every composite leaves a theory with only separable preparations and imperfect measurements. On a reachable face alone, the principle leaves chapter 70's hidden completion exactly observationally equivalent. The global principle is an independent sector postulate **S** relative to the background below. This adjudication removes **zero** independently supplied premises from a reconstruction of complex QM.

## What is quantified

A system has a finite-dimensional real ordered span, a compact convex base of normalized states Ω, affine effect probabilities in [0,1], and a unique deterministic effect u. Keep the allowed preparations, instruments, reversible channels, terminal tests and composite rule as separate data. Allow finite circuits, mixtures, coarse-graining, discarding and conditioning on recorded outcomes. Outcome labels are classical control data. If classical registers themselves are admitted as systems in the universal quantifier, any nontrivial classical register also has to pass the principle.

States are already identified by their probabilities for the declared future tests. Pure means extreme in the specified normalized convex state space. A reversible control is an allowed deterministic affine transformation with an allowed two-sided inverse. In a composite theory it must be a legal channel with spectators. Use the usual finite-dimensional norm topology on the operational state span and the operator norm topology on transformations (uniform convergence on the compact state base is equivalent here). A physical control family requires a permitted continuous implementation, not just a collection of individually admissible endpoints. We specify such implementations in each positive model.

* **Ambient statement.** For every declared system A, including each declared composite, and every p,q in ext Ω(A), there exists a permitted continuous path g from [0,1] into its reversible group, with g(0) the identity and g(1)p=q. Every g(t) preserves pure states since its affine inverse pulls any decomposition back to one of p. The orbit of the path component of the identity must contain every pure state.
* **Reachable statement.** Fix the preparation and control library in advance. If its normalized states form an invariant face F, use ext F and those allowed reversible controls that preserve F with allowed inverses there. The topology is that of the reachable response space. A face satisfies ext F = F ∩ ext Ω. A general convex accessible subset or a quotient need not have this property. For example, a singleton containing a mixed state makes that state extreme relative to the singleton. Defining a “sector” as one orbit makes transitivity on it tautological. Independent preparation access is essential.
* **What a finite device can establish.** Independent control and leakage calibrations can support transfers and inverses for a named menu of preparations at a declared accuracy. Even dense endpoints of finite gate words do not supply a continuous family of allowed controls. Neither an untouched reference nor the fixed post-pass rotations in the pinned source grant arbitrary SU(2) control. Full coverage needs independent input about the carrier, generators, path implementation, errors and measurement frame. No such device evidence was supplied here. The candidate has a direct precedent in Hardy's continuity axiom. We adopt the norm topology and the allowed path as explicit model ingredients. The precedent supplies a mathematical selection proposal, independent of this bath experiment. [Hardy, *Quantum Theory From Five Reasonable Axioms*, §7, Axiom 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0101012#page=15)

## Direct sums, including a quantitative obstruction

Take a finite direct sum of complex matrix blocks. A state is a tuple of positive matrices ρᵢ with Σ tr ρᵢ=1. A pure state occupies exactly one block and has rank one there. Two nonzero block weights give a convex decomposition by central projections, and rank above one gives a spectral decomposition within a block. Conversely a normalized rank-one state in one block is extreme by positivity and extremality within its block.

The continuous affine coordinate wᵢ(ρ)=tr ρᵢ takes only 0 or 1 on pure states. Along a reversible path starting from block i it therefore stays 1. With at least two nonzero blocks the ambient principle fails. The proof uses only purity preservation and continuity of the coordinate in the declared topology. In particular, even if all blocks are identical and discrete block permutations plus unitaries give ordinary transitivity, the continuous condition fails. A finite classical simplex is the scalar-block case. Its reversible permutations are transitive, its pure paths constant. The one-state simplex passes vacuously.

With full central readout there is a quantitative obstruction. Along **any** continuous normalized-state path between different pure blocks, the starting block weight passes through 1/2. That intermediate state has operational distance at least 1/2 from **every** ambient pure state, since their central probabilities are either 0 or 1. Here distance is the supremum of absolute one-event probability differences; it equals half trace norm in the full matrix effects model. A binary noisy central detector with known contrast κ changes this certified lower bound to κ/2. If the endpoints have independently certified weights on opposite sides of 1/2 and w is L-Lipschitz in the path parameter, a uniform mesh with maximum gap h has a sampled point within Lh/2 in weight of 1/2. Its central-effect distance to all pure states is at least κ max(0,1/2−Lh/2), before subtracting estimation and calibration errors. Unknown central effects, purity, leakage or control continuity invalidate a device-level exclusion. A path of arbitrary stochastic preparations can cross perfectly well by becoming mixed.

### Same visible circuit, different global verdict

Let the visible system be a full complex qubit. Compare it with M₂(ℂ) ⊕ ℂ, with the ordinary finite-dimensional algebra tensor product. In the completion prepare (ρ,0), and extend each instrument branch Φᵧ by (σ,h) ↦ (Φᵧσ,wᵧh), wᵧ ≥ 0, Σwᵧ=1. An effect E extends to (E,e), 0≤e≤1. Unitary control extends by acting identically on the hidden block. The face h=0 has continuous transitivity if its library includes the full continuous unitary controls. The uncompleted qubit passes the ambient test, the completion fails it.

For any finite visible reference R the second block becomes an R matrix, multiplied by wᵧ. Its initial zero persists separately on every branch. The visible R block follows Φᵧ ⊗ id. Induct on the length of the record, including early stops, conditionals, parallel visible systems and sums of coarse-grained branches. Every complete reachable response is identical in the two theories for every allowed finite circuit. A longer record or a more accurate estimate of those responses leaves this equality exact. If one takes the reachable response quotient, both libraries give the same operational object. The global question asks about a larger preparation and system quantifier.

Allow any auxiliary finite direct sum of matrix algebras. Its tensor product has blocks labelled by the pair of central indices. A pure joint state lies in just one such block. Its marginal keeps the definite left index, so (ρ/2,1/2) has no pure dilation for any such auxiliary. Nevertheless products of local effects span the Hermitians of this composite block by block, including the central selectors. Local tomography therefore holds for these direct sums with full effects. The tensor and auxiliary rules matter to the purification argument. ## Real, complex and restricted alternatives

| Declared systems and operations | Continuous transitivity | Local product tomography | Purification existence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full finite classical stochastic theory, nontrivial simplex | Fails | Holds | Fails for mixed states |
| Finite direct sums with full complex matrix operations and ordinary algebra tensor | Fails on multiple blocks, holds within each full matrix block | Holds | Fails across occupied blocks |
| Full real matrices with real Hilbert tensor | Holds on every full real system | Fails already on two rebits | Holds by real spectral decomposition |
| Full complex matrices with complex Hilbert tensor and standard instruments | Holds on every full complex system | Holds | Holds by the Schmidt construction |
| Noisy qubits with separable composites and the library below | Holds on every declared qubit register | Holds, with visibility-dependent conditioning | Fails already for the maximally mixed qubit |

**Positive control.** A real pure state is a line. For d≥2 a plane rotation takes one unit representative to a representative of the target, using the identity path when the lines coincide. Vary its angle from zero with the orthogonal complement fixed. This lies in SO(d) and gives a reversible real channel at every time. In complex dimension d, extend the two rays to orthonormal bases, map one basis to the other by U, diagonalize U and interpolate its eigenphases from zero. Conjugation yields a permitted unitary path in each full model. Dimension one is immediate. The same proof works on the full real or complex tensor-product Hilbert space if global controls are permitted. With only local unitaries on the standard complex composite, a product ray cannot reach a Bell ray because its Schmidt rank stays one. Thus the usual composite state space alone does not guarantee the control assumption.

**Real tomography witness.** Put J=[[0,−1],[1,0]] and W=J⊗J. W is real symmetric, W²=I₄ and tr W=0, so ρ±=(I₄±W)/4 are positive trace-one states. For any real symmetric effects A,B, tr(AJ)=tr(BJ)=0. All product probabilities agree since tr[(A⊗B)W]=0. The legal real joint effect (I₄+W)/2 gives probabilities 1 and 0. The unnormalized real composite span has dimension 10; the two local symmetric spans give only 3×3=9 directions. In standard complex theory local Hermitians span d² dimensions and their products span (d₁d₂)². This tests the **standard real tensor** and permitted effects. It supplies no general exclusion of every possible real encoding with modified composites or additional nonlocal resources.

**An effect restriction stable under the declared circuits.** Elementary states are all qubit densities. Allow all local unitaries and all independent preparations. The terminal primitive is a destructive binary measurement in any Bloch direction n with effects
E±(n)=(I±ν n·σ)/2, for fixed 0<ν<1. Include trivial tests, classical randomization, relabelling and coarse-graining. Composites of any finite number of qubits have the convex hull of pure product states. Generate circuits by independent local controls, wire permutations, preparations, destructive tests, discards and classical feed-forward; there is no coupling gate. This is a causal family closed under the declared sequential and parallel composition. The classical outcomes are external records. Every extreme joint state is a pure product: finite convex decompositions into product rays exist by definition and finite-dimensional compactness; each product ray is already extreme in the full matrix state space. Local unitaries therefore give continuous transitivity even on every such composite.

For one unknown input, an arbitrary finite circuit in this library can rotate and route the input repeatedly. Discarding it or measuring it destructively ends access to that copy. Earlier record probabilities are independent of that input. Later processing is a stochastic function of the observed result and independent resources. Thus the single-copy binary effect is in the convex hull of 0, I and the noisy effects. The best one-event probability gap for Bloch vectors r,s is exactly ν‖r−s‖₂/2, attained along r−s. No pair can be discriminated without error. At ν=1/2 opposite poles give 3/4 and 1/4 and equal-prior minimum error 1/4. The single-copy span still contains I,X,Y,Z, so product effects separate all allowed composite states. Each k-body Pauli correlation read via independent noisy signs is scaled by νᵏ. Infinite-data identifiability alone gives no well-conditioned finite tomography.

For any allowed auxiliary register, a pure joint product has a pure first marginal. I/2 therefore lacks a purification. This example simultaneously satisfies the proposed principle on every register and product tomography and violates both CDP perfect distinguishability and purification. Setting ν=1 removes the readout obstruction while leaving the purification counterexample. Operationally, a trusted Bell preparation lies outside this composite: its projector has expectation at most 1/2 on any separable two-qubit state (Cauchy–Schwarz on each pure product, then convexity), versus 1 on the Bell state. Product tomography can infer this linear witness with sufficient precision even when direct Bell readout is unavailable.

**When readout really collapses the quotient.** If only diagonal effects, diagonal phase controls, arbitrary preparations and discarding are allowed on one qubit, the reachable single-copy response is just the diagonal probability p. The quotient is the interval [0,1]; all equatorial rays and I/2 have p=1/2. The two extreme endpoint states admit no continuous reversible transfer on this interval. Adding arbitrary SU(2) pre-rotations would expose the other coordinates through the very same diagonal detector. Effect restrictions must be closed under permitted precomposition. Purity and transitivity computed before a non-separating quotient can give a different answer.

## Adapting the CDP reconstruction

Chiribella, D’Ariano and Perinotti (CDP) start with an operational-probabilistic theory of typed circuits and tests. Their derivation uses five axioms and one postulate in that framework. The following table maps their logical obligations to this assignment. [CDP, *Informational derivation of Quantum Theory*, §§II–III](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.6451#page=4)

| CDP premise | Obligation on the declared system family | Disposition here |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Causality | Later choice of observation cannot alter earlier preparation probabilities. Each system has a unique deterministic effect. | Included in the background. Normalization of each complete instrument realizes it in the examples. |
| 2. Perfect distinguishability | For each state outside the relative interior, some other state can be separated from it with certainty by a legal test. | Full matrix models use a kernel projector. The noisy model has distinct interior-valued responses even for opposite pure states. This is stronger than simple separation of states. |
| 3. Ideal compression | For each state, encode its entire generated face, recover each member by decoding, and use the entire normalized state space of the encoding system. | Full finite matrix families use the support subspace and a system of its rank. A quotient of response functions supplies neither the physical encoding/decoding nor availability of the target. Remains an obligation. |
| 4. Local distinguishability | Each distinct pair of bipartite states differs on some allowed product effect. | Standard real composite fails; standard complex, direct-sum complex and noisy separable composites pass. The latter two show how little separation alone decides. |
| 5. Pure conditioning | An atomic observation on one side of a pure joint preparation leaves a pure conditional state on the other for each positive-probability outcome. | For full quantum effects a rank-one contraction leaves one vector. In a changed library atomicity must be defined by its allowed refinements. A stopped outcome or ordinary conditioning alone gives no such guarantee. |
| Postulate 1. Purification | Every normalized state has a pure bipartite extension. For a fixed purifying system any two such extensions of the same state are connected by an allowed reversible operation on that system alone. | Requires specified composites, discarding and transformations. Ordinary real and complex models implement it with orthogonal/unitary freedom. Classical, cross-block mixed, and separable-only mixed states already fail existence; uniqueness cannot remedy that. |

CDP Lemma 9 obtains ordinary reversible transitivity from uniqueness in purification. Its conclusion specifies a reversible endpoint. Continuity of an implementing path is an additional requirement at that step. A classical bit has the endpoint property and lacks both that path and purification. The full reconstructed complex theory does admit unitary paths. Invoking the reconstruction to justify those paths as its starting premise would be circular. [CDP, §IV.B, Lemma 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.6451#page=12)
