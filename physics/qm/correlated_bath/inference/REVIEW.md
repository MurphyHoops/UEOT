## Which operational quantifiers survive

1. **Preparation and hidden completion.** In M₂(ℂ) ⊕ ℂ take a visible pure preparation diag(1,0,0) and a hidden pure preparation diag(0,0,1). The central effect diag(1,1,0) takes values 1 and 0 respectively. On an ambient pure-state path this continuous scalar must always be either 0 or 1. A path between those preparations would attain 1/2. The midpoint mixture explicitly does, and has rank two. A binary central detector with calibrated contrast κ scales this probability gap by κ. Our four SR testers supply no calibrated central effect or independently prepared hidden endpoint.
2. **Same experiment after completion.** Extend each accessible branch of the persistent process by a separate hidden branch on the additional central sector. For a visible preparation, the full initial hidden block on bath and reference is zero. Each local block-preserving propagator, causal repreparation, instrument branch and terminal tester maps zero to zero on that sector. Induction gives (σᵣ,0) separately for every stopped record r, before the tester and for arbitrary spectator dimension. One can independently give the hidden sector any normalized outcome kernel. Both the calibration and every held-out distribution coincide. If access to the extra preparation and central detector is granted, their one-event gap is one. This checks the exact point at which changing the allowed preparation and test library changes the verdict.
3. **Continuous pure outputs and inverse controls.** A continuously tunable discard-and-prepare channel ρ ↦ tr(ρ)|ψₜ⟩⟨ψₜ| can trace a path of pure outputs. At the intermediate ray (3,4)/5 it maps two orthogonal inputs to exactly the same density operator. It has no left inverse. Our break uses exactly this kind of irreversible operation on S. Neither a pure output nor the signed bath response supplies the continuous group action A requires. Even a countable dense set of implementable gate words cannot contain a nonconstant continuous path in the finite-dimensional matrix topology. A real linear functional separating its endpoints would map that path onto an interval inside a countable image.
4. **Finite tests and the operational quotient.** The two valid terminal states (I ⊗ I ± X ⊗ I/2)/4 have eigenvalues 1/8 and 3/8, each twice. All four measured product expectations XX, XY, YZ and ZZ agree. Adding a test of the +1 eigenspace of XI gives an exact probability gap of 1/2. This is a counterexample to unrestricted terminal tomography from the four testers. It concerns a fixed terminal menu; pre-rotations or an expanded reference library change the effect span. It places no restriction on a channel bound independently obtained from an explicit Hamiltonian and Choi enclosure.
5. **Composites and classical labels.** SO(d) plane rotations on real rays already satisfy the single-system path premise. Orthogonal conjugations on the *full* real composite give the corresponding positive composite example. Product-only controls on ordinary two-qubit complexes preserve Schmidt rank and cannot join a product to a Bell ray. In the separable-only composite all extreme preparations are products and local unitaries can connect them. The qubit, its permitted composite types, and a classical output register therefore require separate quantifiers. Treating external outcome labels as additional classical physical systems would itself violate the universal path premise for a nontrivial register.
6. **Reconstruction.** CDP Lemma 9 obtains a reversible endpoint from uniqueness of purification on the chosen system family. The statement does not assert a continuously implementable family starting at the identity. A discrete classical bit swap meets that endpoint requirement for its two pure states. Central-sector and rebit counterexamples check distinct obligations in A's table. The actual experiment has no operational test of universal purification, ideal compression or unrestricted composite control. [CDP, §IV.B, Lemma 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.6451#page=12)

## A separable competitor the available data can test

Let σ_f be the subnormalized first-failure state, t = tr σ_f and Mⱼⱼ = tr[(j⊗j)σ_f]. For any product of two qubit states, rₓsₓ + r_z s_z ≤ 1 by Cauchy–Schwarz and the Bloch-ball bound. By convexity and homogeneity, Mₓₓ + Mzz ≤ t for any separable σ_f. With the common binary readout visibility η ≤ 1, the observed-law contrast

    H = P(f,+ | XX) − P(f,− | XX) − 2P(f,− | ZZ)
      = η(Mₓₓ + Mzz) − t

is therefore nonpositive for the ideal separable competitor. This uses a common first-failure state independent of the terminal tester, trusted Pauli axes/product interpretation, and the specified symmetric readout law. The same contrast applies to the noisy separable model when the two measured product moments have the common attenuation for the named axes. Arbitrary correlated, setting-dependent detectors require a different bound.
