`η₀(u) = p₀(u)` on `K`. The initial safe row is not renormalized.

The promoted chain constructs or proves the following identities from these

### Boundaries carried by the statements

Time zero is included. In particular, initial mass outside `K` contributes to
failure before any transition. An empty safe set and zero survival remain
valid cases for the unconditioned identities. A conditioned PMF requires
positive survival; there is no invented conditional law at a zero-probability
conditioning event. QSD consequences retain their actual eigenvector and
normalization assumptions.

The exit-flux telescope reuses
`SameSourceConditionedTerminalCheck.actual_survival_mass_at_zero` to identify
`s₀ = p₀(K)`. It introduces no duplicate time-zero theorem. Its final event
identity is

`Pr(failure by T) = p₀(Kᶜ) + ∑ₙ<T Pr(first exit at n + 1)`.

Each positive-time first-exit event requires safety through time `n` and
failure at `n + 1`, so an initially unsafe path contributes only to the
initial term. Each summand uses the coherent prefix of the same original
policy-driven experiment. The telescope therefore also covers `T = 0`.

The tail limit may be zero. Positive survival at every finite time does not
entail positive probability of surviving forever. Conditional-terminal QSD
stability does not remove this distinction.

The source feedback in this chain is time-homogeneous and depends on the
current observation. The next section constructs a more general causal
policy, but does not identify that policy with this feedback or with this
fixed killed matrix. Such an identification needs its own realization
theorem.

The action-erasure identity retains the complete state history. Transporting
an event through it requires the event to depend on that retained history;
it does not identify action-dependent events with state-only events or
assert that the observed process is Markov.

## 2. Finite-horizon existence yields one all-times causal policy

Fix the **entire** source data before quantifying the horizon: `p₀`, `q`, the
possibly time-dependent PMF transitions `P n x a`, and the possibly
time-dependent row certification predicate `certified n x a`. Let
`Uₙ(π)` be the real probability of an uncertified state/action row in the
actual deployment row law driven by `π`.

For finite state and action types with measurable singletons, and a supplied
complete causal fallback policy, the principal new statement is

`(∀ T, ∃ πT, ∀ n < T, Uₙ(πT) = 0) ↔ (∃ π∞, ∀ n, Uₙ(π∞) = 0)`.

The witnesses on the left need not be consistent. The proof does not exchange
quantifiers by assumption. It constructs a new witness from finite-action
compactness expressed as nested continuation choices.

For a support `S` at time `n`, `InfiniteViable` means that **every** finite
remaining horizon is viable. Actions that certify the current support and
leave every nonempty observation successor viable for `k` further steps form
a decreasing family of nonempty subsets of the finite action type. Their
intersection is nonempty. A selected action therefore preserves
`InfiniteViable` at every possible observed successor, independently of a
requested horizon.

`infiniteViablePolicy` makes this choice at viable histories and retains a
supplied complete causal fallback at all other histories. Its supported
histories are proved to preserve the same invariant. The zero-occupancy
theorem then applies to **this policy's own** actual row law. Existing finite
support viability theorems supply the reverse implication and connect the
finite witnesses to the initial supports.

This construction permits fixed nonautonomous transition and certification
sequences and arbitrary observation types. It is a noncomputable existence
construction in the current Lean implementation. It is not yet the finite
support-memory fixed-point algorithm from the earlier written contract, and
it does not assert prefix identity with the old horizon-dependent
`viablePolicy`.

In particular, the selected action may depend on time as well as the current
support; the construction supplies no bound on the memory needed to realize
that dependence. The homogeneous support fixed point, its finite-memory
implementation, and its controller-to-source identity remain separate
proof obligations. The present theorem establishes the displayed quantifier
strengthening and the resulting policy's actual all-times occupancy.

Zero uncertified occupancy becomes physical safety only after adding initial
safety and genuine support-preserving certified transitions. Likewise,
agreement on source-supported histories does not alter the fallback or
checking budget of a different reference process that may visit additional
histories.

## 3. Rowwise scalar experiments feed the existing SISC interfaces

Let `J` be a finite probe family and fix each `Nⱼ > 0` before sampling. Under
one probability measure `μ`, the measurable sample variables in row `j`
are independent and each has the actual marginal `(row j).toMeasure`.
The measurable read for each probe lies in `[0,1]`. Define a common event
`G` on which every row's empirical mean differs from its own population
mean by at most `u ≥ 0`.

`RowwiseProbeSampling.coordinateGood_measurable` proves that `G` is
measurable. Scalar Hoeffding per row and a union bound give the real-valued
probability bound

`μ.real(Gᶜ) ≤ min(1, ∑ⱼ 2 exp(−2 Nⱼ u²))`.

The general bound does not require independence across rows. The explicit
product experiment constructs its actual coordinate laws and independence
on the sample space `(Σ j, Fin (N j)) → X`; the sample counts are not silently
replaced by one common count. Empty probe families are handled as a boundary,
while the N3 consumer retains the original protocol's `observed_nonempty`
requirement.

### The model/source matching obligation

Concentration around a sampling law's own mean is insufficient for model
identification. The adapter therefore requires every population coordinate
to match the read from the specified `kernel / source / action` data of the
registered model. It also proves that the candidate prediction table is the
same table used by the original coordinate risk. Those identities connect
the population risk to the existing SISC model risk; they are not inferred
from concentration alone.

On the same `G`, the deterministic Lipschitz bound controls the empirical
risk of every candidate simultaneously. The existing risk is the **sum** of
absolute coordinate discrepancies, so the constructed N3 interval has

`statisticalRadius = r = (Fintype.card J : ℝ) * u`,

and `driftRadius = 0`. Its radius is not `u` unless the probe count makes
those expressions equal. The registered kernel, source, action, prediction
table, causal gate, tolerance, and nonempty-probe field remain those of the
original protocol.

The existing N4 separation theorem then supplies N3 resolver correctness
for a registered causal candidate `c` under the explicit margins

`risk(c) + 2r ≤ tolerance`,

`tolerance + 2r < risk(d)` for every other registered causal candidate `d`.

The interval constructor and resolver take the observations and public
protocol; the unknown correct candidate occurs only in the correctness
statement. Coordinate accuracy on `G` gives the risk bound for all
candidates, including when the ambient candidate type is infinite and the
registered family is finite. There is no additional union bound over
candidates. Numerical informativeness still depends on the actual scalar
budget and these separation margins.

### N6 and the measurable-event boundary

The N6 audit adapter has its own population-matching premise: the sampled
coordinate means must equal the specified parent's registered reference
responses. N3 kernel matching does not supply that premise automatically.
It also retains registration, externally supplied transfer authentication,
and `LocalResponseGap` over the **entire parent type**: for every distinct
parent there must be a probe whose response gap exceeds `2u`. This
quantifier is not reduced to the registered competitors.

The audit constructor uses the empirical response vector without taking the
correct parent as an input. Although its structure supplies that vector as
an observed-response function for all children, each recovery theorem
concerns the specified child with its own transfer and model-matching
premises. It does not establish that other children share the experiment.
Separate treatment groups are not thereby identified with a joint
counterfactual record for the same physical unit.

The two failure endpoints use `measureReal_mono`: resolver failure or audit
failure is a subset of the measurable complement `Gᶜ`. They therefore bound
`μ.real` of the failure set as a real-valued outer measure. The current API
does **not** separately export a `MeasurableSet` theorem for either failure
set or a measurability certificate for the resolver. The explicitly
measurable `G`, success on every point of `G`, and the bound on `Gᶜ` are
proved; no additional failure-event measurability result is counted here.

### A nonempty finite product experiment and a wrong-kernel boundary

`SISCRowwiseProbeWitness` instantiates the constructors with two probes, two
registered candidates, and unequal fixed allocations `128` and `256`.
The actual post-action law in both rows is the deterministic PMF `δfalse`,
the read is the Boolean indicator, and the two candidate prediction vectors
are constantly `0` and constantly `1`. The sample law is the actual
`productRowLaw` on the dependent coordinate space, with its coordinate
marginals, measurability, and row independence proved. This is a formal
finite probability experiment; no empirical observations are claimed.

The true coordinate means are `0`, their identity with the declared
kernel/source/action/read is proved, and the registered candidate risks are
`0` and `2`. At `u = 1/8`, the N3 radius is `r = 1/4` and the tolerance is
`1/2`; the required two-radius margins hold. Both the candidate resolver
and the N6 audit consume this same product experiment. The toy audit's
authentication predicate is explicitly supplied; the example does not
infer an empirical parent relation from response agreement.

The exact common scalar budget and a conservative bound are proved:

`2 exp(−4) + 2 exp(−8) ≤ 28/45 < 1`.

Thus the failure bounds in this example have a budget strictly below one.
They are the scalar bounds furnished by the general interface, not a claim
that this conservative estimate equals the deterministic experiment's
exact failure probability.

For the negative example, keep the actual sampling rows `δfalse` and change
the declared kernel to `δtrue`. The actual means remain `0` while the
declared kernel means are `1`, so model matching is formally false. At the
explicit all-false record, every empirical mean is exactly its actual mean:
the record belongs to `G` even with `u = 0`. Nevertheless the resulting
zero-radius interval is not `Calibrated` for the wrong kernel. This proves
a failed calibration conclusion, rather than merely omitting a matching
assumption. No separate probability-one statement for that record is needed
or claimed by this boundary endpoint.

This is a scalar concentration budget. It does not replace the registered
total-variation budget, and it does not automatically cover random stopping
or an arbitrarily dependent single trajectory.
