## 1. Weighted mismatch under one relabeling

For encoders

\[
q:X\to Y,
\qquad
r:X\to Z,
\]

and one quotient equivalence `e : Y ≃ Z`, define

\[
m_{e,\mu}(q,r)
=
\sum_{x:e(qx)\neq r(x)}\mu(x).
\]

Lean definition:

`weightedMismatch mu q r e`.

`weightedMismatch_nonneg` proves nonnegativity directly from simplex
nonnegativity.

## 2. Support-level zero semantics

The theorem

`weightedMismatch_eq_zero_iff_supportAligned`

proves the exact equivalence

\[
m_{e,\mu}(q,r)=0
\iff
\forall x,\ \mu(x)>0\Rightarrow e(qx)=r(x).
\]

Thus a zero weighted mismatch is **not** a global exact quotient statement.  It
is exact alignment on the positive-mass support of the chosen source law.

This distinction is essential for learned representations: a partition can
change on currently unvisited source states without affecting the weighted
distance.

## 3. Optimal weighted gauge

For equal-size finite quotient carriers, define

\[
d_{G,\mu}(q,r)
=
\min_{e:Y\simeq Z}m_{e,\mu}(q,r).
\]

Lean definition:

`weightedGaugeMismatch mu q r`.

The finite relabeling space again guarantees that the minimum is attained:

`exists_optimalEquiv_weighted`.

Theorems

- `weightedGaugeMismatch_le`;
- `weightedGaugeMismatch_nonneg`

provide the basic order properties.

## 4. Zero optimal cost

`weightedGaugeMismatch_eq_zero_iff_exists_supportAligned` proves

d_{G,\mu}(q,r)=0

This is the correct occupation-relative notion of representation identity.

## 6. Symmetry and triangle inequality

For one fixed source law `mu`, inverse relabeling preserves weighted mismatch:

\[
m_{e^{-1},\mu}(r,q)=m_{e,\mu}(q,r).
\]

Therefore

\[
d_{G,\mu}(q,r)=d_{G,\mu}(r,q).
\]

Lean theorems:

- `weightedMismatch_symm`;
- `weightedGaugeMismatch_symm`.

Similarly, the composite mismatch support is contained in the union of the two
component mismatch supports.  Nonnegative simplex weights turn the set-union
bound into

\[
m_{f\circ e,\mu}(q,s)
\le
m_{e,\mu}(q,r)+m_{f,\mu}(r,s),
\]

and minimization yields

\[
\boxed{
d_{G,\mu}(q,s)
\le
d_{G,\mu}(q,r)+d_{G,\mu}(r,s)
}.
\]

Lean theorems:

- `weightedMismatch_trans_le`;
- `weightedGaugeMismatch_triangle`;
- `weightedGaugeMismatch_self`.

Thus for one fixed occupation law, weighted quotient gauge again behaves as a
pseudometric-like representation distance modulo relabeling.

The present theorem does **not** call M-OI and must not be described as an

Its interface is deliberately compatible with M-OI:

- M-OI can produce or certify invariant/occupation laws;
- one such law can then be used as the weight `mu`;

## 8. Important boundaries

### One common source law

Symmetry and triangle are proved for comparisons under the **same** `mu`.

If two times use different laws `mu_t` and `mu_{t+1}`, the observed change can
come from both representation drift and occupation drift.  The current theorem
does not identify those effects.

### Zero mass hides structure

Changes on `mu(x)=0` states are invisible.  This is intended, not a defect, but
it means weighted zero is weaker than global exact identity without full
support.

### Still no control bound from representation mass alone

Small weighted gauge mismatch does not by itself bound reward error,
transition-TV error, value loss, or GOA displacement.  A small-mass source set
can still have large conditional reward or transition error.

To obtain control guarantees one needs additional bounded local semantic
defects, for example

\[
|\Delta r(x,a)|\le B_r,
\qquad
D_{TV}(P_x,\widehat P_x)\le B_p,
\]

or directly certified P-QUO-02-type errors after gauge alignment.

### Equal quotient cardinality remains

The finite alignment space is still built from equivalences.  Split/merge
events are outside the current theorem.
